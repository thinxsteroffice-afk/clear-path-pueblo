What "Large Furniture" Actually Means for Pricing

Junk removal isn't priced by item. It's priced by truck volume, and large furniture is deceptive because it's mostly air. A 96-inch three-piece sectional is bulky but hollow; it eats roughly 1/6 of a 15-cubic-yard truck. A 1970s solid oak armoire is half the size and weighs four times as much, and weight is what costs money at the landfill.

Here's the real math for our market. The Pueblo County Landfill (Waste Connections, 22 miles from central Pueblo West) charges by the ton. Our loaded round trip from Pueblo West runs 45 to 70 minutes including scale time. A single sofa weighs 90 to 150 lbs. A sleeper sofa with a steel folding frame weighs 180 to 260 lbs. A slate-bed pool table is 650 to 1,000 lbs in three slate pieces plus a frame.

So the honest breakdown:

  • Single sofa, loveseat, recliner, dresser, or mattress set — $95–$185, curbside or first-floor
  • Sectional (3+ pieces) or a bedroom set — $175–$285
  • Full living room + bedroom, roughly 1/3 truck — $250–$450
  • Slate pool table (disassembly included) — $350–$600 depending on stairs and slate count
  • Safe, gun safe, or upright piano — $300–$750; these are quoted on-site only, never over the phone

Add $25–$60 for a second-story carry down an interior staircase, and $40–$75 for a basement walk-up with a turn at the landing. We tell you the adder before we touch anything.

> The single biggest cost variable in Pueblo West isn't the furniture. It's the 14 feet between the couch and the truck — the doorway width, the stair turn, and whether your HOA lets us stage in the driveway.

Why Pueblo West Specifically Changes the Job

Pueblo West is not Pueblo proper, and the logistics are genuinely different in three ways nobody writing generic "furniture removal" pages accounts for.

Lot sizes work in your favor. Most Pueblo West parcels run 0.5 to 1 acre on the McCulloch grid. Wide driveways mean we back a 15-yard dump trailer within 20 feet of your garage on most jobs. In older Pueblo neighborhoods — Bessemer, the Grove, Mesa Junction — we're often parking on a 1920s street with a 30-foot setback and carrying pieces further. That difference alone is worth 10 to 15 minutes of labor per job.

No curbside bulk pickup exists here. Pueblo West Metropolitan District does not run a municipal bulk-item program. Your trash hauler will take a bagged household bin; they will not take a sectional. This is the single most common surprise for people who moved here from Denver or Colorado Springs, both of which have city bulk programs. Pueblo West does not.

Self-haul is genuinely viable. The Pueblo County Landfill accepts residential loads. A pickup-truck load runs roughly $28 to $45 in tipping fees depending on weight, plus about $12 in fuel for the round trip from Pueblo West. That's real, and we'll come back to it, because for a lot of you it's the right answer.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part that costs us bookings. It's also the part that will save some of you $150.

Skip us if you own a pickup and the piece breaks down. If you have a half-ton truck, one able-bodied friend, and a dresser or a disassembled bed frame, you're looking at roughly $40 in landfill fees and fuel and 90 minutes of your Saturday. We'd charge $95–$185 for the same outcome. Paying us for that is paying $100+ an hour to avoid a chore. Some people happily make that trade — but you should make it knowingly. Same logic for [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) and other light, self-haulable loads.

Skip us if the furniture is genuinely good. A sectional in clean condition, under six years old, no pet damage, no smoke — that's a $150 to $400 Facebook Marketplace listing in the Pueblo West group, and it usually moves in 3 to 10 days if you price it at 25–30% of retail. Habitat for Humanity of Pueblo will often take solid-wood furniture in good shape and will sometimes pick up, at no cost, if it meets their standards. Calling us first is throwing away money. Call us when Marketplace has gone quiet for two weeks or the buyer no-showed twice.

Skip us if you're waiting on an insurance or estate determination. We've had families call after a flood or a death, and we've had to say: don't do this yet. Once it's on the truck, it's gone. If an adjuster hasn't documented the loss or an estate hasn't been settled among siblings, wait. A hauled-away heirloom is not recoverable, and we won't be the reason a family fights. The same caution applies to [estate cleanouts](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) — get the inventory done first.

Skip us for one small item. A single nightstand, a kitchen chair, a folding table. Our minimum is $95 because a truck, two people, fuel, and a landfill trip have a fixed cost regardless of what's on board. Paying $95 to remove a $30 nightstand is a bad trade. Bundle it with something else, or wait until you have a real load.

Skip us if you need it moved, not removed. We haul to the landfill, donation, or recycling. We are not a moving company, we do not carry moving-company cargo insurance, and we won't take your sectional to your daughter's place across town. Hire a mover.

The Failure Modes — What Actually Goes Wrong

Real limitations, named plainly:

  • The piece doesn't fit and never did. Sectionals assembled inside a finished basement, or brought in before a wall went up, sometimes cannot come out intact. Our options are cutting the frame apart on site — which we will do, with your written OK — or leaving it. If you want it out whole and it won't go, we can't fix physics.
  • Weather stops us. A wet spring in Pueblo West turns unpaved driveways and the caliche shoulders on the outer roads into something a loaded trailer will sink into. We'd rather reschedule than leave ruts in your yard or a truck in your ditch. Expect 1 to 3 weather reschedules a year on outlying properties.
  • We won't take everything. No paint, solvents, motor oil, propane tanks, car batteries, tires (some haulers take these; we don't), asbestos-containing material, or medical/biohazard waste. If a hide-a-bed is soaked in something biological, that becomes a different job at a different price — see [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo).
  • Same-day isn't guaranteed. We advertise it and we hit it most days. But on a Saturday in May, or the last three days of any month when leases turn over, our board fills by 8 a.m. If your closing is Friday, book Tuesday.
  • "Donated" is not always true. Plenty of haulers promise donation. The reality in Pueblo County: thrift outlets reject 60–70% of used upholstered furniture on sight — stains, sagging frames, bed-bug policy, or simply no floor space. We'll try, and we'll tell you honestly when it went to the landfill instead. Anyone promising 100% donation of used couches is not being straight with you.

What Makes a Quote Go Up After We Arrive

Nobody likes a moving target. Here's what genuinely changes a price, so you can tell us up front and get an accurate number on the phone:

  • Stairs and turns — a straight run is fine; a 90-degree landing with a low ceiling adds real time
  • Weight you didn't mention — a "dresser" that's a 1940s solid-oak highboy, or a "table" with a granite top
  • Disassembly — pool tables, Murphy beds, wall-anchored built-ins, and bunk beds need tools and 30–60 extra minutes
  • Access — gated communities, narrow alley approaches, or a car in the driveway that nobody can move
  • Hidden volume — "just the couch" that turns out to be the couch plus everything stacked behind it

Send two photos when you call, one of the piece and one of the exit path. It takes 30 seconds and it's the difference between a firm quote and an estimate.
