HOARDING HOUSE CLEANUP SERVICES

A hoarding house cleanup service sends a crew to sort, haul away, and dispose of heavy accumulation in a home. In Pueblo West, that usually means 3 to 5 workers for 1 to 4 days. Most whole-house jobs cost about $1,500 to $6,000. The most severe cases, with a packed garage, outbuildings and waste on the floors, can pass $10,000. A good crew searches for valuables and paperwork before anything goes on the truck. It donates what it can and takes the rest to licensed disposal. It does not fix the cause of the hoarding, and it is not the right call for every house. This page explains what the work involves, what drives the price in Pueblo West, and when you shouldn't hire us.

What a hoarding cleanup actually involves

People picture a truck and a few people with trash bags. A real hoarding job is slower and more careful than an ordinary junk haul because the stakes are different. Families regularly find cash, jewelry, deeds, uncashed checks and military records under years of mail and packaging.

A typical job in Pueblo West follows this order:

  • Walkthrough and scope (30 to 60 minutes). We look at every room, the garage and any outbuildings, and agree on what is being kept, what is being searched and what is going.
  • Safety pass. We check for mouse droppings, sharp objects, unstable stacks and floors that may be weak under the weight.
  • Sort zones. We set up three to four marked areas on the driveway or lot: *keep, donate, recycle/scrap, dispose*. The family decides what goes in "keep." We don't.
  • Paper search. Loose paper gets a hand search, not a toss. This is the slowest part of most jobs, and skipping it is the most common shortcut in the industry.
  • Haul and disposal. Metal goes to scrap, usable furniture goes to donation, and the rest goes to licensed disposal.
  • Broom-clean finish. We leave the floors swept and the rooms empty. This is not the same as a sanitized house (more on that below).

If you only need the haul-away side of a cluttered property, our [hoarding cleanout service in Pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) explains the scope and how booking works.

The Pueblo West factor: mice, outbuildings and big lots

Most hoarding guides are written for city apartments. Pueblo West is different. Many lots are half an acre to several acres, and a lot of homes have a detached garage, a shed, a carport or a pole barn. Accumulation rarely stays inside the house here. It spreads to every covered structure on the property, and then to the open ground.

That changes two things.

First, volume. A hoarded 1,400 sq ft house in town might fill 2 to 3 truckloads. The same house on a Pueblo West acre with a stuffed garage and two sheds often fills 5 to 8. If your property has outbuildings, ask for those to be quoted as separate line items so the price doesn't surprise you. If a shed is beyond saving, it may make more sense to remove the structure along with what's in it. See [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).

Second, rodents and hantavirus. Colorado has one of the highest hantavirus case counts in the country, and deer mice are the carrier. Undisturbed piles in sheds and garages are ideal mouse habitat. The CDC's cleanup guidance is specific:

  • Ventilate the space for at least 30 minutes before going in.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings. That puts the virus into the air.
  • Soak droppings and nests with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution and let it sit for 5 minutes before wiping up.
  • Wear gloves and a respirator rated N95 or better.

If you're cleaning a Pueblo West outbuilding yourself, follow those steps. This is the single biggest health risk in the work, and it is more dangerous than people expect.

> On a rural Pueblo West lot, the house is often the easy part. The shed nobody has opened in eight years is where the risk and the volume are.

What hoarding cleanup costs in Pueblo West

Exact numbers depend on volume, access and how much sorting you want. These are realistic ranges for this market:

| Situation | Typical crew time | Typical cost range | |---|---|---| | 1 to 2 rooms, light to moderate | 4 to 8 hours | $500 to $1,500 | | Whole house, moderate (paths still visible) | 1 to 2 days | $1,500 to $4,000 | | Whole house plus garage, heavy | 2 to 3 days | $3,500 to $7,000 | | House, garage and outbuildings, severe | 3 to 5+ days | $7,000 to $15,000+ |

Four things move the price more than square footage:

1. How much sorting you want. A "haul everything" job can run 30 to 50% cheaper than a job where every box gets searched. Searching takes labor, and labor is most of the bill. 2. Weight, not just volume. Paper, books, canned goods and wet material are heavy. Disposal fees are charged by the ton. 3. Access. A long gravel driveway, a steep lot or an upstairs bedroom with a narrow stairway slows the crew down. 4. Outbuildings and yard accumulation. Scrap metal, old vehicles' parts and piles out on the lot are usually priced separately.

Ask any company for a written quote that says whether sorting and searching are included. If two quotes are $2,000 apart, that difference is usually the answer.

When hoarding cleanup ISN'T worth it, and who shouldn't hire us

This section costs us jobs. Read it anyway.

Don't hire us if the person living there hasn't agreed. Forcing a cleanout on someone with hoarding disorder, which affects about 2 to 6% of adults, is one of the most reliable ways to damage the relationship. Without treatment, the house usually fills up again. If the person is alive, living in the home and opposed, call a therapist trained in hoarding disorder or Pueblo County Adult Protective Services before you call a junk crew. A crew can empty a house in 3 days. It can't keep it empty.

Don't hire a junk removal company for biohazard work. That includes a death that went undiscovered, significant human waste, large volumes of needles, or sewage backup. Those jobs need a dedicated biohazard remediation firm with the right disposal permits. On top of any hauling, that work often costs $1,000 to $5,000+ per affected area. We're a junk removal company. We'll tell you plainly if we walk in and see something outside our lane, and we won't touch it.

Don't hire us first if animals are involved. In an animal-hoarding situation, Pueblo Animal Services needs to be involved before any cleanout. The animals come first, and the property may become part of a case.

Don't hire us before an insurance adjuster or inspector has seen the home. If there's water damage, a sagging floor or a possible claim, clearing it out first can destroy your evidence. Get it documented, then clean.

Don't pay for a full-service crew if the job is small. If it's two cluttered rooms and you have family who can lift, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster usually runs $450 to $700 for about 7 days in this area. That can easily beat our price. You trade money for your own labor and time.

Don't expect "move-in ready." Our finish is broom-clean. It doesn't cover odor removal, carpet replacement, drywall repair or deep sanitizing. In a heavy hoarding house, the flooring under the piles is often ruined. Budget separately for that.

Know the failure modes of cheap operators. Here's what goes wrong:

  • Crews that bag everything without searching it, so cash, titles and family photos go to the landfill.
  • No written scope, so the price "grows" once they're inside.
  • Usable items dumped instead of donated, which inflates disposal fees you end up paying.
  • No rodent precautions in outbuildings, which puts workers, and you, at risk.

How to prepare before the crew arrives

You can lower your cost and stress with a few hours of prep:

  • Make a "must find" list. Wills, deeds, vehicle titles, passports, tax records, medications, firearms, keys, jewelry. Give it to the crew lead.
  • Pick one decision-maker. When three siblings disagree in the driveway, the clock runs and so does the bill.
  • Pull out what matters to you first, if you can do it safely. Even 2 hours of family time on photos and keepsakes saves a lot of crew time later.
  • Check utilities. Working lights and water make the job faster and safer.
  • Tell your neighbors. On a quiet Pueblo West street, a truck and crew on the driveway for 3 days gets noticed. A heads-up keeps it from becoming gossip.
