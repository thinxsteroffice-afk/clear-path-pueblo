CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS DUMP COST

Pueblo West doesn't have a construction debris dump. The nearest legal disposal is the Pueblo County Landfill (Southside Landfill) on Highway 78 west of Pueblo, roughly a 20–30 minute drive from most of Pueblo West depending on whether you're off McCulloch or out past Purcell. Expect roughly $45–$70 per ton for mixed construction and demolition debris with a minimum charge in the $30–$40 range, so even a half-loaded pickup costs about $35–$50 once you factor the minimum. A full-size pickup bed of drywall, framing scrap, and shingles typically weighs 0.75–1.5 tons and runs $40–$95 at the scale. Full-service removal for that same load runs $180–$350 in Pueblo West.

Those are the two real numbers to compare. Everything below explains where each one goes wrong.

What the Landfill Actually Charges You (And What It Doesn't)

Landfill pricing is by weight, not by volume, which is the single biggest surprise for people who've only priced dumpsters. A pickup bed heaped with insulation and vinyl siding might weigh 400 pounds and cost you the $35 minimum. The same bed filled 18 inches deep with concrete, tile, or wet drywall can hit 3,000+ pounds and cost $70–$105.

Rough weights for Pueblo West remodel debris:

  • Asphalt shingles — 230–250 lbs per square (100 sq ft). A 1,600 sq ft reroof tear-off is 4–5 tons, or $180–$350 in tipping fees alone.
  • Drywall — a 4x8 sheet of 1/2" runs 51 lbs. A gutted 12x14 bedroom is roughly 30 sheets, about 1,500 lbs.
  • Framing lumber and OSB — a pickup bed packed tight is typically 800–1,200 lbs.
  • Concrete and flagstone — about 4,000 lbs per cubic yard. Half a yard maxes out most half-ton trucks by weight before it looks full.
  • Dirt and sod — 2,200–2,700 lbs per cubic yard wet, and many facilities charge clean fill at a different rate than mixed debris.

Also budget the things that never show up in a quoted rate: $12–$25 in fuel for a round trip from Pueblo West, 60–90 minutes of your time per run including scale-in and scale-out lines, and the fact that Saturday mornings between 8 and 11 are the busiest window at any Front Range transfer site.

When Hauling It Yourself Is Clearly the Right Call

Be honest about the math. Self-haul wins outright in these cases:

  • You already own a truck and trailer. One trip, under a ton, no help needed — you're out maybe $60 all-in versus $180+ for a service call. That's not close.
  • The debris is clean and sorted. Clean concrete, clean wood, or scrap metal often goes to separate recyclers at reduced or even $0 rates. Scrap steel and copper can pay you: steel runs roughly $100–$180 per ton at Pueblo-area yards, and you keep that.
  • The job is spread over weeks. DIY basement finish where you generate two contractor bags a week? Toss them in the truck on your normal Pueblo run. Paying a haul-off crew for a trickle is wasteful.
  • You're within your budget's tightest margin. If the project has $300 left and $300 buys materials, drive it yourself.

> The honest rule: if you own the truck, the load is under a ton, and it's one trip — haul it. Everything past that, the numbers start turning.

When You Should Not Call Us at All

This is the part most junk removal pages skip.

Do not hire full-service removal for a single small load if you own a truck. A quarter-truckload minimum from most Pueblo-area haulers is $140–$200. The landfill will do the same load for $35–$50. You'd be paying $100–$150 for one hour of not driving. If that hour is worth $150 to you, fine — but say it out loud so the decision is deliberate.

Do not hire us for large-volume clean concrete or dirt. Concrete crushes the pricing model. A dumpster rental with a dedicated clean-fill rate, or a direct trip to a recycler, beats per-truckload pricing badly once you're past a yard or two. We'll say so on the phone. See [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) for where the line actually falls, but the short version is that anything over about 3 cubic yards of clean concrete belongs in a dumpster or a dump trailer, not a junk truck.

Do not hire anyone — us included — for an ongoing new-build. If you're framing a house on a two-acre Pueblo West lot and generating debris for four months straight, a 20-yard roll-off at $400–$550 for a 7–14 day rental is the correct tool. Per-load removal on an active site means calling someone every nine days and paying a trip charge every time. Roll-offs exist for exactly this.

Do not assume we can take everything. Real limitations, plainly:

  • Asbestos-containing material — popcorn ceilings, old floor tile, pipe wrap in pre-1980 Pueblo West homes. We can't touch it. Colorado requires abatement by a licensed contractor, and testing alone runs $300–$800. Neither can you legally dump it.
  • Wet paint, solvents, motor oil, pesticides — household hazardous waste. Pueblo County runs collection events; the landfill won't take liquid paint in the regular stream.
  • Loose fluorescent tubes, ballasts, and old thermostats — mercury. Separate handling.
  • Anything buried in mud after a storm. If your debris pile has been sitting through August monsoon runoff, it can weigh double what it did dry, and both your landfill bill and any weight-based quote go up accordingly.

Failure modes worth naming. Weight-based quotes over the phone are estimates, and the estimate can be wrong by 30–40% when someone describes "a little bit of tile" that turns out to be 300 sq ft of mortar-bedded ceramic. We'd rather look at photos than guess. Second: a two-person crew moving debris down a set of stairs from a finished basement can take three times longer than the same volume sitting in a driveway, and pricing reflects labor, not just volume. Third: if the material is mixed — drywall, shingles, and a couch in the same pile — nothing gets recycled and everything goes to landfill weight. Sorting on site before anyone arrives genuinely lowers your bill.

When Full-Service Removal Actually Wins

Not to be coy about it, here's where paying us makes financial sense:

  • You don't own a truck, or yours isn't rated for it. A half-ton pickup carrying 2,000 lbs of tile is over payload. Blown leaf springs cost $400–$900. One trip's savings doesn't cover that.
  • The debris isn't loaded yet. Landfill pricing assumes the material is already in your vehicle. Loading 4 tons of tear-off shingles by hand is 6–10 person-hours. At any honest valuation of a Saturday, that's the whole cost difference right there.
  • It's more than two trips. Three round-trips to Highway 78 is 4.5 hours and $50 in fuel before tipping fees. Now the $250 service call is competitive.
  • You need it gone on a deadline. Closing, inspection, HOA notice, tenant turnover. A crew shows up once; you don't spend three weekends.
  • Mixed material with heavy items. Old cast-iron tub, hot water heater, and a pile of lath and plaster is not a self-haul job in a sedan-adjacent truck.

For a standard remodel haul-off in Pueblo West, we typically land at $180–$250 for a quarter to third truckload, $300–$420 for a half load, and $500–$700 for a full 15-cubic-yard truck including labor, tipping fees, and drive time. Those are real ranges, not teasers — the variable is weight and access.

What's Different About Pueblo West Specifically

A few things that don't apply in Colorado Springs or Denver:

  • Lot sizes work in your favor. Most Pueblo West properties are one acre or larger with real driveway access. A truck can back to within 20 feet of most debris piles, which is why our pricing here is generally 10–15% below what the same job costs in dense Pueblo neighborhoods with alley-only access.
  • No curbside bulk pickup. Pueblo West Metro District doesn't run a bulk-item program the way some municipalities do. There's no free option to wait for.
  • Wind matters. Sustained 30–40 mph gusts are ordinary here in spring. An uncovered load of loose insulation or shingles on Highway 50 will scatter, and an unsecured load is a citable offense. Tarps and ratchet straps, every trip, no exceptions.
  • Burn restrictions. Burning construction lumber is not a legal disposal method in Pueblo County, and fire restrictions are frequently in effect from May through September.

If your project also involves tearing the structure down, that's a different service and different pricing — see [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) or [deck fence removal pueblo](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo), where the demo labor is usually 60–70% of the total and the hauling is the smaller half.

The Fastest Way to Decide

Answer three questions:

1. Do you own a truck rated for the load? No → get a quote. Yes → keep going. 2. Is it already loaded, or loadable in under 90 minutes? No → get a quote. Yes → keep going. 3. Is it one trip or two? Three or more → get a quote. One or two → drive it yourself and keep the $200.

That's the whole decision. We'd rather you self-haul a small load and call us for the big one than feel oversold on the first job.

If you land on the "get a quote" side, send photos of the pile — not a description. Photos with a person or a door for scale get you a number that holds.
