JUNK REMOVAL COST PER TRUCKLOAD

Junk removal in Pueblo West runs roughly $150 to $650 per truckload, and the price moves with how much of the truck you fill, not how long the crew stays. A typical single-item pickup (one couch, one washer) lands at $125–$185. A quarter-load — a small garage corner, maybe 4 cubic yards — runs $200–$275. A half-load is $300–$425, three-quarters is $425–$525, and a packed full truck (roughly 15–16 cubic yards, about six pickup-truck beds) tops out around $550–$700. Heavy material breaks that model entirely: concrete, dirt, tile, and wet shingles get priced by weight, usually $85–$150 per ton on top of or instead of volume. Below is exactly how those numbers get built, and when hiring anyone is the wrong call.

What "per truck" actually means, and why the number moves

A standard junk removal truck around here holds 15 to 16 cubic yards. That's a specific volume: about 12 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4.5 feet high of stacked material. Most people badly overestimate what they have. A queen mattress, box spring, frame, dresser, and two nightstands — a full bedroom — is about 3.5 cubic yards, or a quarter of a truck. A two-car garage that "looks completely full" but has a walking path through it is usually 8–10 cubic yards, not 16.

Here's the measuring trick that will save you an argument at the curb: a standard 6-foot pickup bed, filled level with the rails, is about 2 cubic yards. Stand in front of your pile and count how many pickup beds it would take. Six beds equals one full truck. Four beds is roughly a three-quarter load. This is the single fastest way to sanity-check any quote you're given, from us or anyone else.

The reason honest companies won't quote a firm price over the phone is that volume and weight both move, and customers routinely describe a 5-yard pile as "just a few things." A photo text usually gets you within one pricing tier. An on-site look gets you exact.

Real Pueblo West price ranges by job type

These are the ranges we actually see in the 81007 zip and the surrounding county roads:

  • Single appliance or single piece of furniture: $125–$185. A fridge or freezer adds $25–$40 for refrigerant handling and recycling.
  • Standard two-car garage cleanout: $425–$650, typically 10–14 cubic yards, 2.5–4 hours of crew time.
  • Yard and tree debris after a windstorm: $250–$500 for a half to three-quarter load. Green waste is bulky and light, so it fills volume fast without hitting weight fees.
  • Above-ground pool or hot tub removal: $400–$750 depending on size, deck attachment, and whether the crew can get equipment to the back of the lot.
  • Full estate or hoarding cleanout on a 1-acre Pueblo West lot: $1,800–$6,500, spread across 3 to 10 truckloads over one to three days.
  • Concrete, rock, dirt, or brick: priced by weight, typically $120–$200 per ton delivered to the pit, because 3 cubic yards of concrete weighs about 6,000 pounds and will max a truck's axle rating long before it fills the box.

That last one is the number most cost pages leave out, and it's the one that generates the most surprised phone calls. If your "small" pile is a broken sidewalk section, you are not buying a truckload. You are buying tonnage. Our [concrete and rock removal page](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) breaks that math out separately for exactly this reason.

What's inside the price (the part nobody itemizes)

A $400 half-load isn't $400 of profit. Roughly:

  • Disposal fees: $60–$140. Pueblo-area landfill and transfer station rates run in the neighborhood of $45–$70 per ton for general municipal solid waste, with minimum charges that make small loads disproportionately expensive to dump. Mattresses and tires carry their own per-item fees — commonly $15–$35 per mattress and $5–$15 per tire. Call the county to confirm current posted rates before you plan around them; they change.
  • Labor: $120–$220. Two people, 1.5 to 3 hours, including the drive.
  • Drive time and fuel: $30–$70. Pueblo West is spread out. A run from the north end of Pueblo West to the disposal site and back is generally 35–50 minutes round trip, and a job that needs three loads eats two-plus hours in transit alone.
  • Truck cost, insurance, workers' comp, and overhead: the rest.

That structure explains something useful: the second half of a truckload is always cheaper per cubic yard than the first half. The disposal minimum, the drive, and the setup are already paid for. Going from a quarter-load at $250 to a half-load at $375 costs you $125 for four extra cubic yards — about $31/yard — versus $62/yard on the first quarter. If you have borderline items you're unsure about, throw them on. Splitting one job into two visits three weeks apart is the most expensive thing you can do.

> The cheapest cubic yard you will ever buy is the last one in the truck. The most expensive is the one that makes you book a second trip.

When you should NOT hire us

This is the section that costs us money, and it's the one worth reading twice.

Skip junk removal entirely if you have one or two items and a friend with a truck. The Pueblo County landfill will take a residential pickup load for a fraction of what any service charges. If you can borrow a truck, load it yourself, and give up two hours of a Saturday, you'll spend maybe $30–$60 in dump fees and gas versus $150+ for us. We are selling you labor, a disposal account, and the elimination of a Saturday. If your Saturday is cheap and your back is fine, do it yourself.

Don't call us for a single mattress or a single appliance if there's a free alternative. Most furniture and appliance retailers in Pueblo will haul away the old unit on delivery for $0–$50. If you're replacing a washer next Tuesday anyway, paying us $150 today to remove the old one is a straight waste. Same with a mattress — ask the retailer first. Our [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) and appliance pages exist for the cases where that option isn't on the table, not to talk you out of a free haul-away.

Rent a dumpster instead if your project runs longer than two days. A 10- to 20-yard roll-off in the Pueblo area typically runs $350–$600 for a 7-day rental including a set tonnage allowance. If you're gutting a bathroom over three weekends, or slowly working through a garage, a dumpster sitting in your driveway beats paying a crew to show up four separate times at $250 a visit. Full-service removal wins on speed, single-visit convenience, and not having a container on your lot; it loses on long, self-paced projects. Pueblo West's larger lots mean most homes here can actually fit a roll-off without a right-of-way permit, which is not true in denser parts of Pueblo proper.

We are not the low bidder on heavy debris. If you have 8 tons of broken concrete, a demolition contractor with a dump trailer and a tonnage account will usually beat us. Our efficiency is in mixed household junk, not in dense rubble.

We can't take everything. No liquid paint, motor oil, solvents, pesticides, gas cylinders, or asbestos-containing material. Those go through Pueblo County household hazardous waste channels, not our truck. Ammunition and propane tanks are hard no's. If a third of your pile is hazardous, our quote covers the other two-thirds and you still have a problem to solve.

Access and terrain change the price, sometimes a lot. A lot of Pueblo West properties have 150- to 400-foot driveways, gravel approaches, and side gates under 36 inches. A crew carrying items 200 feet uphill is doing meaningfully more work than one backing to a garage door, and the quote reflects it — commonly $50–$150 more on the same volume. If the truck can't get within reasonable carry distance because of soft ground after a rain, we may reschedule rather than sink a loaded truck in your yard.

Hoarding-level cleanouts are not priced from photos. Anyone quoting you a flat number sight-unseen on a severe cleanout is either padding heavily or planning to renegotiate mid-job. Those need a walkthrough.

How to get an accurate quote in one call

  • Photograph the pile from three angles, including one shot with a person or a door in frame for scale.
  • Name the heavy stuff explicitly — concrete, dirt, roofing, tile, sod, safes, pianos.
  • Say where it is: back yard behind a gate, second floor, detached shed 300 feet from the driveway.
  • Ask whether the quote includes disposal fees. Some outfits quote labor and add tonnage after. Ours includes standard disposal; heavy material is called out separately before we start.
  • Ask for the tier boundaries in writing. "Half load, $375, up to 8 cubic yards" is a real quote. "Starting at $99" is a hook.

Pricing varies by neighborhood mostly through drive time. Jobs near the Pueblo West Metro District core price at the ranges above; runs out toward Colorado City, Rye, Beulah, or the far county roads can carry a $40–$85 travel adjustment because a single load can consume 90 minutes of round-trip driving.
