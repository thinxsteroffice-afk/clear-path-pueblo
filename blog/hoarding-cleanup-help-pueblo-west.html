HOARDING CLEANUP HELP, PUEBLO WEST

If you're searching "hoarding help clean up near me" from Pueblo West, here's the direct answer: ClearPath Junk Removal handles hoarding cleanouts across Pueblo West, Pueblo, Colorado City, Rye, Avondale, and Boone. A typical Pueblo West job runs $2,400 to $6,500 for a full single-story home, takes 2 to 5 working days with a 2–3 person crew, and starts with a free walkthrough where we quote by trailer volume, not by guesswork. We sort — donations to Pueblo-area thrift partners, metal to scrap, the rest to the Southside landfill about 15 miles away. Call or book a walkthrough and we'll tell you the number before anyone touches anything. We do not do biohazard remediation, and we'll say so up front if that's what you actually need.

What a Pueblo West hoarding cleanout actually costs

Pricing on this work is volume-driven. Our 16-foot dump trailer holds roughly 16 cubic yards — about two and a half pickup beds. Here's how that maps to real jobs in the 81007 area:

  • Single room or garage bay (Level I–II clutter): $450–$900, usually one crew day
  • Whole 1,100–1,500 sq ft home, Level III: $2,400–$6,500 across 2–5 days
  • Level IV–V with structural damage, rodent activity, or blocked egress: $9,000–$18,000+, and often needs sub-trades we coordinate but don't perform
  • Detached shop or outbuilding on a Pueblo West acre lot: add $600–$2,200 depending on what's stacked outside
  • Per-item surcharges that hit almost every job: refrigerant appliances $35–$50 each, mattresses $40–$65, CRT televisions $25–$45, passenger tires $8–$25

The "Level" numbers come from the Institute for Challenging Disorganization's Clutter-Hoarding Scale, a five-point rating that professional organizers and cleaning companies use. Level I is a cluttered but functional house. Level V means no working plumbing, no safe electrical, and animal or human waste present. Knowing your level before you call saves everyone an hour, and it changes who you should be calling.

Why Pueblo West jobs price differently than Pueblo proper

Two local factors move the number, and neither shows up on national pricing pages.

Lot size. Standard Pueblo West residential lots run about one acre, with plenty of 2.5- and 5-acre parcels out toward the McCulloch Boulevard fringe and south of Highway 50. That's a blessing operationally — we can back a trailer within 20 feet of a garage door instead of shuttling to a curb. It's also a liability, because an acre absorbs decades of overflow. Roughly a third of the hoarding calls we take in Pueblo West involve more volume outside the house than inside it: dead vehicles, lumber piles, collapsed sheds, sun-rotted patio furniture. Budget for it. If the exterior is the bulk of the problem, [yard debris removal pueblo](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) or [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) may be the cheaper line item to book separately.

Disposal distance and fees. Everything leaving a Pueblo West property crosses roughly 15 to 20 miles to disposal on the south side of Pueblo — a 25–35 minute haul each way. A five-trailer job burns 4 to 6 hours in windshield time alone. Landfill tipping fees are charged by the ton and get adjusted periodically, so we confirm current rates the week of your job rather than quoting a stale number. Ask any company that gives you a flat "we handle disposal" answer to break out the tipping line — if they won't, they're padding it.

When a hoarding cleanout is NOT worth it — read this part

This section costs us work. It's still the most useful thing on the page.

If the person living there hasn't agreed, don't book us. Forced cleanouts — the adult children who fly in for a weekend, the landlord who schedules a surprise — have a bad track record. Homes cleared without the resident's buy-in commonly refill within 6 to 24 months, and the relationship damage tends to outlast the clean floor. Hoarding disorder is a recognized clinical diagnosis with prevalence estimates around 2% to 6% of adults, climbing sharply past age 60. It responds to therapy. It does not respond to a dump trailer. If nobody in the picture is working with a clinician, you're buying a very expensive 90-day result.

> A cleanout without treatment isn't a solution. It's a reset button on a process that will run again — and you'll have spent $4,000 to press it.

If there's biohazard, we're the wrong call. Human waste, decomposition, needle debris, or heavy animal urine saturation requires licensed remediation with containment, respirators, and antimicrobial treatment. That work runs $3,000 to $10,000+ on top of hauling and is legally and medically distinct from junk removal. We'll walk it, tell you plainly, and hand you names. Any hauler who says "yeah, we'll take care of that" for $1,200 is either lying or about to do it wrong.

If you can do it yourself, do it yourself. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster rents in the Pueblo market for roughly $400–$550 for a 7-day window with a 2-ton included weight and $65–$95 per additional ton. If you have three able-bodied helpers, a free weekend, and the clutter is Level I–II boxes and furniture rather than embedded debris, you'll spend maybe $600 total and save $1,800. We'd rather tell you that than take the job. The math flips when the volume passes about four trailer loads, when stairs are involved, or when nobody available can lift 50 pounds repeatedly for eight hours.

Other honest failure modes:

  • Sentimental sorting kills schedules. A homeowner who wants to review every item turns a 3-day job into 9 days. We bill hourly past the quoted scope — typically $85–$125 per hour for a two-person crew — so indecision has a meter running.
  • Hidden pests or structural rot. We've opened rooms in Pueblo West where the subfloor was gone. That's a contractor's problem, discovered at your cost, mid-job.
  • We can't legally take everything. Paint, solvents, pesticides, propane cylinders, ammunition, and asbestos-containing material stay behind. Household hazardous waste goes to Pueblo County's collection program, and quantities are capped per visit. Plan a separate afternoon.
  • Bad weather stops us. Pueblo West wind gusts past 40 mph make loading an open trailer genuinely unsafe. We reschedule; that's not padding.

How we run the job, step by step

1. Free on-site walkthrough, 20–30 minutes. We photograph, estimate cubic yards, flag hazards, and give a written range with a not-to-exceed ceiling. 2. You designate a keep zone. One room, one table, or one clearly-taped area. Anything in it never gets touched. 3. Sort-as-we-go. Donatable goods to local thrift partners, metal to scrap, e-waste to recycling, the remainder to landfill. On a typical Level III home, 25% to 40% of volume avoids the landfill. Donation receipts go to you. 4. Cash-and-valuables protocol. Envelopes of money, jewelry, deeds, firearms, and photographs turn up on nearly every hoarding job. Found items go into a labeled bin handed to the homeowner or executor daily. 5. Broom-clean finish and a final walkthrough before we invoice.

Local help that isn't us

Hauling is one piece. These matter more:

  • Colorado Crisis Services — 1-844-493-8255, free, 24/7, for immediate mental health support.
  • 2-1-1 Colorado — dial 211 for Pueblo County social services referrals.
  • Pueblo County Adult Protective Services — the right call if an older adult or at-risk adult is living in unsafe conditions and can't self-protect. Reporting is not the same as evicting someone.
  • Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenant office — if you've received a notice about exterior storage, call them and ask for a documented compliance window before deadlines run. Many enforcement timelines allow 30 days or more when there's a scheduled remediation plan in writing.
  • A therapist experienced with hoarding disorder or OCD-spectrum treatment — the only intervention on this list that changes the outcome twelve months out.
