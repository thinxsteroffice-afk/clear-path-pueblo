What "near me" actually means out here

On a quarter-acre lot in a city neighborhood, a junk crew parks at the curb and walks 30 feet. That carry distance is real labor, and honest companies price it in. We don't charge a separate "long carry" fee, but a job with a 150-foot haul-out takes about 40 minutes longer than the same volume at the curb, and that shows up in which volume tier you land in once the trailer is packed.

What we charge and what drives it up

Volume sets the base price. Four things move it:

  • Weight-dense material. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, and rock hit the scale long before they fill the trailer. A trailer of shingles from a single-layer tear-off can run 3,000+ lbs at maybe 40% of visual capacity. We price those loads separately — see [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Regulated items. Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units carry a $35 refrigerant recovery fee each. Mattresses and box springs are $25 per piece. Passenger tires run $6–$10 each because Colorado bars whole tires from landfill burial.
  • Electronics. Colorado's 2013 landfill ban on e-waste means TVs, monitors, and computers go to a recycler, not the dump. Old rear-projection and CRT TVs are the worst offenders — expect $20–$45 each depending on size.
  • Disassembly. A hot tub is a 90-minute cut-down job for two people before a single piece goes in the trailer. Same for a swing set anchored in concrete footings.
  • Stairs and tight interior access. A second-floor bedroom set adds roughly 25–30 minutes over the same load sitting in an open garage.

Most single-item calls — one couch, one fridge, one mattress — land at the $180 minimum. Most garage cleanouts land between $380 and $650. A full [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) with 20 years of accumulation, shelving, and a dead treadmill typically takes 2.5 to 3.5 hours and fills the trailer.

When you should not hire us

This is the part most junk removal pages skip, and skipping it costs homeowners real money.

If you own a truck and have a free Saturday, DIY beats us on price — not close. Pueblo County disposal minimums for a pickup load are in the neighborhood of $30 to $55 depending on the facility and what you're bringing. Two trips, some fuel, and three hours of your own time replaces a $380 call. If your back is fine, the items are light, and the stuff is already at the curb, we are the expensive option and we'll say so on the phone.

If you're generating debris over weeks, rent a dumpster instead. A 20-yard roll-off in the Pueblo area runs roughly $425 to $600 for a 7-day rental with a tonnage allowance. If you're gutting a bathroom over three weekends, or slowly clearing a property before a sale, that container sitting in your driveway is cheaper per cubic yard than three separate service calls. We do full-service loading, which is worth paying for when you can't or won't lift — not when you have time and a container would sit there catching your debris as you produce it.

If it's clean scrap metal, sell it, don't pay to remove it. Pueblo has active scrap yards. A dead water heater, a steel swing set, cast iron radiators, or a pile of appliance carcasses can be worth $0.04 to $0.11 per pound as ferrous scrap. A ton of clean steel might return $80 to $180. Paying us $260 to haul something a yard would pay you for is a bad trade, and we'd rather tell you now than after you've read your invoice.

If it's genuinely usable furniture, donate it and take the deduction. Habitat ReStore and local charities will often pick up furniture in good condition at no cost. A donated sofa with a documented fair market value of $150 is a free removal plus a write-off. The catch: they decline stains, tears, pet damage, sagging frames, and nearly all mattresses. A rejected donation pickup wastes 10 days of your calendar, so be honest with yourself about condition first.

There are things we cannot take, ever. Paint and stains that aren't dried solid, motor oil, solvents, pool chemicals, pesticides, propane tanks, ammunition, medical sharps, and asbestos-containing material. Pre-1980 popcorn ceiling, floor tile, and pipe wrap in Pueblo West homes are real possibilities, and Colorado requires abatement by a licensed contractor before demolition-type work. If we open a wall on a shed teardown and find suspect material, we stop, and you'll have paid us for a partial job.

Where DIY quietly stops making sense

The DIY math flips fast, and here's the honest threshold. Renting a 6x12 dump trailer runs about $85 to $120 per day locally, and you need a vehicle with a real hitch and brake controller to pull 4,000+ lbs safely. Add fuel, add two landfill minimums, add a friend you owe a favor to, and add the fact that a chest freezer weighs 180 lbs and a hot tub shell weighs 400. By the time a job needs three pickup loads, you're at roughly six hours of your weekend and $150 in hard costs — and our number is often within $150 of that with none of the lifting or scheduling.

The other tipping point is liability. A dropped safe, a torn rotator cuff, or a gouged garage door on your own homeowner's policy costs more than any quote on this page.

Local specifics worth knowing before you call

  • Metro District covenants. Pueblo West Metropolitan District enforces standards on visible outdoor storage — inoperable vehicles, accumulated debris, and materials stored in view. If you've received a compliance letter, note the correction deadline when you call; those are typically 10 to 30 days, and we prioritize scheduling around them.
  • Wind is a design constraint here. Gusts over 40 mph are routine in spring. A partially loaded trailer with loose insulation or cardboard is a hazard, so we tarp before leaving your property. If you're staging debris outdoors for a week, weigh it down or you'll be picking it out of your neighbor's fence line.
  • Hail seasons create backlogs. After a significant hail event, roofing tear-offs flood every disposal facility in the county. Expect longer scale lines and 2 to 5 day scheduling delays in June and July.
  • Estate and probate timelines. If you're clearing a home for sale, book 3 to 4 weeks out from your listing date. Our [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) jobs average 1.5 days, and the sorting stage — deciding what's kept — is always the long pole, not the hauling.
  • Photos beat descriptions. Text us pictures and we'll give a price range within about 20 minutes. On-site is where the number becomes firm.
