What a Pueblo West appliance pickup actually costs

  • Curbside or garage, single unit: $85 to $145. Washer, dryer, dishwasher, range, standard fridge.
  • Each extra unit, same trip: $35 to $60. A matched washer-dryer pair usually lands around $125 to $175 total.
  • Full kitchen set of four: $210 to $320. Fridge, range, dishwasher, over-range microwave.
  • Basement or second floor: add $25 to $50 per unit. Stairs are the single biggest cost driver we have.
  • Water heater: $95 to $160, higher if it has not been drained.

A quick reference on weight, because this is what people underestimate when they consider doing it themselves:

| Appliance | Typical weight | |---|---| | Side-by-side refrigerator | 250 to 320 lb | | Chest freezer | 150 to 200 lb | | Washer | 150 to 200 lb | | Electric dryer | 100 to 125 lb | | Electric range | 130 to 160 lb | | Dishwasher | 75 to 100 lb | | 40-gallon water heater, drained | 120 to 140 lb |

That last row is the trap. Water weighs 8.34 pounds per gallon, so a full 40-gallon heater carries another 333 pounds of water inside it. People try to tip one and it goes through the drywall.

What we do with the appliance after it leaves your driveway

Refrigerators, freezers, window air conditioners, and dehumidifiers all contain refrigerant. Under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act, that refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped or landfilled. It is not legal to just crush a fridge, and reputable scrap yards will not take one with the charge still in it.

Older units carry more than refrigerant. Anything built before 1979 may have a PCB-containing capacitor. Chest freezers and some ranges made before 2000 can have mercury switches in the lid or door. Units from before 1995 typically ran R-12, which is a far more aggressive ozone-depleting compound than the R-134a in a 2005 fridge or the R-600a isobutane in a 2020 one.

Practically, that means your fridge goes to a processor, not straight into a hole. The steel shell, the copper in the compressor, and the aluminum in the condenser coil get separated and sold. Roughly 75% of a typical appliance by weight is recoverable metal.

> The refrigerant recovery step is the whole reason appliance pickup costs more than hauling an equivalent weight of furniture. You are paying for a legal disposal path, not for muscle.

When you should NOT hire us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out. There are several situations where calling us is the wrong move, and we would rather tell you now than take a $95 job you resent.

  • The appliance still works and is under 8 years old. A functioning washer or fridge has real resale value. A clean, working unit moves on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo West for $75 to $250, and the buyer hauls it. You go from paying $95 to collecting $150. That swing is worth two days of waiting for a message back. We are not the answer for a working appliance unless you have already tried and given up.
  • You are buying a replacement anyway. Big-box retailers commonly haul the old unit away for $25 to $50 with delivery, and often for free on certain categories during promotions. If your new dryer is already on order, ask for the haul-away add-on. It is cheaper than us every time. The catch: most retailers will only take the unit if it is disconnected and accessible, and they will refuse a gas appliance that is still hooked up.
  • You have a truck, a helper, and a free afternoon. A pickup, a set of ratchet straps, and one strong friend gets a dryer to a disposal facility for the cost of gas and the gate fee. Budget $15 to $35 for the appliance disposal fee, roughly $8 to $12 in fuel for the round trip from Pueblo West, and about 90 minutes of your day. If you value that time under $40 an hour, do it yourself.
  • You are chasing scrap money. Shred steel has traded roughly $150 to $220 per ton in recent years. A 250-pound refrigerator is an eighth of a ton, so the scrap payout is realistically $15 to $25 before you subtract fuel. Anyone telling you there is money in hauling your own fridge is off by an order of magnitude. The copper in the compressor is worth more per pound, but you are not getting that unless you tear it down yourself.
  • You need the gas line or water line permanently capped. We disconnect appliances. We shut off a gas valve and disconnect a flex line, but we are not licensed plumbers, and we will not cap a line, pull a permit, or sign off on anything a home inspector needs to see. If you are selling the house and the inspection is next week, call a licensed plumber for the cap and us for the haul.
  • The unit is frozen in place by a remodel. Built-in wall ovens and cabinet-trapped dishwashers sometimes cannot come out without cutting trim or countertop. We will tell you on site rather than start sawing. That is a cabinet guy's job.

The other honest limitation: we cannot promise your appliance gets recycled rather than landfilled in every case. Refrigerant recovery, yes, always. But a rusted-through washer with a seized motor sometimes has a disposal path that ends at the landfill because nobody will process it. We will tell you which it is if you ask.

What to do before we get there

Fifteen minutes of prep drops your price and speeds up the visit. Our crew averages 15 to 20 minutes per appliance on a clean job and 60 to 90 minutes for a full kitchen set with stairs.

  • Empty the fridge or freezer completely. Food weight adds up, and a leaking freezer in the truck bed is a bad afternoon for everyone.
  • Drain the water heater. Hose to the bottom valve, run it out the door, allow 20 to 45 minutes for a 40-gallon tank with sediment. If you skip it, we will do it, but we bill the wait.
  • Shut off the gas at the appliance valve on gas ranges and dryers before we arrive, and let it sit an hour.
  • Measure your door. A standard side-by-side is 32 to 36 inches wide. Interior doors are often 30 inches, and older Pueblo West manufactured homes sometimes run 28 inches. If the fridge came in before the door frame went up, we may have to pull the doors off the unit.
  • Clear the path. Move the shoe rack. Sounds trivial. It is not.

Pueblo West specifics that trip people up

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, and that changes the disposal picture in ways city residents do not deal with. There is no municipal bulk-item pickup day here. Trash service is private subscription, and bulky-item allowances vary by hauler, with most excluding refrigerant-bearing appliances outright. Calling your trash company and waiting for a special pickup that never comes is the most common way people lose two weeks.

Second: Pueblo West covenants restrict outdoor storage of junk and inoperable equipment. A dead fridge parked on the side of the house is exactly the kind of thing a neighbor complaint gets filed on. Half-acre lots make it feel invisible. It is not.

Third: appliances carry a model and serial plate. Dumped units on county land get traced back to the property that bought them, and illegal dumping enforcement in Colorado runs into the hundreds of dollars in fines plus cleanup cost. If a stranger offers to take your fridge for free, ask where it is going. Some of those guys are legitimate scrappers. Some drive it out toward the reservoir and leave it.
