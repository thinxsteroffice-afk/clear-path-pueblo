COMPANIES THAT REMOVE OLD FURNITURE

Yes. Several furniture retailers will take your old furniture away, but nearly always with conditions. The usual deal is that they haul off one old piece for each new piece they deliver, charge $25–$150 for it, and only take it if it's ready at the door when the truck arrives. If you aren't buying new furniture, have more pieces than you're replacing, or need something moved out before delivery day, a retailer won't help. That's when Pueblo West homeowners call a local hauler like ClearPath Junk Removal. This page explains how retailer haul-away really works, what hiring a junk removal crew costs, and when you shouldn't hire us.

Which furniture companies actually remove old furniture

Retailer take-back programs fall into three groups:

  • National chains with delivery. Most big furniture and department-store chains offer "haul-away" or "removal" as an add-on at checkout. Prices usually run $25 to $150 per order. Some chains waive the fee during promotions or for mattress purchases.
  • Mattress retailers. Most will take your old mattress when they deliver a new one, often for $0–$50. Many require the old mattress to be bagged, and many refuse one with visible stains or signs of bed bugs.
  • Local and independent stores. Policies vary widely. Some Pueblo-area stores will haul off an old piece for free to close a sale, and some have no removal service at all. Ask before you pay.

A lot of Pueblo West households buy furniture in Colorado Springs, roughly 45 miles north. That matters because the delivery crew is often a third-party contractor working a tight route. They schedule you into a 4-hour window and have little room to wait while you empty a dresser or unbolt a sectional.

The fine print retailers don't advertise

Few people read the haul-away terms until the delivery crew is standing in the doorway. These are the conditions that cause the most trouble:

  • One-for-one only. Buy one sofa and they take one sofa. They won't take the loveseat, the recliner, and the broken coffee table too.
  • Same category. A new mattress usually gets you mattress removal only. It doesn't cover the old headboard or the dresser.
  • Must be ready at delivery. Drawers empty, electronics unplugged, and the piece moved somewhere the crew can reach. If it isn't ready, most crews leave it behind and the fee isn't always refunded.
  • Condition refusals. Crews can decline pieces that are wet, moldy, pest-infested, or so broken they're unsafe to carry.
  • No basements or tight spaces. Some retailers won't carry old pieces up from a basement or across steep, icy, or unpaved approaches. Plenty of Pueblo West homes sit on gravel drives on half-acre to multi-acre lots, and crews may refuse a long carry.
  • Power recliners and sleeper sofas. Crews often won't disconnect power recliners or lift sleeper sofas, which can weigh 200+ pounds because of the steel frame inside.

> Retailer haul-away works best when you're swapping exactly one piece for one new piece and the old one sits near the front door. Outside that situation, it tends to fall apart.

What removal costs if you don't go through a retailer

These are the options Pueblo West residents usually weigh, with typical Southern Colorado numbers:

  • Do it yourself. A rented in-town pickup or cargo van generally starts around $19.95 plus about $0.79–$1.29 per mile, plus fuel and landfill gate fees. A round trip from Pueblo West to a Pueblo-area drop-off is often 25–40 miles. It's the cheapest route, provided you have two people who can safely carry a 100–350 pound couch.
  • Donation pickup. Charities pick up for free, but scheduling often runs 1–3 weeks out. Drivers also reject a large share of what they're offered, especially upholstered pieces with stains, tears, pet hair, or smoke odor.
  • Junk removal. Pricing is usually based on how much truck space your items take up. Around Pueblo, expect roughly $75–$150 for a single item, $250–$400 for about half a truckload, and $450–$650 for a full load, depending on weight and access. A single-item pickup usually takes 15–30 minutes once the crew arrives.

When hiring ClearPath is NOT worth it

This part costs us business, but it'll save you money. Don't hire a junk removal company in these situations:

  • You're replacing one item and the retailer offers haul-away for under $50. If the old sofa is by the front door and the new one arrives Tuesday, pay the retailer. They're already coming to your house, and our single-item minimum will probably cost more.
  • The furniture is in good, clean condition and you aren't in a hurry. A solid-wood dresser or a clean, recent sectional can go to a Pueblo thrift store, the Habitat ReStore, or a free listing online. It may take a week or two, but it's free, and somebody gets to use it.
  • You have a truck, a strong friend, and one or two items. Doing it yourself costs a few hours and some gas. Paying $100+ to avoid a 30-minute job doesn't make sense if you're able-bodied and have the vehicle.
  • The item might still be worth money. Mid-century pieces, real hardwood, and name-brand recliners less than 3–5 years old often sell. We price by the space items take in the truck, and our quote doesn't include buying anything from you. Try selling first.
  • You're hoping for a guarantee that nothing goes to the landfill. We donate and recycle what we reasonably can, but a stained 12-year-old sectional has almost no donation value, and nobody can honestly promise otherwise. The EPA estimates Americans threw out about 12.1 million tons of furniture and furnishings in 2018, and roughly 80% of it ended up in landfills. Anyone who claims 100% diversion on worn-out upholstery is overselling.

There are also a few things we do poorly or can't do. We aren't the cheapest option for a single small item like a nightstand. We can't match the price of a free retailer haul-away. If a mattress has an active bed bug infestation, it needs to be bagged and sealed first, and you should treat the home before we remove it. Otherwise the problem just follows you.

When hiring us makes sense

Junk removal becomes the better deal when retailer haul-away doesn't fit your situation:

  • You have multiple pieces to clear. Once you're past 3–4 items, a volume price usually costs less than several retailer fees, several donation appointments, or several trips to the landfill.
  • You aren't buying anything new. You're downsizing, clearing a rental, or finally emptying the spare room.
  • Access is difficult. Basements, second floors, narrow halls, and long gravel carries on large Pueblo West lots are the reasons retailer crews most often refuse a pickup.

A 60-second decision checklist

Answer these in order and stop at the first "yes":

1. Am I buying a replacement from a store that offers haul-away for under $50? Use the retailer. 2. Is the item clean, sturdy, and less than about 10 years old? Donate it or sell it. 3. Do I have a truck, a helper, and only one or two pieces? Haul it yourself. 4. Anything else, meaning multiple pieces, heavy items, hard access, or a deadline: call a junk removal company.

Mattresses follow their own timeline. Most last 7–10 years, so if you're replacing one, plan the removal as part of the purchase instead of letting the old one sit in the garage.
