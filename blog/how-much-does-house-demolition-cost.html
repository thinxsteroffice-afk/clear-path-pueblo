HOW MUCH DOES HOUSE DEMOLITION COST

Full house demolition in Pueblo West runs $8 to $25 per square foot, which puts a typical 1,400 sq ft ranch on a slab at roughly $12,000 to $22,000 all-in. A detached garage is $2,500–$6,000. A single-wide mobile home teardown and haul-off is $3,500–$7,500. A shed or barn under 400 sq ft is usually $400–$1,800. Those numbers include machine time, hauling, and landfill tipping — but they do *not* include asbestos abatement, well and septic abandonment, or bringing in fill dirt, and in Pueblo County those three line items are where budgets actually break. Below is the real cost stack for this market, including the parts most quotes leave off, and an honest section on when demolition is the wrong move.

What Actually Drives the Price in Pueblo West

Square footage is the headline number, but it's the third or fourth most important variable. Here's what moves a bid the most, roughly in order:

  • Foundation type. Slab-on-grade is cheapest. A crawlspace adds $1,500–$3,000. A full basement adds $4,000–$9,000 because you're now removing walls *and* buying 60–120 cubic yards of fill to bring the hole back to grade.
  • Asbestos and lead. More on this below — it's the single biggest wildcard, and in Colorado it's not optional.
  • Access. A house on a half-acre Pueblo West lot with 20 feet of clear driveway is a 2-day job. Same house wedged behind a fence line with a 10-foot gate, mature cottonwoods overhead, and neighbors 15 feet off the property line can double labor hours and force hand-demo on 30% of the structure.
  • Disposal weight. Wood-frame debris runs about 0.4–0.5 tons per 100 sq ft of house. Plaster, brick, and tile double that. Concrete is heaviest — a 1,200 sq ft slab at 4" thick is roughly 18–22 tons by itself.
  • Salvage. Hardwood flooring, cabinets, fixtures, and steel can knock $500–$2,500 off a bid if you allow time for pulls. Rushing the schedule kills salvage value.

The Colorado Asbestos Rule Most Homeowners Don't Know About

This is the item that surprises people, and it's specific to Colorado. Under CDPHE Regulation 8, Part B, a demolition of any structure other than a single-family residential dwelling requires an asbestos inspection by a state-certified building inspector before demolition — *regardless of the building's age*. There's no "it's a new building" exemption for commercial, multi-family, or many accessory structures.

Practical costs in this market:

  • Certified inspection and sampling: $400–$900 for a typical residence, with results in 3–7 business days.
  • If asbestos is found and exceeds trigger levels (260 linear feet, 160 square feet, or the volume of a 55-gallon drum), abatement by a licensed contractor typically runs $3,000–$15,000, and a 10-working-day CDPHE notification period applies before demo starts.
  • Popcorn ceilings, 9x9 floor tile, black mastic, transite siding, and duct wrap in Pueblo homes built before 1985 are the usual hits.

Even for a single-family home where the inspection isn't mandated, spend the $500. A landfill that rejects a load on suspicion of asbestos will cost you more than the test did, and Pueblo County's disposal facilities do turn loads away.

> Budget $1,000 for testing you might not need. It is cheaper than a rejected load, a stop-work order, or a CDPHE penalty — and it's the one line item that turns a $14,000 job into a $28,000 job overnight.

Permits, Utilities, and the Pueblo County Line Items

Before a machine touches the structure, this is the checklist for an unincorporated Pueblo West property:

  • Demolition permit through the Pueblo Regional Building Department. Expect a modest flat fee plus plan review — call for current numbers, and allow 1–3 weeks from application to issued permit.
  • Gas disconnect with Black Hills Energy — the meter must be pulled by the utility, not cut. Schedule 2–3 weeks out; homeowner cost is often $0–$300.
  • Electric disconnect with San Isabel Electric or Black Hills, depending on the service. Same lead time.
  • Water/sewer through Pueblo West Metropolitan District — the tap must be properly capped, and the District will want to see it. Failing to cap correctly can leave you paying a base water charge on a vacant lot.
  • Septic abandonment. Many Pueblo West parcels are on septic. The tank must be pumped, crushed or filled, and permitted through the Pueblo Department of Public Health and Environment. Realistic cost: $600–$1,800 plus permit.
  • Well abandonment. If the property has a well, plugging must be done to Colorado Division of Water Resources standards, generally by a licensed contractor: $1,200–$3,000.

Add it up and the "soft costs" on a Pueblo West house demo commonly total $2,500–$6,000 before a single wall comes down. Any bid that doesn't itemize these is not a complete bid.

Sample Cost Breakdowns for This Market

1,400 sq ft slab ranch, clear lot, no asbestos found: Mobilization and utility coordination $800 · Asbestos inspection $600 · Permits $400 · Structure demo and load-out $6,500 · Disposal, ~7 tons plus concrete $3,200 · Slab removal $4,200 · Rough grade and 20 yards of fill $1,400. Total ≈ $17,100.

900 sq ft house with full basement, asbestos tile found: Soft costs $1,900 · Abatement $5,400 · Demo $5,800 · Disposal $3,000 · Foundation removal $5,500 · 90 yards of imported fill at $22/yard delivered and placed $2,000 · Compaction and grade $1,200. Total ≈ $24,800.

Detached 24x24 garage, slab left in place: $2,800–$4,200, typically 1 day on site.

Timeline for a straightforward house: 2–4 days of active demolition, 3–6 weeks door to door once permitting and utility scheduling are counted. Anyone promising next-week demolition either already has the permit or is planning to skip it.

When House Demolition Is the Wrong Call

This section costs us work, and it's the most important part of the page.

  • You still have a mortgage on the property. Demolishing your lender's collateral without written consent is a default event. Get it in writing first or don't proceed. This stops more projects than money does.
  • The problem is repairable and insurable. Fire, hail, water, and mold losses are often covered. Homeowners who demolish first and file second frequently lose the claim. Call your adjuster before you call a demo contractor.
  • You can't fund the site restoration. Demolition creates a vacant lot that still owes property tax and still has to be mowed and kept clean of weeds. If your budget covers teardown but not the $1,500–$5,000 of fill, grading, and seeding, you'll own a liability instead of a buildable lot. Budget the finish before you buy the start.
  • The lot won't support what you want to build. Setbacks, easements, septic percolation, and Pueblo West Metro water tap availability all get re-evaluated on new construction. Confirm you can build the replacement *before* removing the existing structure, which may be grandfathered in ways a new build won't be.
  • The house has rental value in a tight market. A rough 900 sq ft rental in Pueblo can still produce meaningful monthly income. A vacant lot produces none. Run the numbers on a $25,000 rehab against a $20,000 demolition before deciding.
  • You only want the contents gone. Plenty of people ask about demolition when what they actually need is a cleanout at $400–$1,500 instead of a teardown at $18,000. If the structure is sound, [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) or a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) solves it for a fraction of the cost.

How to Compare Bids Without Getting Burned

  • Require the bid to state who pulls the permit and who pays landfill tipping. Tipping is billed by weight and is the most common change order.
  • Ask what happens if the number of tons exceeds the estimate. A per-ton overage rate in writing (typically $60–$110/ton delivered) beats a vague "we'll adjust."
  • Ask for the final grade spec: rough grade, finish grade, or grade plus seed. These differ by thousands of dollars.
  • Verify general liability and workers' comp certificates directly with the insurer, not a PDF the contractor emails you. A subcontractor injury on your uninsured job becomes your problem.
  • Get the debris destination named. Illegal dumping citations in Pueblo County follow the property owner, not just the hauler.

Three itemized bids on the same scope will usually land within 15–20% of each other. A bid that's 40% below the others is missing a line item — find out which one before you sign.
