JUNK REMOVAL PRICE SHEET NEAR ME

Pricing for junk removal in Pueblo West is almost always sold by volume in the truck, not by the item. Here is the honest range you'll see locally in 2026:

| Load size | Truck space | Typical Pueblo West price | |---|---|---| | Minimum / single item | 1/8 truck | $95 – $145 | | Quarter load | 1/4 truck | $185 – $260 | | Half load | 1/2 truck | $320 – $450 | | Three-quarter load | 3/4 truck | $450 – $600 | | Full load | Full 15-yd truck | $600 – $800 |

Add-ons: mattresses +$35–$55 each (Pueblo transfer stations charge a separate mattress fee), appliances with refrigerant +$40–$60, tires +$12–$25 each, paint and hazardous materials not accepted. Same-day service in the 81007 zip usually runs a $25–$50 surcharge.

Why nobody publishes a flat "price sheet" — and what to demand instead

Searching "junk removal price sheet near me" usually turns up national franchise pages with a truck graphic and no numbers, because their pricing is set per-franchise and the corporate site can't commit. That's frustrating and it's also somewhat honest: a half load of pillows and a half load of wet drywall cost us very different amounts at the gate.

What you should insist on is not a fixed sheet but a written volume quote before anyone lifts anything, with the disposal fees itemized. In Pueblo County, the tipping fee at the landfill runs roughly $52–$68 per ton for mixed municipal waste, with a minimum charge around $28–$35 even for a nearly empty trailer. Concrete, dirt, and roofing shingles are priced separately and are heavier per cubic yard than almost anything else you'll load. That's why an honest hauler will quote a garage full of cardboard boxes at $320 and a half-truck of broken patio concrete at $500+ for the identical visual volume.

> Volume sets the price for light junk. Weight sets the price for heavy junk. Any company that won't tell you which one applies to your pile is guessing — and guessing high.

What drives the number on your driveway

Five factors move a Pueblo West quote up or down more than anything else:

  • Stairs and carry distance. A sofa 8 feet from the truck versus a sofa down a finished basement and around a corner is roughly a 20–35% labor difference. Long acreage driveways off Purcell or McCulloch add real minutes.
  • Weight-dense material. Concrete, brick, landscape rock, roofing tear-off, and dirt are priced by the ton, not the yard. Budget $150–$300 per ton hauled for [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Disassembly time. A hot tub that has to be cut into panels is a 90–150 minute job before the first piece hits the truck. Same story for a 24-foot [above-ground pool](/services/above-ground-pool-removal-pueblo) or a bolted-down [trampoline and playset](/services/trampoline-playset-removal-pueblo).
  • Whether we can donate or recycle any of it. Steel appliances, scrap metal, and clean e-waste offset our own disposal cost, and a good hauler passes some of that back — usually $20–$75 off a mixed load.
  • Access and weather. Unpaved Pueblo West driveways after a monsoon storm in July or August, or a February morning where the truck can't back up an iced slope, add time nobody quoted for.

When hiring us is the wrong call — read this part

This is the section most junk removal sites leave out. Here's where you should not pay us.

You have one small item and a truck-owning friend. A single dresser, a couple of bags of clothes, a bicycle. Our minimum is roughly $95–$145 because a truck, fuel, insurance, and two people rolling out to your address costs about that before we touch anything. If you can borrow a pickup, the Pueblo County landfill minimum of about $28–$35 makes a DIY run cost a third of what we charge. We'll tell you this on the phone and lose the job. That's fine.

You're doing a long, slow remodel. If you're generating debris over six weeks, a 10–20 yard dumpster rental at roughly $350–$550 for a 7-day period beats calling us three separate times at $300+ per trip. Junk removal wins on speed and no-labor-required; dumpsters win on duration and cost per yard. If you're gutting a kitchen over a month, rent the box. We're the wrong tool. (Where we do beat a dumpster: HOA-restricted lots where a container can't sit in the street, and jobs where you physically can't carry things out yourself.)

Your load is 80% clean scrap metal or clean appliances. Scrap yards in Pueblo pay for that. A truckload of steel, copper wire, and old water heaters may be worth $60–$200 to you rather than a $400 bill from us. Strip out the metal, then call us for the rest.

You expect a firm price over the phone from a photo. We'll give you a range, and the range will be wide — often ±30% — because photos flatten piles and hide what's under the tarp. If a competitor gives you an exact number sight-unseen, one of two things happens: they padded it heavily, or they'll revise it upward in your driveway once your stuff is half-loaded. Neither is good for you.

You have hazardous material. We do not take paint, solvents, motor oil, propane tanks, asbestos-containing floor tile or siding, medical waste, or ammunition. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events with per-household limits and you'll need to handle those separately. If your garage is mostly old paint cans, we're going to leave most of it and you'll still owe for the trip.

You need it done in the next two hours on a Saturday in May. Spring is our busiest stretch. Same-day is often genuinely unavailable, and when it is available it carries that $25–$50 surcharge. If your timeline is rigid and your budget is tight, those two constraints fight each other.

The failure modes to watch for in any quote

  • The "we'll see when we get there" quote. Get a number in writing before loading starts. Once half your junk is in the truck, your negotiating position is gone.
  • Volume measured in "trucks" without dimensions. A 15-yard dump body and a 10-yard trailer are both "a truck." Ask for cubic yards and the truck's bed dimensions. Ours is roughly 12' x 8' x 4.5'.
  • Surprise weight overages. If your load includes concrete, rock, or tear-off shingles, ask explicitly: *is this priced by volume or by ton?* Get the per-ton rate before we roll.
  • Fuel or travel fees appearing at the end. Pueblo West to the transfer station is a real drive. It should already be in the quote, not bolted on after.
  • No proof of insurance. Ask for a certificate. A dropped water heater through a stair riser is a $1,200–$3,000 repair, and you want that to be someone else's problem.

How to get an accurate quote in ten minutes

Photograph the pile from three angles, plus one wide shot showing the path from the pile to where a truck can park. Measure roughly — a pile 8 feet long, 5 feet wide, and 4 feet high is about 6 cubic yards, which is a little under half a 15-yard truck. Tell us up front about: stairs, concrete or dirt, mattresses, refrigerant appliances, tires, and anything with fuel in it. Those six items account for nearly every surprise charge in this industry.

Then have us confirm on-site before loading. A quote that survives a walk-around is a quote you can hold someone to.
