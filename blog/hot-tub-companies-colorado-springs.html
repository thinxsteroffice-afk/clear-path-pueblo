HOT TUB COMPANIES COLORADO SPRINGS

If you're searching for hot tub companies in Colorado Springs, you're after one of three things: buying a spa, getting one serviced, or getting one hauled off. The Springs has all three — dealers along Academy and Powers sell and service, a handful of haulers remove. But if the tub sits in Pueblo West, a Colorado Springs company drives 45 miles each way down I-25 and builds roughly 90 minutes of round-trip windshield time into the quote. Expect $350–$650 from a Springs-based crew versus $250–$450 from a Pueblo-based one for the same 700-pound shell. This page covers the removal side: real pricing, the 400 gallons of water, the 240-volt disconnect, and when you should not pay anybody at all.

Why a Colorado Springs Company Costs More in Pueblo West

Nobody drives to Pueblo West for free. A hauler based near Fillmore and I-25 is looking at about 50 miles to a Pueblo West address off McCulloch or Purcell — call it 100 miles round trip. At 12 mpg in a loaded dump trailer rig and $3.15/gallon diesel, that's roughly $26 in fuel before anyone touches a wrench, plus two hours of crew time at $45–$60/hour loaded labor cost.

Then there's the disposal leg. A Springs company either hauls your tub back north to the El Paso County transfer station or dumps locally in Pueblo — and if they're not set up with a local account, they pay gate rates like anyone else. The practical result is that out-of-town quotes for Pueblo West hot tub jobs tend to land $100–$200 above local ones, and the arrival window is usually a half-day block instead of a one-hour window, because you're the second stop on a route built around Springs addresses.

That's not a knock on Springs companies. Several of them are excellent. It's arithmetic.

What Hot Tub Removal Actually Costs Here

Real numbers for Pueblo West and Pueblo, based on how these jobs price out:

  • Standard 6–8 person acrylic spa, drained, ground level, clear path to the driveway: $275–$425
  • Same tub, still full of water: add $75–$125, or drain it yourself and keep that money
  • Tub on a raised or wrapped deck requiring cut-out: $450–$700
  • Tub in a backyard with a 36-inch gate and no side access: $500–$800, because the shell gets cut into 4–6 pieces on site with a reciprocating saw
  • Swim spa or 12-foot unit: $700–$1,400, and often two trips

Dry weight on a typical 84" x 84" spa runs 600–900 pounds. Fill it and you're at 3,600–4,800 pounds — which is why nobody moves one with water in it, and why the deck it sits on was engineered for that load whether the previous owner knew it or not.

Disposal at the Pueblo landfill is billed by weight, generally in the $40–$55 per ton range with a minimum charge, so the tipping fee itself on a single spa is usually under $30. Confirm current gate rates before you plan a DIY run — they change, and hours are shorter than people expect.

The Water Problem Nobody Puts in the Quote

A full spa holds 350–500 gallons. Where that water goes matters more in Pueblo West than almost anywhere else in the state, because a large share of properties out here run on septic systems.

Dumping 400 gallons of bromine- or chlorine-treated water into a septic tank is a bad afternoon. That volume alone can push a 1,000-gallon tank into hydraulic overload and flush solids into the leach field, and the sanitizer residual works against the bacteria that make the tank function. A leach field replacement in Pueblo County runs $8,000–$20,000. Do not drain a hot tub into a floor drain, laundry standpipe, or toilet if you're on septic.

The safer sequence:

  • Turn the heater and sanitizer feed off and let the water sit 48–72 hours so free chlorine drops below 0.1 ppm — test strips cost about $12 and settle the question
  • Check pH; 7.2–7.8 won't burn your landscaping
  • Pump it out with a 1/4 HP submersible at roughly 25 gallons per minute — about 20 minutes for 500 gallons — through a garden hose to a spread-out area of gravel or unplanted ground
  • Do not send it downhill toward a neighbor's foundation, a drainage easement, or the arroyo
  • Never drain saltwater-system spas onto anything you want to grow again

> The most expensive part of a hot tub removal is almost never the tub. It's the septic tank, the deck board, or the buried conduit somebody guessed at.

The Electrical Disconnect Is a Real Step, Not a Formality

Nearly every 6-person spa in Pueblo West is hardwired to a 240-volt, 50-amp GFCI disconnect mounted within sight of the tub, usually 5–10 feet away per code. That's not a plug. Somebody has to kill the breaker at the panel, verify dead, and separate the whip.

A reputable hauler will disconnect a spa that's already been shut off at the panel. What a hauler should not do is leave a live conduit stub in your yard. Options after the tub is gone:

  • Leave the disconnect box and conduit in place, breaker off and labeled — free, and useful if you ever add a spa, EV charger, or shed circuit
  • Have an electrician remove the circuit back to the panel — typically $150–$300
  • If the run is buried in PVC across the yard, budget more; nobody knows where it goes until they dig

Same goes for the pad. A 4-inch reinforced concrete pad, 8x8 feet, weighs around 3,800 pounds. Breaking it out and hauling it is a separate job — see [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) — and it typically adds $350–$600. Most people leave the pad and put a shed or a fire-pit seating area on it.

When Hiring Anyone Is the Wrong Call

This is the part that costs us jobs, and it should.

Don't pay for removal if the tub still runs and is under about 8 years old. A functioning 2018-or-newer spa with a good shell and no leaks sells locally for $800–$2,500. Listing it and letting the buyer haul it turns a $400 expense into a four-figure gain. The catch is that most buyers won't move it themselves, so budget a $300 hauling credit into your asking price and be patient — these listings can sit 3–8 weeks.

Don't pay for removal if you have three able-bodied people and access to a trailer. A ground-level tub with a clear path is genuinely a DIY job: pump-out, disconnect, tip it onto a furniture dolly, strap it to a 6x12 utility trailer. Total cash outlay is roughly $70–$90 — a trailer rental at $45–$60 a day, dump fee under $30. You need one person who is not guessing about the breaker. If nobody in the group is comfortable at the panel, stop and hire out.

If the tub is in Briargate, Rockrimmon, or Fountain, a Springs-based crew will beat our number, because we'd be the ones driving 45 miles. Where removal goes sideways:

  • Second-story decks and rooftop installs. Crane time starts around $650/hour with a two-hour minimum. A $400 removal becomes a $1,700 project.
  • Tubs frozen solid. A spa that lost power in a Pueblo West January can hold 3,000+ pounds of ice. It cannot be drained, cannot be tipped, and cannot be moved until it thaws — sometimes 5–10 days of above-freezing weather. Nobody can work around this.
  • Tubs built into a structure. If the deck framing was assembled around the shell, the deck comes apart first. That's carpentry, not hauling, and it usually means a separate [deck fence removal pueblo](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) scope.
  • Sale-pending homes. Buyers occasionally want the spa. Removing it two weeks before closing has cost sellers real negotiating room.
  • Recycling expectations. An acrylic shell over closed-cell polyurethane foam is not recyclable in any practical sense in southern Colorado. The pumps, heater element, and steel frame get pulled for scrap — realistically 40–90 pounds of recoverable metal out of an 800-pound unit. Any company promising a "green" hot tub disposal is describing that 10%, not the shell. The shell goes in the ground. We'd rather say so than sell you a story.

Questions Worth Asking Any Company You Call

  • Is the quote all-in, or does it exclude drainage, deck cutting, and the disconnect?
  • Will you cut the shell if the gate is under 40 inches, and is that priced already?
  • What's the arrival window — a one-hour window or a four-hour block?
  • Do you carry general liability, and can you send the certificate? For a crew hauling 800 pounds past your siding, $1,000,000 is the number to look for.
  • Where does the tub actually go, and what gets pulled for scrap?

Any company that won't answer the fifth question straight is worth skipping.
