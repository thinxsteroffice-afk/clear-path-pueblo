FURNITURE REMOVAL COST: $75–$800

Furniture removal in Pueblo West runs $75 to $150 for a single item (one recliner, one dresser, one mattress), $150 to $250 for a quarter-truck load (a couple of chairs plus a table), and $550 to $800 for a full 15-cubic-yard truck, which is roughly the contents of a three-bedroom living room and two bedrooms. Most Pueblo West jobs land between $180 and $375 because the typical call is two or three pieces, not a whole house. Add $25 to $50 per flight of stairs, and expect the low end of every range if your items are already in the garage or driveway. Same-day and weekend slots typically carry a $25 to $50 premium. Those are cash-out-the-door numbers, disposal fees included.

What You're Actually Paying For

The price is not for the furniture. It's for truck space, labor minutes, and the tipping fee at the other end — and that third one is why Pueblo West pricing looks different from what you'll find on a national cost calculator.

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no municipal bulky-item pickup here. Colorado Springs residents inside city limits can sometimes schedule a large-item collection; a Denver homeowner has Denver Large Item Pickup. In Pueblo West, your trash service is a private contract, and almost none of those contracts include a couch. That single fact removes the "just wait for the free city pickup" option that most articles on this topic assume you have.

The rest breaks down roughly like this on a $200 job:

  • Disposal: $40 to $65 in landfill tipping fees for a partial load, plus $15 to $25 per mattress if the facility charges a separate mattress fee
  • Labor: two people for 30 to 60 minutes on site, plus 35 to 50 minutes of round-trip drive time from most Pueblo West addresses to the disposal site
  • Truck: fuel, insurance, and maintenance on a dually with a 15-cubic-yard dump bed
  • Everything else: scheduling, the phone call, the guy who shows up when it's 19 degrees and blowing sideways off the mesa

> The mile-and-a-half gravel driveway is the Pueblo West surcharge nobody warns you about. A 20-minute job that takes 40 minutes of driving is priced like a 60-minute job, because it is one.

Real Pueblo West Price Examples

These are typical all-in ranges for this market. Your quote moves with access, weight, and how much of the truck you fill.

  • Recliner or armchair, garage-level: $75 to $115
  • Queen mattress and box spring: $85 to $130 for the pair
  • Standard three-seat sofa: $110 to $165
  • Sleeper sofa (150 to 250 lbs of steel frame): $125 to $185 — the hide-a-bed mechanism is why it costs more than a regular couch
  • Large L-shaped sectional, 3 to 5 pieces: $175 to $295
  • Full bedroom set (bed, dresser, mirror, two nightstands): $225 to $325
  • Upright piano: $250 to $450; console or baby grand, $400 to $650
  • Full dining set with china hutch: $200 to $300
  • Whole-home furniture clearout, 12 to 15 cubic yards: $550 to $800

The single biggest lever on your final number is whether the truck leaves full or half-empty. Two customers with identical sofas can pay $110 and $165 — the difference is one of them also had a broken dresser and a bag of pillows to throw in, and the other made us drive 14 miles for one item.

The Three Ways to Get It Cheaper

Consolidate. If you know a mattress is going out in three weeks when the new one arrives, hold the couch and do both at once. Two separate single-item calls run $170 to $260 combined. One combined pickup is usually $145 to $190. That's a real $50 to $80 saved for zero effort.

Stage it. Furniture already at the garage door or curb, disassembled where it comes apart, cuts labor time enough to move most quotes down 10% to 20%. A bed frame that's already broken down saves 10 to 15 minutes; a sectional with the legs off fits in half the truck footprint.

Bundle it with the project you're already doing. Furniture is rarely the only thing leaving. If you're clearing a [garage cleanout in Pueblo West](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or handling an [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo), the furniture rides along at volume pricing instead of item pricing — usually 30% to 40% cheaper per piece than calling separately.

When Furniture Removal Is Not Worth Paying For

This is the part that costs us money to write, so read it carefully.

You own a pickup and you have exactly one item. A half-ton truck plus one helper plus a $40 to $65 tipping fee gets a sofa out of your house for well under what we charge. The drive from most of Pueblo West to a Pueblo County disposal site is 20 to 25 minutes each way. Total investment: about 90 minutes and $50. If your Saturday morning is worth less than $60 an hour and your back is fine, do it yourself. Call the landfill first to confirm hours and current rates — they change, and a wasted trip is the whole savings.

The piece is genuinely worth something. A solid-wood dresser, a real leather sofa in good shape, a mid-century set — those sell. Pueblo West and Pueblo Facebook Marketplace move usable furniture fast, and a decent sofa clears $75 to $250. Paying us $140 to haul away something you could have sold for $180 is a $320 swing in the wrong direction. Post it. Give it two weeks. Call us for whatever doesn't sell.

It's donatable and you can get it there. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and local thrift operations take clean, structurally sound furniture — no rips, no stains, no smoke, no water damage, no bedbug history. Some will pick up for free. That's $0 versus $110 to $185. The catch is real: donation centers reject more than people expect, pickup windows can be two to four weeks out, and they will not take mattresses, box springs, or anything with a broken frame.

You're clearing an entire house over multiple weekends. A 20-yard roll-off runs roughly $325 to $475 for a 7-day rental with a weight allowance. If you're going to fill it — furniture plus flooring plus drywall plus the shed contents — the dumpster wins on cost per cubic yard. It loses if you only half-fill it, and it loses if you don't have a driveway that can take a 22-foot container without cracking.

Failure modes, named plainly:

  • Weight overages. A truck-based quote is volume-based. Sopping-wet upholstery from a leaking basement, a waterlogged sofa off a covered patio, or a sand-filled sectional can trip weight surcharges at the scale. Say something upfront if the item's been wet.
  • Access you didn't mention. A sectional that came in before the stair railing went up may not come out. A 400-lb armoire on a second floor with a 32-inch turn at the landing sometimes requires disassembly — that's an extra 45 to 90 minutes and a higher price than the phone quote.
  • Contamination. Bedbugs, mold, rodent nesting, or biohazard change the job entirely. Those pieces get wrapped and handled differently, and the price goes up 50% to 100%. Nobody is happy about it, but hiding it means the crew declines on arrival and you've lost a day.
  • Landfill-only items. Not everything gets a second life. Particle-board furniture that's delaminated, foam mattresses past their life, and anything that got rained on for a season goes to the landfill regardless of who hauls it. If keeping it out of the landfill is your reason for hiring a service, ask directly what percentage of your specific load will actually be diverted. The honest answer on most furniture jobs is 10% to 30%, not 90%.

How to Get a Quote That Doesn't Move

Vague calls produce vague numbers, and vague numbers get revised in your driveway. Give whoever you call these five things and the phone price should hold:

  • Exact item count and type — "sectional" could be three pieces or seven
  • Where it sits right now — upstairs, basement, garage, back forty
  • Driveway and gate width, plus whether a 22-foot truck can turn around
  • Condition — wet, broken, infested, or fine
  • Whether anything else is going — this is where the money is

Ask for the price to include disposal fees, fuel, and labor. A quote that excludes tipping fees isn't a quote. Ask whether the crew charges by item or by truck volume; for three or more pieces, volume pricing is almost always cheaper, and any company that won't tell you which one they're using is quoting you loose on purpose.
