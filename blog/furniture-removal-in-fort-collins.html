Handling a Pueblo West Furniture Job From 165 Miles Away

About a third of our furniture calls come from someone who doesn't live here. Usually it's an adult child in the Front Range metro dealing with a parent's house. The process works without you on site if a few things are set up first:

  • Access: a lockbox code, a neighbor with a key, or a property manager meeting us. We'll text a photo of the locked door when we leave.
  • A clear "stays vs. goes" list: blue tape on anything that stays works better than a written list, because nobody has to guess what "the brown dresser" means.
  • Photos before quoting: send 4–6 photos of each room and we'll give you a price range, usually within 1 business day.
  • Photos after the job: we send pictures of the empty rooms so you can confirm everything's gone without a 5-hour round trip.
  • Payment: card over the phone. You don't need to be there.

If the job is more than furniture, like a full house of 40 years of belongings, see our [estate cleanout service](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo). It's priced and scheduled differently from a few-item pickup.

What Furniture Removal Costs in Pueblo West

These are typical southern Colorado ranges, not a fixed price list. Your quote depends on volume, weight, stairs and how far we carry things. We price by how much of the truck your items fill. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly one single-car garage packed to shoulder height.

  • Single item (armchair, dresser, table): about $75–$150
  • Sofa or loveseat: about $100–$175. Sleeper sofas run higher because the metal frame pushes them to 250–350 lbs.
  • Sectional (3–5 pieces): about $150–$275
  • Bedroom set (bed frame, dresser, 2 nightstands): about $175–$300
  • Quarter truckload: about $175–$250
  • Half truckload: about $300–$400
  • Full truckload: about $500–$650

Things that raise the price:

  • Stairs: a second-floor sectional takes 2 people about 3 times as long as a ground-floor one.
  • Disassembly: bunk beds, wall units and entertainment centers that won't fit through a door intact take an extra 15–30 minutes.
  • Pianos, gun safes and pool tables: these are specialty items, not ordinary furniture, and are quoted separately. An upright piano weighs 300–500 lbs. A slate pool table can top 700 lbs.
  • Mattresses: many facilities charge extra to take them. Our [mattress removal page](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) covers this.

Get the quote as a single all-in number that includes labor, loading, transport and disposal. If a hauler quotes a low truck price and then adds a separate "dump fee" when they arrive, that's how a $150 job becomes $260.

When Hiring Us Is Not Worth It

Most of the time, the cheapest way to get rid of furniture isn't paying a junk removal company. Here are the cases where you should keep your money.

You have 1 or 2 pieces in good shape and a free weekend. A clean couch or solid-wood dresser listed on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist with the price set to $0 usually gets picked up within 24–72 hours in Pueblo County. Free furniture goes fast. You pay nothing and someone else does the carrying.

The furniture is donation-quality and you can reach a drop-off. Thrift stores and the Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Pueblo accept clean, working furniture. Some offer free pickup for larger items, though scheduling can take 1–3 weeks. If you aren't in a hurry, that's free and the furniture gets reused. Be realistic, though. Donation centers turn down a large share of what's offered. Stains, pet damage, tears, missing hardware, particleboard that's been moved twice, and anything with a smoke smell usually get rejected. Call first so you don't haul a sofa across town only to be turned away.

You own a truck and have a friend who owes you a favor. If you can load a pickup and drive to the transfer station yourself, you'll pay the disposal fee and nothing more. Our labor, truck and insurance are what you're paying for. If you already have the truck and the help, you don't need them.

You're moving and the furniture could go with you. Before paying to remove a piece, check what it costs to replace it at the new place. Hauling a $1,200 sectional away and buying a new one next month is a bad trade if the movers would have charged $60–$90 to bring it.

You want it gone today and it's a Saturday in peak season. We can't always do same-day. From late spring through early fall, and at the end of each month when leases turn over, our schedule often fills 2–4 days out. If "gone in 3 hours" is a hard requirement, call a few companies. Don't wait on us.

> Paying us to remove one decent couch you could give away free by Tuesday is not a good use of $125. We'd rather tell you that than take the job.

Our honest limitations:

  • We serve the Pueblo area, not the Front Range. See our [service areas](/areas). Anything north of Colorado Springs is outside what we can price fairly.
  • We don't resell or consign your furniture. If a piece is worth real money, like mid-century designer pieces or solid antiques, sell it yourself or through a consignment shop. We donate usable items when we can, but you won't be paid for them.
  • We can't take hazardous material hidden in furniture: propane tanks in a patio set, paint cans in a cabinet or fuel in a stored mower. Take those out beforehand.
  • Bed bugs: tell us upfront. We can still take the furniture, but it has to be wrapped, and nothing from that load can be donated.

When Paying for Removal Makes Sense

The fee is worth it when your time, your back or the deadline costs more than the invoice. Examples:

  • Heavy or awkward items: sleeper sofas, recliners with power mechanisms, and armoires that take 2–3 people and a dolly.
  • Several rooms at once: once you pass about 5–6 large pieces, a truckload price usually beats renting a truck ($20–$40 plus mileage), paying disposal fees and losing a Saturday.
  • Rental turnovers and closings with a deadline: a landlord losing $50–$60 a day in rent on an empty unit comes out ahead paying to clear it in one afternoon.
  • Out-of-town owners: the remote setup described above.
  • Anyone who shouldn't be lifting 150+ lbs: older homeowners, people recovering from injury and anyone living alone.

For furniture-only jobs in town, our main [furniture removal service page](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) covers what we take and how scheduling works. If the furniture is mixed with appliances or garage clutter, one truck visit for everything usually costs less than separate trips.

How a Pueblo West Furniture Pickup Works

1. Send photos or book an on-site estimate. Photos are faster. On-site estimates are free and make sense for more than half a truckload. 2. Get a price range. We confirm the final price on site before loading anything. If it's higher than you expected, you can say no and pay nothing. 3. Point, we carry. You don't have to move anything to the curb. We bring it from wherever it sits: basement, second floor or the back bedroom. 4. Sorting. Usable pieces go to donation when possible, metal frames go to scrap recycling, and the rest goes to disposal. 5. Sweep-up. We clear the spot where the furniture was. A couch that's been in place for 12 years leaves a surprising amount behind.

Most single-room jobs take 30–60 minutes on site. A full house of furniture with a 2-person crew usually runs 3–5 hours.
