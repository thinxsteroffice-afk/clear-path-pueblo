DO I NEED A JUNK REMOVAL LICENSE?

No. Colorado does not issue a junk removal license, and neither does Pueblo County. There is no statewide occupational license for hauling junk, and because Pueblo West is unincorporated — a metropolitan district inside Pueblo County rather than a city — there is no municipal business license to pull either. If you are a homeowner hiring someone, "are you licensed?" is the wrong screening question; ask for a certificate of insurance and a USDOT number instead. If you are starting a hauling business, what you actually need is business registration, insurance, workers' compensation the day you hire your first person, and a USDOT number once your truck or truck-and-trailer crosses 10,001 lbs gross vehicle weight rating.

That answer surprises people on both sides of the transaction, so the rest of this page explains what *does* apply, where the real legal lines sit in Pueblo County, and — the part most hauling companies won't write — the situations where hiring any junk removal company is a waste of your money.

Two different people ask this question

Roughly half the people searching this are Pueblo West homeowners who want to know whether the guy with the dump trailer who quoted them $280 is operating legally. The other half are people thinking about starting a side hustle with an F-250 and a 14-foot dump trailer.

Both get the same core answer and need different follow-ups:

  • Homeowners: hauling your own junk from your own property in your own truck requires zero licensing. No permit, no registration, nothing. You can load a couch, drive it to the landfill, and pay the gate fee.
  • Business starters: the absence of a "junk removal license" does not mean the absence of obligations. It means the obligations live in vehicle law, tax law, employment law, and waste law instead of in one tidy license.
  • Property managers and real estate agents: your broker or insurer may require a "licensed and insured" vendor. Since the license doesn't exist, the only enforceable half of that phrase is the insurance. Get the certificate.

What actually applies in Pueblo County

Colorado has no general business license at the state level. What a legitimate Pueblo West hauler typically carries:

  • Entity registration with the Colorado Secretary of State. An LLC filing runs about $50 online, with a periodic report around $25 each year. A sole proprietor using a business name files a trade name registration for roughly $20.
  • A Colorado sales tax license, sometimes. Hauling labor is generally not a taxable service in Colorado, so many haulers don't need one. If the company resells scrap, appliances, or furniture it pulls out of your garage, it does. A standard retail license is about $16 for a two-year period plus a $50 deposit that is refunded after enough tax is remitted.
  • Workers' compensation from the first employee. Colorado requires coverage for any employee, including part-time and family. Penalties for going without can reach $500 per day for a first offense and climb from there. A two-person crew at a hauling company is not cheap to insure — physical labor classifications are among the higher rates.
  • Commercial auto and general liability. A single-truck operation in southern Colorado commonly pays $2,500–$6,000 per year for commercial auto and $800–$2,500 for a $1M/$2M general liability policy. A personal auto policy will not cover a truck used for paid hauling, and that is where uninsured operators actually get exposed.
  • A USDOT number for intrastate commercial vehicles over 10,000 lbs GVWR. This is the one most Pueblo West side-hustlers miss. A 3/4-ton pickup pulling a loaded dump trailer blows past 10,001 lbs combined almost immediately. The number itself is free to obtain; operating without one when required is not.

> The phrase "licensed and insured" is half marketing. In Colorado junk removal, only one of those two words describes something a regulator actually issues.

Where a commercial driver's license comes in: the federal line is 26,001 lbs. Under that, no CDL. A truck-and-trailer combination over 26,001 lbs with a trailer rated above 10,000 lbs requires a Class A. Most residential [junk removal in Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) runs on equipment deliberately specced to stay under that threshold, because the insurance and driver-pool math changes sharply above it.

The jobs where a real permit or certification is required

This is the honest version of "do I need a license." For general household junk, no. For these, yes — and the requirement attaches to the work, not to the hauling:

  • Refrigerant-containing appliances. Federal rules require a certified technician to recover refrigerant from a fridge, freezer, window AC, or dehumidifier before it is disposed of or scrapped, and the receiving facility is supposed to get a signed statement confirming it. Civil penalties for improper venting run well into five figures per day per violation. Any [appliance removal in Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) should include a straight answer about who does the recovery and where.
  • Asbestos. Colorado's residential trigger levels for regulated abatement are 32 square feet of surface area, 50 linear feet of pipe, or the volume of a 55-gallon drum. Below that you are outside the abatement rules; above it you need a state-certified contractor, not a junk hauler. This matters most on pre-1980 structures — old shed siding, floor tile, duct wrap, and transite panels.
  • Demolition permits. Pueblo County Planning & Development handles permits for unincorporated Pueblo West. Small accessory structures — commonly in the 120 to 200 square foot range, depending on how the code is applied — often don't need one. Anything with a foundation, electrical service, or plumbing usually does. Confirm before a crew shows up for [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Electrical disconnects. A hot tub on a 50-amp 240V circuit needs that circuit killed properly. A hauler cutting live conductors is not doing you a favor.
  • Electronics. Colorado has banned electronic devices from landfill disposal since July 1, 2013. CRT televisions and monitors are the expensive exception — recycling fees commonly land around $0.40–$0.60 per pound, which is why that 200-pound console TV costs more to remove than a sofa.
  • Scrap metal sales. Colorado commodity-metals law requires dealers to record seller identification, and catalytic converter sales are tightly restricted. Shred steel has traded roughly $60–$140 per ton in recent years, so "we'll scrap it and credit you" is usually worth less than people expect.

When you should not hire us

Plainly, because it costs us work:

  • One or two items and you own a truck. A Pueblo-area landfill run for a single couch is typically a $30–$60 gate charge plus about 45 minutes round trip from Pueblo West. Any professional minimum load — ours included — will exceed that. If you have the vehicle and the back, do it yourself.
  • Good furniture, appliances that run, or usable building materials. Habitat ReStore and local thrift operations will often take these, and some will pick up free. Paying $150 to haul away a working dryer that someone would collect for free is money lit on fire.
  • Paint and household chemicals. Colorado's PaintCare program accepts architectural paint at participating retail drop-off sites at no charge, commonly up to 5 gallons per container per visit. No reputable hauler will put liquid paint, solvents, pesticides, or motor oil on a trailer — those go through the Pueblo City-County Health Department's household hazardous waste channels. If your cleanout is mostly chemicals, we are the wrong call.
  • Yard debris you could legally burn or chip. On larger Pueblo West lots, open burning with a permit from the Pueblo City-County Health Department is a legitimate option for part of the year, at a fee far below a hauling charge — though it is suspended during high wind and county fire restrictions, which in dry years can run for months. That seasonal uncertainty is the actual argument for [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo), not safety theater.
  • You're starting a competing junk business. Everything above is the real answer. We'd rather you operate insured than undercut the market by $40 a load while uncovered, because the uninsured operators are what make homeowners distrust all of us.
  • You need items moved between homes rather than disposed of. Transporting household goods for hire between residences in Colorado falls under Public Utilities Commission mover authority. A junk removal company is not a licensed mover, and hiring one for a move puts your belongings outside any mover-liability framework.

Our own limits, stated: we do not perform asbestos abatement, we do not haul liquid hazardous waste or ammunition, we schedule around wet-ground conditions that make heavy equipment a liability on soft Pueblo West driveways, and spring through early summer we are frequently booked 3–7 days out. If you need something gone this afternoon in April, call anyway, but plan for a no.

What to ask instead of "are you licensed?"

Four questions that separate real operators from a guy with a trailer:

  • "Can you email a certificate of insurance with my address listed?" A real policy produces a real COI in minutes.
  • "What's your USDOT number?" If their rig is over 10,001 lbs and they don't have one, you've learned something.
  • "Where does the material go, and will you text me the dump receipt?" Illegal dumping on vacant Pueblo County land is a persistent local problem, and liability for dumped material can follow the generator.
  • "Do you carry workers' comp?" If an uninsured helper is injured on your property, your homeowner's policy becomes the conversation.

Verify the numbers before you rely on them

Filing fees, tipping fees, scrap prices, and penalty amounts all move. The Colorado Secretary of State, the Colorado Department of Revenue, the Division of Workers' Compensation, the Pueblo City-County Health Department, and Pueblo County Planning & Development all publish current figures, and a five-minute phone call beats a blog post from any company — including this one. Code thresholds in particular get applied with local judgment, so confirm demolition and permit questions with the county directly.

What is not going to change is the core answer: there is no junk removal license in Colorado. Judge a hauler on insurance, disposal documentation, and whether they'll tell you when not to hire them.
