HOARDING CLEANUP NEAR ME: PRICES

If you're standing in a house in Pueblo West that has become unmanageable, here's the direct answer: a full hoarding cleanout here runs $450–$900 for a single room and $2,400–$5,500 for a typical 1,400–1,900 sq ft house at moderate severity, takes one to four working days, and starts with a free on-site walkthrough — not a phone quote, because nobody prices a hoarding job accurately over the phone. ClearPath Junk Removal covers Pueblo West, Pueblo, Colorado City, Rye, Avondale, and Beulah, and we can usually be on site for the walkthrough within 48 hours and start the work 3–10 days out. If the person living in the home hasn't agreed to the cleanout, stop and read the section below before you book anyone — including us.

What "hoarding cleanup" actually means at each severity level

Most companies quote off square footage. That's the wrong variable. The number that predicts cost is item density and contamination, which the Institute for Challenging Disorganization scales from Level I to Level V. Researchers Randy Frost and Gail Steketee's Clutter Image Rating does something similar on a 1–9 photo scale — you can find it free online, photograph your rooms, and match them. Do that before you call anyone. It turns an emotional conversation into a number.

Here's how those levels map to real Pueblo West pricing and crew time:

  • Level I–II (CIR 1–4): Cluttered but walkable, no odor, all utilities working. One room: $450–$900, 4–6 hours, 2 crew, one 16-foot dump trailer (12–14 cubic yards, roughly 7,000 lbs).
  • Level III (CIR 5–6): Narrow pathways, one or more rooms unusable, some rodent evidence, maybe a non-functioning bathroom. Whole house: $2,400–$5,500, 2–4 days, 3–4 crew, 6–14 trailer loads.
  • Level IV–V (CIR 7–9): Structural damage, non-working plumbing, animal or human waste, insect infestation. $6,000–$15,000+, and part of that job is not ours — see below.

A packed two-car garage on its own, which is the single most common Pueblo West version of this call, usually lands at $650–$1,400 and one long day. That's closer to our standard [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) work than to a clinical hoarding job.

The Pueblo West-specific problems nobody warns you about

This is high desert at about 4,700 feet, on 1- to 5-acre lots, and that changes the job in ways a Denver or Colorado Springs article won't tell you.

Deer mice. Dry stacked cardboard and fabric on an acre lot is prime rodent habitat, and Colorado has one of the higher hantavirus exposure profiles in the country. Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings — that aerosolizes the virus. CDC guidance is to spray the area with a bleach solution of 1 part bleach to 10 parts water, let it soak 5 minutes, then wipe up with a paper towel while wearing gloves and an N95. Our crews run N95s and nitrile on any job with visible droppings, at no upcharge.

Outbuildings and the back acre. Pueblo West hoarding rarely stops at the house. There's usually a shed, a carport, two dead vehicles, and 8–20 cubic yards of accumulated material behind the fence line. That outdoor portion often costs 30–40% more than the interior because it's heavier — soaked lumber, concrete, tires at $8–$15 each in disposal surcharges. If it's mostly outdoor, look at [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) or [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) instead of a full hoarding package.

Metro District covenants. The Pueblo West Metropolitan District enforces property-appearance rules, and a covenant or county nuisance complaint is what triggers a good share of these calls. If you're working against a written compliance deadline, bring us the letter at the walkthrough. It changes sequencing — we clear what's visible from the road first, which sometimes buys an extension.

Dump logistics. A round trip from a Pueblo West address to the landfill with unload time runs 45–70 minutes. On a 10-load job, that's 8–11 hours of pure driving baked into your invoice. Tipping fees of roughly $30–$55 per ton are passed through at cost on our estimates, and you should ask any competitor whether theirs are marked up.

When you should not hire us

This is the section that costs us jobs, and it's the most useful part of the page.

  • The resident hasn't agreed. Forced cleanouts done over someone's objection — by adult children, by landlords, by well-meaning siblings — have a bad track record. Hoarding disorder has been a distinct diagnosis in the DSM-5 since 2013, and the compulsion doesn't disappear because the stuff did. Homes get refilled, sometimes within 6–18 months, and the person often stops answering the door for family afterward. If we arrive and the resident is distressed and objecting, we stop. You still owe the 2-hour minimum, $180–$240. We'd rather tell you that now than collect it later.
  • It's Level I–II and you're physically able. If a pickup truck and two weekends would do it, do it yourself. Your cost is dump fees — realistically $60–$180 — versus $500–$900 for us. We are worth hiring when the volume, the weight, or the emotional load makes DIY unrealistic, not when it's merely annoying.
  • There's biohazard. Human waste, decomposition, hypodermic needles, heavy black mold. We are a junk removal company, not a trauma remediation firm. Our general liability policy doesn't cover it and our crews aren't bloodborne-pathogen certified. You need an IICRC-certified remediation contractor, typically $3–$8 per square foot on top of hauling. Any hauler who says yes to that job is either lying about their certification or about their insurance.
  • You need sorting, not hauling. If the real problem is that nobody can decide what to keep, a professional organizer at $60–$120/hour — ideally one with ICD hoarding training — is a better first purchase than a truck. We'll happily come after they're done. Hiring us first means paying $150–$220/hour for a crew to stand around while a family debates a box of photographs.
  • You expect insurance to pay. Standard homeowners policies in Colorado do not cover hoarding cleanup as such. They may cover resulting water or fire damage. Assume out of pocket unless your adjuster says otherwise in writing.
  • You're a landlord clearing a tenant's belongings. Colorado has specific rules about abandoned property and eviction procedure. We won't remove a tenant's possessions without a writ or documented abandonment, and neither should anyone else. That's [foreclosure and eviction cleanout](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) territory, with paperwork requirements attached.

Free and low-cost help before you spend a dollar

"Help with hoarding cleanup" doesn't always mean hiring a hauler. Local and state options exist:

  • Health Solutions is the community mental health provider for Pueblo County and can connect a resident to a therapist experienced with hoarding disorder. Cognitive behavioral therapy specific to hoarding typically runs 16–26 sessions and is the intervention with actual durability behind it.
  • Colorado Crisis Services — 1-844-493-8255, or 988 — for immediate mental health crisis, 24/7.
  • 211 Colorado (dial 211) for local assistance programs, including some senior services.
  • Pueblo County Adult Protective Services, through the Department of Human Services, if an older or at-risk adult is genuinely unsafe.
  • Senior Resource Development Agency (SRDA) in Pueblo for older residents needing case management.

Also: hold a free dump day. Pueblo County periodically runs household hazardous waste collection events, and paint, solvents, pool chemicals, and pesticides cannot go in our truck — we're not licensed for it. Those have to be separated out either way, so setting them aside early saves you money on our end.

How we run the job, and what changes the price

At the walkthrough we photograph every room, estimate cubic yards, note surcharge items, and give a written range with a not-to-exceed ceiling. We take a 25% deposit to hold the crew, and the balance on completion. What moves the number:

  • Stairs and distance to the trailer. Anything past 75 feet of carry adds crew time.
  • Surcharge items: mattresses at $35–$50 each, refrigerant appliances at $25–$45 each, e-waste, tires. See [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) and [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for those rates standalone.
  • Sort-as-we-go. Having us search for documents, jewelry, or cash slows a crew by roughly 30–50%. It's often worth it — we've pulled envelopes of cash out of book stacks more than once — but price it honestly.
  • Donation diversion. We typically route 15–30% of volume to local donation rather than the landfill. That lowers tonnage, which lowers your fee.
  • Weather. Pueblo West wind above 35 mph shuts down open-trailer loading. Summer heat pushes crews to 6 a.m. starts in July and August.
