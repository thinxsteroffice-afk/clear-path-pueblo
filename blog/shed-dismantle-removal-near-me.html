SHED DISMANTLE & REMOVAL NEAR ME

Yes, we dismantle and haul away sheds in Pueblo West, and for most homeowners here the whole job runs $300 to $1,400 and takes 90 minutes to a full day. A snap-together resin shed is usually $250 to $450. A 10x12 wood shed on skids runs $450 to $750. A 12x16 with shingles and a loft lands around $800 to $1,400. Larger barn-style buildings on a slab go $1,500 to $3,000 and up. Price is driven by three things: square footage, how the shed is anchored into our caliche and rock, and whether a truck or skid steer can get within 40 feet of it. Below is what actually happens on a Pueblo West teardown, including the situations where you should not hire anyone at all.

What drives the price on a Pueblo West lot

Most of Pueblo West sits on one-acre and 2.5-acre parcels, which is good news and bad news. The good news is access. On a typical lot off Purcell, McCulloch, or Joe Martinez, we can back a dump trailer to within 20 feet of the shed and cut haul time roughly in half compared to a fenced city lot in Pueblo proper.

The bad news is what the shed is bolted to. Sheds out here get anchored hard because they have to be, and that anchoring is what separates a two-hour job from a six-hour job.

  • Auger or arrowhead ground anchors with cable over the roof. Four to eight of them, each buried 18 to 30 inches. Pulling these out of hardpan adds 30 to 60 minutes.
  • Rebar pins driven through the skids into the ground, sometimes 24 inches of #4 bar per corner. These bend rather than pull and often have to be cut at grade.
  • Concrete piers poured at each corner, usually 12 inches across and 24 to 30 inches deep, weighing 150 to 250 pounds apiece.
  • A full slab, which is its own decision. See below.
  • Rock and caliche within 6 to 14 inches of grade, which is why hand-digging an anchor here takes four times as long as it does in loam.

If your shed is wired, that matters too. A buried circuit run from the house panel needs to be killed at the breaker and, if it is on its own subpanel, disconnected by a licensed electrician before we touch it. That is a $150 to $250 service call and it is not something a junk crew should be doing.

The wind is why your shed is in this condition

Pueblo West gets gusts over 60 mph several times most springs. That wind does a specific kind of damage that we see constantly and that shed manufacturers do not design around at 4,900 feet.

Metal sheds fail at the fasteners first. The panels flex, the self-tappers elongate their holes, and after five or six seasons the whole box racks out of square. Once a metal shed is racked, the doors will never close again and no amount of bracing fixes it. Vinyl and resin sheds fail differently: UV at this elevation makes the panels chalky and brittle, and a resin shed that was flexible at year three will crack like a dinner plate at year ten. That changes the teardown. A brittle resin shed cannot be disassembled panel by panel for resale. It comes apart in shards, and you need eye protection.

Wood sheds usually survive the wind and lose to water instead. The failure point on nine out of ten Pueblo West wood sheds is the floor, not the walls, because a shed set directly on gravel wicks moisture into the joists from below.

> If the roof and walls are solid and only the floor is spongy, you are looking at a $250 to $600 floor replacement, not a $3,000 rebuild. Stand inside and bounce on it before you call anyone.

What is living under the floor

This is the part most removal pages skip. A shed that has sat on gravel or piers for a decade in Pueblo West is a wildlife structure, and there are two real hazards.

Rattlesnakes. Prairie rattlesnakes den in exactly the kind of dark, rock-adjacent cavity a shed floor creates. We check under every shed with a mirror and a pole before anyone puts a hand near the skids. From April through October this is not theoretical. If you are doing this yourself, do not reach blind under a floor, and lift skids with a pry bar from the far side.

Rodent droppings. Packrats and deer mice nest in shed insulation, stored cushions, and under floorboards. Colorado has recorded well over 100 confirmed hantavirus cases since tracking began in 1993, and the exposure route is aerosolized dust from dry droppings. Do not sweep or shop-vac a shed floor. Wet the material down with a solution of one part household bleach to nine parts water, let it sit five to ten minutes, then wipe it up with paper towels while wearing gloves. It takes four extra minutes and it is the single cheapest safety step in this entire job.

Black widows in the bottom corners and wasp nests in the roof peak are near-universal. If you can, treat the shed 24 to 48 hours before the crew arrives. We work around them either way, but it slows things down.

The concrete slab decision, honestly

If your shed sits on a 4-inch slab, do the arithmetic before you ask anyone to break it out.

A 12x16 slab is 192 square feet. At 4 inches thick that is about 2.4 cubic yards of concrete weighing roughly 9,500 pounds, or just under 5 tons. Breaking, loading, and hauling that typically runs $3 to $8 per square foot, so $575 to $1,500 on top of the shed itself. Tipping fees for clean concrete in the Pueblo area generally run $40 to $60 a ton depending on the facility and the day.

Now the other side: a sound, level 4-inch slab is a finished patio pad, a hot tub base, a future garage corner, or a place to park a trailer. Pouring that same pad new costs $1,200 to $2,000. If the slab is not cracked through and is not in the footprint of something you are building, leave it. We will grind off the anchor bolts flush and you keep a $1,500 asset. If it is spalled, heaved, or in the way, we handle it as [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo), often with a [skid steer](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) on the same visit.

When you should not hire this out

Four of these cost us the job. We would still rather you read them.

  • Small resin and plastic sheds. A Rubbermaid or Suncast 7x7 comes apart with a socket wrench and a Phillips head in 60 to 90 minutes. The panels stack flat in a truck bed. If you are able-bodied and own a wrench, paying $250 for this is paying for a Saturday morning you were going to spend at home anyway.
  • Anything with a live storm claim. If a hailstorm or windstorm took out your shed and you are filing on it, do not demolish it before the adjuster has been out and photographed it. We have watched homeowners forfeit $1,800 in coverage to save four days of waiting. Get the claim number and the inspection first.
  • Sheds worth selling. A structurally sound 10x12 wood shed sells on local marketplace listings for $300 to $900. A shed mover with a mule can relocate it within a few miles for $250 to $600, and buyers around here will often handle that themselves. Selling instead of demolishing is a swing of roughly $1,000 in your favor. List it for two weeks before you call us.
  • Repairable structures. New T1-11 siding on one wall is $150 to $400 in materials. A roof re-shingle on a 10x12 is about 1.5 squares, $200 to $450 installed. Compare that to $650 removal plus $2,500 to $6,000 for a replacement shed.
  • Asbestos or pre-1978 painted structures. Older transite panels and lead paint are a licensed abatement job, not a junk removal job. If your outbuilding predates 1980 and has cement-board siding, get it tested for $50 to $150 before anyone swings a hammer. We will tell you to stop and we will not take the job.
  • Sheds attached to the house or garage. Once a structure shares a wall or roof with the dwelling, it is a permitted demolition with different rules.

There are also limits on our end worth naming. From roughly mid-December through February the ground here can be frozen 8 to 12 inches down, and buried anchors and piers will not come out cleanly. We can still do the structure, but slab and pier work gets deferred or priced higher. In heavy mud we will not run equipment across an unfrozen septic field or leach lines, and if your shed sits over one, tell us before we arrive. And if the only access is a 32-inch backyard gate, everything is hand-carried and cost typically climbs 30 to 50 percent. Pulling two fence panels yourself the night before is the highest-return 20 minutes of work on the entire project.

The scrap-value math nobody runs

People often ask whether the metal offsets the price. Run the numbers. A 10x12 steel Arrow-style shed weighs about 250 to 350 pounds. Light iron and shred scrap has been trading in the range of $0.04 to $0.10 per pound. That is $12 to $35 at the yard, before you spend an hour loading it and 40 minutes driving. Scrap value is real, it is just small. We recycle the steel because it should not go in a hole, not because it changes your invoice.

Wood is worse. Painted, treated, or shingled lumber is not burnable under Pueblo County open-burn rules and cannot go to green waste. A 10x12 wood shed with a shingled roof produces roughly 2,000 pounds and 4 to 6 cubic yards of mixed debris that has to be tipped.

What a DIY teardown really costs you

If you do it yourself on a 10x12 wood shed, budget realistically:

  • 12 to 16 hours of actual work across a weekend, including cleanup
  • 4 to 6 dump runs in a half-ton pickup, because real payload is 1,300 to 1,700 pounds and concrete or shingles max that out fast
  • $150 to $300 in tipping fees, plus 45 to 60 minutes per round trip
  • 3 to 4 demolition blades at $6 to $10 each, because embedded nails eat them
  • $350 to $550 if you rent a 15-yard dumpster for a week instead

The step everyone underestimates is the last one. Roughly a third of the labor on a shed teardown is the final sweep: magnet-rolling the pad for nails and screws, picking glass, and raking the shadow of dead grass and debris underneath. Skip it and you will find a roofing nail with a lawnmower tire or a bare foot. A tire on a zero-turn is $200 to $300.

Booking it in Pueblo West

We typically quote from four photos: each side of the shed, one of the interior, and one showing the route from the driveway. Most Pueblo West teardowns get scheduled within 3 to 7 days, and we usually give a two-hour arrival window rather than an all-day one. Crews arrive with reciprocating saws, pry bars, a magnet roller, and a dump trailer, and on jobs with piers or slabs, a skid steer.

Two practical scheduling notes. Late fall, roughly October into early December, is our lowest-demand stretch and the easiest time to get a next-day slot. Late April through June is the busiest, because that is when spring wind finishes off what winter started.

If the shed is full, say so when you book. Contents change the plan and the price, and a packed shed is closer to a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) with a demolition attached. Fuel cans, propane cylinders, old paint, and pool chemicals cannot go to the landfill and need to be routed to household hazardous waste collection, so pull those out and set them aside. For the pile of branches and old fencing that always ends up next to a shed, we can fold that into the same trip as [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo).

One last thing that costs people money. If you intend to rebuild, confirm your setbacks and any Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenant requirements for the new structure before the old one comes down. Accessory buildings under 200 square feet are commonly exempt from a building permit in this region, but zoning setbacks and district architectural rules still apply, and they are not the same thing. Verify with Pueblo Regional Building and the district office. We have seen homeowners clear a pad in March and still be waiting on approval in July, staring at a rectangle of dead grass where their storage used to be.
