LOADUP JUNK REMOVAL REVIEWS (2026)

If you're searching for LoadUp junk removal reviews, here's the short version. LoadUp is a national booking platform based in Atlanta. It sends local independent haulers to do the work, and the crew that shows up in Pueblo West is probably not a LoadUp employee. Its online reviews mix thousands of jobs from hundreds of markets. A 4.5-star average tells you how the platform does across the country. It doesn't tell you much about the truck that would come to your driveway on Purcell Boulevard. To judge any junk removal company here, us included, read only reviews from the last 90 days that mention Pueblo or Pueblo West. Then check two things: did the final price match the quote, and did the crew show up in the promised window? The rest of this page explains how to do that, how LoadUp's model differs from a local crew's, and when you shouldn't hire us.

What LoadUp's reviews actually measure

LoadUp works as a marketplace. You get an upfront price online and book a slot, and LoadUp passes the job to a vetted hauling partner in your area. That model has real strengths, like the instant quote and booking at 10 p.m. without a phone call. It also means a review of "LoadUp" is really a review of three separate things:

  • The booking software. Was the quote easy to get, and did the confirmation arrive?
  • The customer service desk. Did someone fix it when the job went sideways?
  • The local hauler. Were they on time and careful, and did they charge what was quoted?

Most glowing marketplace reviews praise the first item. Most angry ones are about the third. If 200 five-star reviews come from Atlanta, Dallas and Phoenix, they say nothing about which partner covers Pueblo County this month, or whether there is one. Partner networks change, and a hauler who did great work in 2024 may have dropped off the platform by 2026.

> A national star rating is an average of strangers in other cities. Your job gets done by one crew on one Tuesday. Judge the crew.

How to read junk removal reviews in 10 minutes

You don't need to read 500 reviews. This filter works on Google, Yelp, the BBB or the Trustpilot page for any hauler, LoadUp included:

1. Sort by newest and read only the last 90 days. Ownership, crews and pricing change faster than star averages do. A company can sit at 4.8 stars on old reviews while its last 20 jobs went badly. 2. Search the reviews for "Pueblo." For a national brand, count how many recent reviews mention Pueblo or Pueblo West. If it's zero, you're guessing. 3. Read the 1-, 2- and 3-star reviews first. Every company gets some. What matters is the pattern. One late arrival in 50 jobs is normal. Five reviews in a row about surprise charges at the curb is the company's model. 4. Look for price-change complaints. The most common junk removal complaint nationwide is a final bill well above the online quote. If a $300 quote becomes $450 on site, that's a 50% jump. Check whether reviewers say the crew explained the difference *before* loading or *after*. 5. Check whether the owner replies. A reply within 48 hours that names the problem and offers a fix is a good sign. Copy-pasted "please contact us" replies under every bad review are a bad one. 6. Check the review rate against the company's age. A company that's been open 18 months and has 900 reviews is either very busy or buying reviews. Look at whether the reviewer profiles have photos and other reviews.

Run that filter on us too. ClearPath has a 5.0-star rating across 332 Google reviews as of November 2026. Sort them by newest and look at what people say about arrival windows and final price. That's the test we want you to use.

Where a national platform and a local crew really differ in Pueblo West

These are the practical differences we see on actual Pueblo West jobs.

Lot size and access. A lot of Pueblo West homes sit on half-acre to multi-acre parcels with long gravel drives, detached shops and outbuildings a long way from the street. A quote built from an online slider ("1/4 truck, 1/2 truck") usually assumes curbside or garage pickup. A 150-foot carry from a back shed, or a hot tub sunk into a deck, changes the labor a lot. Online quotes break most often on jobs like this.

Volume estimates. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards. Most people underestimate their pile by 25% to 50%, especially with yard debris, scrap wood and broken fencing, which pack loosely. Whoever quotes you, send photos with something for scale in the frame, like a person, a car or a trash can.

Heavy and awkward items. An empty hot tub typically weighs 500 to 1,000 pounds, and many need cutting before they'll fit through a gate. Refrigerators and freezers have refrigerant that has to be handled correctly at disposal. Concrete and rock are dense enough that a truck hits its weight limit long before it's full. Our [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo), [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) and [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) pages cover what each job involves.

Who you talk to when something goes wrong. With a marketplace, a problem usually goes through a support ticket to a central desk, which then contacts the local partner. With a local company, you call the people who own the truck. Neither is automatically better. The question is which one actually picks up.

Equipment beyond a truck. Some Pueblo West jobs aren't really junk removal: grading, digging out a dead shed pad, moving rock or clearing a lot overgrown with brush. Most junk-only haulers can't do that work. We run a skid steer for [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) and [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo), which can save you from hiring a second contractor.

When ClearPath is NOT the right call

These are the cases where we'd tell you to go elsewhere or do it yourself.

  • You have a single small load and a pickup truck. If it's a few bags, an old chair and some boxes, and you can lift them, driving to a transfer station yourself costs a fraction of any hauler's minimum. Paying a crew to drive 15 to 20 minutes each way for 10 minutes of lifting is poor value. That applies to us, LoadUp and everyone else.
  • You're doing a multi-week remodel. If you'll generate debris over 3 to 6 weeks, renting a roll-off dumpster you can fill at your own pace usually beats calling a crew back four times. We'll still haul the final pile, but we shouldn't be your whole debris plan.
  • You need one vendor across several states. Property managers with units in Pueblo, Denver and out of state may be better served by a national platform with one invoice and one account login. We only serve southern Colorado, so if that's your situation, LoadUp's structure probably fits you better.
  • You want a binding price without anyone looking at the job. We give quotes from photos, but on big or unusual jobs we may adjust once we see the actual pile, and we tell you before loading anything. If you want a fixed number online at midnight with no conversation at all, a platform with instant checkout suits you better.
  • You have hazardous waste. We don't haul paint cans with liquid in them, solvents, pesticides, fuel, propane tanks, asbestos or medical waste. Those need a household hazardous waste program or a licensed hazmat contractor. Anyone who says they'll take them in a regular load is a red flag.
  • You're outside our area. We focus on Pueblo West and [Pueblo](/areas/pueblo). If you're well up toward Colorado Springs or out past Cañon City, a crew based closer to you will likely respond faster and charge less for drive time.
  • You need it gone today and we're booked. We aren't always available same-day. When we're full, we'll say so rather than promising a window we'll miss. A late arrival is the second most common complaint in this industry, and we'd rather lose the job than earn that review.

Questions to ask any hauler before you book

Whether you choose LoadUp, ClearPath or someone else, ask these five questions. Their answers will tell you more than another hour of reading reviews.

  • "Is the quote a ceiling or an estimate?" Find out exactly what would make the price go up, and whether they'll tell you before they load.
  • "Who is actually coming, and are they insured?" For a marketplace, ask whether the local partner carries its own liability insurance, and ask for proof if the job involves your house, deck or fence.
  • "How wide is the arrival window?" A 2-hour window is reasonable. If you get a 4-hour window plus "we'll call when we're close," plan to lose half a day.
  • "Where does it go?" Ask how much gets donated, recycled or sent to the landfill. Good haulers have a real answer for furniture, metal and appliances.
  • "What happens if you damage something?" Get a clear answer before the crew starts carrying a sofa down your stairs.
