24 HOUR JUNK REMOVAL, PUEBLO WEST

If you need round-the-clock junk hauling in Pueblo West, here's the honest version: ClearPath answers calls 24 hours a day and can usually put a truck in your driveway within 2 to 4 hours of a confirmed booking, including nights, Sundays, and holidays. Same-day daytime jobs are more often 60 to 90 minutes out. Overnight and holiday work carries an after-hours surcharge of roughly $75 to $150 on top of the standard load rate, which runs about $150 for a quarter truck up to $650 for a full 15-yard load. Call or text, describe the pile, get a price before we roll.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this page is the detail most "24/7 junk removal" pages leave out — including the times you should not call us at 2 a.m.

What "24 Hour" Actually Means Here

There is a meaningful difference between a company that answers the phone 24 hours and one that operates a truck 24 hours. Most national-brand franchises route overnight calls to an offshore call center that books you for the next available daytime window — which in Pueblo County during spring cleanup season is frequently 3 to 5 days out.

Our overnight coverage works differently, and the limits are worth stating up front:

  • Dispatch is live 24/7. A person in Pueblo County reads your text. Typical response to an overnight text is under 20 minutes.
  • Truck rolls 24/7 for qualifying jobs. Emergency, access-blocking, liability, or deadline-driven work.
  • Overnight crew is 2 people, not 4. Between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m. we run a light crew. A four-bedroom [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) is not happening at midnight — that's a 6 to 10 hour daytime job with a full crew.
  • Disposal facilities are closed overnight. The Pueblo County landfill on Goodnight Ave. operates roughly 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. weekdays and closes Sundays. Anything we pick up at 1 a.m. sits in the truck or our yard until the gate opens.

That last point drives more of our pricing than anything else, and almost no competitor page mentions it.

Why Overnight Costs More (The Actual Math)

The surcharge isn't opportunistic. Here's where it goes:

A daytime crew disposes of a load and immediately takes the next job — the truck turns over 3 to 4 times per shift. An overnight load ties up that truck's capacity until morning. One 11 p.m. hot tub pickup can consume the truck bed that would otherwise have served two morning customers.

Add the labor differential. Overnight and holiday pay runs 1.5x. A 90-minute job that costs us about $60 in daytime labor costs roughly $90 to $110 overnight, before fuel and the 22-mile round trip from Pueblo West to the transfer station.

> The surcharge is not a premium for urgency. It's the cost of a truck that can't dump until 7:30 the next morning.

Real numbers from our own jobs:

  • Single mattress and box spring, 11:40 p.m., McCulloch Blvd: $125 base + $75 after-hours = $200. Same job at 10 a.m. Tuesday: $125.
  • Refrigerator blocking a garage door, 2 a.m.: $150 + $75 = $225. Freon-bearing units add $35 for certified reclamation regardless of hour.
  • Post-water-heater-failure debris, 4 a.m., Sunday: roughly half truck, $375 + $150 holiday-weekend surcharge = $525.
  • Full 15-yard load, 3 a.m. eviction turnover: $650 + $150 = $800, plus a $100 second-crew fee because two people couldn't safely move a gun safe.

Pay attention to that first line. The mattress cost 60% more for no reason other than the clock.

When You Should NOT Call Us at 2 A.M.

This is the section that costs us money, and it's the most useful thing on this page.

Do not pay the overnight surcharge if the junk isn't hurting you tonight. A pile of yard debris in the side yard, an old couch in the garage, a shed you've been meaning to tear down — none of that gets more expensive by waiting until Tuesday morning. You will pay $75 to $150 extra to solve a problem that has been sitting there for eight months. Book a daytime slot on [our services page](/services) instead and keep the money.

Specific cases where waiting is the right call:

  • Spring and fall cleanouts. If you're clearing the [garage](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or hauling [yard debris](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo), the job is 3 to 6 hours. Overnight rates on a 5-hour job add $150 for nothing. Worse, we can't run loud equipment past 10 p.m. in most Pueblo West neighborhoods without generating a complaint.
  • Anything under $150 total. If it's one item, the surcharge can be half the bill. Wait.
  • Jobs that need a [skid steer](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) or excavation. We don't run heavy equipment in the dark on unfamiliar property. Buried irrigation lines, septic laterals, and unmarked utilities are invisible at night. Colorado 811 locate requests also take 2 to 3 business days — no locate, no dig, no exceptions.
  • Hazardous materials. Paint, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, propane tanks, tires, batteries, asbestos-containing material. We will not take these at any hour, at any price. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events several times a year — that's your route, and it's free. If a "24 hour" hauler tells you they'll take five gallons of used motor oil at midnight, they're dumping it somewhere illegal and you may be the one on the hook for it.
  • You're hoping we can quote sight-unseen at 3 a.m. We can give you a range from photos. We cannot commit to a firm price on a [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) without walking it, and hoarding jobs routinely come in 40% to 200% above the phone estimate once we see floor conditions, biohazard exposure, and whether the structure is sound.

Who should not hire any junk removal company:

If you're clearing a whole house and you're not in a hurry, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster from a Pueblo rental company runs roughly $400 to $550 for a 7-day rental with a 3-ton limit. You load it yourself over a week. That beats our full-load price on volume — you're trading roughly 10 to 15 hours of your own labor for maybe $250. If you're physically able and have the week, take the dumpster. We're the better deal when you don't have the time, the back, the truck, or the driveway space for a 22-foot container.

Similarly, if it's one working appliance or a decent couch, call Habitat for Humanity ReStore or a local charity first. Free pickup, tax deduction, and the item doesn't go in a hole in the ground. We'd rather lose that $125 job than haul a functional dishwasher to the landfill.

What Genuinely Justifies an Overnight Call

The surcharge is worth paying when delay costs more than $150. In our experience, that's four situations:

  • Access is blocked. A collapsed [deck](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo), a fallen tree, debris across a driveway when someone in the house needs to get to a dialysis appointment at 6 a.m.
  • A property transaction has a hard deadline. Closing at 9 a.m. and the seller left a garage full of junk. A delayed closing costs a lot more than $150. Same math on [foreclosure and eviction cleanouts](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) where the sheriff's schedule doesn't flex.
  • Liability exposure. Broken glass, exposed nails, an unsecured pile in a rental property's common area. If a tenant or guest gets hurt at 3 a.m., your deductible dwarfs the surcharge.
  • Commercial downtime. A restaurant on Purcell that has to open at 6 a.m. with a broken walk-in cooler in the kitchen aisle. Four hours of lost service exceeds the surcharge by an order of magnitude. Same logic on [commercial office cleanouts](/services/commercial-office-cleanout-pueblo) scheduled around business hours.

Pueblo West Specifics That Change the Job

Pueblo West is not Pueblo proper, and several local realities affect a 24-hour call:

  • Lot sizes. Many Pueblo West parcels run 1 to 2.5 acres. Junk is frequently 200+ feet from where a truck can park. Long carries add 30 to 45 minutes and, past about 150 feet, a $50 to $75 access fee. Tell us the distance when you call — it's the single most common reason an overnight quote moves.
  • Unpaved access. Numerous County Lane addresses have dirt or gravel drives. After a summer monsoon, a loaded truck at 2 a.m. on a soft drive is how you get a rut you'll be filling for a year. We may decline until daylight or until it dries. That's not us being difficult; a stuck truck costs $400 to $800 to recover.
  • Metro District rules. Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenants restrict visible outdoor storage. If you've gotten a notice, you generally have 10 to 30 days to comply — which means you almost never need an overnight call to satisfy one.
  • Wind. Pueblo West sees 40+ mph gusts regularly in spring. We will not tarp and run a loaded open-bed truck on Highway 50 in a high-wind advisory. Debris off a truck at 65 mph is somebody's windshield.
  • Distance and fuel. From central Pueblo West, the round trip to the transfer station is roughly 22 to 30 miles. Outlying jobs past Colorado City or toward Rye can add $50 to $100 in travel.

How to Get the Fastest, Cheapest Overnight Response

Six things cut both your wait and your bill:

  • Text photos, not descriptions. "A couple boxes" and "a couple boxes" differ by 400% in volume. Photos from two angles with a person or door for scale get you a firm number in about 10 minutes instead of a range.
  • Measure the carry distance. Pace it off. 40 feet versus 180 feet is a real price difference.
  • Separate metal. Appliances, water heaters, and scrap steel have salvage value. A clean metal-only load can knock 10% to 20% off, because we recover some of it. Mixed loads get no credit.
  • Say if there's Freon. Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, and [hot tubs](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) with heat pumps require EPA-certified recovery. Undisclosed Freon at 2 a.m. means we come back in daylight and you pay twice.
  • Confirm gate codes and dog situation up front. Roughly 1 in 8 overnight jobs stalls on a locked gate or a loose dog. Every stalled minute is billed.
  • Ask whether daytime works. We will tell you honestly. About 30% of our overnight inquiries get talked into a next-morning slot, and those customers save an average of about $95.

Straight Answers to the Common Objections

"You're more expensive than the guy with a pickup on Facebook Marketplace." Frequently true — he might do that mattress for $60. He also may not be insured, may not have a landfill account, and the risk of an illegal dump on BLM land near Lake Pueblo lands back on you if the load is traced. If price is your only variable and the item is small and legal, hire him. We're not going to pretend otherwise.

"Can you guarantee a 1-hour overnight arrival?" No. We run a limited overnight fleet. We commit to a 2 to 4 hour window and we hit it about 90% of the time. Snow, a job that runs long, or two simultaneous emergencies push that out. Anyone guaranteeing 60 minutes at 3 a.m. in Pueblo County is either exaggerating or already sitting in their truck nearby.

"Is 24-hour service available in my area?" Overnight coverage extends across Pueblo West, Pueblo, Blende, Salt Creek, and Avondale. Farther out — Beulah, Rye, Colorado City — we usually run daytime only, with overnight by arrangement and a travel add. Check [our service areas](/areas) or the [FAQ](/faq) for specifics.
