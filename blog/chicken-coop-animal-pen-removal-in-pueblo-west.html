CHICKEN COOP & ANIMAL PEN REMOVAL IN PUEBLO WEST

Out here on the acreages, half the properties we visit have a coop, a rabbit hutch, or a dog run somebody built fifteen years ago and stopped using ten years ago. The roof is sagging, the wire is rusted, and it's slowly turning into a snake hotel. Here's how we clear it.

What Comes Off the Property

Coops, hutches, kennels, chain-link dog runs, T-post-and-wire pens, feed bins, hog panels, waterers, and the old straw and bedding underneath. If it was part of the setup, it goes on the trailer. We handle the demo — you don't need to swing a hammer first.

The Wire Is the Real Work

The structure comes apart fast. Buried poultry wire is what eats the day. It gets grown through with weeds, staple-gunned to rotted posts, and half-buried in dirt, and it does not want to leave. We cut it, roll it, and pull it out in sections so you're not finding wire scraps with the mower next spring. The old fencing and rotted lumber go out with the rest of the yard debris Pueblo properties tend to collect along the back fence line.

When the Skid Steer Comes

Posts set in concrete are the reason people put this job off. Instead of digging, we bring the skid steer Pueblo jobs like this actually call for — forks to pop footings and a bucket to grade the spot back level. On bigger pens, that's the difference between one visit and three.

What It Runs

A small backyard coop usually lands in the $175–$300 range. A full run with chain-link, footings, and years of accumulated junk inside runs more like $350–$700 depending on the size and how much concrete is in the ground. Send a couple photos and you'll get a real number, not a "starting at" number.

While We're Out There

Most folks have us knock out a second thing on the same trip, since the trailer's already hooked up. Garage cleanout Pueblo customers ask about, a hot tub that hasn't held water since 2019, scrap metal behind the shed. One trip, one price.
