APPLIANCE REMOVAL IN 8 MINUTES?

Yes, in most Pueblo West homes a two-person crew can get a standard appliance out the door and onto the truck in about 8 minutes. That covers a fridge, washer, dryer, range or chest freezer that's already unplugged, empty and on a ground-level path to the driveway. The clock starts when the crew walks in, not when you call us. Most of that time goes to setting a dolly, strapping the unit, protecting the floor and walking it out. The lift itself takes under 90 seconds. Some jobs take 30 to 45 minutes instead, and in a few cases hiring us is the wrong call. Both are covered below.

What actually happens in those 8 minutes

Here's a typical refrigerator pickup at a single-story ranch home off Joe Martinez Blvd or Purcell, minute by minute:

  • Minutes 0–1: Walk the path from the kitchen to the truck. Check door widths, rugs, thresholds and any step down into the garage.
  • Minutes 1–3: Pull the fridge out from the wall, confirm it's unplugged and the water line is shut off and capped, and tape the doors closed.
  • Minutes 3–5: Tip it onto an appliance dolly and ratchet-strap it. A side-by-side fridge weighs about 250 to 350 pounds, so this is a two-person job no matter how strong anyone is.
  • Minutes 5–7: Roll it out, usually through the garage. Most Pueblo West homes have an attached garage with a single step or none, and that's the main reason our times here beat older homes in central Pueblo.
  • Minutes 7–8: Load and secure it. You sign off, and the crew moves on to anything else you want hauled.

The 8 minutes doesn't include drive time, the quote conversation or payment. Plan on the crew being at your house for 15 to 20 minutes for a single item. That's still shorter than most people's lunch break.

> The lift is the fast part. The prep, done by you before we show up, is what makes 8 minutes possible.

The prep that keeps it at 8 minutes (and what blows it up)

Almost every slow appliance pickup we've done came down to one of these. You can handle most of them yourself the day before:

  • Empty it completely. A full chest freezer can add 50 to 100 pounds of frozen food, and we won't haul a unit with food in it. Rotting meat in a truck in July, when Pueblo West regularly tops 95°F, is not something anyone wants.
  • Defrost the freezer 24 hours ahead. Ice build-up melts on the dolly ride and leaves water on your floors.
  • Shut off and cap the fridge water line. If your ice maker runs off an old saddle valve, it may not seal fully. We'll come back to this one.
  • Unplug it and pull it 6 inches from the wall if you can do that safely. That saves us 2 minutes.
  • Clear the path. Move the dining chairs, the dog gate and the boxes in the garage. A cluttered garage is the most common reason a pickup takes 25 minutes instead of 8.
  • Washers: turn off both hot and cold valves and drain the hoses into a bucket first. A washer drum holds 1 to 2 gallons of standing water even after a full cycle.

When it takes much longer than 8 minutes

Some jobs simply take more time. If your situation is on this list, expect 30 to 60 minutes of labor and a quote that reflects it:

  • The fridge is wider than your door. Standard interior doors are 30 to 32 inches wide. Many French-door refrigerators are 35 to 36 inches wide and around 30 inches deep with the handles on. Taking the doors and handles off adds 15 to 20 minutes, and putting the house door back on its hinges adds more.
  • Basements or a second story. A 300-pound fridge on a narrow basement staircase with a turn at the landing is a 30-to-45-minute job done carefully. Pueblo West has fewer basements than Pueblo proper, but the custom homes out toward the west side and some of the larger lots often have them.
  • Built-in or panel-ready units. These are secured into the cabinetry. Removing one without damaging the cabinets can take an hour, and some need a cabinet installer rather than a junk crew.
  • Stacked washer-dryer units in a closet. They often have to be unstacked in place, which needs room you may not have.
  • Gravel driveways and long walks. Many Pueblo West properties sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots with gravel or dirt drives. If the truck can't park within about 50 feet of the door, dolly time goes up, and rolling a dolly through gravel is slow.

When this ISN'T worth paying for

This is the section most junk removal companies leave out. Here are the situations where hiring us, or anyone, to haul a single appliance is a poor use of your money.

1. You're buying a new appliance from a big-box store. Most major retailers offer haul-away of the old unit when they deliver the new one, often free or for a small add-on fee. That's almost always cheaper than a separate junk removal trip for one item. Check the box at checkout and save your money. Haul-away usually requires the old unit to be disconnected and ready, so the prep list above still applies.

2. The appliance works and is less than about 8 to 10 years old. A working washer, dryer or fridge has resale value. Post it locally for free and someone with a pickup will often grab it within 48 hours. Habitat for Humanity ReStores also accept working appliances, sometimes with free pickup, so call them first to check what they're taking right now.

3. Your utility is running a recycling rebate. Electric utilities, including Black Hills Energy, which serves most of Pueblo West, have offered programs at various times that pick up an old working fridge or freezer and pay you a rebate. These programs come and go, so check before you book anyone. If you can get paid to have it hauled, do that.

4. You have a truck, a strong friend and a ground-level path. We're not going to pretend a washer is hard to move with two people and a rented appliance dolly. If you have both, plus a plan for disposal, you can do it yourself. Disposal is the catch, so read the next point before you decide.

5. You need a gas line disconnected. We won't disconnect gas dryers or gas ranges, and neither should most junk crews. A bad gas disconnect is a fire and carbon monoxide risk. If your range or dryer is still hooked to gas, have a licensed plumber or gas fitter cap the line before we arrive. That service call costs extra, and if it's the only reason you were calling a junk hauler, it may change the math.

6. The water line won't shut off cleanly. If your fridge's shutoff valve is corroded or it's a leaky saddle valve, we'll stop and tell you rather than risk a flood. Pulling a fridge off a line that drips at 1 cup a minute will soak a subfloor within an hour. You'll need a plumber, and we'll come back after.

The honest summary: hiring a crew for one appliance makes the most sense when you have no truck, no help, no retailer haul-away and no time. It makes much more sense when the appliance is one of several things going out at once.

Why disposal is the part people underestimate

Refrigerators, freezers, dehumidifiers and window AC units contain refrigerant. Under Section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act, that refrigerant has to be recovered by certified technicians before the unit is scrapped. Venting it is illegal. Many landfills and scrap yards won't take a cooling appliance without proof the refrigerant was removed, or they charge an extra fee to handle it.

That's the real value of a professional pickup. The 8 minutes in your kitchen isn't what you're paying for. You're paying for the unit to go to a facility that handles the refrigerant, separates the steel (a typical fridge is roughly 50% to 60% steel by weight) and keeps the compressor oil out of the ground.

If you haul it yourself, call your disposal site before you load it. Ask:

  • Do you accept refrigerators and freezers?
  • Does the refrigerant need to be removed first, and is there a tag or certificate requirement?
  • What's the per-unit fee?

Washers, dryers, ranges and dishwashers have no refrigerant and are much simpler. Most scrap metal recyclers will take them, and some will pay a few dollars for the steel.

Getting the most out of the trip

Because the appliance itself only takes about 8 minutes, the trip makes better financial sense when the truck leaves with more than one item. Pueblo West customers commonly combine an appliance pickup with:

  • The old garage fridge plus whatever has built up around it. A [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) often takes 1 to 2 hours and usually costs less per item than separate trips.
  • The mattress or couch that got replaced in the same remodel.
  • Yard debris after a spring windstorm. Gusts above 50 mph aren't unusual out here in March and April.

Pricing is based on how much of the truck your items fill, so adding a second or third item to an appliance pickup usually costs much less than the first item did. Ask for a firm quote on-site before any work starts, and if the number doesn't make sense, say no. You won't hurt our feelings.

  • Mention stairs, door widths under 32 inches, gas connections and gravel drives up front.
