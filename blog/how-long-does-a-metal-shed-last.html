HOW LONG DOES A METAL SHED LAST?

A metal shed in Pueblo West lasts 15 to 30 years, and the spread comes down to four things: steel thickness, whether it sits on a slab, whether it's anchored, and whether sprinkler spray hits it. A $700 big-box galvanized kit set on bare dirt usually gives you 8 to 15 years out here. That same kit on a 4-inch concrete slab, properly anchored, gets 18 to 25. A heavy 26- or 24-gauge steel building with a bolted tube frame runs 30 to 40 years and often outlives the shingles on your house. Pueblo West's dry air — roughly 12 inches of precipitation a year — is unusually kind to galvanized steel. The hail and the wind are not.

Why the dry climate buys you years you wouldn't get elsewhere

Corrosion engineers rate atmospheres on a scale from C1 (dry interior) to C5 (marine or heavy industrial). Pueblo West sits near the bottom of that scale — call it C2 — because there's no salt air, low humidity, and about 245 sunny days a year. Zinc coating loss in a C2 atmosphere runs roughly 0.1 to 0.7 microns per year. A G90 galvanized panel carries about 20 microns of zinc per side, which is why a decent panel out here can go 25+ years before the steel underneath ever sees daylight.

Compare that to a coastal C5 site, where zinc loss can hit 4 to 8 microns a year and the same panel is finished in under a decade. If somebody online told you metal sheds rust out in 10 years, they were probably writing from Florida or New Jersey.

So the panels are rarely what fails first in Pueblo West. Three other things are.

The three real failure modes out here

1. Fasteners and washers go before the steel does. Every screw in a metal shed roof has a rubber washer — EPDM or neoprene — that makes the seal. At 4,721 feet, UV intensity is roughly 10 to 15% higher than at sea level, and those washers chalk, shrink, and crack in 8 to 15 years. The panels still look fine. Water is now running down the inside of the screw shank. This is the single most under-discussed number in metal shed lifespan, and it's cheap to fix: a bag of 250 replacement screws with fresh washers costs $25 to $45.

2. The bottom 6 inches. Alkaline clay and caliche soils hold moisture against the base rail and wick it up. A shed sitting directly on dirt or gravel will show perforation at the floor rail in 7 to 12 years while the roof is still watertight. A slab with a 4-inch curb above grade essentially eliminates this.

3. Sprinkler overspray. Fifteen minutes a day of treated irrigation water hitting the same wall panel — three times a week, April through October — concentrates minerals and keeps steel wet in an otherwise dry climate. We have pulled sheds in Pueblo West with one wall rotted through and the other three in near-new condition. Every time, there was a rotor head 8 feet away.

> The shed usually doesn't die. The screws, the base rail, and whatever your sprinklers hit die, and the shed gets blamed.

Lifespan by what you actually bought

  • Thin-gauge big-box kit (29–33 gauge, $500–$1,200 for 8x10 or 10x12): 8–15 years on dirt, 18–25 on a slab. Dents from 1-inch hail. Door tracks are usually the first complaint, often inside 3 to 5 years.
  • Mid-grade galvanized or Galvalume kit ($1,400–$2,800): 20–30 years. Galvalume (zinc-aluminum, typically AZ50 or AZ55) outlasts plain galvanized by a factor of 2 to 4 in test panels.
  • Tube-frame steel building (24–26 gauge, $3,500–$8,000 for 12x20): 30–40+ years. These are the ones still standing on properties off McCulloch and Purcell from the late 1980s.
  • Used shed off Marketplace ($150–$400): assume 5 to 10 years of remaining life, less if it's already been disassembled once. Screw holes don't reseal well the second time.

Add $6 to $12 per square foot for a concrete slab — about $720 to $1,440 for a 10x12. That slab is the best lifespan dollar you will spend, often doubling service life for 40% of the shed's cost.

Hail and wind: the Pueblo County asterisk

Colorado ranks second nationally in hail claims, and Pueblo County sits inside the corridor. Hail around 1 inch will dent 29-gauge panels cosmetically. At 1.5 to 1.75 inches, thin panels can split at the ribs, and that's a leak, not a blemish.

Wind matters more. Design wind speeds in this part of the county land in the 105 to 115 mph range (verify specifics with the Pueblo Regional Building Department before you build). An unanchored 8x10 metal shed weighs only 150 to 250 pounds empty — a 50 to 60 mph gust out of the southwest can walk it or flip it. Anchor kits run $60 to $150 and are the difference between a 20-year shed and a shed you find in a neighbor's fence line. Local ground snow load is generally around 25 to 30 psf, which thin-gauge roofs handle poorly if you also let 2 feet of wet March snow sit on a flat-ish span.

Maintenance that pays, and maintenance that doesn't

Worth doing:

  • Re-screw the roof at year 10 — about $40 in parts and 3 hours. Highest-return task on the list.
  • Move the sprinkler head. Free. Adds years.
  • Hit base-rail rust early with a rust converter and enamel: $30 to $60 in materials, 4 to 6 hours. Done at the first orange bloom, it buys 5–8 years. Done after perforation, it buys nothing.
  • Clear the drip line so soil and mulch aren't piled against the walls.

Not worth doing: full repaints of a thin-gauge shed ($200 to $500 in materials and a weekend, on a structure worth $600), and replacing more than two or three wall panels. Panels run $40 to $90 each plus shipping, and once you're into four panels you're past half the cost of a new shed with a full warranty.

When a metal shed is the wrong answer — and when hauling it off is a waste of your money

This is the part that costs us work, so here it is plainly.

Don't buy a metal shed if you want a workshop, a she-shed, an office, or anything you'll spend hours inside. Uninsulated steel in a Pueblo West July hits 130°F+ inside by 2 p.m. and the diurnal swing — frequently 30 to 40 degrees between afternoon and dawn — drives condensation that drips on your tools. Wood or a properly insulated structure costs 40 to 80% more up front and is the right call for occupied space. Metal is for mowers, bikes, and tubs of holiday decorations.

Don't buy thin-gauge at all if you're in a Pueblo West Metropolitan District neighborhood with covenants that care about appearance. A dented, chalky shed at year 12 can become a letter in your mailbox. Check your covenants before you buy, not after.

Don't pay us to remove a shed that still works. If yours is under 12 years old, the roof is dry, and you've got surface rust on the base rail only, you have a maintenance job, not a demolition job. Spend the $60.

Don't pay us if you have a trailer and a Saturday. A 10x12 metal shed is a few hundred screws and a socket driver. Two people can have it flat in 3 to 5 hours. It holds 400 to 700 pounds of steel — roughly 0.2 to 0.35 tons — and at recent light-scrap prices of $100 to $200 a ton a local yard may hand you $20 to $70. That's not a payday, but it beats paying for the haul. Honest math: our [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) typically runs $250 to $900 for a small metal shed and $700 to $1,800 for larger or collapsed structures. If the DIY route is open to you, take it.

Limitations you should hear before you call:

  • The slab is a separate job. Breaking and hauling a 10x12 concrete pad is heavy work and priced separately — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo). Plenty of customers leave the slab in place for the next shed.
  • A collapsed shed costs more than a standing one, sometimes 40–60% more. Twisted panels are sharp, unpredictable, and slow. If yours is failing, calling before it comes down is cheaper.
  • Rodent-infested sheds need caution. Deer mice in Southern Colorado carry hantavirus risk. Don't sweep dry droppings. Ventilate, wet the area, mask up — or let a crew that does this handle it.
  • We can't make a $600 shed worth $600 again. If you're hoping removal plus replacement nets you something, it doesn't. It's a cost.

Getting the old one gone
