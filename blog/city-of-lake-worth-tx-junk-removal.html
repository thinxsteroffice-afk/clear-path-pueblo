CITY OF LAKE WORTH TX JUNK REMOVAL

If you searched "city of lake worth tx junk removal," here's the short answer: ClearPath Junk Removal does not serve Lake Worth, Texas. We work in Pueblo West, Colorado, about 650 driving miles from Lake Worth. If your junk is in Lake Worth, TX, start with the City of Lake Worth's own sanitation and bulk-pickup services. Call the city's utility billing office or check its website for the current bulk schedule and item limits. If the city won't take something, a Tarrant County hauler will. Why Pueblo West Works Differently Than a City Like Lake Worth

Lake Worth is an incorporated city, so trash and bulk collection come bundled with city services. Pueblo West isn't a city. It's an unincorporated community of roughly 33,000 residents, governed by the Pueblo West Metropolitan District. The district handles water, roads, parks and fire. It does not run residential trash collection. Each household hires its own private hauler.

That has real consequences for anyone clearing out a property here:

  • There's no city bulk-pickup day. If your weekly hauler won't take a couch, nobody else picks it up for free.
  • Your regular trash contract probably covers only the cart. Most private haulers charge extra for anything that doesn't fit in it, and many won't take furniture, appliances or construction debris at all.
  • Lots are big. Plenty of Pueblo West parcels run from half an acre to several acres. Sheds, old fencing, scrap piles and dead trees build up over years in a way they rarely do on a quarter-acre suburban lot.
  • Wind is a real factor. A pile of loose debris left in a yard can end up across three neighbors' properties in a single afternoon. Fast removal matters more here than in sheltered neighborhoods.

So the Texas habit of "set it on the curb and the city takes it" doesn't carry over. If you're relocating, plan for that before move-in day.

If You're Moving From Lake Worth to Pueblo West

This is the most common reason a Texas search ends up on a Colorado page. The most useful advice we can give is this: get rid of junk before the move, not after.

Long-distance moves are usually priced mostly on weight. A 650-mile move from Tarrant County to Pueblo County can easily involve 8,000 to 12,000 pounds of household goods for a three- or four-bedroom home. Every old dresser, broken treadmill and box of garage odds and ends adds to the cost twice: once to ship it, and again to dispose of it once it gets here.

A practical sequence:

1. 60 days before the move: Walk every room and the garage. Tag what's going, what's being sold, and what's being donated. 2. 30 days out: List anything worth selling. Things that sell in a week in a metro area of about 8 million people (Dallas–Fort Worth) can take a month to sell in Pueblo County. 3. 14 days out: Book junk removal in Texas for whatever is left. Use a Lake Worth-area company, not us. 4. After arrival: Book a Pueblo West cleanout only for the packing debris and anything that broke in transit.

> If it isn't worth $1 a pound to you, it isn't worth hauling 650 miles. Sell it, donate it or toss it in Texas.

If you've already moved and the garage is stacked to the ceiling with boxes you regret bringing, our [garage cleanout service in Pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) is the place to start.

What Junk Removal Costs in Southern Colorado

Prices vary by company, so treat these as typical ranges you'll see around Pueblo County, not a quote. Always get a written price before work starts.

  • Single bulky item (a couch, mattress or recliner): roughly $75 to $175
  • Quarter truckload (about 4 cubic yards): roughly $150 to $275
  • Half truckload: roughly $275 to $450
  • Full truckload (a standard 15-cubic-yard junk truck): roughly $450 to $750
  • Appliances with refrigerant (fridges, freezers, AC units): often an extra $25 to $75 each, because federal rules under the Clean Air Act require the refrigerant to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped

Two Colorado details surprise people coming from Texas. First, since July 1, 2013, Colorado law has banned covered electronics, including TVs, computer monitors, laptops and printers, from landfills. They have to go to an e-waste recycler, which can add a per-item charge. Second, heavy materials like concrete, dirt and roofing shingles are priced by weight or by the load, because a truck full of broken concrete weighs many times more than a truck full of furniture. For that work, see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).

When Hiring Junk Removal Isn't Worth It

This is the part most junk removal websites leave out. There are plenty of situations where you shouldn't pay us, or anyone like us.

You're in Lake Worth, Texas. We can't help, and any company that says it serves both Lake Worth and Pueblo West is either a lead-reselling website or a franchise call center that will pass you to someone local anyway. Call the City of Lake Worth first. A city bulk pickup that's already included in your utility bill will beat any private price.

You have a pickup truck and one or two items. If it's a single old mattress or a few bags of yard waste, and you can lift it and have a truck, taking it to the landfill or transfer station yourself will usually cost much less than our minimum charge. Paying $100 or more to save yourself a 30-minute drive is a bad deal if you have the vehicle and the back for it.

The items are actually worth money. Solid-wood furniture, working appliances under about 5 years old, tools and scrap metal can all be sold or given away. We donate what we can, but we can't price your dining set for you, and you'll get more from a Facebook Marketplace listing than from a credit on a junk invoice.

It's a long remodel, not a one-time cleanup. If you're gutting a kitchen over three or four weeks, a roll-off dumpster parked in the driveway is often cheaper than calling a truck out three or four times. We're the better fit when debris is already piled up and you want it gone in one visit.

It's hazardous material. We don't haul:

  • Paint, solvents, pesticides and pool chemicals
  • Propane tanks and fuel cans with fuel still in them
  • Asbestos-containing materials (common in homes built before 1980)
  • Medical waste or sharps

Those need a household hazardous waste program or a licensed abatement contractor. Anyone who offers to throw them in a junk truck "no questions asked" is taking a legal risk, and that risk can come back on you as the property owner.

You need it done today and it's a full house. We often do same-day or next-day work for small loads. A full estate or hoarding cleanout usually needs a booking 3 to 7 days out, and sometimes more in late spring and early fall, which are the busiest seasons for moves and estate sales.
