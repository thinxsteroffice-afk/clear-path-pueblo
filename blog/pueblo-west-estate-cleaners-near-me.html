"Estate cleaners" is three different businesses, and people call all of them the same thing

The phrase covers three trades that most Pueblo West families end up needing in sequence:

  • Estate sale companies — they price, stage, and sell contents on-site over a weekend, then take a commission, commonly 30–35% of gross. They want *sellable* homes. They will usually decline a house where the resale total looks under roughly $3,000–$5,000.
  • Estate cleanout / haul-off crews (that's us) — we remove everything that isn't going with the family, donate or recycle what we can, and leave the structure broom-clean for a realtor or a new tenant.
  • Deep-cleaning / turnover janitorial — carpets, ovens, grout, odor treatment. Separate vendor, separate invoice, typically $400–$900 on a 1,600 sq ft ranch.

Order matters. Run the sale first if there's one worth running, then the cleanout, then the cleaner. Calling us first on a house with a full gun safe and a garage of tools means you're paying to haul away things a sale would have paid you for.

The Pueblo West house is not a Denver house, and pricing guides written for Denver are wrong here

This is the part most national cleanout pages miss. Pueblo West was platted as one-acre-plus lots, and the housing stock skews 1975–1995 ranch, slab or crawlspace, frequently no basement. What that means practically:

  • The "attic and basement" storage that a Front Range cleanout guide assumes doesn't exist here. The contents live in a detached metal shop, a carport, two sheds, and under a tarp behind the propane tank.
  • 60–70% of the total volume on a typical Pueblo West estate is outside the house. We quote accordingly, and any company that quotes you off interior square footage alone is going to "discover" more work on day two.
  • Outbuildings that have been sealed since 2019 are rodent territory. Deer mice are endemic to Pueblo County and hantavirus is a genuine, documented Colorado risk. Do not dry-sweep or shop-vac droppings. CDC guidance is to spray the area with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution, let it sit 5 minutes, then wipe with a damp towel. Our crews mask and treat before they move a single box in a shed.
  • Wind. Loading an open trailer on an exposed acre off Purcell or Joe Martinez when it's gusting 35 mph turns paper records into a neighbor's problem. We tarp before we're full, not after.

> The cheapest estate cleanout in Pueblo West is the one where somebody spent twenty minutes with a phone camera before anybody picked up a box.

Do this before anyone touches anything

Twenty minutes, one phone, before the first item moves:

  • Video-walk every room and every outbuilding, narrating. This is your record if a sibling in Colorado Springs asks later where Dad's watch went.
  • Pull the paper first. Life insurance policies, savings bonds, vehicle titles, deeds, and the safe deposit key are almost always in a dresser drawer, a filing cabinet, or a shoebox in a closet — not in a lockbox.
  • Check the freezer, the toilet tank, and the bookshelves. Cash and silver coins turn up in all three more often than you'd expect.
  • Photograph the gun safe closed, then open it with a second family member present.
  • Agree in writing who has authority to say "throw that out." One person. Not a committee.

Where the money actually hides in a Pueblo West estate

Southern Colorado shops hold value that generic cleanout guides never mention:

  • Reloading equipment. A single-stage press, powder measure, dies, and a tumbler can retail $600–$2,000 used. Primers and powder are hazardous material — we cannot transport them, and neither can a dumpster company.
  • Ammunition. Common here, and it does not go in the trash. Pueblo County Sheriff's Office will advise on surrender; some local ranges and FFL dealers will take it.
  • Scrap steel and appliances. Pueblo yards have recently paid in the range of $90–$160 per ton for shred steel and more for clean copper and aluminum. On a shop cleanout, that's often $150–$400 back — enough to notice, not enough to change your life.
  • Tools. Snap-on, Craftsman USA-era hand tools, and welders hold 50–70% of retail resale.
  • Everything else — pressboard furniture, mattresses, CRT televisions, exercise equipment — has a negative value. You pay to move it. Budget that honestly.

When you should NOT hire us

This is the section that costs us jobs, and you should read it before you call.

  • The estate has real resale value and you're in a hurry. If the contents would gross $5,000+ at a sale, calling a haul-off crew first is the single most expensive mistake in this process. We will tell you on the walkthrough if we think you should call an estate sale company instead — but we're haulers, not appraisers, and our read is a gut check, not a valuation.
  • You have time, a truck, and one or two rooms. A partial cleanout you could do across three weekends is not worth $900. The Pueblo landfill on the south side takes household loads for a modest per-load fee. If you're healthy, unhurried, and the volume is small, do it yourself. Our value shows up at volume and under deadline, not on small jobs.
  • Probate hasn't cleared and you're not the personal representative. In Colorado, a will must be lodged with the court within 10 days of death, creditors get a 4-month claim window after published notice (up to 1 year from date of death), and the small-estate affidavit path only applies below an indexed threshold — around $80,000+ in personal property, adjusted annually, so verify the current figure. If you haven't been appointed, hauling contents can expose you personally. We will ask. If the answer is unclear, we'd rather push your date than put you in that position.
  • The family isn't aligned. If two heirs disagree about what stays, no crew can fix that, and the discarded-heirloom fight afterward is real. Settle it first.
  • You expect us to sort, appraise, or itemize. We don't. Our crews are trained to flag obvious documents, firearms, and cash and stop — not to distinguish a $40 print from a $4,000 one. Anything you can't afford to lose should be out of the house before we arrive.
  • The property is severely biohazard-affected. Heavy animal waste, sewage backup, or a death that went undiscovered requires licensed remediation, not junk removal. We'll refer you out rather than pretend otherwise.

What we won't haul, and where it goes instead

Plan for these separately, because they will not fit in anyone's truck:

  • Paint, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, antifreeze — Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection; call to confirm the current schedule and drop-off site.
  • Propane tanks (the 100-gallon yard tanks and the 20-lb grill bottles) — exchange programs and the supplier take them.
  • Asbestos-containing materials — 1970s popcorn ceiling and old duct wrap require abatement.
  • Ammunition, powder, primers, and flares.
  • Prescription medications — Pueblo pharmacies and law enforcement run take-back boxes.
