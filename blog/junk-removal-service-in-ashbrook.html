How pricing works (with real numbers)

Most junk haulers in Colorado price by volume, meaning how much of the truck your items take up, not by the hour. A standard junk truck holds roughly 15 cubic yards. For comparison, a full-size pickup bed holds about 2 to 3 cubic yards. Typical pricing ranges in the Pueblo market look like this:

| Load size | Roughly equals | Typical range | |---|---|---| | Single item / minimum | One couch or one appliance | $75 – $150 | | 1/4 truck | A small bedroom set | $150 – $250 | | 1/2 truck | One-car garage, half full | $275 – $400 | | Full truck | Whole-garage or estate cleanout | $450 – $650+ |

Some items carry an extra fee because they cost more to get rid of:

  • Refrigerators, freezers and AC units: often $25 – $50 extra, because the refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician.
  • Mattresses and box springs: often $15 – $40 each, depending on where they're taken.
  • Tires: typically $5 – $15 each, more for truck tires or tires still on rims.
  • Concrete, dirt and rock: priced by weight, because a truck can carry its full weight limit while only 1/4 full. See [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).

> The number that matters isn't the advertised minimum. It's the written quote after someone has seen your pile. Any hauler that won't commit to a price before loading is leaving room to raise it later.

These are market ranges, not a fixed price list. Your quote depends on what we actually see.

Why Pueblo West jobs are different from in-town Pueblo jobs

Most competitors write the same page for every city. Pueblo West has specific conditions that change both the job and the price:

  • Larger lots mean longer carries. Many Pueblo West properties sit on half-acre to multi-acre parcels. A pile at the back fence can be 150+ feet from where the truck can park, which takes more labor time than a curbside pickup in a city neighborhood.
  • Outbuildings pile up. Large lots tend to have sheds, old horse shelters, broken fencing and leftover building materials. Those jobs often need demolition before hauling. That's a different job from picking up a couch, and the quote should show it.
  • Wind-scattered debris. Spring winds across the mesa can blow loose trash and tumbleweeds into fence lines. If that's your problem, a raking-and-hauling job is usually priced by the hour or by area cleared, not by truck volume.
  • Soft ground after storms. A loaded truck can rut a dirt or gravel driveway after heavy rain. We may park on the street and hand-carry instead. That adds time, but it saves your driveway.

If your job involves grading, moving soil or clearing large brush, [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) or [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) may cost less than paying a junk crew to hand-load.

When hiring us is NOT worth it

A crew is the wrong choice in some situations, and this section may cost us a sale. Here they are:

1. You have one small item and a truck of your own. If you own a pickup and have a single recliner or a few bags of trash, a trip to the transfer station will probably cost you less than our minimum charge. Self-hauling a small load usually costs a fraction of a $75 – $150 pickup fee.

2. Your project will take weeks. If you're remodeling a kitchen and producing debris for 3 to 6 weeks, a roll-off dumpster is usually cheaper. A 10- to 20-yard dumpster rented for about 7 to 14 days lets you fill it as you go. We'd have to make several trips for the same job. Our [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) makes sense for the final cleanup or for jobs where a dumpster won't fit, not for ongoing demolition.

3. The items still sell. A working washer and dryer, a solid wood dining set or a newer riding mower can sell locally within a few days. Charging you to haul off items that could earn you $200 doesn't help you. Try to sell them first, then call us for whatever is left.

4. Hazardous materials. We do not take paint thinners, solvents, pesticides, motor oil in bulk, pool chemicals, propane tanks, asbestos or medical waste. Any hauler who says they'll "just take it" is putting you at legal risk. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection for these items, and that's where they should go.

5. You need an answer within 2 hours and the schedule is full. We fill up, especially on Fridays, Saturdays and around the end of the month when tenants move out. If you need same-day pickup, ask, but we won't promise a time slot we can't keep.

6. Your HOA or deed restrictions forbid the work. Some parts of Pueblo West have rules about outbuildings, fencing and where vehicles can park. Removing a shed is simple. Getting a compliance letter afterward is your responsibility, not ours.

7. The house is a hoarding situation with health hazards. We do [hoarding cleanouts](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo). But if there is heavy biohazard, such as animal waste soaked into the floors, sewage or a death scene, you need a licensed biohazard remediation company first. We can come afterward for the bulk removal.

How to get the most accurate quote

You can usually save money with about 10 minutes of preparation:

1. Put everything in one area if you can. A single pile is quicker to estimate and quicker to load. 2. Separate the heavy material. Concrete, rock, tile and dirt mixed into household junk raise the weight, and weight costs more. 3. Text a photo with something for scale, like a person, a door or a car. We can give you a price range before we arrive, usually within about 20% of the final price. 4. Point out anything with refrigerant or a fee attached so it isn't a surprise on site. 5. Ask what will be donated. If something matters to you, we'll tell you where it is likely to go.
