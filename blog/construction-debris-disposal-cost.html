CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS DISPOSAL COST

Construction debris disposal in Pueblo West runs $180 to $650 for most single-load jobs, and the majority of remodel cleanups land between $275 and $450. Broken down by volume: a quarter load (about 4 cubic yards — a gutted bathroom) is $180–$240; a half load (8 yards — a kitchen tear-out or a 12x14 deck) is $320–$420; a full 16-yard truck is $520–$650. Dense material is priced differently. Concrete, tile, dirt, and asphalt shingles typically run $95–$165 per ton because they hit the truck's legal weight limit long before they fill the box. Same-day and next-day pickup in Pueblo West carries no surcharge from us. Below is the honest math on all of it, including the jobs where you should not call us.

What Pueblo West Customers Actually Pay, Job by Job

These are real job shapes we quote in the 81007 zip and the surrounding county, not national averages scraped from a directory:

  • Bathroom gut (vanity, tub, tile, 300 sq ft of drywall): 3–5 cubic yards, $195–$285. Usually 45 minutes on site.
  • Kitchen tear-out (cabinets, countertop, appliances, subfloor): 7–10 yards, $340–$475. Add $35–$60 if a cast-iron sink or a slab of granite is in the pile — a 10-foot granite counter alone weighs around 400 lbs.
  • Re-roof debris, 20-square asphalt roof: roughly 4,600–5,200 lbs. Priced by weight, $420–$610. A single bundle of architectural shingles is about 70 lbs, and there are 3 bundles to a square — that math surprises people more than anything else on this page.
  • Deck demolition and haul, 200 sq ft: $550–$900 including the tear-down labor. See [deck & fence removal in Pueblo](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) for how we price the demo half separately.
  • Broken concrete patio, 10x10 at 4 inches thick: about 3.3 cubic yards, which weighs close to 4 tons. That is $380–$560 and it is a separate service line — [concrete & rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) — because it never travels in the same truck as light debris.
  • Whole-house remodel debris, staged over three weeks: 3 to 5 separate hauls, $900–$1,800 total. This is the job where a dumpster usually beats us, and we'll tell you that on the phone.

Why Weight Beats Volume in Every C&D Quote

A junk truck has two ceilings: cubic yards and pounds. Light debris — framing lumber, insulation, cabinetry, vinyl siding — fills the box first, so volume pricing is fair. Dense debris hits the axle rating first.

Real numbers to sanity-check any quote you get:

  • One 4x8 sheet of ½" drywall: ~51 lbs. A 1,200 sq ft drywall job is close to 1,900 lbs.
  • One cubic yard of broken concrete: 2,000–2,400 lbs.
  • One cubic yard of damp dirt or sod: 2,200–2,700 lbs.
  • One cubic yard of mixed C&D (the usual remodel mix): 350–600 lbs.

A 16-yard truck legally carries roughly 5 tons. Fill it with concrete and you're overweight at four and a half yards — barely a quarter of the box. Any company quoting you a flat "full load" price for concrete either hasn't seen the pile or is planning to renegotiate in your driveway. Colorado transfer stations and landfills bill C&D by the ton, commonly in the $42–$68 per ton range with $30–$45 minimum fees, and disposal is typically 30–45% of what you pay us. Fuel, labor, insurance, and the 25–40 minute round trip from Pueblo West to a Pueblo-area disposal site make up the rest.

> The cheapest quote on a construction debris job is almost never the cheapest invoice. Ask what happens when the scale ticket comes back heavier than the estimate — a company that can't answer that in one sentence hasn't thought about it.

The Three Real Options, Priced Against Each Other

Full-service haul (us). $180–$650 per load, we supply the labor. Best when the debris already exists, it's on the ground or in a pile, and you want it gone in one visit. Zero driveway occupancy, no permit questions, no loading.

Roll-off dumpster rental. In the Pueblo market, a 10-yard C&D box runs about $310–$460 for 7 days with a 1–2 ton included weight allowance and $65–$95 per ton over. A 20-yard runs $425–$625. You load it. Rental days beyond the first week are commonly $12–$20 each.

DIY with your own truck. A standard 6-foot pickup bed holds about 2 cubic yards and legally maybe 1,200–1,600 lbs of debris. Disposal minimum plus fuel is realistically $45–$70 per trip, and a bathroom gut is 3 trips. Call it $150–$210 in hard cost plus 4 hours of your Saturday — plus whatever a torn tailgate seal or a nail in a tire costs you.

Straight comparison on that bathroom gut: DIY ~$180, dumpster ~$350, us ~$240. DIY wins on cash. We win on time. The dumpster loses at this size and wins above roughly 12 cubic yards spread over more than four days.

When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out, so here it is plainly.

Don't hire us if your project runs longer than about four days. Multi-week framing, siding, or whole-house remodels generate debris continuously. Paying us $300+ per visit five separate times costs more than a 20-yard box sitting in your driveway for a month. Rent the dumpster. We will say this out loud when you describe the job.

Don't hire us for clean, separated concrete if you can haul it yourself. Clean concrete with no rebar, no dirt, no wood is a *recyclable*, and Pueblo-area aggregate recyclers sometimes accept it at reduced rates or, for genuinely clean loads, near zero. If you have a trailer rated for the weight, driving it there yourself can beat our $380–$560 by a wide margin. If you don't have that trailer — and most homeowners don't have anything rated for 8,000 lbs — the math flips back.

Don't hire us for a single, small, light load if you already pay for weekly service and your hauler permits it. Some Pueblo West waste contracts allow limited bagged construction material at extra cost. Most exclude C&D entirely — read your service agreement. If yours allows it, a few bags of drywall scrap over three weeks costs you nothing extra and our $180 minimum is money you didn't need to spend.

Don't hire us — or anyone without abatement certification — if asbestos is on the table. Colorado's CDPHE Regulation 8 requires inspection before renovation or demolition that disturbs more than 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet of suspect material. Pre-1990 popcorn ceilings, 9x9 floor tile, duct wrap, and old linoleum backing are the usual culprits. We are not a certified abatement contractor and we will decline the load. Testing typically runs $300–$800 and abatement runs into the thousands. Finding this out at the permit desk after we've hauled is worse than finding it out now.

Our failure modes, named. Estimates given over the phone from a description are estimates — piles are routinely 20–30% larger than described, and if yours is, the price moves. We can't safely reach debris behind a locked gate narrower than about 36 inches without hand-carrying it, which adds labor time. We do not do interior demolition inside occupied units with no protected path. We can't take paint, solvents, sealed drums, propane tanks, or anything wet-hazardous — those go to Pueblo County household hazardous waste collection, not our truck. And on hot Pueblo West afternoons with the wind up, an unsecured load isn't something we'll rush; that occasionally means a second trip.

Pueblo West Specifics That Change the Number

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, not an incorporated city. Practical consequences:

  • Demolition and structural permits come from Pueblo County Planning & Development, not a city office. Verify before you swing a hammer on anything structural; permit timelines of 5–15 business days are normal and they don't care that your dumpster is already rented.
  • No city curbside pickup exists to fall back on. Pueblo West households contract privately, and nearly every one of those contracts excludes construction and demolition debris by name.
  • Many Pueblo West properties sit on 1-acre-plus lots with long gravel drives. That's usually good news — we can back closer to the pile and cut load time by 20–30 minutes. It's bad news after a monsoon rain, when a loaded truck on soft caliche is a genuine problem. We may ask to reschedule 24–48 hours.
  • Covenant-controlled areas can restrict how long debris or a container sits visible. Worth a five-minute check before you commit to a 30-day dumpster.

Getting an Accurate Quote in One Call

Send photos. Every accurate estimate we've ever given started with three pictures: the whole pile from 15 feet back, a close-up showing material type, and the path from the pile to where a truck can park. Then tell us two things — whether any concrete, tile, dirt, or shingles are in there, and whether the structure predates 1990. Those two answers move the price more than the size of the pile does.

We quote a range on the phone and a firm number when the truck arrives and before anything is loaded. If the pile is bigger than described, you hear the new number before we touch it, and walking away at that point costs you nothing.
