FURNITURE REMOVAL QUOTES NEAR ME

Furniture removal in Pueblo West typically runs $95–$175 for a single item (couch, recliner, dresser) and $250–$450 for a full room of furniture, with most whole-house loads landing between $500 and $750. A legitimate quote should be free, given over the phone or by photo within a few hours, and confirmed in writing before anyone touches your stuff. If a company won't give you a number until the truck is in your driveway, that's not a quote — it's a negotiation you'll lose. Below is what actually drives the price out here on the mesa, what a real quote should contain, and the specific situations where hiring us is the wrong call.

What a Furniture Removal Quote Actually Prices

Nearly every junk hauler in Pueblo County prices by volume in the truck, not by weight or by hour. A standard dump-trailer load is roughly 14 to 16 cubic yards. Your sectional isn't billed as "a sectional" — it's billed as the fraction of the trailer it occupies after it's broken down.

That matters because furniture is bulky and light. A three-piece sectional might weigh 220 lbs but eat a third of a trailer. A cast-iron bathtub weighs 350 lbs and takes up almost nothing. Two loads that cost the same at the landfill scale can differ by $200 in what a hauler charges you.

Typical Pueblo West ranges as of 2026:

  • Single couch or loveseat: $95–$165
  • Mattress and box spring set: $75–$125 (see [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) — these carry a separate disposal fee)
  • Dining set, table plus 4–6 chairs: $135–$225
  • Bedroom set — bed, dresser, nightstands, mirror: $200–$325
  • Full 3-bedroom house, furniture only: $525–$775
  • Quarter trailer minimum: most companies out here won't roll a truck for under $85–$110

Why Pueblo West Quotes Run Different Than Pueblo Proper

This is the part most national-franchise pages skip, because they price the same in Pueblo West as they do in Denver. Local geography moves the number.

Distance to disposal. The Pueblo County landfill and the transfer facilities are on the east and south sides of Pueblo. From a house off McCulloch or Purcell, that's a 20–30 minute one-way haul, sometimes 35 in afternoon traffic on Highway 50. A Pueblo West job burns roughly 45–70 minutes of round-trip drive time that a job on Northern Avenue doesn't. Honest companies fold that into the base rate rather than surprising you with a "travel surcharge" on the invoice.

Lot size and driveway length. Pueblo West acreage lots frequently have 80 to 200 feet between the front door and where a trailer can safely park. Every extra 100 feet of carry adds about 10–15 minutes per large item. On a 12-piece load that's an hour of labor nobody quoted for.

Wind and unpaved access. Spring gusts on the mesa regularly hit 40+ mph, which is not a joke when two people are carrying a mattress across open ground. Unimproved dirt driveways after a monsoon storm can mean a trailer stays at the road.

Landfill tipping. Pueblo-area disposal fees run roughly $45–$65 per ton for general waste, with mattresses and box springs frequently assessed $15–$30 each on top. That's a real, verifiable line item — ask any company to show you their scale ticket if you want to see it.

> A quote given without knowing your driveway length, your stair count, and whether the item fits through the door is a guess. Treat it as one.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in Under 15 Minutes

The fastest path to a firm number, in order:

  • Photograph everything from two angles, including one wide shot showing the path from the item to the driveway. This single step is worth more than a ten-minute phone conversation.
  • Count stairs and measure the narrowest doorway. A sleeper sofa that measures 38" deep does not come down a 32" stairwell without disassembly, and disassembly is billable.
  • Say whether the item is going to donation, transfer station, or scrap. Working furniture in good condition may qualify for donation routing, which can shave $20–$50 off a load.
  • Ask for the price in writing — a text message counts. "Around a hundred and something" is not a quote.
  • Ask what triggers a change order. The honest answer exists: hidden bed bugs, a piano nobody mentioned, a second load, or an item bolted to the structure.

Most Pueblo West photo quotes can be turned around in under two hours during business days, and same-day or next-day service is realistic for 60–70% of single-item calls.

When Furniture Removal Is Not Worth Paying For

This section costs us jobs. We'd rather lose the $120 than have you feel taken.

Don't hire anyone for one lightweight item you can move yourself. A single dresser, a papasan chair, an office chair — if you own or can borrow a pickup, the Pueblo County transfer station will take it for a minimum charge in the $25–$40 range on a small load. You'll spend an hour and about $12–$18 in fuel from Pueblo West round-trip. Paying $95 for that is paying $75 for convenience. Sometimes convenience is worth $75. Often it isn't.

Don't pay to remove furniture that's genuinely sellable. A solid-wood dining set, a real leather sofa in decent shape, mid-century pieces, or anything with a recognizable brand often clears $150–$600 on Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo/Colorado Springs corridor within 7–14 days, and buyers usually haul it themselves. You'd be paying us $200 to destroy $400 of value. If you have two weeks of patience, list it first. Call us for whatever doesn't sell.

Bulk-item pickup may already be included in what you pay. Some residential trash accounts in the area include a limited number of bulk pickups per year at no additional charge, typically with size and quantity restrictions. Check your provider's terms before you pay anyone. If you have one bulky item, no deadline, and a bulk pickup available, use it.

Charities will sometimes come for free — with real limits. Local and regional donation programs will schedule free pickup for furniture in resale condition. The limits are strict and worth knowing before you build your plan around it: no rips, stains, pet damage, smoke odor, or structural damage; typically no mattresses, no particleboard, no sleeper sofas; and scheduling windows that can run 2–4 weeks out. If you're closing on a house Friday, that door is closed.

We are the wrong call if you need something moved, not removed. We haul things away. Moving a piano across the house, relocating a gun safe, or shuttling furniture to a storage unit is a mover's job, and a licensed mover carries the cargo insurance for it that a junk hauler doesn't.

Failure modes you should know about before booking anyone:

  • The item doesn't fit and never did. Sectionals and armoires that were assembled inside a room sometimes can't leave intact. Cutting or disassembly is often the only path, and if you needed that piece intact somewhere else, it's gone.
  • Bed bugs or heavy infestation. Disclose it. Undisclosed infestations contaminate the trailer and the next three customers' loads, and every reputable hauler will either decline or charge a containment premium once it's discovered on site.
  • Weather holds. Snow, ice on unimproved driveways, or 40+ mph wind will push a job. Any hauler who promises a hard time window in a March windstorm is selling you something.
  • The quote assumed one load. If you're eyeballing volume by yourself, you'll underestimate. Furniture always takes more trailer than people expect — plan for the possibility of a second-load charge on large clear-outs.
  • Documentation is on you. Junk haulers don't provide donation receipts or itemized appraisals for tax purposes. If you want a charitable deduction, donate directly.

Red Flags in a Furniture Removal Quote

  • No written estimate. If it only exists as a spoken number, it doesn't exist.
  • A price that ignores access entirely. Anyone who quotes a sectional without asking about stairs is either padding the number or planning to raise it.
  • Cash-only, no invoice, no business name on the truck. Illegal dumping is a real problem on the county roads west of town, and if your furniture ends up in an arroyo it can be traced back to the address it came from.
  • A quote 40–50% below every other bid. That gap gets recovered on site, or the load doesn't reach a legal disposal site.
  • Refusal to name what's excluded. Paint, solvents, tires, and certain electronics are commonly excluded or surcharged, and you should hear that upfront.
