ESTATE SALE CLEANOUTS: BEST RATES

The lowest total cost on a Pueblo West estate usually comes from doing things in this order: hold the estate sale first, then book a cleanout priced by truck volume for whatever's left. Don't hire the hauler first, and don't pay the estate sale company to clear the house. A sale turns sellable items into cash. The cleanout is then priced only on the leftovers: unsold furniture, kitchen odds and ends, closet contents, and the garage and shed. On a typical 3-bedroom Pueblo West ranch home, the leftovers often fill half a truck to a full truck. That's roughly $350–$800 at common Front Range volume rates. Clearing a fully furnished house with no sale first often runs $1,500–$3,500. Below are the numbers, the right order, and the cases where a paid cleanout is the wrong call.

*Pricing note: every dollar figure on this page is a typical Pueblo County range for 2026, not a promise. A written, on-site quote is the number that counts.*

How Estate Cleanout Pricing Actually Works in Pueblo West

Most junk removal companies, including us, price by how much space your items take up in the truck, not by the hour. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, about the same as 6 or 7 full pickup beds. Pricing usually comes in tiers:

  • Minimum load (a recliner and a few boxes): about $100–$175
  • Quarter truck (about 4 cubic yards): about $200–$300
  • Half truck (about 7–8 cubic yards): about $350–$500
  • Full truck (15 cubic yards): about $600–$800
  • Surcharges for some items: mattresses, tires, and appliances that hold refrigerant often add $25–$75 each

Two things push Pueblo West estates above the city-lot average:

1. Outbuildings. Pueblo West lots often run half an acre to more than an acre, which means detached garages, sheds, horse setups, and RV pads. Out there we often find another half to full truckload that nobody counted. A shed with 30 years of lumber scraps and old paint can cost more than a whole bedroom. 2. Heavy materials. Concrete pavers, landscape rock, and old railroad ties are priced by weight, not volume. If the yard is part of the job, ask about [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) separately so it doesn't swell your cleanout quote.

The Order That Saves the Most Money

The biggest mistake we see is doing things out of order. Here is the order that keeps the total lowest:

Step 1: Take out the keepers and paperwork (week 1). Family picks items first. Pull tax records, titles, insurance policies, photos, and anything with a Social Security number *before* anyone else walks through the house. A hauler's crew is fast. That's the point of hiring one, but it also means a shoebox of bonds can end up in the truck if nobody flagged it.

Step 2: Get anything valuable appraised (weeks 1–2). Firearms, coins, Native American jewelry, Western art, and mid-century furniture can be worth far more than they look. A written appraisal often costs $150–$400 and can easily pay for itself.

Step 3: Hold the estate sale (weeks 2–5). Professional estate sale companies generally take a 30–40% commission of gross sales. Many won't book a sale expected to bring in less than about $3,000–$5,000. Setup usually takes 1–2 weeks, and the sale runs 2–3 days, typically Friday through Sunday.

Step 4: Offer the leftovers to donation (sale weekend). Charities that pick up usually take clean, working furniture but often refuse anything with stains, tears, pet damage, or missing parts. Get a donation receipt if the estate or heirs can use the deduction.

Step 5: Book the cleanout for Monday or Tuesday after the sale. At this point you're paying only for what's truly left. For most Pueblo West homes, the crew clears the house in one day. Adding a packed garage and outbuildings can stretch it to two.

> Every item that sells, gets donated, or goes home with a relative is one you don't pay to haul. The sale doesn't just earn money. It shrinks the cleanout bill.

Why Waiting Costs More Than You Think

Families often put off the cleanout to save money, and it usually backfires. Here's why:

  • Carrying costs add up. If the mortgage, utilities, HOA or metro district fees, and insurance on the house come to $1,800 a month, each extra two weeks of delay costs about $900. That's more than a full truckload.
  • Vacancy clauses in insurance. Many homeowner policies limit or drop coverage for vandalism, theft, and water damage once a home has sat empty for 30 to 60 consecutive days. Read the policy before the house has been empty for a month.
  • Listing timelines. An agent can't photograph a home full of a lifetime of belongings. A week's delay on the cleanout is a week's delay on the listing.

When a Paid Cleanout Isn't Worth It (Read This Before You Call Anyone)

We'd rather lose a job than have you overpay. In these situations, a professional estate cleanout is the wrong purchase or the wrong timing:

  • What's left fits in a pickup or two. If the sale cleared almost everything and you have a truck, a free Saturday, and a strong back, hauling it to the landfill yourself will beat our minimum. Gate fees for a pickup load usually cost far less than a professional pickup. Be honest about the labor, though: stairs, a sleeper sofa, and a chest freezer are how people get hurt.
  • You have a week or more and willing helpers. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster typically rents for about $450–$650 for 7 days, with a weight limit of 2–3 tons and overage fees per extra ton. If family will do the carrying, that often beats a full-service crew on a large house. The trade-off is that you do all the loading, and the dumpster sits in the driveway all week.
  • You're selling to an as-is cash buyer. Many investor buyers will take a house with the contents still inside and handle the cleanout themselves. Their offer already reflects that. If you've accepted that kind of offer, paying for a cleanout just gives them money back.
  • The estate sale company offers a buyout or cleanout package. Some companies buy the leftovers outright or include clearing the house in their commission. Compare that price to a volume quote. Sometimes the package wins.
  • The home needs biohazard remediation. If there was an unattended death, a sewage backup, heavy mold, or large amounts of rodent waste, you need a licensed remediation company *first*. A junk removal crew isn't a biohazard cleaner, and anyone who says they are without the certifications is cutting corners. We can remove contents after remediation, and we handle standard [hoarding cleanouts](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo), but that isn't the same service.
  • Nobody has legal authority yet. In Colorado, the person clearing out an estate should be the personal representative named by the court, or should have that person's written permission. If heirs disagree about who gets what, clearing the house before that's settled can turn a family argument into a legal one. Wait.

Things we can't take, so don't expect them gone: paint cans with liquid still inside, pesticides, pool chemicals, fuel, propane tanks, and similar household hazardous waste. These need a household hazardous waste drop-off. Televisions and computer monitors are covered by Colorado's landfill ban on electronics, in effect since 2013. They have to be recycled, which is why they may cost extra.

How to Get a Truly Low Quote

You can bring your price down without switching companies:

  • Group items in one room or the garage before the crew arrives. Loading goes faster, and the crew can judge the volume more accurately.
  • Break down what you easily can. Flattened boxes and a disassembled bed frame take up much less truck space than intact ones.
  • Separate metal. Old appliances and scrap metal can sometimes be picked up for free by scrap collectors, which takes volume off your load. Otherwise, [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) is often priced per item.
  • Get the quote in writing, on site, after the sale. A phone estimate made from a full house before the sale will be high. An on-site quote made from the leftovers is real.
  • Ask whether the price covers labor, disposal, and donation drop-off. Some low advertised rates don't include disposal fees. Ours don't separate them. Still, ask every company you call.
  • Bundle the outbuildings. Clearing the house, garage, and shed on one visit is cheaper than three separate trips. See our [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) pages if the structure itself needs to come down.

What a Pueblo West Estate Cleanout Day Looks Like

The crew arrives, walks the property with you or your agent, and confirms the price before lifting anything. If you aren't local, which is common since many heirs live out of state, we can do the walk-through over a video call and text photos when the job is done. Items marked "keep" stay put. Tape or sticky notes work well. Donation-quality items get set aside for drop-off. Everything else is loaded, the floors are broom-swept, and the house is ready for the agent's photos, often the same afternoon.
