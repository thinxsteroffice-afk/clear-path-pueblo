METAL SHED REMOVAL COST NEAR ME

Metal shed removal in Pueblo West runs $175 to $650 for most jobs, with the typical 10x12 or 10x16 steel Arrow-style shed landing at $300–$425 fully torn down, hauled, and gone. A small 6x8 metal storage shed on a gravel pad is usually $175–$250. A 12x24 or larger steel building with a poured slab and anchored posts runs $650–$1,400, and that number climbs if we have to break concrete. Price is driven by four things: footprint, whether there's a concrete slab, how far the truck can back up to it, and what's still inside. Most jobs are done in 2–5 hours, same-day or next-day.

That's the honest range. Here's what actually moves the number, and — more usefully — when you should not hire anybody at all.

What You're Actually Paying For

A metal shed is not a heavy structure. A 10x12 Arrow or Handy Home steel shed weighs about 180–320 pounds of sheet panels, and the whole thing collapses into a stack of scrap in under an hour with two people, a drill, and tin snips. If someone quotes you $900 to remove a bare 10x12 metal shed on gravel, they're pricing a wood shed job or padding for a slow week.

The cost breakdown on a standard Pueblo West job:

  • Labor: 2 techs × 2–4 hours. This is 60–70% of the invoice.
  • Disposal: Steel panels are scrap, not landfill. Clean steel currently moves at roughly $95–$150 per ton at Pueblo-area scrap yards, so the metal itself often costs us nothing to dispose of — it may even offset $20–$45.
  • Slab and footing work: Only if you want it gone. Concrete disposal in Pueblo County runs about $45–$75 per ton at aggregate recycling.
  • Truck and travel: Pueblo West's spread-out lots off McCulloch, Purcell, and out toward Colorado City add drive time. Most companies build in a $45–$85 trip minimum.

> A metal shed is cheap to demolish and expensive to dispose of only when you let the wrong things pile up inside it. The shed isn't the job. The 900 pounds of paint cans, tires, and busted lawn equipment inside it is the job.

Real Pueblo West Price Examples

These are the shapes of jobs that actually come through here:

  • 6x8 Arrow shed, empty, gravel base, truck can pull within 30 feet: $175–$250, about 90 minutes.
  • 10x12 steel shed, half-full of yard tools and old fencing, sitting on 4x4 skids: $325–$450, 3 hours.
  • 10x16 shed anchored to a 4-inch slab, slab stays: $400–$550.
  • 10x16 shed with the slab broken out and hauled: add $450–$900 depending on thickness and rebar. Slab removal is frequently more expensive than the shed.
  • 20x30 steel carport-style building, wind-damaged, partially collapsed: $900–$1,600. Collapsed metal is slower and more dangerous than standing metal, not faster.
  • Backyard-only access through a 36-inch gate, no vehicle entry: add 20–35% for hand-carrying every panel out front.

Hail is the local wildcard. A 2023-vintage shed that's been through two Pueblo West hail seasons often has panels so perforated and brittle they shred instead of unbolting. That's a real add — usually $50–$100 — because everything gets cut and stacked rather than disassembled.

When Metal Shed Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

This is the part most contractor pages leave out, so read it before you call anyone including us.

Do not hire us if your shed is genuinely small, empty, and standing. A 6x8 or 8x10 metal shed with nothing in it is one of the most DIY-able demolition jobs that exists. You need a cordless drill, a 3/8" nut driver bit, leather gloves, tin snips, and a helper. Two moderately capable adults take 2–3 hours. You will save $175–$250. If you have a pickup, Pueblo-area scrap yards will take the clean steel and hand you $15–$50 back. Paying a company $200 to do a job that nets you $40 in scrap is a bad trade unless your time or your back is genuinely worth more than that.

Do not pay for slab removal reflexively. Roughly half the Pueblo West customers who ask us to break out a shed slab don't actually need to. If you're putting a new shed, a hot tub, a shipping container, or a gravel parking pad in that spot, the existing 4-inch slab is an asset worth $1,500–$3,000 to replace. Removing it can cost $450–$900 and then you re-pour later. Only pull the slab if you're re-seeding to lawn, planting, or the concrete is spalled and heaving.

Do not hire a junk removal company for a metal building you want to keep. If it's a decent 10x12 that's under 8 years old and structurally sound, list it free-to-take-away on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist Pueblo. Metal sheds get claimed within 24–72 hours in this market because they disassemble and re-erect cleanly. You pay $0. The catch is real and you should know it: about 40–50% of free-shed claimants no-show, and you're then coordinating strangers in your backyard for two weeks. If you have a hard deadline — closing, an inspection, an HOA notice — skip Marketplace entirely.

Do not expect us to be the cheapest option for a big multi-item project. If you're clearing an entire property — shed, fence, deck, dead vehicles, 6 tons of debris — a 20-yard roll-off dumpster from a Pueblo hauler at roughly $375–$525 per week is usually cheaper per ton *if you supply the labor*. Full-service removal is priced on labor, not volume. When labor is your bottleneck, we win. When it isn't, a dumpster wins. We'll tell you that on the phone.

Where this service fails outright:

  • Asbestos. Some sheds built before 1985 used transite or cement-board panels and floors. We are not an abatement contractor. If your shed's panels are cement-like rather than sheet steel, stop and get testing — Colorado requires licensed abatement, and that's a $1,500–$4,000 conversation, not a junk removal one.
  • Anything with a fuel or chemical spill under it. Sheds that housed generators, mowers, or 55-gallon drums sometimes sit on saturated soil. We haul the shed. We don't remediate contaminated ground.
  • Sheds sharing structure with something else. If a neighbor's fence or a carport ties into the shed frame, removal becomes a partial-demo job requiring a plan and possibly a permit.
  • Snow and hard-freeze days. Anchored sheds set into frozen Pueblo West caliche in January are genuinely harder to pull. We'll often recommend waiting three weeks for a thaw rather than charging you for the fight.
  • Structures over 120 square feet with electrical or plumbing runs. Those may fall under Pueblo County permitting for demolition. That's a real check, not a hypothetical.

The Pueblo West Specifics Nobody Mentions

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, not the City of Pueblo. That matters in two concrete ways.

First, the Pueblo West Metropolitan District's covenants apply to most residential lots here, and they govern accessory structures. If your shed was non-conforming and a neighbor complained, you may already be on a compliance clock. Get the removal documented with a dated photo and an invoice; that paperwork resolves the file faster than a phone call.

Second, half-acre and one-acre lots mean truck access is usually great — which is why Pueblo West metal shed removal often prices $50–$100 cheaper than the same shed in an older Bessemer or Belmont backyard where we're carrying panels 120 feet through a side gate. If you're comparing quotes you got from a friend in central Pueblo, yours should come in lower, not higher.

Third: the wind. Sustained spring gusts along Purcell and Industrial regularly hit 45–55 mph. We won't disassemble sheet metal panels in that. A loose 3x6 steel panel in a 50 mph gust is a genuine injury hazard. Expect an occasional weather reschedule — that's a safety call, not a scheduling excuse.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in One Call

Most bad quotes come from bad information. Have these four answers ready:

  • Exterior dimensions and rough age. "10 by 12, maybe 2016."
  • What it's sitting on. Gravel, pavers, wood skids, or poured concrete — and if concrete, whether you want it gone.
  • How full it is, described in trash-bag equivalents or "waist-high across the whole floor."
  • Closest a full-size truck can park. Pace it off.

Send three photos: one wide shot from the driveway showing the approach, one of the shed's base where it meets the ground, and one of the interior. A company that quotes from those photos and then changes the price on arrival for anything you disclosed accurately is a company to skip.
