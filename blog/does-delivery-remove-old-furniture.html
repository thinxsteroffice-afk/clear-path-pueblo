DOES DELIVERY REMOVE OLD FURNITURE?

Short answer: no. Rooms To Go does not haul away your old furniture as part of a standard delivery. Their delivery crews bring the new pieces in, set them up, and take the packaging — they will not carry out your existing sofa, bed frame, or dresser, and they won't move furniture between rooms to make space. The one recurring exception has been mattresses: in some markets Rooms To Go has offered old-mattress-and-box-spring removal for roughly $30 to $60, or bundled free with a higher-end mattress purchase. That offer is market-specific and has changed more than once, so confirm it in writing on your sales order before you count on it.

There's a second wrinkle nobody writing about this mentions, and it matters more than the policy itself if you live in Pueblo West.

Rooms To Go Probably Doesn't Deliver to 81007 in the First Place

Rooms To Go is a Southeast-and-Texas operation. Their showroom footprint runs through Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, Virginia, Texas, and a handful of Southwest metros. There are no Rooms To Go showrooms in Colorado, and their home delivery is tied to the regions their own trucks cover — not a national freight network.

Before you spend an hour configuring a sectional, punch 81007 into the delivery ZIP field on their site. If it bounces, the removal question is academic, and the stores that actually put a truck in your driveway in Pueblo West are the ones worth asking:

  • American Furniture Warehouse — serves the Pueblo market and is the volume leader on the Front Range. Their delivery teams generally do not remove old furniture either; mattress removal has been offered as a paid add-on in the $20–$40 range per set.
  • Furniture Row / Denver Mattress — Denver Mattress has historically been the most willing of the bunch on mattress takeaway with a new set purchase.
  • Ashley, Costco, Wayfair, Amazon — Wayfair and Amazon ship by third-party freight carriers who are contractually barred from touching anything that isn't on the manifest. Costco's mattress deliveries have included old-set removal in many markets; their furniture deliveries have not.

The pattern across all of them: retail delivery crews are paid by the stop, insured for the new product only, and have no legal path to dispose of your old goods. A crew that hauls off your couch is a crew that now owns a disposal problem and a liability claim. That's why the answer is almost always no, regardless of the logo on the truck.

Why Retailers Say No (It's Not Laziness)

Three structural reasons, and knowing them keeps you from arguing with a driver who genuinely can't help:

  • Weight and crew size. A two-person crew already moved a 180–350 lb sectional *in*. Moving one *out* doubles the stop time from about 20 minutes to 45, and the route has 12–18 more stops behind you.
  • Disposal access. Retail trucks don't have landfill accounts. They'd have to deadhead to a transfer station, pay a tipping fee, and log it — for free.
  • Bed bugs and liability. This is the real one. One infested mattress contaminates a 26-foot box truck and every delivery behind it for the rest of the day. The remediation bill runs into the thousands. No regional chain accepts that risk for a $79 delivery fee.

Also: Colorado has no mattress stewardship program. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon fund recycling through a point-of-sale fee, which is why mattress takeaway is standard in those states and spotty here. Pueblo County residents carry that cost themselves.

What Pueblo West Residents Actually Have to Work With

Pueblo West is a metro district, not a city. There is no municipal curbside service and no annual bulk-item amnesty week. You contract your own hauler, and bulk pickup is an add-on — typically $25–$75 per item with a scheduling window of one to two weeks, and most will not take a mattress or anything over about 50 lbs without a special arrangement.

Your realistic options:

  • Self-haul to the landfill. From central Pueblo West it's roughly 25–30 miles round trip to the transfer/landfill facilities on the east side of Pueblo — about 20–25 minutes each way. Expect a minimum charge in the $25–$45 range for a pickup-bed load, with mattresses often assessed a separate $10–$25 each.
  • Rent a truck. Home Depot's load-and-go pickup runs about $19 for the first 75 minutes, then roughly $5 per additional 15 minutes. U-Haul pickups start near $19.95 per day plus about $0.99 per mile — call it $50–$60 all-in for a Pueblo West round trip once you add fuel and the dump fee.
  • Donate it. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar outfits will do a free pickup *if* the piece is clean, structurally sound, and currently sellable. Lead time is commonly 1–3 weeks, and scheduling tightens in spring. Expect a flat no on mattresses, box springs, sleeper sofas with worn mechanisms, pressboard furniture, and anything with a pet-urine history.
  • Sell it. A clean mid-grade sofa moves on Pueblo-area Facebook Marketplace for $75–$250 if you price it to go and insist the buyer brings help.
  • Hire a hauler. Full-service junk removal in this market generally runs about $95–$175 for a single large item, $200–$300 for a quarter-truck load, and $450–$650 for a full load — labor, drive time, and disposal included, usually with a 2–3 person crew and a 30–45 minute on-site window.

> If your old furniture is genuinely sellable or donatable, hiring anyone to haul it — including us — is the worst financial choice on the list. Spend a weekend on Marketplace instead.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Call

We'd rather tell you this up front than take a job you'll resent paying for.

  • One small item you can lift. A nightstand, a kitchen chair, a single recliner that fits in an SUV with the seats down. A $120 minimum charge for a 15-minute job is bad value. Make the dump run.
  • You own a truck and have a free Saturday. Your real cost is a $30 tip fee and 90 minutes. We can't beat that, and we won't pretend to.
  • Your remodel already has a dumpster on site. If a 20-yard roll-off is sitting in the driveway for a kitchen job, your old furniture goes in it at zero marginal cost. Don't pay twice.
  • The piece has real resale value. Solid wood dressers, leather in good condition, recognizable mid-century, name-brand sectionals under three years old. We will haul it and it will be gone, but you just paid us to throw away $300.
  • You're on a hard deadline tied to a retail delivery window. Retail delivery slots slip by a day or more routinely, and you typically get a 4-hour window with a 1–2 hour heads-up call. If you have us pull the old bed on Tuesday and the new one lands Thursday, that's two nights on the floor. Schedule the haul *after* the new furniture is physically in the house, not before.

Our Own Limitations, Named Plainly

  • "Eco-friendly disposal" is oversold industry-wide. A stained, broken, or bed-bug-exposed sofa goes to the landfill no matter who picks it up. We donate and recycle what's genuinely viable — metal frames, appliances, intact and clean soft goods — but on a typical furniture job a realistic diversion rate is 20–40%, not the 60–80% some national brands advertise. Anyone promising otherwise on a worn-out couch is guessing or lying.
  • Access drives price, and we can't always quote it blind. A basement sofa with a 90-degree stair turn may have to be cut apart in place. That's extra labor, usually $50–$100, and it's a decision we make on sight. Narrow stairs and second-story walk-ups commonly carry a 10–20% adjustment.
  • We're not a donation broker. We can't guarantee a specific charity takes your piece, and we won't issue a tax receipt. If the write-off matters, schedule the charity pickup directly and wait the three weeks.
  • Same-day isn't guaranteed. Spring and early summer are peak. In April and May, a 24–48 hour lead time is realistic; booking three days out is better.
  • Some things we can't take at all — wet paint, automotive fluids, solvents, and certain chemicals are excluded by law, not by preference. Ask before you stack them next to the dresser.

The Practical Sequence for a Pueblo West Furniture Swap

1. Confirm the delivery ZIP before you shop a non-Colorado retailer. 2. Get mattress takeaway in writing on the sales order if the store offers it — verbal promises from a salesperson don't bind the delivery contractor. 3. Triage the old pieces into three piles: sell, donate, haul. Most households find one or two items worth money and the rest genuinely spent. 4. Book the haul for the day after delivery, not before. 5. Batch it. If the old sofa is going, look at the garage and the shed too. A quarter-load and a half-load differ by maybe $150, and you'll never have a cheaper chance to clear the rest.
