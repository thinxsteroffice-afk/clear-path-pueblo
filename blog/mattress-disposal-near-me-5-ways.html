MATTRESS DISPOSAL NEAR ME: 5 WAYS

In Pueblo West you have five real ways to get rid of a mattress, and they price out very differently:

  • Haul it yourself to the Pueblo County landfill — expect a flat per-mattress charge in the $15–$40 range on top of any gate minimum, plus your fuel and 45–60 minutes round trip.
  • Retailer haul-away with a new bed — commonly $0–$50, but only if you arrange it when you buy, not after.
  • Bulky-item pickup from your private hauler — typically $25–$75 per item with 1–2 weeks of notice.
  • Donate or resell — almost always a dead end in Colorado (explained below).
  • Junk removal crew — for us, roughly $85–$150 for a single mattress, same-day or next-day, carried out of the bedroom.

Rates change. Call the landfill and your hauler to confirm current numbers before you load anything.

Why a Mattress Costs More Than Its Weight

A queen mattress weighs about 60–70 pounds and occupies close to 40 cubic feet. At a landfill charging by the ton, 65 pounds of anything should cost about a dollar. So why the flat fee?

Because mattresses wreck equipment. Innerspring coils unwind under a compactor and wrap around the axles and shafts of landfill machinery, and getting a spring unit cut off a dozer takes hours of downtime. That's the actual reason transfer stations and landfills across Colorado quote mattresses as a flat per-item charge rather than by weight — you're paying for the machine risk, not the tonnage. It also means the fee usually applies per piece: a mattress and box spring is two items, not one. Budget accordingly.

Foam and hybrid mattresses avoid the spring problem but create a volume problem — a memory-foam king can't be compacted meaningfully and eats airspace the site would rather sell to construction debris.

The DIY Landfill Run, Honestly Costed

If you own a pickup, this is the cheapest path and we'll say so plainly. From most of Pueblo West, a round trip to the county landfill is roughly 20–30 miles. Call it $8–$12 in fuel, $15–$40 in disposal fees, and about an hour of your Saturday. Total: under $55.

If you don't own a truck, run the numbers before you commit. A rental pickup at $19.95/day plus $0.99/mile across a 30-mile round trip is about $50 in rental and mileage, plus fuel, plus the disposal fee, plus the deposit hold on your card, plus the two trips to the rental counter. You're at $70–$95 and three hours of your day — which is inside our price band with none of the lifting.

Three things reliably ruin the DIY run:

  • Gate hours. Landfill and transfer station hours are shorter than you think, and many are closed Sunday. Showing up at 4:15 p.m. on a Saturday is a wasted trip.
  • Payment. Some scales are card-only, some are cash-only. Ask on the phone.
  • The load law. Colorado prohibits driving with an unsecured load (C.R.S. 42-4-1407). Pueblo West gets steady spring wind off the mesa, and a mattress in an open bed acts like a sail. Ratchet straps over the top and through the bed, not bungee cords. A mattress that leaves your truck on Purcell Boulevard is a citation and, potentially, someone else's insurance claim.

> A mattress that blows out of your truck bed at 55 mph costs more than every disposal option on this page combined.

Why "Free Mattress Recycling" Doesn't Exist Here

Search this topic and you'll hit articles describing free mattress drop-off recycling programs. Those articles are written for California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon — states with mattress stewardship laws where buyers pay a recycling fee of roughly $10.50–$22 at the register, which funds free take-back sites.

Colorado has no mattress stewardship program. Nobody collected a fee when you bought the bed, so nobody is funded to take it back for free. Any Pueblo West page promising free mattress recycling is either copying out-of-state content or burying the fee somewhere else. Plan on paying something, to somebody.

Genuine recycling — pulling the steel, foam, and fiber apart — happens on the Front Range at a handful of processors, and the haul from Pueblo makes it uneconomic for a single unit. Breaking a mattress down yourself takes 45–90 minutes with a utility knife and tin snips and yields roughly 20–25 pounds of scrap steel. At $80–$150 per ton, that's about $1.50 at the scrap yard. It is not worth your afternoon.

Donation and Resale Rarely Work in Colorado

Colorado regulates the sale of used bedding. Secondhand mattresses generally must be sanitized and labeled before resale, which is why Goodwill, Salvation Army, and most Pueblo-area thrift operations decline used mattresses outright, even clean ones in good condition. Shelters and churches occasionally accept them, but almost always only if the mattress is under about 5 years old, stain-free, and still in a factory-sealed or newly bagged state.

Free-listing it online works occasionally, but understand the failure mode: you'll field a dozen messages, three people will confirm and not show, and the mattress sits in your garage for two weeks. If it's a good bed and you're not in a hurry, try it. If you need the room by Friday, skip it.

Bed Bugs Change Everything

If you suspect bed bugs, say so when you call. It changes our handling, not our willingness — but hiding it makes the problem worse for everyone downstream.

Rules that matter:

  • Bag it in place, in the bedroom, before it moves through the house. A $12 zippered mattress bag from a hardware store prevents dropping bugs down the hallway on the way out.
  • Some facilities refuse visibly infested bedding, which means a rejected load and a second disposal fee.
  • Don't leave it at the curb overnight. In Pueblo West, an unbagged mattress at the road is both an eyesore your neighbors will note and an invitation for someone to take it home.
  • Never dump it on the mesa or a vacant lot. Illegal dumping in Pueblo County carries fines that reach four figures plus the cleanup cost, and the county traces loads back through paperwork left in the pile more often than people expect.
