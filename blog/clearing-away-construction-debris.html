What it costs, and why weight matters more than size

Most junk removal pricing depends on how much room your stuff takes up in the truck. Construction debris is different because it's heavy, and weight drives landfill fees. A pile of couch cushions and a pile of broken concrete can be the same size, but the concrete costs far more to dump.

Here's roughly what common materials weigh:

  • Half-inch drywall: about 1.6 to 2 lbs per square foot. Gutting a 12×12 bedroom with 8-foot walls means about 528 sq ft of walls and ceiling, which is roughly 1,000 lbs of drywall.
  • Concrete: close to 4,000 lbs per cubic yard. Breaking out a 10×10 slab 4 inches thick gives you about 1.2 cubic yards, or nearly 5,000 lbs.
  • Asphalt shingles: about 230 to 400 lbs per square (100 sq ft), depending on the shingle. Tearing one layer off a 25-square roof produces 3 to 5 tons.

Typical Pueblo West price ranges for mixed construction debris:

  • Small pile (a bathroom tear-out, a few sheets of drywall, one pickup bed): $150 to $250
  • Quarter to half truck (a kitchen remodel's worth): $300 to $475
  • Full truck (about 15 cubic yards of mixed debris): $650 to $850
  • Dense material (concrete, brick, shingles, wet soil): priced by weight, and often capped at a partial load because the truck hits its weight limit first

The quote you get is the price you pay unless the pile changes after we arrive. If you add a stack of pavers you didn't mention, we'll tell you the new price before we load them.

> A 20-yard dumpster full of drywall and a 20-yard dumpster full of roofing are not the same job. The roofing can weigh five times as much, and that's where overage fees come from.

Dumpster rental vs. a haul-away crew: the honest math

A roll-off dumpster in the Pueblo area often runs about $400 to $600 for a 20-yard box on a 7-day rental. That usually includes 2 to 3 tons of weight. Each extra ton typically costs $60 to $90, and extra days often add $10 to $20 each. Prices vary by hauler, so get the tonnage allowance in writing.

Your crew still loads the dumpster, and that labor has a cost. Two laborers at $30/hour each loading for 3 hours adds $180. With a haul-away crew, the price already includes the loading. We also take the debris the same day, so it doesn't sit in the box for a week.

On a Pueblo West lot there's another cost. A tall box that's half full catches wind, and spring gusts here regularly go over 40 mph. Loose foam, insulation and felt paper end up in your neighbor's yard or out in the open land. Tarping a dumpster every night is easy to forget.

When hiring us isn't worth it

We'd rather lose the job than have you pay for something you don't need. Here are the cases where we're the wrong choice:

  • Your build or remodel will produce debris for 3 weeks or more. A new home or a full-house remodel keeps making waste. If we'd have to come back 4 or 5 times, a roll-off parked on site for the whole project is almost always cheaper. We're a good fit for a final cleanup at the end, not for weekly pickups.
  • It's almost all concrete, brick or dirt. Say you're breaking out a 600 sq ft driveway. That's many tons of material, and a clean-fill dumpster or a concrete recycler that takes clean concrete at a low rate will usually beat our weight-based price. Mixed loads are a different story. Clean concrete by itself often isn't.
  • You already have a trailer and a free Saturday. If you own a dump trailer and the pile is under about 2 cubic yards, hauling it yourself costs you a landfill fee and a few hours. Paying us $150 or more to save that time is up to you, but it isn't a smart saving.
  • Your contractor's contract already includes haul-off. Many remodel bids have a "debris removal" or "site cleanup" line item. Read your contract before you call anyone. If haul-off is in there and the pile is still sitting there, call your contractor, not us. Otherwise you're paying twice.
  • The debris might contain asbestos or lead paint. Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. Buildings from before the mid-1980s can have asbestos in popcorn ceilings, joint compound, vinyl tile, pipe wrap and some siding. We don't haul material we suspect is contaminated. In Colorado, disturbing and disposing of asbestos falls under state rules, and it needs a licensed abatement contractor. If we see suspect material when we arrive, we'll stop and tell you. That can mean a wasted trip for everyone, so test first if you're unsure.
  • You need it gone in the next two hours. Summer weekends often book 3 to 5 days out. If your inspector is coming this afternoon, we may not be able to make it.

We also turn down or change jobs for a few specific reasons:

  • Septic fields. Many Pueblo West homes are on septic, not sewer. We won't drive a loaded truck over a leach field. If your pile sits on the far side of one, we carry it by hand, which adds labor and cost. Tell us where your septic system is when you book.
  • Debris mixed with soil. Drywall mixed with dirt, or tear-out soaked by a storm, weighs much more than it looks. Keep your debris pile separate from dirt if you can.
  • Hazardous leftovers. We don't take paint cans with liquid inside, solvents, fuel, propane tanks or treated chemicals. Ask Pueblo County about household hazardous waste drop-off options.

How a typical Pueblo West job runs

1. Send photos or call. A few pictures of the pile, plus the nearest driveway or gate, usually gets you a quote range within the hour during business hours. 2. Pick a time window. We usually come within 24 to 48 hours on weekdays. 3. Confirm the price on site. We look at the pile, confirm the price and flag anything we can't take. 4. Load and sweep. A two-person crew loads everything, then sweeps or rakes the area. Most jobs take under 90 minutes. 5. Sort and dispose. Scrap metal goes to recycling. Usable cabinets, doors and fixtures get donated when they're in good shape. Everything else goes to a licensed disposal facility.

For contractors, we can come after drywall hangs, after trim-out and once more before the final walkthrough. Three smaller visits often cost less than one dumpster on a short project, and the site looks finished when the homeowner shows up.

A checklist before you call

  • Check your contract for a haul-off or cleanup clause.
  • Separate heavy material like concrete, brick, shingles and soil from lighter debris. Weight changes the price.
  • Test for asbestos if you tore into a pre-1985 ceiling, floor or wall.
  • Mark your septic tank and leach field, plus any sprinkler heads near the driveway.
  • Cover the pile if wind is in the forecast. Pueblo West wind will spread insulation and foam across the neighborhood.
  • Take photos so we can quote accurately before we drive out.
