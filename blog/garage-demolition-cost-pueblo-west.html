GARAGE DEMOLITION COST: PUEBLO WEST

Most detached garage demolitions in Pueblo West run $2,400 to $4,800 for a standard two-car structure (roughly 20x22, 440 sq ft), including teardown, loading, hauling, and landfill fees. A single-car garage runs $1,500 to $2,800. A three-car or oversized shop lands at $4,500 to $7,500. Add $900 to $1,800 if you want the concrete slab out too, and $5,500 to $12,000 if the garage is attached to the house, because someone has to frame, sheathe, and weatherproof the wall you just opened up. Permits, asbestos testing, and utility disconnects add a few hundred more. Those are Pueblo West numbers on a one-acre lot with truck access — not national averages pulled from a cost-estimator site.

What Actually Drives the Price Here

The single biggest lever is not square footage. It's how many tons hit the ground and how far a truck has to carry them.

A 440 sq ft detached garage with asphalt shingles, stick framing, and T1-11 siding produces roughly 6 to 9 tons of debris. Swap the shingles for two layers of composition plus a plywood deck and you're at 10 to 12 tons. Tipping fees at the Pueblo-area transfer stations generally run $45 to $75 per ton for mixed construction debris, so the disposal line alone swings between $300 and $900 before anyone picks up a hammer.

Other real cost drivers:

  • Slab and footer. A 4-inch slab runs $2.00 to $4.00 per sq ft to break out and haul. Footers and thickened edges push toward the top of that. Concrete is heavy — a 440 sq ft slab is about 8 tons on its own, roughly doubling your disposal tonnage.
  • Access. Pueblo West lots are typically 0.5 to 2.5 acres with wide frontage, which is genuinely good news. A skid steer can drive right to the structure, and that mechanized approach cuts labor by 30 to 50% versus hand-demo. Older in-town Pueblo properties with alley-only access frequently cost $600 to $1,200 more for the same building.
  • What's inside it. Demolition pricing assumes an empty shell. If the garage holds 20 years of accumulation, that's a separate scope — see [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) — typically $350 to $1,200 depending on volume.
  • Power, gas, and water. A subpanel, a gas line to a heater, or a hose bib means a licensed disconnect before demo. Budget $150 to $600, and expect a 3 to 10 business day lead time on utility coordination.
  • Structure type. Cinder block and brick garages cost 20 to 40% more than stick-built. Metal buildings often cost *less*, sometimes $1,200 to $2,200, because scrap steel offsets disposal.

Permits, Asbestos, and the Pueblo West Paperwork

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, so building permits go through the Pueblo Regional Building Department, not a city office. A residential demolition permit generally runs $75 to $250 depending on valuation, with a typical turnaround of 2 to 10 business days. Call and confirm current fees — they change, and anyone quoting you an exact number in a blog post is guessing.

Asbestos is the one that surprises people. Colorado's CDPHE Regulation No. 8 governs asbestos in demolition, and garages built before 1985 are the ones to watch: asbestos-cement siding panels, roofing felt, joint compound in finished interiors, and old vinyl floor tile. A 3-sample bulk test runs $75 to $200 with a 2 to 5 day lab turnaround. If it comes back positive, abatement typically adds $1,500 to $4,500 and pushes the project out by a week or more. Single-family residential structures carry certain exemptions under state rules, but "exempt from abatement licensing" is not the same as "safe to bulldoze" — verify with CDPHE or the county before you assume.

One more Pueblo West-specific item: Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenants. Before you demolish, check whether your lot's covenants and your current setbacks allow you to rebuild the same footprint. Some older garages were built under prior rules and are legally nonconforming. Tear it down and the right to rebuild in that exact spot can evaporate.

> Demolition is the cheap part. Not being able to rebuild what you tore down is the expensive part.

DIY vs. Hiring It Out — Honest Math

A determined homeowner with a truck and a weekend can do a small stick-built garage. Here's the real accounting for a 240 sq ft one-car garage:

  • 20-yard roll-off dumpster delivered to Pueblo West: $400 to $650 for a 7-day rental, with overage at $50 to $90 per ton past the included weight
  • Sawzall blades, pry bars, respirators, gloves, tarps: $120 to $200
  • Two people × 16 to 24 hours of actual swinging
  • One or two additional dumpster pulls if you underestimated, at $300 to $450 each

Realistic DIY total: $700 to $1,400 and a full weekend, against a $1,500 to $2,800 hired price. The gap is narrower than most people expect, and it closes to zero the second you hit concrete or need a second dumpster. If you have the tools, the time, and no slab, DIY is legitimately the better deal on a small garage. We'd rather tell you that than sell you a job you didn't need.

When Garage Demolition Is a Bad Idea

This is the part most contractors leave out. Skip demolition — or hire someone other than us — in these situations:

  • The structure is sound and you just want the space back. A cluttered garage is not a demolition problem. If the roof doesn't leak and the framing is square, a cleanout at $350 to $1,200 solves the actual issue for a fifth of the cost. We turn down two or three of these a month after walking the site.
  • It's an attached garage. Once you're cutting into the house envelope, you need a general contractor for the re-siding, re-roofing, header work, and weatherproofing. Junk removal crews do the demo half well and the rebuild half not at all. Hire the GC first and let demo be their subcontract — you'll usually save $800 to $2,000 versus running two separate scopes.
  • You're rebuilding immediately. If a new garage is going up in the next 60 days, your builder's demo line item is often 10 to 20% cheaper bundled, and you avoid paying two mobilization fees. Get their number before calling us.
  • You're selling within 12 months. In Pueblo West, covered parking and shop space carry real weight with buyers. Removing a functional detached garage can cost you more in list price than the demo cost. Ask an appraiser, not a contractor, before you swing.
  • Pre-1985 with suspect siding and a tight budget. If asbestos comes back positive and you can't absorb another $1,500 to $4,500, you'll have a half-started project and a stop-work order. Test *before* you schedule anything.
  • The garage is on a shared property line or an easement. Utility easements are common on Pueblo West's original plat. Demo across one without notice and you own the repair bill.

Where things go wrong even on good jobs: buried footers nobody knew about (add $400 to $900), oil-saturated soil under an old shop floor requiring separate disposal, and wind. Pueblo West gets sustained 25 to 40 mph spring gusts, and no responsible crew drops walls in that. Expect 1 to 3 weather delays on a March–May project.

Getting an Accurate Number Instead of a Range

Phone quotes on demolition are close to worthless. Ask any bidder to walk the site and confirm, in writing:

  • Whether the price includes the slab, footers, and apron, or shell only
  • Tonnage assumptions and what happens if actual weight exceeds them
  • Who pulls the permit and whether that fee is in the number
  • Whether backfill and grading are included — an open footprint with a two-foot hole is not a finished job, and rough grade typically adds $300 to $800
  • Asbestos responsibility if a test comes back positive mid-project

Build in a 15 to 25% contingency. On demolition specifically, that's not padding — that's the base rate at which crews find something unexpected under a 40-year-old slab.

Typical timeline for a detached two-car in Pueblo West: 1 day of teardown and load-out, half a day for slab breakout if included, 2 to 4 hours for final grade and cleanup. Most jobs are done inside 48 hours once the permit clears.
