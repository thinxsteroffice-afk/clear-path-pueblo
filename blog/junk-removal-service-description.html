JUNK REMOVAL SERVICE DESCRIPTION

A junk removal service is a crew that comes to your property, loads your unwanted items, and hauls them away in one visit. The price covers labor, the truck, and disposal. You point at what goes, and they lift it, carry it, load it, sweep up, and take each item to a landfill, recycler, scrap yard, or donation center. In Pueblo West, most jobs take 30 to 90 minutes on site. You're usually quoted by how much of the truck your stuff fills, and most loads land somewhere between $100 for a single item and $600+ for a full truck. Unlike a dumpster rental, you never touch the junk yourself.

That's the short answer. The rest of this page covers what a good service description should spell out, because two quotes for the same pile can cover very different work. It also covers the situations where hiring us is the wrong choice.

What a Junk Removal Service Actually Includes

When you book [junk removal in Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) or Pueblo West, a complete service should cover each of these. If a quote leaves one out, ask about it before you agree.

  • On-site estimate before any work starts. The crew looks at the pile, gives you a firm price, and starts only after you say yes. You shouldn't pay a trip fee just to get a number.
  • All the lifting and carrying. That includes basements, upstairs bedrooms, backyard sheds, and the far corner of a 2-acre lot. In Pueblo West that last one matters, because many lots run half an acre to 5 acres and the junk is often 150 feet from the driveway.
  • Light disassembly. Taking apart a bed frame, cutting a sectional down to fit through a door, or unbolting a swing set.
  • Loading and hauling. One trip if possible, more if the volume needs it.
  • Sorting at disposal. Metal goes to scrap, usable furniture goes to donation, electronics go to a certified recycler, and the rest goes to the landfill.
  • Basic cleanup. Sweeping the garage floor or raking the spot where the old shed sat.

What it usually *doesn't* include is deep cleaning, hazardous waste handling, or demolition of structures attached to your house. Some of those are separate services. [Shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) and [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) are priced on their own because they take tools, time, and sometimes a second crew member.

How Pricing Works (and What Changes the Number)

Most junk removal in the Pueblo area is priced by volume: how much of a standard box truck or trailer your items take up. A typical haul truck holds about 12 to 15 cubic yards. For scale, one cubic yard is roughly a washing machine.

Typical ranges in this market:

  • Single item (one couch, one fridge, one mattress): about $100 to $175
  • Quarter load (a small bedroom's worth, or 3 to 4 large items): about $175 to $275
  • Half load (a one-car garage that's half full): about $300 to $400
  • Full load (a packed two-car garage or a small estate room set): about $500 to $650

Four things push a price up or down:

1. Weight, not just volume. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, and brick are dense. A half-load of broken concrete can weigh 3,000+ pounds, which costs more at the scale house and wears on the truck. That's why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is quoted separately from household junk. 2. Items with mandatory handling fees. Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units contain refrigerant. Federal rules (EPA Section 608) require it to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped, which adds roughly $25 to $50 per unit. Tires and mattresses also carry per-item disposal fees. 3. Access. A clear driveway with the garage door open is the cheapest job. A third-floor walk-up or a sofa wedged into a basement adds labor time. 4. Distance. Pueblo West covers about 50 square miles, and the run to Pueblo disposal sites can be 15 to 25 minutes each way. Most local crews build that into the base price, but jobs out past the edge of the metro district sometimes add a fuel charge.

> A good quote names the price, what's included, and what would change it, in writing, before anyone picks anything up. If a company won't commit until the truck is loaded, that's a warning sign.

Where Your Junk Actually Goes

This is the part most service pages skip, and it's the part that tells you whether a company is cutting corners.

In Colorado, electronics have been banned from landfills since July 2013. TVs, computers, monitors, and printers have to go to an electronics recycler, and a reputable hauler pays that fee rather than sneaking them into a landfill load. Leftover latex and oil-based paint goes through Colorado's PaintCare drop-off program, which has run since 2015.

Here's how a typical load breaks down after sorting:

  • Scrap metal (appliances, bed frames, grills, swing sets, water heaters): recycled. This is often 20 to 30 percent of a garage cleanout by volume.
  • Donatable furniture and household goods: taken to local charities when they're in usable shape. Torn, stained, or broken pieces aren't accepted.
  • Yard waste: branches and brush can often be diverted from the landfill. See [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) for those jobs.
  • Everything else: the landfill.

Ask any hauler you're considering where the last load went. If they hesitate, they may be dumping illegally, and in Pueblo County the dumped junk can be traced back to the property it came from, which is yours.

When Junk Removal Is NOT Worth It

We'd rather lose a job than have you overpay for one. In these cases, hiring a junk removal crew is the wrong call:

You have a pickup and the load fits in one trip. If it's a broken recliner and a few boxes, and you or a neighbor have a truck, haul it yourself. You'll pay a small gate fee instead of $100+ for our minimum. The value of a crew starts with the heavy lifting, multiple trips, and items over 100 pounds.

You're remodeling over several weeks. If debris is going to keep piling up over a 2 to 6 week kitchen or bathroom project, a roll-off dumpster you fill at your own pace usually costs less than calling us 3 or 4 times. Junk removal wins when the debris already exists and you want it gone today. For a single big tear-out, our [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) page explains where the line falls.

Your items have real resale value. A working fridge less than 5 years old, a decent riding mower, or a solid-wood dresser can often sell for $50 to $300 on local marketplace listings, and buyers will haul them for free. Scrap metal is the same story: if you have a lot of copper, aluminum, or steel and a way to get it there, a scrap yard pays *you*.

A charity will pick it up for free. Some local donation groups pick up good-condition furniture at no charge. They often book 1 to 3 weeks out and turn down anything damaged, but if you have time and the pieces are clean, try that first.

It's hazardous waste. We don't haul (and no legitimate junk removal company should haul) gasoline, motor oil, pesticides, pool chemicals, propane tanks, asbestos, or unlabeled drums. Those have to go to Pueblo County's household hazardous waste programs or a licensed contractor. If a hauler offers to "just toss it in," that's a liability that comes back to you.

You need it done for under $50. We can't do it. Truck, fuel, two people's time, insurance, and disposal fees put a floor under every visit. If your budget is that tight, the DIY route is the right one.

A few limits are also worth stating plainly:

  • Same-day isn't guaranteed. In busy seasons, especially spring cleanup and the month-end move-out rush, booking can run 2 to 4 days out.
  • Estimates are firm for what's shown. If more items show up after we've quoted, the price goes up to match.
  • Weather matters. Snow on unpaved Pueblo West roads, or a muddy back lot after rain, can delay jobs that need the truck close to the pile.

How to Compare Two Quotes

When you get more than one quote, put them side by side and check:

1. Is the price all-in, including disposal and appliance fees, or will fees be added later? 2. Does the quote list how many crew members are coming? A one-person crew takes longer and may not be able to move a piano or a hot tub at all. 3. Are they insured? A dropped dresser against your drywall or a scraped driveway should be their problem, not yours. 4. Will they tell you where the load goes? 5. Is there a minimum charge, and how much is it?

The lowest number isn't always the cheapest. A quote that's $40 less and then adds $75 in "disposal fees" at the end costs you more.
