JUNK REMOVAL BUSINESS STARTUP COST

Starting a junk removal business in Pueblo West runs $6,500 to $65,000 depending entirely on how you buy your truck. Three realistic tiers: lean — $6,500–$11,000 (a pickup you already own, a $7,000 used dump trailer, insurance, licensing, first ad spend). Standard — $26,000–$38,000 (used dump-body F-450, commercial auto policy, wrap, 90 days of operating cash). Full — $55,000–$80,000 (newer 16-foot dump truck, a second driver, six months of runway). The hard floor before you touch a truck is about $3,400: a $50 Colorado LLC filing, an insurance down payment, DOT compliance, and roughly $2,000 in working cash for fuel and tipping fees. Everything below is Pueblo-specific, and every figure is an estimate you should verify with a phone call.

What the $6,500 lean setup actually buys

This is the version that works if you already own a ¾-ton or bigger pickup with a functioning hitch and brake controller. If you don't own the truck, this tier doesn't exist for you — skip to the standard tier and add $18,000–$35,000.

  • Used 12–14 ft dump trailer, 14k GVWR: $6,000–$9,500 used, $10,500–$14,000 new. Watch Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace across Pueblo, Colorado Springs, and Cañon City — inventory in Pueblo County alone is thin enough that you'll wait 6–10 weeks for the right unit.
  • Colorado LLC filing: $50 through the Secretary of State, plus roughly $25 a year for the periodic report. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County under the Pueblo West Metropolitan District — there is no municipal business license to buy, which saves you the $75–$200 that a City of Pueblo address would cost.
  • Commercial general liability, $1M/$2M: $600–$1,300 a year. Non-negotiable. Property managers and real estate agents will ask for a certificate before they'll book you.
  • Commercial auto: $2,400–$5,200 a year for one truck in southern Colorado, and the first-year quote for a brand-new entity with no loss history lands at the top of that band.
  • Hand tools, straps, dollies, sawzall, PPE, tarps: $900–$1,400.
  • First 60 days of marketing: $1,200–$2,500.

Total: about $11,000 if you buy new-ish, about $6,500 if you find a tired-but-sound trailer and do your own lettering.

The Pueblo West cost line nobody budgets: drive time

National startup-cost articles are written for metros where you stack three jobs inside a four-mile radius and dump once. Pueblo West is roughly 48 square miles of one-acre and half-acre lots with a population near 34,000. The math is different, and this is the single biggest reason first-year owners here miss their revenue projections.

A round trip from a Pueblo West job to the transfer station or landfill south of Pueblo runs 25–35 minutes each way plus 10–20 minutes to weigh, dump, and pay. Call it 70–90 minutes and 30–36 miles per dump run. At $3.10/gal diesel and 9 mpg loaded, that's $11–$13 in fuel per run before tipping.

> Two jobs a day is a good day in Pueblo West. Three is a great day. If your business plan assumes four, your business plan is for Denver.

Budget $45–$70 per ton for municipal solid waste at the Pueblo-area landfill, with a minimum charge that makes a half-empty run painful. Add the surcharges that eat margin on exactly the jobs customers call about most:

  • Mattresses: $15–$35 each, and a queen set is two units.
  • Appliances with refrigerant: $25–$60 per unit for certified recovery. Never cut a line yourself.
  • Tires: $5–$14 each. Colorado prohibits landfilling whole waste tires, so they route to a processor, not the tip face.
  • Electronics: Colorado has banned most electronics from landfills since July 2013. TVs and CRTs run $10–$45 each at a recycler. A "free TV haul-away" offer will lose you money on every single call.
  • Concrete, brick, and rock: priced by weight at a separate facility. A pickup bed of concrete can weigh 2,500 lbs — this is why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is quoted separately from household junk everywhere in this county.

Licensing, insurance, and the DOT threshold

Colorado requires an intrastate USDOT number for commercial vehicles at 10,001 lbs GVWR or above — which most ¾-ton trucks pulling a 14k trailer clear on combined weight. The number itself is free to obtain. What it costs you is compliance:

  • Annual DOT inspection: $150–$300 per vehicle.
  • Driver qualification file, medical card, maintenance records: 8–15 hours of setup and a few hours quarterly.
  • A CDL becomes mandatory at 26,001 lbs combined — which is why nearly every junk truck in this market is spec'd at 25,999 lbs or below. Buying a truck that crosses that line without noticing is a $4,000–$7,000 mistake in CDL training and a shrunken hiring pool.

Junk hauling is a service, so Colorado sales tax generally does not apply to the haul itself. The moment you resell salvaged items, you need a state sales tax license — $16 for two years plus a one-time $50 deposit.

What you can realistically charge here

Pueblo West is not a $700-minimum market. Median household income in Pueblo County sits well below the Colorado average, and price sensitivity is real. Honest current ranges:

  • Single item (couch, fridge, mattress): $95–$185.
  • Quarter truck: $180–$275.
  • Half truck: $290–$420.
  • Full load: $475–$725.
  • Garage or estate cleanout, multi-load: $900–$2,800.

At an average ticket of $285 and 2.2 jobs a day, five days a week, that's about $3,135 a week gross. Subtract 14–18% for disposal, 9–12% for fuel and maintenance, $310/week for insurance and overhead, and a solo operator nets roughly $1,700–$1,950 a week before taxes — in a good month. April through October carries the year. January and February in Pueblo West routinely run 40–60% below the spring peak, and a community cleanup weekend can flatten a week of calls outright.

When you should NOT do this

This is the part most startup-cost pages leave out, so read it twice.

Don't start this business if:

  • You have less than $8,000 liquid after buying equipment. Your first commercial auto renewal, a $1,900 transmission, and a slow February will arrive together. Undercapitalization, not competition, is what closes hauling companies in year one.
  • You need income within 60 days. Google Business Profile rankings in a market this size take 4–9 months to mature. Expect $1,800–$4,000 total revenue in month one.
  • Your back, knees, or shoulders are already a problem. This is 8,000–15,000 lbs lifted per day, on stairs, in 96°F July heat and 18°F January wind. There is no version of this where you supervise.
  • You're counting on resale to make the numbers work. Assume $0. Real resale income in a market this size is $150–$500 a month, seasonal and unreliable.
  • You want passive income. At two trucks you're a dispatcher, an HR department, and a fleet manager. Owner-operators here typically don't get out of the truck until truck three, and truck three needs $45,000+ in reserve.

And plainly: this market is small. Pueblo County has roughly 170,000 residents, and there are already established haulers, a dozen owner-operators with a trailer, and the landfill's own drop-off option competing for the same calls. A fourth or fifth serious competitor doesn't create demand — it splits it. If you're entering because the startup cost looked low, low startup cost is exactly why the field is crowded.

When hiring a junk removal company is also the wrong call

If you landed here as a homeowner pricing options rather than a would-be founder, the honest answer is that you sometimes shouldn't hire anyone — including us.

  • One or two light items and you own a truck: a self-haul trip costs you $25–$45 in tipping and 90 minutes. Hiring out costs $95–$185. Do it yourself.
  • A long, slow remodel over 3+ weeks: a 20-yard roll-off dumpster at $380–$575 for a week beats repeated on-demand hauls, because you're paying for staged access, not muscle.
  • Genuinely valuable furniture or tools: consignment or a marketplace listing nets more than we can pay you, which is nothing — we charge to remove it.

A 90-day plan if you're still going

1. Days 1–14: File the LLC ($50), open a business bank account, get quotes from three commercial insurance agents, call the landfill and write down every per-ton and per-item rate in a spreadsheet. 2. Days 15–45: Buy the trailer or truck. Claim and fully build out your Google Business Profile — 12+ real photos, service list, service area set to Pueblo West and Pueblo. 3. Days 46–60: Do six jobs at cost to generate six reviews. Reviews are the highest-ROI asset in this market, worth more than $3,000 of ad spend. 4. Days 61–90: Turn on paid search with a $25–$40/day cap. Expect $8–$19 per click and $70–$160 in ad spend per booked job at first. Track cost per booked job weekly or you'll burn $2,000 learning nothing.

Track two numbers above all others: cost per booked job and revenue per truck-hour. Anything under $135 per truck-hour in Pueblo West means you're pricing too low, driving too far, or both — and the fix is usually the route, not the rate.
