HOARDING CLEANUP FROM NEW JERSEY

Many people who search for New Jersey hoarding cleanup are adult children on the East Coast trying to deal with a parent's house out here. If that's you, we can handle the cleanout without you flying in for every step. If the house is in New Jersey, hire a crew based within an hour of it. How a Long-Distance Hoarding Cleanout Works in Pueblo West

Most of the hoarding jobs we get through our [hoarding cleanout Pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) service start with a phone call from out of state. Often the homeowner has moved into assisted living, has died, or has been given a deadline by the county or an insurer. The family lives 1,500 miles away and can take maybe 3 days off work.

A typical job runs like this:

  • Video walkthrough (day 0). We walk the house on FaceTime or Zoom with you, room by room. You point out what to keep: documents, photos, firearms, jewelry, anything sentimental. This takes about 45–90 minutes for a 3-bedroom house.
  • Written scope and price. You get a range in writing within 24 hours. We base it on how many truckloads we expect, not on hours worked.
  • Keep-box protocol. Every room is sorted before anything goes in the truck. Cash, mail with account numbers, titles, deeds, and medication go into labeled boxes. In hoarded homes we regularly find cash tucked into books, freezer bags, and coat pockets. A crew that skips the sort will throw that money away.
  • Photo log. You get photos of each room before, during, and after the job, plus a photo of every keep box before it's sealed.
  • Hand-off. Keep boxes can be shipped to you, held at a storage unit, or left in one cleared room for the realtor or executor.

If the house is being settled as part of an estate, our [estate cleanout Pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) page covers how we work with probate timelines and realtors.

What It Actually Costs

Hoarding jobs are priced by volume. Our trucks hold about 15 cubic yards per load. Here are typical numbers for Pueblo West:

| Situation | Typical volume | Typical price range | Crew time | |---|---|---|---| | One or two rooms, paths still clear | 6–12 cubic yards | $450–$900 | 4–8 hours | | Whole 2–3 bedroom house, moderate | 25–45 cubic yards | $1,800–$4,000 | 2–3 days | | Whole house plus garage and outbuildings, severe | 60–100+ cubic yards | $5,000–$10,000+ | 4–7 days |

Three things push a Pueblo West job toward the top of its range:

  • Lot size. Many Pueblo West parcels are 1 acre or larger. Sheds, shipping containers, dead vehicles, and scrap piles in the yard can double the volume. Families often only budgeted for the inside of the house.
  • Heavy material. Stacked newspaper and magazines weigh far more than furniture of the same size. A pickup bed of wet paper can weigh more than 1,000 pounds, and landfill fees are charged by weight.
  • Sorting time. If you need every page of paper checked for financial records, plan on 20–40% more labor than a straight haul-out.

These figures are typical ranges, not quotes. The walkthrough is what sets your actual price.

The Hantavirus Problem Nobody From Out of State Knows About

This is the main thing New Jersey cleanup guides won't tell you, because it barely applies there. Southern Colorado has deer mice, and deer mice carry hantavirus. Colorado has recorded more than 100 cases of hantavirus pulmonary syndrome since tracking began in 1993. The disease kills roughly 1 in 3 people who get it. The usual source is breathing dust from droppings in closed-up spaces, which describes most hoarded homes, sheds, and garages.

A house that sat closed for months while a parent was in the hospital is the highest-risk version. Before anyone, family or crew, starts digging through it, CDC guidance says to:

  • Open doors and windows and air the space out for at least 30 minutes before going in.
  • Never sweep or vacuum droppings dry. Spray them with a 1-part-bleach-to-10-parts-water solution and let it soak for 5 minutes before wiping them up.
  • Wear gloves, and wear a respirator rated N95 or better, not a paper dust mask.

Our crews follow these rules, but we're telling you so that you don't walk in on a weekend visit and start pulling boxes out of the garage. Family members doing a "quick look" before the crew arrives are some of the people most exposed to this.

> The most dangerous person in a hoarded Pueblo West house is the well-meaning relative who flew in Friday night and wants to "get a head start."

When You Should NOT Hire Us

We would rather lose the job than take one we're the wrong company for. Here are the situations where hiring ClearPath, or any junk removal company, is a mistake.

1. The person still lives there and hasn't agreed to it. Hoarding disorder is a recognized mental health condition that affects an estimated 2–6% of adults. Clinicians who treat it consistently report that forced, all-at-once cleanouts usually fail. The house fills back up, often within a year, and the person's trust in the family who arranged it is damaged. If your parent is living in the home and objecting, spending $4,000 on a weekend purge may only buy a few months. A therapist who works with hoarding, or a professional organizer who works at the person's pace, is the better first call. We can come in later for the hauling.

2. There is biohazard material. Junk removal is hauling, not remediation. If the home has any of the following, you need a certified biohazard or trauma cleaning company first:

  • Human or animal waste in large amounts
  • An unattended death
  • Used needles
  • Widespread animal carcasses

We can haul after they're done. Anyone who says they'll handle all of it with a regular hauling crew is cutting corners at your expense.

3. You need the house cleaned, not just emptied. We take things out. We don't scrub floors, treat mold, exterminate pests, or deodorize. In a severe hoarding case, plan on a second contractor for sanitizing and a third for any repairs. Budget for all three, not just ours.

4. The volume is small and you live nearby. If the job is one bedroom and a hallway, around 10 cubic yards or less, and someone in the family can give it 2 weekends, renting a trailer and hauling it yourself can cost under $300 plus dump fees. Paying us $700 for that only makes sense if your time or your back is worth more to you than the difference.

5. The contents are worth real money. Some hoarded homes hold collections: coins, tools, firearms, vintage items. If you suspect more than a few thousand dollars in sellable goods, bring in an estate sale company or appraiser before us. We donate what we can, but we're not set up to get top dollar for a collection, and a cleanout crew is the wrong people to price one.

6. The house is in New Jersey. We don't travel for these jobs, and we wouldn't recommend any crew that does. Hire someone local to the property.

Questions to Ask Any Hoarding Cleanup Company

Whether you hire us or a crew in New Jersey, ask these before signing anything:

  • Is the price by volume or by hour? Hourly pricing on hoarding jobs can run far over the estimate. Volume pricing with a written cap protects you.
  • What is your sorting process for documents and cash? If they don't have a clear answer, keep looking.
  • What gets donated and what goes to the landfill? Ask for donation receipts if the estate wants the tax deduction.
  • Will you send photos? If you can't be there, photos are how you know the work got done.
  • What won't you handle? A good company tells you its limits up front. If a company says it handles everything, be wary.
