HOT TUB REMOVAL NEAR ME: REVIEWS

Pueblo West hot tub removal runs $275 to $650 for a standard 6–8 person acrylic spa in 2026, and most crews are in and out in 90 minutes to 3 hours. ClearPath Junk Removal quotes flat-rate: $325 for a straightforward backyard spa with gate access, $450–$550 when it has to come over a fence or down a deck, and $650+ for swim spas, second-story decks, or units built into a permanent surround. What You're Actually Paying For

A hot tub is not furniture. It's a 700–1,000 lb dry weight fiberglass-and-foam shell wrapped in cedar or synthetic skirting, wired to a 50–60 amp 240V circuit, and — nine times out of ten — sitting on a concrete pad in a backyard whose only exit is a 36-inch gate.

The price breaks into four real costs:

  • Labor. Two to three people, 90 minutes to 3 hours. A spa that has to be cut apart in place is closer to 3–4 hours.
  • Disposal. Pueblo County's transfer station charges by weight. A cut-up 6-person spa lands around 0.4–0.6 tons of mixed C&D material, running $45–$85 in tipping fees at 2026 rates.
  • Equipment. Reciprocating saws, blades (a spa shell eats 3–6 demolition blades), dollies, and sometimes a skid steer if the tub can be dragged intact across open ground.
  • Risk. Damaged fence panels, cracked concrete, and torn sod are the real liability, and any crew that has done 50 of these has a story about all three.

> A hot tub that took four guys and a crane to install is not going to leave through a side gate on a Saturday afternoon. Access, not size, is what sets the price.

When Hot Tub Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

This is the part most junk removal pages skip, so here it is plainly.

Don't pay us if the tub still works and you just don't want it. A functioning spa with a good pump and no shell cracks is worth $400–$1,500 on Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist in the Pueblo/Colorado Springs corridor. Post it "free, you haul" and it will move in 48–72 hours — we've watched dozens of tubs we quoted disappear that way before we showed up. You'd be paying $325 to throw away something with resale value. Test it first: fill it, run it 24 hours, check for leaks under the skirting. If it holds and heats, sell it.

Don't pay us if you're already renting a dumpster. If you're mid-remodel with a 20-yard roll-off in the driveway, a hot tub is roughly 6–8 cubic yards once cut down. If you have the room and a willing friend with a Sawzall, you're paying for blades and a Saturday instead of $325. Same logic applies if you're doing a broader [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and already have haul capacity lined up.

Don't hire anyone before you handle the electrical. We will not pull a live 240V whip. If your panel isn't labeled and you can't confidently kill the circuit, a licensed electrician has to come first. Homeowners who skip this end up rescheduling and paying a second trip charge — typically $75–$100. Book the electrician a week ahead.

Where DIY genuinely goes wrong: the shell is fiberglass over closed-cell foam, and cutting it releases fiberglass dust that itches for days and shreds cheap respirators. A saturated foam core can hold 200–400 lbs of trapped water — a "600 lb" tub that's been sitting open through two Pueblo West winters can weigh 1,100 lbs when you try to tip it. That's the single most common DIY injury: three guys tip a tub they estimated at 600 and it's actually 1,100 with the water still in the foam.

What Actually Happens on Removal Day

1. Confirm power is dead. Breaker off, whip disconnected, verified with a meter. Non-negotiable. 2. Drain. A 400-gallon spa drains in 45–90 minutes on the factory spigot, or 12–20 minutes with a submersible pump. Do this the day before. Pueblo West is on district water and drain-to-yard is fine once chlorine has dissipated — stop chemicals 72 hours before draining, or run it to the street gutter. 3. Strip the skirting. Cedar panels and corner posts come off in 15 minutes and haul separately. 4. Cut or carry. If the gate clears, it goes out whole on dollies. If not, the shell gets cut into 4–6 sections with a recip saw and 9-inch demolition blades. 5. Load, sweep, go. Metal separated for scrap, everything else to the transfer station.

How to Read Hot Tub Removal Reviews Before You Book

Since you're searching reviews, here's what actually predicts a good outcome versus a bad one:

  • Look for reviews that mention a specific price. "Great service, fair price" is worth nothing. "Quoted $350, charged $350" tells you the company doesn't upcharge on arrival. Upcharging at the truck is the number one complaint in this trade.
  • Look for damage mentions — and how they were handled. A company with 60 reviews and zero mention of a scuffed fence either has few reviews or filtered ones. What you want is a review that says something broke and it was fixed.
  • Check whether reviews mention the electrical. If a company routinely shows up to live wiring and cancels, customers write about it.
  • Ignore review count, weigh recency. A local outfit with 35 reviews from the last 18 months is a better bet than a national brand with 400 reviews averaged over eight years and franchise turnover in between.
  • Ask for insurance proof, not a badge on a website. General liability of $1M is standard. Ask for the certificate. It takes the company 10 minutes to email.
