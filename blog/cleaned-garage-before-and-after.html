CLEANED GARAGE: BEFORE AND AFTER

A real before-and-after garage cleanout in Pueblo West usually means turning a two-car garage (about 400 to 480 square feet) where you can't park anything into one that holds both vehicles again. For a typical cluttered garage, that takes 2 to 5 hours with a two- or three-person crew and fills a quarter to a full truckload, roughly 4 to 15 cubic yards of junk. The "after" isn't a magazine-style garage with epoxy floors and slatwall. It's bare concrete, clear walls, the items you decided to keep grouped together, and enough room to open both car doors. Below we cover what changes, what it costs, what the photos leave out, and when you shouldn't hire anyone at all.

What "After" Looks Like in a Pueblo West Garage

Most before-and-after photos online come from suburban garages back East or on the coast. Garages here are different, and so are the results.

Typical "before" in Pueblo West: - Boxes stacked 4 to 6 feet high along one or both side walls, often from a move that happened years ago - Old appliances, like a second fridge or a dead chest freezer, that were "going to get hauled off" - Yard and acreage gear: broken mowers, rolls of rusted fencing wire, old drip irrigation tubing, cracked stock tanks - A thin layer of blown-in dust and grit on everything, because Pueblo West wind gets under garage doors that don't seal well - Signs of mice or packrats in corners and inside boxes

Typical "after," the same day: - Floor space back to about 85 to 95 percent clear, with kept items moved to one wall or a shelf unit - Two vehicles parked inside, or one vehicle plus a workbench area - Floor swept or blown out (we don't pressure-wash or seal floors as part of junk removal) - Every item that left the property sorted into landfill, recycling, scrap metal, or donation

The biggest difference between the two photos is usually what you can't see: the decision-making. Hauling goes fast. Deciding what to keep is what takes the time.

The Numbers Behind a Typical Cleanout

These are realistic ranges for the Pueblo area, so you can sanity-check any quote you get, including ours:

  • Time on site: 2 to 3 hours for a moderately full two-car garage, and 4 to 6 hours for a packed three-car garage or one with a lot of heavy items
  • Volume: A full junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards. Most single-garage jobs use 25 to 50 percent of a truck, and a packed two-car garage often fills one completely
  • Pricing structure: Most local haulers price by volume, not by the hour, with surcharges for items like mattresses, tires, and refrigerant appliances
  • Turnaround: A same-week appointment is common outside of late spring, when demand peaks around May and June as people clean up for summer

If you're paying for a storage unit to hold the overflow, check that math too. A 10x10 unit in the Pueblo area commonly runs $70 to $130 a month, or $840 to $1,560 a year. Two years of rent on boxes you never open often costs more than a full garage cleanout.

What Before-and-After Photos Don't Show You

This is the part most junk removal blogs skip.

1. Rodent contamination. Pueblo West's open lots and prairie edges mean deer mice get into a lot of garages, and deer mice carry hantavirus. Colorado logs a handful of cases most years, and the CDC's guidance is specific: ventilate the space for at least 30 minutes before you start, then spray droppings with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution and let it soak for 5 minutes before wiping. Never dry-sweep or vacuum droppings, because that's how the virus gets into the air. If your "before" has nesting material in boxes, the cleanout needs to be handled more carefully than the photos suggest.

2. Snakes and black widows. Prairie rattlesnakes are native to Pueblo West, and they will shelter in cool, cluttered garages from about April through October. Black widows love undisturbed boxes against garage walls. Wear gloves and move stacks from the top down. Don't reach blindly into the back of a pile.

3. Things that can't go in the truck. Colorado has banned electronics such as TVs and computer monitors from landfills since 2013, so those must be recycled. Most junk haulers, us included, can't take: - Liquid paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals - Full or partially full propane cylinders and gas cans with fuel - Motor oil and antifreeze in open containers - Asbestos-containing materials (older floor tiles or pipe wrap)

Leftover latex and oil paint can go to Colorado's PaintCare drop-off sites, a program that has run since 2015. Plan for these items separately. They're often the last 5 percent of a garage, and they can hold up the "after" photo for a week.

4. The floor you find. Once everything is out, you'll see what the clutter was hiding: oil stains, cracked slab sections, rodent damage on drywall, or a water heater leaking into a corner. Budget mentally for one surprise.

> The truck can remove 3 tons of stuff in an afternoon. It can't decide whether you keep your late father's table saw. Make those calls before the crew shows up, and your job gets faster and cheaper.

When a Garage Cleanout Isn't Worth Paying For

We'd rather lose a job than have you pay for something you didn't need. Hiring a crew is often the wrong move in these situations:

  • Your garage is mostly decision-making, not hauling. If the clutter is 80 percent small items you need to sort, like paperwork, photos, kids' keepsakes, and tools, a crew will stand around while you decide. Spend two weekends sorting first, then call for the haul-away. Or skip us entirely if what's left fits in your trash cans over a few weeks.
  • You have a pickup and the junk is light. If you're getting rid of a few broken shelves and some yard waste, one trip to the landfill in your own truck costs a fraction of a minimum service charge. You don't need us for half a pickup bed of light debris.
  • Most of it is sellable. If the garage is full of working tools, a decent riding mower, or a gun safe, sell them on Facebook Marketplace or at a Pueblo West yard sale first. We donate usable items when we can, but you won't get paid for them.
  • You'll refill it within a year. Garages bounce back. If the clutter came from a habit, like buying in bulk or never dealing with items from a remodel, a cleanout only buys you 6 to 12 months. Without shelving, a "nothing goes on the floor" rule, or a plan for overflow, you'll be looking at the same "before" photo next fall. That's money spent on a reset, not a fix.
  • The real problem is hazardous materials. If your garage is mostly old chemicals, fuel, and paint, a junk removal company is the wrong call. Use county household hazardous waste options and PaintCare.
  • You want an organized, finished garage. We clear space. We don't install cabinets, coat floors, or design storage systems. If you expect the Pinterest-style "after," you'll need a garage organizer or contractor too, which commonly adds $1,500 to $5,000 or more on top of the cleanout.

If any of these sound like you, the honest answer is to do some or all of it yourself.

How to Get a Better "After" for Less Money

If hiring out does make sense, these steps lower the cost and improve the result:

1. Take your own "before" photos from all four corners and send them for a quote. Photos let a hauler estimate volume within about 10 to 20 percent, which helps you avoid surprises on the day. 2. Mark keepers with painter's tape or move them to the driveway before the crew arrives. Anything not taped goes. 3. Separate hazardous items in advance. Put paint, fuel, and chemicals in one corner so they don't slow the job down. 4. Bundle jobs. If you also have an old hot tub, a broken trampoline, or a pile of yard waste, doing it all in one visit is usually cheaper than two separate trips. See all our [services](/services) to see what can be combined. 5. Plan the 48 hours after. Have shelving bought and ready before cleanout day. A $150 to $300 set of heavy-duty steel shelves installed right after the cleanout does more to keep the "after" than anything else.

Pueblo West-Specific Tips for Keeping It Clean

  • Seal the door. A $30 to $60 bottom seal replacement on the garage door cuts down on blown-in dust and keeps mice and snakes out. In Pueblo West wind, it's the cheapest maintenance you can do.
  • Get stuff off the floor. Clutter on the ground is what turns into rodent habitat. Keep things on shelves at least 6 inches off the slab.
  • Do a 15-minute reset each season. Sweep, toss the obvious junk, and check corners for droppings. Four of these a year keeps you from needing another full cleanout.
  • Watch your heat-sensitive items. Unshaded Pueblo West garages regularly top 100°F in July. Aerosol cans, candles, vinyl records, and photos shouldn't be stored there.
