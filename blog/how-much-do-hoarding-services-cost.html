HOW MUCH DO HOARDING SERVICES COST

Most hoarding cleanouts in Pueblo West run $2,400 to $4,500 for a moderate single-story home — roughly 1,200 to 1,600 sq ft with cluttered but walkable rooms. Light cases (one or two rooms, no biohazard) land at $850 to $1,500. Severe cases — floor-to-ceiling accumulation, structural damage, animal waste, or hazmat — run $6,000 to $12,000, and we've quoted a 2,800 sq ft Pueblo West property with a detached shop at $18,400. Price is driven by volume hauled, labor hours, and disposal weight, not by square footage. A firm number requires a walkthrough; anyone quoting a hoarding job over the phone is guessing, and that guess almost always moves up on the day of work.

What Actually Drives the Number

Three inputs set the price, in this order:

  • Volume. We price by how much of the truck you fill. A 16-cubic-yard load runs $550 to $700 in the Pueblo West service area. A moderate hoarding home is typically 4 to 7 loads. A severe one can be 12 to 20.
  • Labor hours. Hoarding is not "toss it in the truck." Every box gets opened because cash, deeds, jewelry, and firearms turn up constantly. Sorting labor runs $65 to $85 per technician-hour. A 3-person crew for a 10-hour day is roughly $1,950 to $2,550 in labor alone before disposal.
  • Disposal weight. Household junk averages about 200 lbs per cubic yard. A 16-yard load is often 1.5 to 2 tons. Tipping fees at the Pueblo County landfill are billed by weight, and heavy categories — plaster, tile, soaked carpet, concrete — can double the disposal line on an otherwise ordinary load. Call the scale house for current per-ton rates before you budget; they change.

Add-ons stack on top: biohazard remediation (rodent droppings, urine-saturated subfloor, sharps) adds $2,000 to $5,000 depending on how much flooring comes out. Bed-bug-infested mattresses require wrapping and separate handling, roughly $100 to $150 each above standard [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) pricing.

Real Pueblo West Numbers, Not National Averages

National hoarding-cost articles quote $1,000–$30,000 and stop there. That range is useless because it blends Manhattan labor rates with rural Colorado. Here's what's actually different about pricing this work in Pueblo West specifically:

Lot size changes everything. A large share of Pueblo West sits on parcels of an acre or more, and hoarding rarely stays inside the house. Roughly 4 out of 10 hoarding jobs we quote here include an exterior component — dead vehicles, scrap piles, 15 years of lumber behind the shop, a collapsed shed. That exterior work often costs more than the interior, because it's heavy and it's a separate disposal stream. Expect $900 to $2,200 added for a typical yard component, and see [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) or [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) for how those get priced standalone.

Access is easy here, which saves you money. In a Denver or Colorado Springs condo, crews carry every load down a hallway and an elevator. In Pueblo West, we back a truck within 30 feet of the door on most properties. That access difference is worth roughly 15% to 25% off comparable Front Range hoarding pricing. If you got a Colorado Springs quote and it feels high, that gap is real, not a negotiating tactic.

Metro District covenant enforcement drives timing. A visible-from-the-street junk accumulation can trigger a compliance notice, and those come with clocks — often 10 to 30 days. If you're working a deadline, the exterior gets cleared first and the interior can be phased. That sequencing alone can cut your immediate out-of-pocket by 50% or more without changing the total.

> The cheapest hoarding cleanout is the one you don't pay a crew to sort. Every hour we spend opening boxes you could have opened is an hour billed at labor rates.

Ways to Cut the Cost 30–50%

  • Pre-sort what you can. If the family pulls the obvious keep-items and clears one staging room before we arrive, we routinely take 20% to 35% off the labor estimate. This is the single largest lever you control.
  • Phase it. Two truckloads a month for four months costs the same total but never requires $4,000 at once. We'll write the estimate as phases if you ask.
  • Separate the metal. Appliances, water heaters, and scrap steel have salvage value. Pulling those out yourself and hauling them to a Pueblo scrap yard can offset $150 to $400 on a typical job.
  • Skip the "haul everything" reflex. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and local donation channels will take usable furniture and building materials. Every yard donated instead of dumped is a yard you don't pay to haul.
  • Book a weekday. Weekend crews cost more everywhere, including here.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part most cleanout companies leave off their pricing page. Read it before you call.

If the hoarding is active and untreated, do not hire anyone yet. Hoarding disorder is a recognized psychiatric condition, and a forced cleanout without the person's genuine buy-in has a documented tendency to reverse. We have watched a house we emptied refill to roughly its prior state inside 18 months. If you're spending $4,000 to clear a home whose occupant hasn't agreed to the process and isn't working with a therapist, you are very likely buying an expensive 12-to-24-month delay, not a solution. Spend that money on treatment first. We'd rather tell you that than take the job twice.

If it's one room and you have a pickup truck and a weekend, don't hire us. A single spare bedroom of boxes is genuinely a DIY project. Two trips to the transfer station and a Saturday will beat our $850 minimum. Same for a moderately cluttered two-car garage — look at [garage cleanout pricing](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and compare honestly against your own time.

If you're a landlord who mainly needs volume gone, rent a dumpster instead. A 20-yard roll-off for a week runs roughly $450 to $650 in the Pueblo area. If nothing needs sorting and nobody cares what gets thrown away, a dumpster beats full-service hauling on cost per yard, every time. Our value is sorting, valuables recovery, and the fact that you never touch it — if you don't need those three things, you're overpaying us.

We are not a biohazard remediation contractor. We handle general debris, heavy rodent contamination, and standard sharps protocols. We do not do mold remediation, meth-lab decontamination, or unattended-death scenes. Those require licensed specialists, and if we walk in and see any of the three, we will stop and refer you out — after you've already spent time on a walkthrough. Tell us up front if any of that is possible so nobody wastes a day.

Our estimates move. Honestly: a hoarding walkthrough is often limited to the rooms we can physically enter. If a back bedroom is blocked and we can't see it, that room is priced on an assumption. Somewhere around 1 in 5 severe jobs comes in 10% to 20% over the written estimate once the blocked areas open up. We call before continuing, and you can stop us at any point — but a company promising a locked-in flat price sight-unseen on a severe hoard is either padding heavily or planning to renegotiate mid-job.

Insurance almost certainly won't pay. Homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental damage, not accumulation. Unless a covered event — a burst pipe, a fire — caused the loss, expect to pay out of pocket. Don't build your budget around a claim.

Getting an Accurate Quote

Send photos or a walkthrough video of every room, including closets and the garage, before we schedule. Photos alone get you a range within about 20%; an on-site walkthrough, which is free within our standard Pueblo West and [Pueblo](/areas/pueblo) service area, gets you a written fixed scope. We ask for a 25% deposit on jobs over $3,000 to hold crew days, and the balance on completion.

If the property is being sold, in probate, or subject to a court-ordered timeline, say so on the first call — those jobs get sequenced differently, and [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) or [foreclosure and eviction cleanout](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) pricing may fit better than a standard hoarding scope. Full details on process, timing, and what we can't take are on the [hoarding cleanout page](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) and the [FAQ](/faq).

One last thing worth budgeting for: a moderate hoarding cleanout takes 2 to 4 working days, not an afternoon. Plan for the house to be unusable during that window, and plan for the emotional load on whoever is making keep-or-toss calls. That person needs breaks. Jobs where a family member tries to supervise all 10 hours a day, three days running, are the ones that stall out at 70% complete — and a stalled job still gets billed for the hours worked.
