What Appliance Removal Looks Like in Pueblo West

Most calls we get are for one of four things: a dead refrigerator, a washer and dryer pair replaced during a remodel, a chest freezer that quit in the garage, or a whole kitchen of appliances from an estate or rental turnover. Here's what an ordinary pickup looks like:

  • Arrival window: usually a 2-hour window, often the same week you call.
  • Crew size: 2 people. A full-size refrigerator weighs 200–300 lbs, and getting one down Pueblo West's split-level stairs safely takes two people.
  • Time on site: 15–30 minutes for one appliance. Longer if it has to come up from a basement or through a door narrower than 30 inches.
  • What you do: nothing beyond pointing at it. Unplug it and shut off the water supply valve beforehand if you can. We'll explain why below.

What It Costs (And Why Prices Vary)

Junk haulers in southern Colorado usually price by how much truck space your items take, with a minimum charge for single-item pickups. As a rough market range, one appliance pickup usually costs $75–$200. Where you land in that range depends on:

  • Weight and access. A 150 lb dryer in an open garage is a quick job. A 300 lb side-by-side fridge in a basement with a turning staircase is not.
  • Refrigerant. Fridges, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant. Federal law (Clean Air Act, Section 608) requires it to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped. That step costs money, so these items usually cost more than a washer or a range.
  • Volume. If you add a mattress, a couch, or garage junk, the price per item drops because you're filling more of a truck we're already sending. Get a firm quote before the crew starts loading. A hauler who won't give you a number until everything is on the truck is the one to avoid.

Where Your Old Appliance Actually Goes

People rarely ask this, but most out-of-state guides that rank for this kind of search don't answer it either.

A typical refrigerator is about 60–75% steel by weight, so most appliances are worth more as scrap metal than as landfill. Most of our appliances go out in this order:

1. Resale or donation. If it works and is under roughly 8 years old, it may go to a reuse outlet such as the Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Pueblo. 2. Refrigerant recovery. Cooling appliances go to a facility where the refrigerant is drawn off. Older units from before the mid-1990s may still contain R-12, an ozone-depleting refrigerant that's been banned from new equipment for about 30 years. 3. Scrap metal recycling. The stripped shell, motor, and copper lines go to a scrap processor. 4. Landfill. Only what's left: foam insulation, plastic liners, glass shelves.

> A refrigerator dumped in an arroyo off a Pueblo West dirt road isn't just ugly. It's a federal refrigerant violation, and the steel in it is worth more recycled than buried.

The "Second Fridge in the Garage" Math

Many of our pickups are garage fridges, and the numbers are worth knowing before you decide to keep one running.

A refrigerator built in the 1990s can use 1,000–1,400 kWh per year. A new ENERGY STAR model uses about 400–500 kWh. At roughly $0.15 per kWh, that old garage fridge may cost you $100–$200 a year in electricity to hold a few cases of drinks. Pueblo West garages also run past 100°F in July and below freezing in January. Most refrigerators aren't rated for either extreme, so the compressor runs harder and the unit wears out faster.

Fridges last about 10–13 years, washers about 10–14, and dryers about 13. If yours is near the end of that range and already having trouble, repairing it often costs more than half the price of a new one. A common rule of thumb says to replace it at that point.

When Hiring Us Isn't Worth It

These are the situations where we'd tell you to keep your money:

  • You're buying a new appliance from a big-box retailer. Many retailers will haul away the old unit when they deliver the new one, either free or for a small fee, often well under the cost of a separate pickup. Ask at checkout. If they'll take it, let them.
  • The appliance works and you have a few days. A working washer, dryer, or fridge under about 8 years old will often sell on Facebook Marketplace for $50–$200, or someone will pick it up free. You make money, or at least pay nothing, instead of paying us.
  • You have a pickup truck, a hand truck, and a strong friend. A washer or electric range (no refrigerant) can go straight to a scrap yard, and the yard may pay you a few dollars. Fridges and freezers are harder because many yards won't take them unless the refrigerant has been recovered and documented.
  • Your utility is running a recycling program. Electric utilities, including some in Colorado, have offered programs that pick up a working old fridge or freezer for free and pay a rebate, historically around $50. Check with your electric provider before calling any hauler. Programs come and go, so confirm it's active.
  • It's one small appliance. A microwave, toaster oven, or mini-fridge doesn't justify a minimum truck charge on its own. Hold it until you have more junk to clear, or drop it at an e-waste or household hazardous waste collection event.

There are also jobs we can't do well:

  • We don't disconnect gas lines. If you have a gas range or gas dryer, hire a licensed plumber or gas technician to cap the line before we arrive. Don't do it yourself unless you're qualified. A poorly capped gas line is a real hazard, and moving the appliance first makes it worse.
  • We don't remove hardwired or built-in units. Built-in dishwashers, wall ovens, and some cooktops are wired or plumbed into cabinetry. Pulling them out is a contractor's job. We can haul them once they're loose.
  • Water lines can leak. If the fridge has an ice maker or there's a washer on the second floor, shut off the supply valve before we come. A valve that's been frozen in place for 15 years will sometimes start dripping when it's finally turned. That's a plumbing problem, and no hauler is the right person to fix it.
  • Large commercial refrigeration is a different job. Walk-in coolers and restaurant reach-ins with large refrigerant charges usually need an HVAC/R contractor to remove them, not a junk crew.

If any of these apply, the honest answer is that someone else is the better fit. We'll tell you that on the phone too.

How to Get Ready for Pickup Day

If you do book a pickup, a little prep makes it faster and cheaper:

  • Unplug the fridge or freezer 24 hours ahead so it can defrost, and put a towel under it. A freezer full of ice leaves a trail of water across your floor.
  • Empty everything. Shelves and drawers can stay, but take out the food. Spoiled food in a dead fridge in a 95°F Pueblo West garage gets bad fast.
  • Shut off the water to ice makers and washers, and disconnect the hoses if you're comfortable doing it.
  • Clear a path at least 36 inches wide from the appliance to the door.
  • Tape the doors shut if there are kids around. An abandoned fridge with a working latch is a known entrapment hazard.
