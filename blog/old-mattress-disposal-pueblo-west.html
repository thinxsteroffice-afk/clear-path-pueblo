OLD MATTRESS DISPOSAL PUEBLO WEST

If you live in Pueblo West and need an old mattress gone, you have three real choices. Haul it yourself to a Pueblo-area landfill or transfer station, which typically runs $25 to $45 for a minimum load plus a $15 to $30 mattress surcharge, and eats 45 to 70 minutes round trip. Let the retailer take it when your new set is delivered, which is free to about $50 and is the cheapest option that exists. Or book a pickup. ClearPath charges $85 to $125 for a single mattress in Pueblo West and $110 to $160 for a mattress and box spring, usually next day, with two people carrying it out in under 20 minutes. Everything else costs more than it saves.

What a Mattress Actually Weighs and Why That Matters

The reason mattress disposal is priced like a two-person job is that it is one. A queen innerspring runs 120 to 160 pounds. A king pillowtop can hit 180. Memory foam is lighter, roughly 90 to 110 pounds for a queen, but it has no rigid frame, so it folds around doorways and stair rails and fights you the entire way.

Dimensions beat weight as the real problem. A queen is 60 by 80 inches. A standard short-bed pickup measures about 78 inches with the tailgate up, so a queen does not lie flat. It rides at an angle with the tailgate down, held by whatever straps you own.

On Highway 50 and the McCulloch corridor, spring gusts regularly run 35 to 50 mph. A mattress is a sail with a 33-square-foot surface. Two ratchet straps front and back, not bungees, and not the trip's only strap through the handles.

The DIY Numbers, Honestly

Here is the arithmetic most Pueblo West homeowners are actually deciding between:

  • You already own a truck: roughly 20 to 26 miles round trip from central Pueblo West to a Pueblo disposal site, $8 to $12 in fuel, $25 to $45 minimum load fee, $15 to $30 mattress surcharge. Call it $50 to $85 and about an hour, plus a second person to lift.
  • You rent a truck: $19.95 per day plus $0.99 per mile is the advertised rate, but 30 miles and fuel puts a one-mattress trip at $60 to $80 before disposal fees, so $90 to $115 all in. At that point a pickup service is cheaper and you keep your Saturday.
  • You have a private hauler: Pueblo West has no municipal trash collection, so you contract your own. Most haulers charge $25 to $75 for a bulky item pickup, and a meaningful share exclude mattresses outright. Read the policy before you drag it to the road.
  • You wait for a community cleanup event: these run seasonally and are genuinely free when they happen, but bulky bedding is one of the most commonly excluded categories. Confirm mattresses are on the accepted list before you plan around it.

If you own a truck, have a helper, and it is one mattress, do it yourself. That is the honest answer and we will tell you the same thing on the phone.

Colorado Has No Mattress Recycling Program, and It Changes Everything

This is the part almost nobody publishes. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon fund statewide mattress recycling through a fee added at purchase, which pays for free drop-off. Colorado has no such program. There is no state-funded free mattress recycling anywhere in Pueblo County.

A mattress is about 75% recyclable by weight. An innerspring queen holds 20 to 30 pounds of recoverable steel, plus foam, felt, and a hardwood frame in the box spring. Recyclers exist for exactly this reason, but the nearest real mattress recycling operation is in the Denver metro, roughly 115 miles each way from Pueblo West. That is a four-hour round trip and $50 to $70 in fuel to recycle one mattress that costs $85 to have hauled locally.

> We will not pretend your mattress gets recycled. From Pueblo West, almost every mattress goes to a landfill, whoever hauls it. Any company telling you otherwise is charging you for a truck trip they are not making.

If recycling genuinely matters to you and you are moving a full bedroom set, batching four to eight mattresses makes a Denver run defensible. One mattress does not.

When You Should Not Pay Us

We would rather lose the job than take money for something you can solve for free. Skip a paid pickup in these cases.

  • You are buying a new mattress. Most retailers haul the old set for free with delivery, or charge $25 to $50. It is the single cheapest disposal in the state. Ask at the point of sale, before delivery day, because crews cannot always add it on the spot.
  • It is one mattress and you own a truck. You save $30 to $70 doing it yourself. Bring a second person and two straps.

  • You only need it moved out of a bedroom. We are a disposal service, not a moving service. If the mattress is going to a friend's house, hire movers.
  • You want us to drive 30-plus minutes for a single item. Our minimum charge exists because a truck, fuel, two crew members, and a disposal fee do not scale down. Bed Bugs, Which Is Where This Gets Expensive

Tell us before we arrive. Not at the door.

Bed bug adults survive two to five months without feeding, and a wrapped mattress in a truck bed is a transfer risk to the next four customers that day. We handle these jobs, but they require sealing the mattress in 4-mil or heavier plastic inside the room, not in the hallway, and they carry a $40 to $75 surcharge to cover disposal handling and truck treatment afterward. A crew that finds out on site may have to reschedule.

There is also a cheaper path worth knowing. If the infestation is caught early and the mattress is under five years old, a zippered encasement runs $40 to $70 and traps existing bugs while letting you keep the mattress. That beats a $1,200 replacement set plus a disposal fee. Talk to a pest control operator before you decide the mattress is trash.

Donation Almost Never Works

People call us after four failed donation attempts. Save yourself the week. Goodwill, the Salvation Army, and the ARC in southern Colorado generally decline used mattresses, and most shelters do too. The liability around bed bugs and bodily fluids ended mattress donation as a practical option years ago. Box springs are occasionally accepted where the mattress is not, but treat that as the exception.

Selling is similarly grim. Used mattresses in decent condition list on local marketplaces for $40 to $80 and sit for weeks, and you still have to be home for strangers who do not show up.
