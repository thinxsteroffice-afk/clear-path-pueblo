JUNK REMOVAL COST IN PUEBLO WEST

Junk removal in Pueblo West typically runs $125–$225 for a single item or small pickup load, $275–$450 for a quarter-to-half truckload (a garage corner, a bedroom set, a small shed's worth), and $550–$850 for a full 15-cubic-yard truckload — the equivalent of about four heaped pickup beds. Most Pueblo West jobs we run land between $300 and $500. Heavy material is priced differently: concrete, dirt, rock, and roofing shingles are charged by weight, usually $85–$140 per ton plus labor, because a half-load of flagstone weighs more than a full load of furniture. Prices below are ours, current as of August 2026, and include labor, loading, hauling, and disposal fees.

What Actually Drives the Number

Almost every honest quote in this trade comes down to four inputs. Understanding them lets you predict your own price within about 15% before anyone shows up.

  • Volume. Standard trucks hold 14–16 cubic yards. A quarter truck is roughly 3.5–4 cubic yards — one heaped 8-foot pickup bed. A "we filled the garage bay" job is usually 6–9 cubic yards.
  • Weight. Disposal is billed to us by the ton at the transfer station, generally $45–$70 per ton for mixed municipal waste depending on the facility and load type. A truck of couches might weigh 1,200 lbs; the same truck of tile and mortar can hit 8,000 lbs and legally max out the axle before it looks half full.
  • Labor time. Two techs at a curbside pile move 4 cubic yards in about 20 minutes. The same 4 cubic yards carried down from a bonus room over a garage, around a corner, takes 75 minutes. Stairs, narrow gates, and long carries add roughly 25–40%.
  • Drive time. This is where "near me" stops being a search phrase and starts being math.

Why "Near Me" Costs Real Money in Pueblo West

Pueblo West sprawls. It's unincorporated Pueblo County, not a city, spread across roughly 50 square miles of one-acre-plus lots between Highway 50 and the reservoir. A job off Purcell Boulevard and a job on the far west end past Joe Martinez can be 14 miles and 22 minutes apart, and neither is close to a disposal site.

From a typical Pueblo West address, the round trip to the transfer station in Pueblo runs 35–55 minutes without traffic on the 50. That's real, unavoidable cost baked into every job, and it's why almost every legitimate hauler here has a minimum charge in the $125–$175 range. Nobody drives out to Pueblo West, loads a single nightstand, and dumps it for $40. The nightstand isn't the cost. The hour and the fuel are.

Two other Pueblo West specifics that out-of-town price guides never mention:

  • There is no municipal bulky-item pickup out here. The City of Pueblo periodically runs cleanup events for city residents; Pueblo West Metro District is a special district providing water, sewer, parks, and fire — not curbside trash. Your trash is a private contract, and most residential contracts cap you at one or two bulky items per year, if any.
  • Covenant and weed enforcement is real. Pueblo West filings carry covenants, and Pueblo County nuisance rules apply to accumulated debris and inoperable vehicles. People often call us with a 10-to-30-day compliance letter in hand, which limits their ability to shop slowly on price.

When Hiring Us Is a Bad Deal — Read This Before You Call

This section costs us jobs. It should. Full-service junk removal is the wrong purchase in more situations than our industry likes to admit.

Don't hire us if you have one or two items and a truck-owning friend. A single mattress to the transfer station costs you around $25–$35 in disposal and 90 minutes of your Saturday. We'd charge $145 for the same outcome. You are paying $110 for the 90 minutes and the lifting. That is a completely reasonable trade for some people and a bad one for others. Do the arithmetic honestly.

Don't hire us for a multi-day remodel. If you're gutting a bathroom over three weekends, a 10-yard roll-off dumpster runs about $350–$500 for a 7-day rental in this market with a tonnage allowance included, and you load it on your own schedule. We charge by the trip. Three trips beats one dumpster only if the debris comes out in one push. Where we win is single-visit demolition and haul — see [construction debris removal pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) — not slow drip.

Don't hire us to haul away things that still have value. A working washer, a decent dining set, a running mower: Habitat for Humanity ReStore and several Pueblo-area charities will often pick up donatable furniture at no charge, and Facebook Marketplace "free, curb, first come" clears usable items from a Pueblo West driveway in under 48 hours more often than not. Paying $400 to remove $600 of sellable goods is a loss twice over.

Don't hire us if the minimum charge dominates your bill. If your job is genuinely 2 cubic yards, you're paying largely for the drive. Wait, accumulate, and call once. A customer who batches a spring cleanout with a shed teardown and an old swing set pays roughly 30–40% less per cubic yard than three separate visits.

We can't take everything, and we'll tell you on the phone. Asbestos-containing material, wet paint in volume, solvents, pesticides, medical sharps, ammunition, propane tanks, and drums of unknown liquid are all outside what we're licensed to move. Hoarding-condition properties with biological contamination need a remediation contractor with the right certifications first; we handle the volume portion afterward, which you can read about on [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo).

Our estimates have a failure mode. Phone and photo quotes are estimates, not fixed bids, and they miss things: a "half garage" that turns out to have a concrete workbench footing under the shelving, a shed with a poured slab, buried irrigation under the rock you want removed. Our on-site number is binding before we load; the phone number is not. Anyone who gives you a firm price sight-unseen on a complex job is either padding it heavily or planning to revise it in your driveway.

Peak season pricing is real. March through June, everyone in Pueblo County cleans out at once. Lead times stretch from 1–2 days to 5–9 days, and there's less room to discount. January and February are the cheapest months to book — expect roughly 10–15% more flexibility on scheduling and price.

How to Get a Quote That Holds

  • Photograph the pile from three angles, including one shot with a person or trash can in frame for scale. Volume estimates from photos without a reference object are off by 30% routinely.
  • Say the word "heavy" out loud if there's concrete, dirt, rock, roofing, plaster, or brick. This changes the pricing model entirely, not just the number.
  • Describe the path, not just the pile: stairs, gate width, distance from where a 12-foot box truck can legally park.
  • Ask what's included. Fuel, labor, dump fees, and sweep-up should all be inside the quoted figure.
  • Get two quotes on anything over $600. We'd rather lose a job to a fair competitor than win one you resent.
