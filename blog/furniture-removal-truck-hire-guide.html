FURNITURE REMOVAL TRUCK HIRE GUIDE

Note before the article: "furniture removal truck hire perth" is a search from Perth, Australia. A Pueblo West page is unlikely to rank for that query or turn its traffic into customers. Below, I wrote for the intent behind the search (rent a truck and do it yourself, or hire a crew) and set it entirely in Pueblo West. If you're deciding whether to rent a truck and haul old furniture yourself or hire a removal crew in Pueblo West, here's the short answer. For one to three pieces and two able-bodied people, renting usually wins. A 10-foot rental truck in Pueblo starts around $19.95 plus about $1.29 per mile, and the landfill charges a load fee on top. For a full room or more, stairs, or anything over about 150 lbs, hiring a crew usually costs about the same once you add mileage, fuel, dump fees and a lost Saturday. It also leaves no one with a strained back. The rest of this page shows how we work that out, with numbers you can check against your own job.

What "truck hire" really costs for a Pueblo West furniture haul

The advertised rental rate is the smallest part of the bill. Here is a realistic do-it-yourself trip for a couch, a recliner and a dresser, starting from a home off Purcell Blvd:

  • Truck base rate: about $19.95 to $39.95, depending on whether you take a 10-, 15- or 20-foot box truck
  • Mileage: Pueblo West to a Pueblo-area landfill and back runs roughly 35 to 45 miles once you add the trip to pick up the truck. At around $1.29 a mile, that's $45 to $58.
  • Fuel: you return the tank at the level you got it. Box trucks get about 10 to 12 mpg, so plan on $12 to $18.
  • Damage coverage: usually $15 to $30 extra per rental
  • Landfill gate fee: a pickup-sized load of bulky household items usually costs $40 to $70. It varies by facility and by whether they charge by weight or volume, so call ahead the same week you plan to go.
  • Furniture dolly and moving pads: $10 to $20 if you rent them

Realistic total: about $140 to $215, plus 3 to 5 hours of your time.

For comparison, Front Range junk removal crews usually charge $75 to $175 for a single item and $250 to $400 for a quarter to a third of a truckload, which is about what three large pieces fill. That price includes labor, loading, driving and disposal. So for a three-piece job, the gap is often under $150. That's what you'd be paying to do the lifting yourself.

Why Pueblo West changes the math

Most "rent a truck vs. hire a crew" advice is written for cities with apartment buildings and a transfer station 10 minutes away. Pueblo West is different in several ways that matter:

  • There's no municipal bulk-item pickup. Pueblo West homes use private trash haulers. Some offer bulk pickup for a fee, but most won't take a sectional or a sleeper sofa on a regular route. "Just put it at the curb" usually doesn't work here.
  • The distances are longer. Big lots and spread-out streets mean longer drives to everything. The truck rental counter, the landfill and the donation drop-off are all usually in Pueblo proper, so you pay mileage every way.
  • The wind is real. Spring gusts here regularly top 40 mph. A recliner strapped into a pickup bed with one ratchet strap is how furniture ends up on Highway 50. Colorado's unsecured-load statute (C.R.S. 42-4-1407) lets an officer ticket you for it. That doesn't apply to an enclosed rental box, which counts in renting's favor.
  • Dirt and gravel driveways. Several Pueblo West neighborhoods have unpaved roads and driveways. Dollies don't roll well on gravel. Plan to carry anything heavy the whole way, and that changes how many people you need.

> The rental truck is the cheapest part of hauling furniture yourself. The expensive parts are the miles, the dump fee and the second person you have to talk into helping.

When you should NOT hire us (read this before you call)

We'd rather tell you this now than show up for a job that didn't need us. In these situations, removal service is the wrong buy:

  • The furniture is in good shape and someone wants it. A clean dresser or dining set often sells in 2 to 7 days on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo, and the buyer brings the truck. Paying someone to haul away something sellable costs you money twice.
  • You're moving the furniture, not getting rid of it. We remove and dispose of things. If the couch is going to your new house in Canon City, rent the truck or hire movers. We aren't a moving company, and we won't pretend to be one.
  • It's one light piece and you own a pickup. A nightstand, a folding table or a particleboard bookcase under 50 lbs doesn't justify a service call. Break it down, load it and drop it off yourself.
  • You want donation receipts for every item. We donate usable items when we can, but we can't promise that a specific piece will be accepted or that you'll get an itemized receipt. If the tax deduction matters to you, take the items to a charity like the Pueblo Habitat for Humanity ReStore yourself and get the receipt in hand.
  • The furniture has bed bugs and isn't wrapped. We can take infested upholstered pieces only if they're fully sealed in plastic first. If you can't do that safely, you need a pest treatment before you need a haul.
  • You're on a strict budget and you have time. If $150 matters more than your Saturday, renting is the honest answer. We'd rather you come back for the next big job than feel overcharged on this one.

### Where removal services (including ours) fall short

Here are the failure modes we see most often, from us and from competitors:

  • Volume pricing surprises. Most crews price by how much of the truck you fill, and estimates over the phone can be off. A "quarter load" turns into a third once a sleeper sofa is on the truck. Ask for a price range in writing before loading starts, and ask what moves you into the next tier.
  • Scheduling windows. On busy weeks, especially the first and last 5 days of each month when leases turn over, you may get a 2- to 4-hour arrival window, not a set time.
  • We won't take some things. Paint, solvents, propane tanks and anything soaked in chemicals are excluded. Refrigerated appliances have their own handling rules (see [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo)).
  • Weight limits on stairs. Our crews handle most items, but cast-iron clawfoot tubs, gun safes over about 500 lbs and pianos may need extra people or a specialist mover. We'll tell you up front if a job is outside what we can do safely.

When hiring a crew clearly wins

The numbers flip quickly once any of these apply:

1. More than about 6 large pieces. That usually means a second trip in a 10-foot truck or upgrading to a 20-foot, and either one pushes do-it-yourself costs past $250. 2. Stairs or tight turns. A sleeper sofa can weigh 200 to 350 lbs because of the metal frame inside. Two untrained people carrying it down a split-level staircase is how drywall, banisters and lower backs get damaged. 3. Estate or whole-house situations. Clearing out a parent's home means furniture plus everything else. Our [estate cleanout service](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) handles it in one visit, so you aren't making 5 dump runs over 3 weekends. 4. Mattresses mixed in. Many landfills charge a separate per-mattress surcharge, often $10 to $25 each. See [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for how we handle them. 5. You have a deadline. Closing dates, lease move-outs and listing photos don't wait for a free weekend. Same-week service is common. Rental trucks in Pueblo can be booked up on summer weekends.

A quick decision checklist

Answer these honestly:

  • Is it 3 or fewer pieces, all under 150 lbs, on the ground floor? Rent a truck or borrow a pickup.
  • Can it be sold or given away? List it first. Give it a week.
  • Is it more than half a room, upstairs, or on a deadline? Get a removal quote.
  • Is it mixed junk: furniture, boxes, yard waste, an old grill? Hire a crew. Separate dump runs for mixed loads are where do-it-yourself costs grow fastest. Our general [junk removal service](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) prices it all as one load.
