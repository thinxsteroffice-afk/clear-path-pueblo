AFFORDABLE JUNK YARD PUEBLO CO

Pueblo doesn't have a cheap public "junk yard" where you can back up a truck and dump a couch for five bucks. What people usually mean by that search is one of three things: the Pueblo City-County landfill (self-haul, roughly a $25–$35 minimum load fee and $40–$55 per ton after that), a scrap metal yard on the south or east side of Pueblo (they *pay you* for steel, copper, and aluminum, but take nothing else), or a hauling service that does the loading and the driving. For a Pueblo West household, the honest math is: if your junk is metal, take it to a scrap yard and get paid. If it's under a pickup load and you own a truck, self-haul to the landfill for about $40 all-in. If it's neither, call somebody.

Below is what each option actually costs from Pueblo West specifically, including the cases where hiring [junk removal in Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) is the wrong call.

What self-hauling to the landfill really costs from Pueblo West

The gate fee is the number people quote each other, and it's the smallest part of the bill. Run the whole thing:

  • Gate fee: minimum-load charges in the Pueblo area run about $25–$35 for a pickup-sized load; heavier loads get weighed at roughly $40–$55 per ton. A standard half-ton pickup bed of mixed household junk weighs 800–1,200 lbs, so most single loads land in that minimum-to-$60 window.
  • Drive time: from the center of Pueblo West to the disposal sites east and south of the city is a 35–50 mile round trip, or 70–100 minutes of windshield time — more if you hit the Highway 50 backup at 5 p.m.
  • Fuel: at 17 mpg and $3.10/gallon, that round trip burns about $8.
  • Loading: a garage's worth of junk takes 90 minutes to 3 hours to load by hand, and most people underestimate this by half.
  • Trips: the single biggest budget-breaker. A two-car garage or a full basement is almost never one pickup load. It's three to five. Five trips is $150 in gate fees, $40 in gas, and most of a Saturday and a Sunday.

One trip, self-hauled: call it $40 and three hours. That's genuinely cheap and we'll tell you to do it. Five trips: $190 and two weekend days, plus whatever you did to your back and your tailgate.

Scrap yards will pay you — for a narrow list of things

This is the part most junk-removal pages leave out, because it costs them money. Pueblo's scrap yards buy metal by the pound, and prices as of mid-2026 run roughly:

  • Shred/light steel: $0.04–$0.08 per pound (about $80–$160 per ton)
  • Aluminum cans: $0.45–$0.60 per pound
  • #1 bare bright copper: $3.50–$4.25 per pound
  • Insulated copper wire: $1.10–$1.90 per pound depending on gauge and jacket
  • Car batteries: $7–$14 each

Bring a photo ID; Colorado law requires yards to record seller identification on scrap transactions, and cash payouts above certain thresholds get held or issued by check.

Where this actually pays: a chain-link fence teardown, an old swing set frame, a pile of pipe or rebar, a stripped-out furnace, a cast-iron tub. A 40-foot run of chain link with posts can be 250–400 lbs of steel — $12–$30, which covers your gas and a burrito. Where it doesn't pay: a 50-gallon water heater is around 50 lbs of usable steel once it's drained, or roughly $3. You will spend more in fuel getting it there than you'll be handed at the scale.

> A scrap yard pays for the metal, not for your time. If the load is worth $18 and takes four hours to strip, load, and deliver, you've paid yourself $4.50 an hour to do it.

The Pueblo West wrinkle: covenants, dirt lots, and long driveways

Pueblo West is not Pueblo, and the disposal math is different here in ways that catch new residents:

  • Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenants restrict visible outdoor storage of junk, appliances, and inoperable vehicles. A pile that would sit unnoticed behind a fence in the city can generate a covenant notice here — and the clock on those is typically 10–30 days to cure, which is what turns "someday" projects into same-week calls.
  • Acre-plus lots with unpaved drives. Half of Pueblo West sits on 1 to 5 acre parcels. A 30-yard roll-off dumpster on a soft dirt drive after a spring storm leaves ruts you'll pay a $200–$600 grading bill to fix. Ask any dumpster company about their damage waiver language before they drop.
  • Distance from the transfer station adds 15–25 minutes each way compared to hauling from Bessemer or the East Side. That's the real reason self-haul is less attractive out here.
  • Wind. Sustained 25–40 mph gusts are normal March through May. An untarped load is a ticket and a mess; a proper tarp and four ratchet straps is $45 at any hardware store on Purcell.

What we charge, and how to check whether it's fair

Volume-based pricing in this market, for a 16-foot box truck holding roughly 18 cubic yards:

  • Minimum load (single item, e.g. a couch or a fridge): $95–$145
  • Quarter truck: $175–$250
  • Half truck: $300–$400
  • Full truck: $550–$700

Add-ons that are standard across every honest operator: $25–$45 for appliances requiring EPA-certified refrigerant recovery, $15–$30 per mattress or box spring for disposal surcharges, $5–$12 per tire, and heavy-materials pricing by weight rather than volume for concrete, brick, and soil — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) for how that's priced, because volume pricing on a load of flagstone would bankrupt any hauler who quoted it.

Compare against a 15-yard roll-off dumpster, which runs about $325–$450 for a 7-day rental in the Pueblo area with a 2–3 ton included weight allowance and $60–$85 per ton overage. The dumpster is cheaper per cubic yard. You supply the labor and you eat the driveway risk.

When you should not hire us

Plainly, because it matters more than anything else on this page:

  • You have one small item and a pickup. A single mattress or a broken office chair is a $40 self-haul. Paying $95 for us to do a 12-minute job is paying for convenience, and you should know that's what you're buying.
  • Your load is mostly clean metal. Strip it, take it to a scrap yard, keep the check. We'll haul metal, but we're not going to pretend we can beat "they pay you" with "you pay us."
  • You're renovating over six weeks. Intermittent debris over a long timeline is a dumpster job, not a hauling job. Three of our truckloads at $550 each is $1,650 against roughly $400 for a dumpster you fill at your own pace. If your project is a bathroom gut or a re-roof, price [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) against a roll-off before you book us.
  • You're price-shopping to the dollar. We are not the cheapest number you'll get. The cheapest quote in this market is usually an unlicensed truck that dumps in an arroyo off Siloam or McCulloch, and the illegal-dumping citation attaches to the waste generator — you — not the guy who took your cash.
  • You want us to take hazardous material. We cannot legally take paint, solvents, motor oil, pesticides, propane tanks, car batteries, asbestos-containing tile or siding, or medical sharps. Those go to a household hazardous waste event, and turning us away at your door still costs you a trip charge if we're already dispatched.

Other real limitations, said out loud: we can't get a box truck up a steep, soft, unpaved drive after heavy rain without risk. Loads over roughly 2,000 lbs of concentrated weight — dirt, concrete, tile, roofing shingles — are priced by the ton and get expensive fast. Anything requiring a licensed demolition permit, structural cutting, or utility disconnection is outside a hauling crew's scope, and any company that says otherwise on a shed or [deck and fence teardown](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) is guessing with your property. And on a hoarding-condition property, a two-hour estimate is fiction; those are multi-day jobs and the number will reflect that.

The decision, compressed

Ask three questions in order. Is it metal? Scrap yard. Does it fit in one pickup load and do you have a free morning? Landfill, about $40. Is it more than that, or heavy, or awkward, or on a deadline? Then hire someone — us or anyone else licensed and insured — and expect $175–$700 depending on volume.

The number that should decide it isn't the gate fee. It's how many trips the pile actually is, multiplied by three hours each. Most people are honest with themselves about that only after trip number two.
