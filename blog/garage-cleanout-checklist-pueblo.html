GARAGE CLEANOUT: THE CHECKLIST THAT ACTUALLY WORKS

Most garages get cleaned out approximately never. The reason isn't laziness — it's that the project always feels enormous when you walk in and stare at it. Here's the system Pueblo families use to actually finish (or skip ahead to: just call ClearPath and we do it for you).

Step 1: Sort by Decision, Not by Item

Don't start by moving stuff. Start by deciding the four buckets:

KEEP — used in the last 12 months OR sentimental.
DONATE — works, but you don't need it. Pueblo has Salvation Army, Habitat ReStore, and ARC for donation.
SELL — high-value items (tools, equipment) you can post on Facebook Marketplace.
JUNK — broken, useless, or hazardous.

Mark four corners of the garage with these labels (literally — use painter's tape). Now you're sorting, not deciding.

Step 2: Hazardous Items Need a Separate Plan

Paint, motor oil, batteries, propane tanks, pesticides, fluorescent bulbs — these can't go in regular trash and most haulers won't take them.

Pueblo Hazardous Waste: The Pueblo County HHW facility accepts these by appointment. Worth a separate trip.

ClearPath option: we have 12+ years of hazmat handling experience. We can take most household quantities of hazardous items as part of a cleanout job.

Step 3: Tackle the Easy Wins First

Start with the empty boxes, the obviously-broken stuff, and the items you immediately recognize as 'why did I keep this?' This gives you visible momentum in 30 minutes — most people quit when the first hour shows no progress.

Step 4: The 'One Year' Rule for Anything Borderline

If you haven't used it in a year and it's not seasonal, it goes. Be honest. You're not going to refinish that chair. You're not going to use the bread maker. The neighbor's not coming back for the ladder.

Step 5: Plan the Haul-Off

Once you have your JUNK pile, the question becomes: how does it leave the garage?

Option A: Multiple trips to the dump in your truck/SUV. Pueblo's transfer station charges by weight, so most full garages run $80–$200 in dump fees alone — plus your time, gas, and the back pain.

Option B: Rent a dumpster. $300–$500 for a week. Plus you load it. Plus they charge if you go over weight.

Option C: ClearPath. Average Pueblo garage cleanout is $400–$800 all-in. We bring the labor and the trailer, you point, we lift, we sweep. You're done in a day.

Step 6: Don't Forget the 'Leaves of Doom' Corner

Every garage has it: the dust-covered pile of bags, oil-stained cardboard, and 'I'll get to it later' debris by the side door. That's typically 30% of your job. Plan for it.
