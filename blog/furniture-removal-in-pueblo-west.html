What Furniture Removal Actually Costs in Pueblo West

Volume-based pricing means you're paying for the space your stuff occupies in a 15-cubic-yard truck rated to about 4,000 pounds, not per item. Rough guide:

  • Single item (sofa, recliner, dresser, desk): $89–$149
  • Quarter truck (about 3–4 pieces): $189
  • Half truck (a full living room set — two sofas plus a recliner and a coffee table): $329
  • Full truck (bedroom set, living room set, and odds and ends): $549–$649
  • Mattress or box spring, added to another job: $45–$75 each

Those numbers include labor, loading, driving, and disposal fees. What moves the price up: stairs (roughly $25 per flight on heavy pieces), disassembly of anything bolted together, and items we have to take through a narrow interior stairwell rather than a garage.

A detail worth knowing: a queen sleeper sofa weighs 180 to 250 pounds because of the steel frame inside. That's why a sleeper often prices closer to a two-item job even though it's one piece — it's a genuine two-person carry, and it eats the same truck space as a sectional section.

Three more local realities:

  • Lot size and driveways. A lot of Pueblo West parcels run around an acre, with long gravel or dirt driveways. That's great for us — we can back a truck to the garage door instead of parking on the street. But if your driveway is soft after a storm, tell us; a loaded truck will rut it.
  • Wind. Spring gusts here regularly hit 40 to 60 mph. Furniture staged at the end of a driveway overnight does not stay at the end of the driveway. Cushions end up three properties east.
  • Covenants. Pueblo West Metro District covenant enforcement does respond to visible accumulation complaints, and notices typically give you a short correction window — often around 72 hours — before follow-up. If you've already got a notice, say so when you call. We'll prioritize you.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

Here's where we cost ourselves work. Several situations make hiring any furniture removal company the wrong move:

A minimum dump fee is commonly in the $30–$45 range for a light pickup load, plus maybe $8 in fuel. If you can lift a sofa with a buddy, you've just done a $149 job for about $50 and 90 minutes. Do that. Call us when the load is bigger than your truck bed or heavier than your back.
  • The item is worth more than about $200 on resale. A solid-wood dining table, a real leather sectional in good shape, a mid-century dresser — list it. Pueblo-area Facebook Marketplace free and cheap listings move fast, and "curb alley, free, come get it" will often clear a decent sofa in 24 to 48 hours in warm months. Paying us $129 to haul off something someone would pay you $250 for is a $379 mistake.

  • You're clearing an entire house and have a week plus labor. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster in the Pueblo area runs roughly $425 to $550 for a week. What We Turn Down or Charge Extra For

Plain list, no surprises on the invoice:

  • Suspected bed bugs. Tell us upfront. We can often still take it, but it requires wrapping and a separate disposal path, and it carries a surcharge. If you don't tell us and we find evidence mid-load, we stop. That protects the next four customers that day.
  • Anything over about 400 pounds as a single unit. Upright pianos, gun safes, commercial machinery — we're a furniture crew, not a rigging outfit. Some of these we'll take with advance notice and the right gear; some we'll refer out.
  • Hazardous materials. Paint, solvents, fuel, propane tanks, and automotive fluids go to Pueblo County household hazardous waste collection, not into our truck.
  • Third-floor apartment with no elevator. We'll do it, but stair fees stack per flight and you should hear that number before we start, not after.
  • Wet, moldy, or rodent-infested upholstery. We handle it, but it's priced higher because it's a wrapped, sealed, straight-to-landfill item with real cleanup on our end.

The Donation Question, Answered Honestly

Most furniture removal companies advertise that they donate. We do route roughly 30% of what we collect to donation or recycling, and we'd rather do that than pay tipping fees. But you should know what actually gets accepted before you pin your hopes on it.

Pueblo-area thrift and ReStore operations routinely reject upholstered furniture with stains, pet damage, tears, or smoke odor. Mattresses are the hardest category — many donation centers won't accept a used mattress at all regardless of condition, and those that do want the original law tag intact. Cribs and older recliners with worn mechanisms get declined for liability reasons. Particleboard furniture that's been apart and back together once has essentially no donation value.

So: if your sofa is genuinely clean and structurally sound, tell us, and we'll try the donation route first. If it's been in a garage through two Pueblo West summers, it's going to the landfill, and any company that promises otherwise is telling you what you want to hear.
