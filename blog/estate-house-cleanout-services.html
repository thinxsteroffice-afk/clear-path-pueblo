ESTATE HOUSE CLEANOUT SERVICES

If you're cleaning out an estate house in Pueblo West, most families have four options: do it themselves, rent a dumpster, hire an estate sale company, or hire a full-service cleanout crew. A crew sorts, hauls, and disposes of everything in one visit. Around here, a full cleanout of a typical 3-bedroom home costs $1,500 to $4,000. The price depends mostly on volume, which usually runs 3 to 6 truckloads. Most homes take one to two days. If the house has less than about half a truckload of unwanted items, or valuables worth selling first, you probably don't need a full cleanout yet. This guide explains how to tell which situation you're in.

What an estate cleanout service actually covers

"Estate cleanout" means different things depending on who you ask. A full-service job usually includes:

  • Walkthrough and quote. The crew estimates volume in person or from photos. The price should be set before anyone lifts anything.
  • Sorting against your keep list. You mark what stays (tape, sticky notes, or a closed-off room), and the crew removes everything else.
  • Hauling and loading. This covers heavy items like upright pianos, chest freezers, gun safes, and sleeper sofas, plus stairs, basements, and crawl spaces.
  • Donation and recycling separation. Usable furniture, metal, and working appliances are pulled out of the landfill pile when their condition allows.
  • Outbuildings and yard. Many Pueblo West properties sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots, so the house is often only part of the job. Sheds, detached garages, horse property, and old RV pads can hold as much as the house does.
  • Broom-swept finish. Floors are swept so a realtor, appraiser, or buyer can walk through.

A cleanout does *not* usually include deep cleaning, carpet shampooing, repairs, or biohazard remediation. If a quote doesn't say what's excluded, ask.

What it costs in Pueblo West and why

Junk removal is priced by volume, meaning how much of the truck your items fill. Weight and labor adjust the price from there. Typical ranges in this market:

  • Quarter truckload (a bedroom set and some boxes): roughly $150–$275
  • Half truckload (one full room or a one-car garage): roughly $300–$450
  • Full truckload (about 15–17 cubic yards): roughly $500–$800
  • Whole-house estate cleanout (typical 1,500–2,200 sq ft home): $1,500–$4,000
  • Heavily packed or long-occupied homes (40+ years of belongings, full basement and outbuildings): $4,000–$7,000+

Three things push Pueblo West jobs toward the high end:

1. Outbuildings. A packed 12x16 shed can add a full truckload by itself. 2. Heavy materials. Concrete, old tile, cast-iron tubs, and yard rock are charged by weight because disposal fees are charged by weight. 3. Specialty disposal. Refrigerators and freezers need refrigerant recovery. Tires and mattresses often carry per-item disposal fees of $10–$40 each.

> The best way to lower an estate cleanout bill isn't to haggle. It's to have the family pull every keepsake and every sellable item *before* the crew arrives, so you only pay to haul things nobody wants.

The hidden cost of waiting

Most families underestimate what an empty-but-full house costs every month. A realistic monthly carrying cost for an inherited Pueblo West home looks like this:

  • Mortgage (if one remains): varies widely
  • Homeowners insurance: $120–$250/month
  • Utilities kept on for showings and freeze protection: $100–$200/month
  • Property taxes and any district fees: $150–$300/month spread over the year

That's often $400–$750 a month before any mortgage payment, and it keeps adding up while the house sits full.

Insurance is the bigger risk. Many standard homeowners policies limit or exclude coverage for certain losses, such as vandalism and water damage from frozen pipes, once a home has been vacant for 30 to 60 consecutive days. Pueblo West winters regularly drop below freezing. A burst pipe in an unoccupied house can easily cause $10,000+ in damage. Call the insurance carrier the week you take over the property, and ask whether a vacancy endorsement is needed.

Colorado probate matters for timing too. After a personal representative publishes notice to creditors, creditors generally have four months to file claims. Many families use that window to sort and clear the house so it's ready to list when the estate can sell.

DIY vs. dumpster vs. estate sale vs. cleanout crew

Here's how the options compare honestly:

Doing it yourself with family and a pickup. The cash cost is lowest, but the time cost is high. Clearing a full 3-bedroom house usually takes a family 3 to 5 weekends, plus landfill trips, and each trip can take an hour or more round trip from parts of Pueblo West. Dump fees still apply. This option makes sense when family lives nearby, is healthy enough to lift, and isn't grieving too hard to make decisions.

Renting a roll-off dumpster. A 20-yard dumpster usually rents for $400–$700 for about a week, with overage fees if you go past the weight limit. It's cheaper than a crew if you supply the labor. The downsides: you do all the carrying, many items (appliances, tires, paint, electronics) aren't allowed in, and the dumpster sits in the driveway where neighbors may add to it.

Estate sale company. These companies typically keep 30% to 40% of gross sales. Many won't take a job unless they expect about $10,000 or more in sellable goods. A good one can make you money. What they rarely do is empty the house afterward. Most leave the unsold 20–50% behind, and that's where a cleanout crew comes in.

Full-service cleanout crew. This option costs the most per load, but it's fastest: typically 1 to 2 days from start to broom-swept. It suits out-of-state heirs, tight listing deadlines, and families who can't face the job themselves.

Many households do best with a combination: family pulls keepsakes, an estate sale runs if the contents justify it, and a crew clears what's left the following week.

When a cleanout service is NOT worth it

This section may cost us some jobs, but these are the situations where you shouldn't hire us, or at least shouldn't hire us yet.

You haven't finished sorting. We can't un-throw something away. Old houses hide cash in coffee cans, savings bonds in books, titles in freezer bags, and jewelry in sock drawers. If the family hasn't gone through every drawer, closet, and pocket, wait. A rushed cleanout that hauls off a missing will or a vehicle title can cost far more than the hauling saves.

You don't have legal authority yet. If no personal representative has been appointed and the heirs disagree, clearing the house can create legal and family problems. Get authority first, and get written agreement from the heirs on what goes.

You're selling as-is to a cash buyer. Many investors will buy a house with the contents inside and factor cleanup into their offer. If you've already accepted an as-is offer, paying $3,000 to empty the house may just be a gift to the buyer. Ask them before you book anything.

The volume is small. If the house is mostly empty except for a couch, a mattress, and a fridge, you don't need an "estate cleanout." Book single-item pickups instead. It'll often cost under $300.

The contents are valuable. If there are antiques, firearms, coins, tools, or collectibles, get an appraisal or an estate sale consult first. A junk removal crew is not an appraiser, and you shouldn't count on one to spot a valuable piece.

The house needs biohazard remediation. Unattended death, heavy rodent contamination, sewage, or mold growth calls for a certified remediation company, not a junk hauler. We can remove belongings *after* remediation, but we're not the right first call. Serious hoarding situations may also need a slower, family-involved approach. See our [hoarding cleanout Pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) page for how those jobs differ.

Hazardous materials. Most junk removal crews, including ours, can't haul old gasoline, pesticides, pool chemicals, asbestos-containing materials, or large amounts of liquid paint. You'll still need to take those to a household hazardous waste drop-off yourself.

How to prepare the house before the crew arrives

A few hours of prep can save hundreds of dollars and prevent the most common mistakes:

  • Check every hiding spot. Look inside mattresses, freezer bags, book pages, heating vents, and the backs of drawers.
  • Pull paperwork first. This includes tax returns (keep 7 years), deeds, titles, insurance policies, bank statements, and anything with a Social Security number. Shred what you don't need; don't haul it.
  • Mark the keep items clearly. Put everything staying in one locked room, or tag it with bright tape. Take photos so everyone agrees.
  • Photograph everything. Take a walkthrough video before and after for the estate record.
  • Keep utilities on through the cleanout. Crews need lights, and in winter the pipes need heat.
  • Tell the neighbors. Pueblo West streets are quiet, and a truck making several trips draws attention.

Getting a quote

Get quotes from at least two companies, and get them in writing. A trustworthy quote spells out what's included, what's extra (appliances, tires, heavy materials), and whether the price is fixed or can change based on the final volume. Ask how items are separated for donation and recycling, and ask for donation receipts if the estate wants them for tax purposes.

If the estate also has a rented storage space, bundling it into the same visit often costs less than two separate trips.
