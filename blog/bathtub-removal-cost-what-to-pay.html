BATHTUB REMOVAL COST: WHAT TO PAY

Removing a bathtub in Pueblo West typically runs $125 to $250 if the tub is already disconnected and sitting in the driveway or garage, and $350 to $700 for a full job where a crew disconnects the drain, cuts the tub free, hauls it out, and takes it to the landfill. Add $150 to $400 more if a tile or fiberglass surround has to come down with it. Cast iron costs more than acrylic — expect the top of those ranges when the tub weighs 300+ pounds. A second-floor bathroom, a narrow hallway, or a wall that has to be opened to get the tub out pushes a job past $700. Below is what actually drives the number, and when hiring anyone at all is the wrong call.

What You're Actually Paying For

Bathtub removal pricing looks confusing because two very different services share the same name.

Haul-away only means the tub is already out. Somebody — you, your plumber, your remodeler — disconnected the drain and overflow, cut it loose, and dragged it out of the bathroom. A two-person crew loads it and drives it off. That's a 15-minute stop, and in Pueblo West it prices between $125 and $250 depending on tub material and whether it's the only item on the truck.

Full removal means the crew does the disconnect, the demolition, the extraction, and the haul. That's $350 to $700 for a standard 60" x 30" alcove tub, typically 2 to 4 hours of on-site work for two people. The spread inside that range comes down to five things:

  • Material. A cast iron tub runs 250 to 400 pounds. Stamped steel runs 60 to 80. Acrylic or fiberglass runs 45 to 90. Cast iron is the single biggest price driver because it changes the crew size and the method.
  • Access path. A 60-inch tub does not turn a 90-degree corner into a 36-inch hallway while staying in one piece. Bathroom doors are frequently 28 to 30 inches wide.
  • Floor level. A slab-on-grade ranch is straightforward. Getting 350 pounds of iron down a staircase adds real time and real risk.
  • Surround. A tub with a one-piece fiberglass shell often comes out clean. A tub with mortar-bedded ceramic tile above it means the tile, backer board, and sometimes a stud bay of drywall get demoed too.
  • Disposal weight. Landfill tipping in the Pueblo area generally runs $40 to $60 per ton with a minimum charge, and it's a 12 to 18 mile round trip from most Pueblo West addresses.

Why Pueblo West Tubs Are Harder Than Average

Most of Pueblo West was built between the mid-1970s and the late 1990s, and a large share of those bathrooms were framed with the tub set first, then drywall or tile installed down onto the tub flange. That construction sequence matters more than anything else in the price. You cannot slide a flanged tub out of that alcove — the flange is buried behind the wall board. Somebody has to cut a 6 to 8 inch band of wall material off the studs above the tub before it moves at all.

That's why an online national average of "$150 to remove a bathtub" doesn't survive contact with an actual Pueblo West bathroom. That figure describes haul-away, not removal.

The other local wrinkle: Pueblo West is a metropolitan district, not an incorporated city. The Metro District handles water, sewer, parks, and fire — not trash. Residential garbage is a private subscription, and no subscription hauler in this market takes a bathtub at the curb. There's no municipal bulk-item day to fall back on. That's a difference from Pueblo proper and it's the reason bathtubs sit in Pueblo West garages for months.

> A cast iron tub can be broken into truck-sized pieces in about 20 to 40 minutes with an 8-pound sledge. The hard part was never the iron — it was the plumbing under it and the wall around it.

Cast Iron: The Honest Math on Scrap Value

People ask whether the scrap value offsets the removal cost. It doesn't, and the numbers are worth seeing.

Cast iron scrap in southern Colorado has generally paid somewhere around $0.04 to $0.09 per pound at the yard. A 350-pound tub is therefore worth roughly $14 to $32 in scrap — before you account for the enameled coating, which some yards discount or refuse outright because it's a contaminant.

Against that: renting a truck or trailer, an hour of driving, yard wait time, and the fact that you had to break the tub up first. Nobody is paying you for a bathtub. Anyone who tells you they'll take it free "for the scrap" is either bundling it with a larger metal load or planning to leave the rest of your debris behind. Ask which.

When Bathtub Removal Is Not Worth Paying For

This is the part most contractors leave off their pages. There are several situations where hiring a removal crew is a bad use of your money, and you should know them before you call anyone — including us.

  • You already have a demo contractor on the job. If a remodeler is gutting the bathroom anyway, the tub is already in their scope and their dumpster. Paying a separate haul-away crew $300+ for a tub your GC is already handling is a duplicate charge. Ask your contractor to itemize demo and disposal before you shop it out.
  • You're doing a multi-room gut. If you're pulling a tub, a vanity, a toilet, flooring, and cabinets over a two-week window, a 10-yard dumpster at roughly $350 to $500 per week is usually cheaper per cubic yard than three separate truck visits. Junk removal wins on single loads and on jobs where you don't want a container sitting in the driveway; a rented container wins on volume and on long timelines. Run both numbers.
  • The tub is fiberglass, on a slab, and you own a reciprocating saw. A 60-pound acrylic tub cuts into three carryable pieces in about 20 minutes with a demo blade. Two reasonably able adults with a pickup can finish that job in an afternoon for the cost of a landfill trip. Paying $400 for it is paying for convenience, which is legitimate — but call it what it is.
  • You have an active plumbing problem. Removal crews are not plumbers. If the tub is coming out because of a leak, a failed drain, or a suspected supply line issue behind the wall, get a licensed plumber in first at $95 to $150 per hour. A demo crew that cuts a drain line without knowing what's behind it hands you a bigger bill.
  • The home was built before 1980 and has a mortar-bed tile surround. Joint compound, mastic, and some sheet flooring from that era can contain asbestos. Colorado regulates disturbing asbestos-containing material, and a legitimate crew will stop and tell you to test rather than swing a hammer. A sample test typically runs $50 to $125; abatement of a small bathroom can run $600 to $1,500. If a company quotes you demo on a pre-1980 tile bathroom without ever mentioning testing, that's a signal about how they operate.

Other Real Limitations to Ask About

A few more things that will not appear on most quote pages:

  • Most removal crews do not cap or reroute the plumbing. They'll disconnect a drain. They will not leave you with a code-compliant capped stub ready for inspection. If you need that, it's a plumber's line item.
  • Removal is not restoration. When the tub leaves, you have exposed studs, a hole where the drain was, and probably damaged flooring at the tub edge. Nobody is patching that in a $450 removal.
  • A same-day quote over the phone on a cast iron tub upstairs is a guess. Ask for a firm price after someone sees the access path, or expect a change order.
  • Weather matters here. A driveway job in a January cold snap or during a wind event takes longer, and some crews won't run a truck on an unpaved lot after heavy rain.

Cutting the Cost Yourself

If the price feels high, there are legitimate ways to bring it down:

  • Disconnect and extract the tub yourself, then pay haul-away only. That's the difference between roughly $450 and roughly $175 — the labor is most of the bill.
  • Bundle it. Tub plus an old water heater plus a vanity plus a mattress on one truck visit costs meaningfully less than four separate trips. If you're already scheduling [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) or clearing out remodel waste, put the tub on the same load.
  • Break cast iron in place. With a drop cloth, safety glasses, a face shield, gloves, and long sleeves, an 8-pound sledge reduces a cast iron tub to manageable chunks in 20 to 40 minutes. Enamel shards are genuinely sharp and travel — this is not a job to do in shorts. Cover the drain opening so debris doesn't go down it.
  • Ask what the price includes. Get disposal fees, labor, and any surround demo stated separately. A $395 all-in number and a $295 number that adds $150 in dump fees are not the same offer.

Getting a Straight Number

For a standard Pueblo West alcove tub on a slab with a fiberglass surround, plan on $350 to $550 all in, done in about 90 minutes. Cast iron upstairs with tile: plan on $600 to $900. Haul-away of an already-removed tub: $125 to $250.
