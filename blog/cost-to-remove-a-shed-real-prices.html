COST TO REMOVE A SHED: REAL PRICES

Removing a shed in Pueblo West costs $275 to $1,400 for most homeowners, with the full spread running from about $225 for a small metal kit shed to $4,500 for a large permitted outbuilding on a slab. Real numbers by size:

  • 8x8 or 8x10 resin/metal kit shed, empty: $225–$450
  • 10x12 wood shed, shingled roof: $450–$750
  • 12x16 to 12x20 wood shed with loft or plywood floor: $800–$1,400
  • 20x24 or larger outbuilding, framed on a slab: $2,200–$4,500
  • Concrete pad removal (separate line item): $2.50–$6.00 per square foot

Price moves on four things: square footage, what's inside it, whether the slab goes too, and how much equipment can reach the structure. Everything below explains how those four move your number — including the cases where you should not hire anyone.

What You're Actually Paying For

A shed teardown is three costs stacked: labor, disposal, and equipment.

Labor. A two-person crew flattens and loads a 10x12 wood shed in 3 to 5 hours. A 12x20 with a loft, anchor bolts, and 30-year architectural shingles is closer to 6–8 hours, sometimes two crew days. Wood sheds are slower than they look — the demo takes 45 minutes, and the remaining 4 hours are loading, nail sweeping, and raking.

Disposal. Mixed shed debris runs 1.5 to 4 tons for typical residential structures. Pueblo-area transfer and landfill tipping fees land in the $45–$60 per ton range, so disposal alone is often $90–$240 on a mid-size shed. Asphalt shingles are the weight driver: a 10x12 roof holds roughly 300–450 lbs of shingles, and a 12x20 can carry 700+ lbs.

Equipment. If a truck and dump trailer can get within 40 feet of the shed, you pay the base rate. If the shed sits behind a fence line, a retaining wall, or a septic field and everything has to be carried by hand 150 feet, expect a 20–35% surcharge. On the flip side, an accessible one-acre Pueblo West lot with a straight driveway shot is the cheapest scenario in the market — most lots out here have room to back a trailer right up to the structure, which is exactly why shed removal prices in Pueblo West run lower than in older Bessemer or Downtown Pueblo neighborhoods with alley-only access.

The Pueblo West Factors Nobody Quotes You On

Three local realities change the price here more than they do in Denver or Colorado Springs.

Hardpan and caliche. Sheds out here are anchored to survive wind, which means rebar stakes driven 18–24 inches, auger anchors, or concrete piers. Pulling anchors out of caliche takes a jack, a chain, and sometimes a machine. Budget an extra $75–$200 if your shed is anchor-bolted or pier-set rather than just sitting on skids.

Wind. Pueblo West regularly sees 50–60 mph spring gusts, with peaks above 70. This matters twice: it's why half the sheds we remove are already wind-damaged, and it's why partially disassembling a shed yourself and leaving it overnight is a genuinely bad idea. Loose 4x8 sheathing panels travel a long way across an open acre.

What lives underneath. Sheds sitting on blocks with a 4–8 inch crawl gap are prime habitat for pack rats, black widows, and — in this county — deer mice. Nest material and droppings inside a sealed shed are a hantavirus exposure concern that CDPHE takes seriously in southern Colorado. Rodent-heavy sheds get wet-down and respirator protocol before anyone swings a bar, which typically adds $100–$250 and an hour. It's not an upsell; it's the difference between a Saturday and a hospital visit.

> A shed that's been closed since 2019 is not a storage building. It's a wildlife enclosure with a door, and the price should reflect that.

The Concrete Slab Question

This is where quotes diverge wildly, and where homeowners get surprised.

A 10x12 slab is 120 square feet. At $2.50–$6.00 per square foot, that's $300–$720 on top of the shed itself — and the range is that wide because 4-inch unreinforced concrete breaks with a jackhammer, while 6-inch wire-mesh concrete needs a machine. Broken concrete also weighs roughly 150 lbs per cubic foot, meaning a 4-inch 10x12 slab is about 3 tons on its own, doubling your disposal weight.

Two honest options:

  • Leave the slab. It makes a fine patio pad, greenhouse base, or future shed foundation. Plenty of Pueblo West customers keep it and save $400–$700.
  • Remove it properly. That's a [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) job or a [skid steer](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) job, not a hand-labor job, and it should be quoted as one.

A crew that quotes slab removal at hand-labor rates either hasn't seen your slab or is planning to renegotiate on site.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This section costs us work. Read it anyway.

Your shed is under 100 square feet, empty, and made of resin or thin-gauge metal. A Rubbermaid 7x7 or an Arrow steel kit comes apart with a socket set in 90 minutes and fits in two pickup loads. Landfill cost: about $40–$70. You will spend $250+ hiring it out for a job that is genuinely a Saturday morning. Do it yourself.

The shed is worth more than the removal. A structurally sound 10x12 wood shed with a good roof sells on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo West for $400–$900, and shed movers will relocate one for $400–$900. If your shed is solid and you're only removing it to change the yard layout, selling it turns a $650 expense into a $500 gain — a $1,150 swing. We tell people this on the phone regularly. If the roof deck is soft, the floor is rotted, or the door frame has racked, forget it; nobody's buying and you've just wasted three weeks.

You suspect asbestos. Sheds and outbuildings built before the early 1980s sometimes have transite (cement-asbestos) siding or roofing panels — gray, brittle, corrugated, ringing hard when tapped. No general junk removal company should touch those, including us. That's a licensed abatement contractor, and a small residential abatement in Colorado typically runs $1,500–$4,000 before the demo even starts. Anyone who tells you they'll "just be careful with it" is telling you they'll create a regulatory problem on your property.

The shed has live power, gas, or plumbing. A subpanel-fed shed needs a licensed electrician to disconnect and cap at the source first — usually $150–$400. We will not cut a live feed, and a crew that would is a crew you shouldn't have on your lot.

Insurance is paying for wind or hail damage. Get the adjuster's scope in writing before anything moves. Once the structure is in a trailer, your documentation is gone, and re-opening a claim on a demolished shed is close to impossible.

You only need one small thing gone. A single [mattress](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) or a couple of appliances stored in the shed is a $75–$150 job, not a shed job.

The Failure Modes — What Actually Goes Wrong

  • The slab is bigger than the shed. Old sheds get replaced with smaller ones. We've uncovered 12x16 slabs under 10x12 sheds more than once. This changes the concrete quote after the shed is down, not before.
  • The contents were undercounted. "Just some yard tools" turns into 14 boxes, 6 tires, and a chest freezer. Contents are priced by volume, and a packed 10x12 shed can add $200–$500. Send photos of the inside, not just the outside. If the interior is genuinely full, that's really a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo)-style job with a demo attached.
  • Rain and soft ground. After a heavy August monsoon cell, a loaded dump trailer will rut a Pueblo West yard badly. Reputable crews reschedule 24–72 hours. Push a crew to work anyway and the ruts are yours.
  • Spring scheduling. March through June is peak season here. Lead times stretch to 7–14 days, sometimes longer after a big wind event when 40 sheds in the district fail the same week.
  • The half-demo discount trap. Tearing the shed down yourself to save money typically cuts a quote by only 10–20%, because loading — not demo — is the labor. Meanwhile, you now own a pile of nail-studded lumber that a 55 mph gust will redistribute across your neighbor's lot.

Getting an Accurate Number the First Time

Photos beat descriptions. Four shots get a firm price on nearly any Pueblo West shed:

  • The full exterior from 20 feet back, showing driveway access
  • The interior with the door open, contents visible
  • The base — slab, blocks, skids, or bare dirt
  • A tape measure or a person for scale on one wall

With those, a quote should be firm, not "starting at." An estimate that swings more than 15% on arrival wasn't an estimate.

Most Pueblo West sheds land between $450 and $900, done in a single half-day visit, with the pad left in place. That's the honest middle of this market. If you're weighing the whole project — shed, old fence line, and the junk pile behind it — bundling into one visit usually saves 20–30% over three separate trips.
