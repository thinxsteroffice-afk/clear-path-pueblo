CONSTRUCTION RUBBISH REMOVAL COST

Pull a permit-fee schedule and a landfill tipping rate for Pueblo County and you'll find the two numbers that actually drive construction debris pricing. Here's the article.

---

Construction rubbish removal in Pueblo West runs $250 to $650 for a single-truck load (roughly 8–12 cubic yards, 2,000–4,000 lbs), $700 to $1,400 for a full 15-yard truckload of mixed remodel debris, and $1,800 to $3,500 for a whole-house gut or a full roof tear-off hauled in stages. Concrete, dirt, brick, and tile price separately by weight — expect $85 to $145 per ton on top of labor, because those materials blow past the weight allowance built into a volume price. A 20-yard dumpster rental in the 81007 zip runs $425 to $575 for 7 days with a 3–4 ton cap. Which of those three options is cheapest depends almost entirely on one thing: whether you have someone to load it.

The Real Cost Driver Nobody Quotes You On

Every junk removal price in Pueblo West is built from four inputs: volume, weight, labor time, and disposal fees. Volume is what gets advertised. Weight is what wrecks the estimate.

The Pueblo County landfill charges by the ton at the scale house, and rates for construction and demolition material sit meaningfully above household waste. A 12x12 room's worth of drywall demo — call it 40 sheets of half-inch — weighs about 1,600 lbs before you add studs, insulation, or the carpet pad. That's under a ton and fits neatly in a volume-based price. But a 300-square-foot patio slab at 4 inches thick weighs roughly 13,500 lbs. Same visual pile, eight times the disposal cost.

This is why an honest quote for construction rubbish sounds different from an honest quote for a garage cleanout. If a company gives you a firm number for "concrete removal" over the phone without asking thickness, square footage, or whether there's rebar in it, they've either padded the number by 40% or they're going to renegotiate on site.

> A pile of drywall and a pile of concrete can look identical from the driveway and differ by $900 at the scale house. Weight is the number that matters.

What Specific Pueblo West Jobs Actually Cost

Real ranges from typical Pueblo West project types, assuming standard driveway or curbside access:

  • Bathroom gut (tub, vanity, tile, subfloor): $325–$550. One truck, 2–3 hours labor, usually under 1.5 tons.
  • Kitchen tear-out (cabinets, counters, appliances): $550–$900. Cabinet boxes eat volume fast; laminate counters are light, granite adds 400–700 lbs.
  • Roof tear-off, 1,800 sq ft single layer of asphalt shingle: $1,100–$1,900 in disposal and haul alone. Shingles run about 250 lbs per square (100 sq ft), so 18 squares is roughly 4,500 lbs.
  • Deck demo and removal, 12x16 pressure-treated: $600–$1,100 including the tear-down labor. See [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) for how the demolition portion is priced separately from hauling.
  • Detached shed, 10x12 wood frame on skids: $475–$850 depending on whether the floor comes up. Metal sheds run cheaper because scrap value offsets disposal — details on [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Concrete driveway section, 400 sq ft at 4": $1,400–$2,600. Breaking, loading, and hauling roughly 9 tons. This is machine work, not wheelbarrow work.

The spread inside each range is almost entirely access and stairs. A pile stacked at the curb on a Pueblo West half-acre lot with a wide driveway prices at the bottom of the range. The same material behind a locked side gate, down 40 feet of gravel, with a 32-inch opening, prices at the top — sometimes 30–45% higher, because two guys carrying material by hand for 90 extra minutes is real cost.

Dumpster vs. Full-Service: Run the Actual Math

The honest comparison, using Pueblo West numbers for a mid-size remodel generating about 12 cubic yards:

Dumpster rental: $425–$575 for a 20-yard, 7-day rental, 3-ton cap, plus $60–$95 per ton overage. Add a permit if it sits in the county right-of-way. Then add your labor: 12 yards of remodel debris takes two people roughly 6–9 hours to load by hand. If you're paying a laborer $22/hour, that's another $264–$396. All-in: $690–$1,050, plus your own weekend.

Full-service haul: $700–$1,400 for the same 12 yards, loaded by the crew, gone in 2–3 hours, no permit, no driveway damage, no second trip to the scale.

The dumpster wins when the job runs long. If you're a DIY homeowner working evenings and weekends across three weeks, a 30-day container at $650–$900 beats calling a truck five separate times at $300 a pop. The dumpster also wins on pure volume-per-dollar for light material — insulation, packaging, framing scrap.

Full-service wins when the debris is already generated, when it's heavy, when your driveway is a soft asphalt apron that a 10-ton roll-off truck will crack, or when you need it gone before an inspection or a closing date. It also wins on any job where the sorting matters, since a crew separating metal at the truck can shave $40–$120 off a load in scrap credit that a dumpster gives you nothing for.

When Construction Debris Removal Isn't Worth Paying For

Don't hire us if your debris fits in a pickup and you own a pickup. A single Pueblo County landfill trip for a half-ton of clean C&D runs you roughly $25–$45 at the scale plus 45 minutes of driving. Our minimum charge is around $150–$195 because a two-person crew and a truck have to leave the yard regardless. If your entire job is one vanity, four bags of tile, and some baseboard, you're paying $150 for a $30 problem. Rent a trailer for $50 and keep the difference.

Don't hire anyone for clean, sorted concrete if you can wait. Clean concrete with no rebar, no dirt, no wire mesh is a *recyclable* in this region, and some crushing operations accept it at a reduced rate — occasionally near-free for genuinely clean loads. If you can stage a clean pile on your lot for a few weeks and haul it yourself, you may cut disposal to a fraction of the mixed-load rate. The catch is real: the moment there's rebar, soil, or a chunk of asphalt in the pile, it grades as mixed debris and the discount evaporates. Most homeowner piles are not clean.

Don't pay for hauling on material that has a buyer. Structural steel, copper wire and pipe, brass fittings, aluminum window frames, cast iron tub — scrap yards pay for these. A single cast iron tub is 250–350 lbs of scrap. Pulling the metal out before the truck arrives can offset $50–$200 of a job. We will do this sorting for you, but we'd rather tell you that you can capture the value yourself than have you discover it later.

Don't hire the truck if the real problem is demolition. Removal pricing assumes material is already loose. If you call for "debris removal" and the deck is still standing, the concrete is still a slab, or the shed still has a roof, you're buying demolition labor at removal prices, which is the worst rate structure in the trade. Scope it as a demo job from the start and it prices more honestly.

We are the wrong call for these, plainly:

  • Asbestos-containing material. Popcorn ceilings, 9x9 floor tile, old duct wrap, some joint compound in pre-1980 Pueblo West builds. This requires a licensed abatement contractor and state notification. We will not haul it, and a company that says yes without testing is a liability you don't want on your property.
  • Lead paint debris in quantity. Renovation of pre-1978 housing carries federal RRP rules. That's a certified renovator's job.
  • Wet paint, solvents, adhesives, fuel. Household hazardous waste has its own disposal channel. Cans of fully hardened latex paint are usually fine; liquid is not.
  • Contaminated soil. Needs testing and a permitted facility, not a junk truck.
  • Live active job sites needing daily service. If you need a container on site for six weeks of continuous production, a roll-off from a container company is cheaper per yard than repeat truck visits. We'll tell you that on the phone.

The failure modes worth knowing about. Volume-priced quotes get revised on site when the material is denser than described — that's not a bait-and-switch, it's the scale house being non-negotiable, but it feels like one if nobody warned you. Same-day service on a heavy load may not happen if the transfer station closes before the second trip. And a truck cannot get to material behind a 36-inch gate on a sloped lot without hand-carrying every piece, which converts a $400 job into a $700 job. Any of those three should surface *before* the crew shows up. Ask.

How to Get an Estimate That Doesn't Move

The quote that holds is the quote built on the right information. Give a hauler these five things and the number you hear is the number you pay:

  • Material type by category — wood/drywall, roofing, concrete/masonry, dirt, metal, mixed. Don't say "construction debris." Say "roughly 18 squares of shingles plus underlayment."
  • Rough dimensions of the pile — length x width x height in feet. Twelve feet by six feet by four feet is about 10.7 cubic yards. That single sentence prevents most disputes.
  • Access details — driveway width, gate opening, stairs, distance from pile to where a truck can park, whether a skid steer can reach it.
  • Age of the structure — anything pre-1980 changes the hazardous-material conversation before it becomes a problem.
  • Photos. Four photos from four angles beat a ten-minute phone description every time.

For heavy material, ask specifically whether the quote is volume-based or weight-based and what the weight allowance is. "Included up to 2 tons, $95/ton after" is a real quote. "About $500" for a concrete job is a guess.

Pueblo West Specifics That Change the Number

A few local realities that don't apply in Colorado Springs or Denver pricing:

  • Lot sizes help you. Most Pueblo West parcels are one acre or larger with generous driveways and no HOA container restrictions. That access is worth real money — jobs here often price 10–20% below the same job in a dense Pueblo city neighborhood with alley-only access.
  • Wind is a scheduling factor. Spring gusts on the mesa regularly hit 40+ mph. Loose roofing debris and insulation don't get hauled uncovered on those days, and a tarped load takes longer. It's a half-hour, not a crisis, but it moves schedules.
  • Caliche and rock are everywhere. Excavation spoil here isn't fluffy topsoil. A cubic yard of local rocky fill runs 2,400–3,000 lbs, versus about 1,800 for garden soil. Budget accordingly on any [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) job with export.
  • Distance to disposal. Runs to the county facility add drive time compared to in-city Pueblo jobs. On small loads that's absorbed; on multi-trip jobs it's a real line item, which is why staging one large haul usually beats three small ones.
