AFTER A CLEAN GETAWAY ESTATE SALE

If you searched "A Clean Getaway Estate Sales," you're most likely trying to reach that estate sale company, or a company like it, to sell off a household. To be clear up front: ClearPath Junk Removal is not A Clean Getaway Estate Sales and has no connection to them. We can't speak to their pricing, availability or service area, so contact them directly for that. What we can explain is how estate sales usually work for Pueblo West families, what is typically left once the sale ends, and when you need a hauler afterward and when you don't.

How a Typical Estate Sale Works in Pueblo West

Most estate sale companies on the Front Range follow a similar model, though terms vary between companies and you should get each one in writing:

  • Commission, not a flat fee. Rates commonly run 30% to 50% of gross sales. Large, valuable estates usually land near the low end. Smaller sales often pay 40% or more because the company's labor costs stay about the same no matter how much the contents bring in.
  • Minimums. Many companies won't take a sale unless the contents are likely to gross somewhere around $5,000 to $15,000. Below that line, the commission doesn't cover staffing for the weekend.
  • Lead time. Booking 2 to 6 weeks out is normal. Spring and early fall fill up first.
  • Setup and sale days. Staging, pricing and photographing usually take 3 to 7 days, and the sale itself typically runs 2 to 3 days. Plenty of companies drop prices 25% to 50% on the final day.
  • Settlement. Expect a check and an itemized sales report roughly 1 to 3 weeks after the sale closes.

All told, plan for about a month to six weeks from your first phone call to a house that's ready to list. In Pueblo West that schedule can stretch, because some companies based in Colorado Springs, roughly 45 miles north, take Pueblo County sales only when their calendar has room.

What's Usually Left Behind (and Why Nobody Mentions It)

Estate sale contracts don't always spell this out: even a well-run sale rarely empties the house. Leftovers commonly amount to 20% to 40% of the original contents by volume. Most of it falls into a few predictable groups:

  • Heavy, low-value furniture. Particle-board entertainment centers, recliners with worn upholstery and older sleeper sofas don't sell even at 50% off.
  • Mattresses and box springs. Many shoppers won't buy used bedding at any price.
  • Old appliances. A 15-year-old chest freezer in the garage seldom finds a buyer, and the refrigerant has to be recovered before it can be scrapped.
  • Garage and shed contents. Partial paint cans, broken lawn equipment and decades of hardware sorted into coffee cans.
  • Electronics. Tube TVs and old desktop computers. Colorado has banned many electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013, so they can't simply go out with the trash.
  • Paperwork, linens, and personal effects that the family didn't claim and nobody bought.

Before you sign, ask the estate sale company one direct question: "What happens to everything that doesn't sell?" You'll generally hear one of three answers:

1. They handle it. Some companies include the cleanout. Others charge a separate fee or deduct it from your proceeds. 2. They donate the sellable leftovers and leave everything else for you. 3. It's your problem. The contract ends when the sale ends.

Answer #3 is common, and it catches families off guard when they're already paying a mortgage, utilities and insurance on a house they'd like to list.

When to Call a Junk Removal Company (and When It's Too Early)

Call us after the sale, not before it. Families sometimes hire a hauler first because the house looks overwhelming, and that's a mistake. A hauler can end up carrying off a mid-century dresser, a set of Pyrex, or a box of old Pueblo steel-mill memorabilia that would have sold. The estate sale company should see everything before anything is thrown out.

Calling right after the final sale day makes sense for these reasons:

  • The leftovers are mostly unsellable by then. The market has already had its chance at them.
  • Your listing date depends on it. A Realtor can't stage or photograph a house that's still half full.
  • Volume pricing works in your favor. Junk removal is usually priced by how much of the truck your load fills. One trip for everything generally costs less than three or four smaller trips over several weekends.

Our [estate cleanout service in Pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) is built for this handoff: whatever is left after the sale, from the attic to the shed. When the remaining items are mostly big pieces, our [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) and [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) pages cover how those items are handled and where they end up.

> The best time to book a cleanout is the week before the estate sale, for the Monday after it closes. Waiting until you've seen what's left usually costs you 5 to 10 days of carrying costs on the house.

When a Post-Sale Junk Hauler ISN'T Worth It

This section may cost us a job, and it's still the most useful part of this page. In several common situations you shouldn't hire us, or anyone like us:

  • The estate sale contract already includes the cleanout. If you're paying for it in the commission, don't pay twice. Read the contract for words like "broom clean," "cleanout," or "removal of unsold items" before you call anyone.
  • The leftovers fit in two pickup loads and someone in the family has a truck. Pueblo West homes on large lots often come with a family member who owns a trailer. If the leftovers amount to a couple of recliners and some boxes, a Saturday and the landfill gate fee will cost less than a hauler.
  • Nearly everything left is donatable. Clean, working furniture and household goods can often go to a charity pickup at no cost. Pickup windows can run 1 to 3 weeks out, and charities turn down stained, broken or upholstered items with pet hair, but if your leftovers are in good shape, try that first.
  • The house is selling "as-is" to an investor. Some cash buyers in Pueblo County buy houses with contents included and clear them out themselves. If you've accepted that kind of offer, removing the contents doesn't add value and you'd be paying for nothing.
  • The estate is too small for an estate sale at all. If the whole household might gross $3,000, a company taking 40% leaves you about $1,800 for weeks of waiting. In that case it often makes more sense to skip the estate sale, let family take what they want, sell a few good pieces online, and pay for one cleanout. Families tend to overestimate how much ordinary household contents are worth.

Junk removal also has limits you should know about:

  • We don't appraise. If you think an item might be valuable, such as a firearm collection, jewelry, Native American pottery or Western art, get it appraised before anyone hauls anything away.
  • Hazardous materials are restricted. Large amounts of old pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil or unlabeled containers may have to go to a household hazardous waste drop-off instead of on our truck. Ask about these before booking.
  • Severe hoarding situations take longer and cost more. If the home has biohazards, structural damage or pest infestation, a standard cleanout quote won't be accurate. Our [hoarding cleanout page](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) explains how those jobs are scoped differently.

Questions to Ask Any Estate Sale Company Before You Sign

Whether you hire A Clean Getaway or another company, these questions protect you:

  • What's your commission percentage, and is there a minimum fee? Get the number in writing.
  • Are you bonded and insured? Strangers will be walking through the house for 2 to 3 days.
  • How do you handle unsold items, and what does that cost? This is the question most families skip.
  • When will I receive payment and a sales report? Anything past 30 days deserves a follow-up.
  • Do you offer buyouts? Some companies will buy the leftovers outright at a low price. That can make sense on a short timeline.
  • Can you provide 2 or 3 references from Pueblo County sales in the last year? A company that mostly works Colorado Springs or Denver may price items for a different buyer pool.

A Realistic Timeline for a Pueblo West Estate

Here's how a typical, smooth sequence works out:

  • Week 1: Family members take sentimental items and anything they plan to keep. Get valuables appraised.
  • Weeks 2–4: Book the estate sale company and schedule the sale date.
  • Week 4 or 5: Setup, then a 2- to 3-day sale.
  • Monday after the sale: Cleanout of the leftovers. Most single-family homes take one day. Homes with full garages, sheds or outbuildings on larger Pueblo West lots may take two.
  • Week 6: Cleaning, minor repairs, listing photos.

Garages and outbuildings are often where this schedule slips. Many Pueblo West properties have a detached shop or shed packed with decades of tools and materials. If yours does, our [garage cleanout service](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) is often booked alongside the estate cleanout, and an old shed that's falling apart can come down the same week through [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).

The Bottom Line for Pueblo West Families

An estate sale company and a junk removal company do different jobs, and you'll usually get the most out of an estate by using them in that order. The estate sale turns valuable items into money. The cleanout handles the 20% to 40% that doesn't sell, so the house can go on the market.
