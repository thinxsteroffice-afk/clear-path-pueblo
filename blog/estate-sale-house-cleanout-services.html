ESTATE SALE HOUSE CLEANOUT SERVICES

House cleanout services for estate sales handle the two jobs an estate sale doesn't: clearing out items before the sale that aren't worth selling, and hauling away everything left unsold afterward. Unsold leftovers are usually the bigger job. Most estate sales in Pueblo West end with roughly 20–40% of the contents still in the house. That means a few broken recliners, the garage shelving, the basement boxes, and the dresser nobody wanted at 50% off on Sunday. A cleanout crew can usually haul all of that in one day for a typical 3-bedroom home. Cost depends on volume, not hours. Most post-sale cleanouts take 1.5 to 4 truckloads, which comes to roughly $600 to $2,400. The cleanout should be booked before the sale date, not after.

How estate sales and cleanouts fit together

Families often treat the estate sale as the whole plan and only think about the leftovers on Sunday evening. By then the closing date is close, the realtor wants photos, and nobody has a truck.

A smoother timeline for a Pueblo West home looks like this:

  • 2–3 weeks before the sale: Walk through with the estate sale company. Mark what won't sell: stained mattresses, particleboard furniture, expired pantry items, broken appliances, and decades of paperwork. Many estate sale companies won't put these out, and they take up floor space buyers need.
  • 1–2 weeks before: A small pre-sale haul, usually a quarter to half a truckload, clears the garage and spare rooms so the sale company can stage them.
  • Sale weekend: Most sales run 2–3 days, often Friday through Sunday, with markdowns of 25–50% on the last day.
  • Within 48–72 hours after: The cleanout crew removes everything left, sweeps out, and the house is ready for the realtor's photographer or the buyer's final walk-through.

If you book the post-sale cleanout when you sign with the estate sale company, you can line it up for the Monday or Tuesday after the sale. If you wait until the sale ends, you could be looking at a 5–10 day gap in a busy month. Spring and the weeks before Thanksgiving fill up fastest.

What it typically costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by how much of the truck your items fill. These are realistic ranges for our market:

  • Quarter load (one room of odds and ends, about 4 pickup beds' worth): roughly $175–$275
  • Half load (a garage's worth of leftovers): roughly $325–$450
  • Full load (about 15 cubic yards, the leftovers from a large sale): roughly $550–$750
  • Whole-house post-sale cleanout (3–4 bedrooms plus garage and shed): usually $1,200–$2,800

Some items cost extra because Colorado and federal rules require special handling:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units need the refrigerant recovered before scrapping, as federal EPA rules require. Expect a $25–$75 per-unit surcharge from most haulers.
  • TVs, monitors, and computers can't go in a Colorado landfill. The state banned landfill disposal of covered electronics in 2013, so they go to an electronics recycler. That often adds $20–$50 per CRT TV.
  • Leftover latex and oil paint goes through Colorado's PaintCare drop-off program and shouldn't end up in the truck.
  • Mattresses and box springs often carry a $25–$50 fee because they're bulky and hard to compact.

Pueblo West has its own cost drivers. Many lots are a half-acre to several acres. That means outbuildings, horse-property fencing, piles of old lumber, and sheds packed with 30 years of tools are common. A house that looks like 2 truckloads from the street can turn into 4 once someone opens the detached shop. Get a quote that covers the outbuildings, not just the house. If there's a shed that needs to come down too, our [shed demolition removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) crew can handle it on the same trip.

> The most expensive cleanout we see is the one that wasn't planned. A rush job squeezed in three days before closing costs the same per load, but it often means paying to haul items the family could have sold, donated, or kept with another week.

What a good crew actually does after the sale

A real post-sale cleanout is more than "throw it all in the truck." Here's what you should expect from us or from any hauler you hire:

  • Sorting at the curb, not at the dump. Usable furniture, housewares, and linens go to donation where possible. Metal goes to scrap. Electronics go to an e-waste recycler. The rest goes to the landfill.
  • Holding back anything flagged. Families often miss things. In a typical whole-house job, we find envelopes of cash, savings bonds, or jewelry in about 1 of every 10 homes. Our crews set aside anything that looks like documents, photos, keys, or valuables, and they don't toss it.
  • Broom-swept rooms. The crew sweeps out the house. This isn't a deep clean, but it's ready for a realtor walk-through.
  • Whole-property scope. That includes garages, sheds, crawl spaces, and the yard. Our [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) services are often bundled into the same visit.

When a cleanout service is NOT worth it

We'd rather lose a job than have you overpay. Here's when hiring us, or any junk removal company, doesn't make sense.

1. When there's less than a quarter load left. If the sale went well and all that's left is a few boxes and a broken lamp, a family member with a pickup and one trip to the transfer station will spend maybe $40–$80 in disposal fees. Paying a $175+ minimum for that is a poor deal.

2. When you have the labor and the time. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster in the Pueblo area usually rents for around $400–$600 for 7–10 days. If three able-bodied relatives are in town for a week and can carry things themselves, a dumpster is often 30–50% cheaper than a full-service crew for a large house. You're swapping money for sore backs, and that's a legitimate choice. Keep in mind that dumpsters don't accept electronics, appliances with refrigerant, or tires, so you'll still need a plan for those.

3. When you're selling to a cash buyer "as-is." Many investors who buy Pueblo West homes for cash will take the house with the contents still inside and factor the cleanout into their offer. If you've already chosen that route, paying for a cleanout first may just hand the buyer money. Ask before you book anyone.

4. When you don't yet have legal authority. In Colorado, the person clearing a deceased person's home should be the named personal representative, or should be acting with their written approval. If siblings disagree about who gets Grandma's hutch, don't hire a cleanout yet. Once something is in the truck it's gone, and no hauler can undo a family dispute. We'll ask who authorized the job, and if the answer is unclear, we'll wait.

5. When the house needs hazmat or remediation work. We don't remove asbestos, mold remediation materials, biohazards, or drums of unknown chemicals. If the home has a long-term hoarding situation, rodent infestation, or unattended death, that's a different job with different safety requirements. Start with our [hoarding cleanout service](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo), or with a licensed remediation company for biohazards.

6. When you expect us to appraise or sell. We're haulers, not appraisers or estate sale operators. We won't tell you whether a painting is worth $20 or $2,000, and we don't buy contents. If you suspect something valuable is in the house, get it appraised before anyone touches it.

7. When you need guaranteed donation. We donate what local charities will accept. But charities regularly turn down upholstered furniture with any wear, cribs, older mattresses, and entertainment centers. On a typical job, 25–40% of the volume is donated or recycled. The rest goes to the landfill. Anyone who promises "100% donated" is either exaggerating or charging you extra to drive it around town.

Questions to ask any estate cleanout provider

Whoever you hire, these questions separate solid operators from guys with a trailer:

  • Is the quote all-in? Does it include labor, disposal, and appliance or electronics fees? Or will it jump on the day of the job?
  • Are you insured? If a crew member gets hurt on your parent's stairs, you want liability and workers' comp coverage to be theirs, not the estate's.
  • What happens to items you find that look personal? You want a clear yes: "we set them aside."
  • Can you come within 72 hours of the sale ending?
  • Do you need someone on site? Many families live out of state. We handle about half of our estate jobs with only a lockbox code and a phone call before and after, plus photos of the finished rooms.

Getting the timing right in Pueblo West

Estate sales here peak from April through October, when buyers will drive out to the west side. Winter sales draw smaller crowds, so more is left over and you should expect a bigger cleanout. If you're planning a winter sale, budget for roughly one extra half-load compared with a summer sale of the same house.

Book the cleanout when you book the sale. Ask for an on-site estimate, which should take 20–30 minutes and cost nothing. Make sure the estimator sees the shed, the shop, and the crawl space, not just the living room.
