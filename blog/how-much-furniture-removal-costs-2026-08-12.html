HOW MUCH FURNITURE REMOVAL COSTS

Furniture removal in Pueblo West usually runs $75 to $150 for a single item, $120 to $200 for a standard sofa, and $250 to $450 for a full room's worth — bed, dresser, nightstands, and a chair or two. A packed full-size truckload (roughly 15 cubic yards) lands around $700 to $900. Those numbers include labor, loading, hauling, and disposal fees. Two things move the price more than anything else: how much floor space the load eats in the truck, and how hard the item is to get out of the house. A hide-a-bed sofa going down a flight of stairs is a different job than the same sofa sitting in an open garage.

The Actual Price Grid for Pueblo West

Most local haulers, including us, price by volume — the fraction of the truck bed your stuff occupies — not by weight or by the hour. Here's what that looks like in real numbers for a 2026 Pueblo West job:

  • Single item, curbside or garage: $75–$150. This is the minimum-charge tier and it's where most calls land.
  • Standard 3-seat sofa or loveseat: $120–$200.
  • Sectional (3+ pieces): $175–$300. More pieces, more trips, and sectionals rarely come apart the way people expect.
  • Recliner or lift chair: $90–$130. Lift chairs weigh 130–180 lbs with the motor and base still attached.
  • Mattress and box spring set: $75–$110 for the pair. See [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for why these are priced separately from bed frames.
  • Full bedroom set: $250–$400.
  • Quarter truck (about 4 cubic yards): $250–$350.
  • Half truck (about 7–8 cubic yards): $450–$650.
  • Full truck (about 15 cubic yards): $700–$900.

Add-ons that are real and that you should ask about before booking: $25–$50 per flight of stairs, $20–$40 for disassembly if a bed frame or wall unit has to come apart, and a $15–$25 mattress recycling fee in some markets. A good quote states these up front. If a company won't name them on the phone, that's a signal.

Why the Same Sofa Costs $120 at One House and $210 at Another

The item is identical. The building isn't. These are the variables that actually change your number:

Door geometry. A standard interior door is 30–32 inches wide. A rolled-arm sofa is often 36–38 inches deep. If it went in before the door casing did, or through a window, it isn't coming out the easy way. That turns a 20-minute job into a 50-minute job with a screwdriver and a utility knife.

Where the truck can park. Pueblo West has an advantage here that Pueblo proper often doesn't. Most lots out here are half-acre to two-and-a-half acres with wide driveways and no alley-only access. A crew can back a 16-foot box within 30 feet of the front door on the majority of jobs. In older Pueblo neighborhoods off Northern or Abriendo, a 100-foot carry from a street-parked truck adds 15–20 minutes per item. That distance is a genuine cost, and it's why the same job can be $40 cheaper on Purcell than it is on the Mesa.

Stairs and split-levels. A basement sofa is roughly 1.5x the labor of a main-floor sofa. Two-story to curb is closer to 1.7x.

Condition and contamination. Bed bug exposure, cat urine saturation, mold from a swamp cooler leak — those loads can't be donated and sometimes can't go in the same truck as clean material. Expect a $50–$100 premium, and expect an honest hauler to tell you why.

What It Costs to Do It Yourself Out Here

Run the real math before you assume DIY wins. From most of Pueblo West, you're looking at roughly 12 to 20 miles to a Pueblo-area disposal site, or 25 to 40 minutes each way depending on whether you're coming off Platteville or down McCulloch to US-50.

  • Truck rental: a pickup from a local U-Haul runs about $19.95/day plus roughly $0.99–$1.29 per mile. A 40-mile round trip puts you at $60–$72 before fuel and the rental's environmental fee.
  • Dump trailer rental: $60–$90 per day, and you need a vehicle with a 2-inch ball and working trailer lights.
  • Tipping fees: solid waste disposal is typically charged by weight or by load size and changes year to year — call the facility the morning of, because a sofa-plus-mattress load frequently lands in the $25–$50 range and mattresses often carry their own surcharge.
  • Your time: load, drive, wait in the scale line, unload, drive back, return the truck. Budget 3 to 4 hours honestly.
  • Your back and your helper. A queen box spring down a stairwell with one inexperienced helper is how people herniate discs.

DIY for a single sofa realistically costs $90–$130 and half a Saturday. Against a $130 pickup where two people carry it out of your basement in 25 minutes, the gap is narrower than most homeowners assume — which is exactly why the honest answer sometimes is "just do it yourself."

> If your item is already in the garage, you own a truck, and you have a helper who isn't going to hurt themselves, hiring a hauler for one piece is usually the wrong call. Say so out loud before you pay anyone.

When Hiring Us Is a Bad Deal — Read This Part

We'd rather lose the booking than take money for a job that didn't need doing. Skip the hauler in these situations:

Your furniture is genuinely donatable and you have a week. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and other Pueblo-area charities will pick up couches, dressers, and dining sets in good condition at no charge. "Good condition" means no rips, no stains, no pet damage, and no broken frames — their standards are stricter than most people expect, and a rejected pickup wastes a week. But if your piece qualifies, that's $0 versus $150. Take the free option.

You're moving one item and it's already outside. A single dresser at the end of a Pueblo West driveway is a 6-minute job for us and still hits the $75 minimum. That minimum exists because the drive out from the yard, the fuel, and the disposal stop cost the same whether we take one dresser or six. If you own a truck, that $75 buys you nothing you can't do in 90 minutes.

Your item has real resale value. A solid-wood mid-century dresser, a leather sectional under five years old, or a decent dining set often clears $150–$600 on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo County, and buyers here generally haul it themselves. Paying us $200 to remove a $400 asset is a $600 swing. List it first. Give it 10–14 days.

You have an active bed bug infestation and haven't treated. Removing the mattress does not solve it. You'll re-infest the replacement within weeks. Call a pest control company first, then call us.

You want the price of a full cleanout but only a furniture quote. If your garage has furniture plus paint cans, tires, and a broken water heater, a furniture-only quote will be wrong. Tires, e-waste, and hazardous materials carry separate disposal costs and some can't be accepted at all. Get it scoped as a [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) job instead.

You need it done in the next two hours on a Saturday. Same-day is often possible, but a genuine emergency slot may carry a $50–$75 rush fee, and during spring cleanup weeks in April and May the schedule fills 3–5 days out. Planning ahead is worth real money.

Limitations You Should Know Before You Book

No hauler solves everything. Straight talk:

  • We can't donate most of what we haul. Realistically 20–35% of furniture we pick up is clean enough for a charity to accept. The rest is stained, broken, or smoke-damaged. Anyone claiming a 90% donation rate on used furniture is describing a fantasy.
  • We won't cut structural elements. Getting a sectional out sometimes requires removing a door and casing. We'll do the door. We won't open a wall.
  • Wind matters here. Pueblo West regularly sees 30–45 mph gusts in the spring. Loose cushions and thin particleboard get strapped and tarped, which adds a few minutes — and on truly severe wind days, safe hauling means rescheduling.
  • Weight limits are real. A cast-iron sleeper sofa, a full safe, or a slate pool table is a specialty lift, not a furniture pickup.
  • Quotes over the phone are estimates. A firm price comes when the crew sees the item. Ours doesn't move unless the scope does, and you can decline on-site at no charge.
