HOW MUCH DOES JUNK REMOVAL COST?

Most Pueblo West households pay $250 to $550 for a typical junk removal job — that's a quarter to a half of a 15-cubic-yard truck, hauled and dumped, labor included. Single items like a mattress or recliner run $85 to $175. A packed garage cleanout that fills a full truck lands at $750 to $950. Whole-house estate cleanouts requiring two or three truckloads run $1,400 to $2,800. Dense material — concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, wet soil — gets priced by weight instead of volume and often costs more per cubic yard than furniture, because a half-full truck of concrete hits the axle limit before it hits the walls.

Below is how those numbers are actually built, what moves them up or down in Pueblo West specifically, and the situations where hiring us is the wrong call.

How Volume Pricing Actually Works

Almost every junk removal company in Pueblo County prices by how much space your stuff takes in the truck, not by the hour and not by the item. A standard dump-trailer or box truck holds roughly 15 to 18 cubic yards — visualize six to eight pickup truck beds, loaded level with the rails.

Typical Pueblo West brackets:

  • Single item / minimum charge: $75–$175 (one couch, one mattress, one washer)
  • 1/8 truck: $150–$250 (a few boxes, a small dresser, bagged debris)
  • 1/4 truck: $250–$375 (a bedroom set, or a small shed's contents)
  • 1/2 truck: $400–$550 (a two-car garage's worth of accumulated stuff)
  • 3/4 truck: $600–$750
  • Full truck: $750–$950

Common add-ons and item-specific pricing:

  • Mattress or box spring: $85–$125 each — most landfills charge a separate mattress fee, which is why it's never free
  • Refrigerator, freezer, or window A/C: $95–$150 because of the refrigerant recovery requirement
  • Hot tub: $350–$650 depending on whether it's cut apart in place or dragged out whole
  • Tires: $12–$20 each; off-rim truck tires more
  • Paint, solvents, propane tanks, car batteries: usually declined or surcharged $15–$40 per unit, because they can't go in a standard load
  • Stairs, a 100-foot carry from the back of a Pueblo West acre lot, or disassembly: $50–$100 on top

That last one matters more here than in the city. A lot of Pueblo West properties sit on half-acre to one-acre lots with a long gravel drive and no alley access. If a crew has to walk 150 feet each way through gravel with a 300-pound sleeper sofa, that's real time — expect it to be priced in.

The Weight Ceiling Nobody Explains Until the Invoice

Volume pricing quietly assumes household density: furniture, boxes, plastic, wood. Roughly 150 to 250 pounds per cubic yard. A full truck of that is about 1.5 tons.

Now load that same truck with concrete at ~4,000 pounds per cubic yard. Four yards — a quarter of the visual space — is already 8 tons and over legal weight for most single-axle setups.

> If a company quotes you a flat "quarter load" price for a broken concrete patio without asking a single question about thickness or square footage, you are looking at a change order, not a quote.

That's why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) and [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) get quoted separately, usually $150 to $400 per load with a hard tonnage cap, or by the hour with equipment. Same for the sandy, rocky fill common on Pueblo West lots — a "small" pile of excavated soil from a fence-post job weighs far more than homeowners guess. Wet soil is heavier than dry by 15 to 25%, which is why the same pile costs more the day after a monsoon storm in July or August.

What Disposal Actually Costs Us in Pueblo County

Transparency here helps you spot a bad quote. On a $450 half-truck job, a rough breakdown:

  • Landfill tipping fees: $60–$140 depending on tonnage and whether there's a mattress, tire, or appliance surcharge
  • Fuel and round trip to the Pueblo-area landfill: 25–40 miles round trip from most of Pueblo West, $18–$30
  • Two crew for 1.5–2.5 hours including load, drive, tip, and return
  • Insurance, workers' comp, truck payment, and maintenance: the invisible chunk

Which is why prices below roughly $60 per cubic yard should make you suspicious rather than happy. That math generally means one of three things: the material is going somewhere it shouldn't, the "crew" is uninsured, or the quote is a hook and the real number arrives on site.

Where DIY Genuinely Wins

Run the numbers honestly before you call anyone.

A pickup or borrowed trailer, two trips to the landfill, roughly $45–$65 per ton in tip fees plus minimums, and maybe $30–$50 in fuel: you can clear a light garage load for $100–$180 out of pocket. Renting a box truck adds a base day rate around $30–$40 plus roughly $0.99 per mile, which for a Pueblo West round trip is another $30–$45.

So: if you have a truck, a free Saturday, a helper, and a back that works, DIY beats us on cash cost by $200 to $400 on a mid-size load. That's not a close call. What you're buying from a company is the four hours, the second person, the liability, and not having to figure out what to do with the freon appliance and the mattress.

Also worth checking before you spend anything: Pueblo West Metro District and the City of Pueblo periodically run community cleanup or free-dump events, and some scrap yards pay for clean metal. A dryer, water heater, and a bed frame taken to a scrap buyer can put $15–$40 back in your pocket instead of costing $150 to haul.

When Junk Removal Is the Wrong Purchase

The part most of these pages skip. Do not hire us — or anyone — in these situations:

  • You're mid-renovation and will generate debris for weeks. A 10-yard roll-off at $325–$450 per week or a 20-yard at $450–$650 beats three separate $500 pickups. Call for [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) only when the mess is done accumulating, or when you need it gone today and a container can't sit in the drive.
  • You have one small item and a truck. Paying a $150 minimum to move a nightstand 12 miles is a bad trade. That minimum exists because we're spending the same two hours regardless; it isn't a scam, it's just a poor fit.
  • Your stuff has real resale value. A working washer/dryer pair, a mid-century dresser, decent tools — Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo West / Pueblo area moves these in days, and "free, you haul" gets a taker on most bulky furniture within 48 hours. We charge you $150 to remove what someone will pay you $80 for and carry out themselves.
  • You expected us to recycle everything. Realistically, 20–40% of a mixed household load gets diverted — metal, appliances, clean cardboard, usable donations. The rest goes to the landfill. Anyone promising 90% diversion on a mixed load in southern Colorado is describing a market that doesn't exist here.
  • You need it in the next two hours on a Saturday in June. Spring and early summer are the busiest stretch of the year; realistic scheduling is 24 to 72 hours out. Same-day is sometimes possible, sometimes not, and a company that promises same-day every time is either overpromising or idle.
  • The budget is genuinely tight and the junk isn't causing harm. If it's stacked in a corner of the garage and nothing is rotting, leaking, or blocking egress, this is a want, not a need. Wait for a cleanup event or a free weekend.

Two more honest limitations: we can't take hazardous waste, and we can't legally remove asbestos-containing material — old vinyl floor tile, certain popcorn ceilings, some pipe wrap in pre-1980 homes. That requires an abatement contractor and costs several times a junk removal quote. And a genuine [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) is a different job with a different price — often $2,000–$6,000+ across multiple days — because sorting is slow, biohazard risk is real, and the volume is usually 3–5x what the initial walkthrough suggested.

How to Get a Quote That Doesn't Move

Photos are worth more than descriptions. Stand back far enough to get the whole pile in frame, and take a second shot showing the path from the pile to where a truck can park. Then say the four things that change price most: rough dimensions of the pile, whether anything is concrete/dirt/roofing, how many stairs, and the carry distance.

Any competent company can hold a quote within 10% with that information. Without it, you're getting a guess, and guesses get revised upward in the driveway.
