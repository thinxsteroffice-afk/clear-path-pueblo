What a Garage Cleanout Actually Includes

"Cleanout" means different things to different companies, so here is what you should expect for the price:

  • All the lifting and loading. You point, we carry. You don't have to drag anything to the driveway first.
  • Sorting into donate, recycle, and landfill piles. Usable furniture, tools, and bikes go to donation when they qualify. Scrap metal goes to a recycler.
  • A broom sweep of the floor once the space is empty.
  • Disposal fees built into the quote. You shouldn't see a separate dump charge afterward.

It does not include deep cleaning, pressure washing oil stains, pest treatment, or organizing what you keep. If you want shelving installed and your bins labeled, you need a professional organizer. Many of them charge $50–$100 an hour and work well alongside a cleanout.

What Drives the Price in Pueblo West

Most junk haulers, including us, price by how much of the truck you fill, not by the hour. A standard junk truck holds roughly 15 cubic yards, about the size of six or seven pickup beds. Typical tiers:

  • Minimum load (1/8 truck): about $100–$175. This covers a few items like a old lawn mower and some boxes.
  • Half truck: about $300–$450. This is a typical one-car garage with moderate clutter.
  • Full truck: about $550–$800. This is a full two-car garage with furniture and bulky items.

Several things push a Pueblo West job toward the high end:

  • Weight, not just volume. Concrete, roofing shingles, dirt, and tile are dense. A truck that's only half full of broken concrete can hit weight limits before it fills up. Heavy material often gets a surcharge or a separate quote through our [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) service.
  • Appliances with refrigerant. Old garage fridges and chest freezers need their Freon recovered before disposal. Expect $25–$75 per unit on top of the volume price.
  • Tires. These usually run $10–$25 each, more for truck or tractor tires.
  • Detached garages and long carries. Many Pueblo West lots are half an acre or larger. If the shop building sits 200 feet from where the truck can park, loading takes longer.

What We Can't Haul (and What Colorado Won't Let Anyone Landfill)

Garages hold more regulated waste than any other room in the house. Before you book, check your shelves for these:

  • Paint. Latex and oil-based paint are covered by Colorado's PaintCare program, which has run since 2015. Drop sites take leftover paint for free. Fully dried latex cans can sometimes go out as regular trash, but we won't take liquid paint in the truck.
  • Motor oil, antifreeze, solvents, pesticides, and pool chemicals. These are household hazardous waste. Pueblo County runs collection programs for them, so check the county's current schedule. Junk haulers aren't licensed to transport them.
  • Propane tanks. Many hardware stores take back or exchange 20-lb tanks. Partly full tanks are a fire risk in a loaded truck.
  • Electronics. Colorado has banned TVs, computers, monitors, and similar devices from landfills since July 1, 2013. We can take most electronics to a recycler, but some carry a per-item recycling fee.
  • Ammunition, fireworks, and anything unidentified in an unlabeled jug. We'll leave it, and you'll need to call the county.

If a large share of your garage is chemicals, a junk hauler is the wrong first call. Clear the hazardous material out first, then book us for what's left.

When a Garage Cleanout Service Isn't Worth It

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out. In each of these situations, you'd likely be better off not hiring us or anyone like us.

1. You have a truck, a free Saturday, and less than one load. If your junk fits in one pickup bed and nothing weighs over about 75 lbs, taking it to the transfer station yourself might cost $20–$50 in gate fees plus a few hours. Paying $150 or more for us to do the same thing isn't a good deal for you.

2. Most of your stuff has resale value. Tools, compressors, welders, and riding mowers often sell quickly in Pueblo West, where plenty of people work on their own property. A working riding mower can bring $400–$1,200 on Marketplace. We donate what we can, but we don't pay you for your items. If you have more than about $1,000 in sellable equipment, sell it first and call us for the leftovers.

3. You haven't decided what goes. Our crews move quickly. If you're likely to change your mind mid-job or need to go through every box for paperwork, do that first. Half the job is already done once the "keep" pile is set aside.

4. The garage is mostly construction debris or yard waste. Leftover drywall, lumber from a remodel, or piles of branches cost less through a roll-off dumpster you fill on your own schedule. A 10- to 20-yard dumpster for 7 days often runs $350–$600 in this area. That beats a junk truck when you're producing debris over several weekends instead of clearing it all at once.

5. You need a hoarding-level cleanout but booked a garage cleanout. If there's rodent activity, animal waste, mold, or no clear path to walk through, a standard crew will stop and requote. That job needs protective gear and more time, so it's priced as a [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) job. You'll hear about it up front instead of finding out halfway through.

6. You expect a spotless, organized garage when we leave. We leave it empty and swept. Oil stains, cobwebs in the rafters, and bins for what stays are not part of the job.

Where we *do* fall short sometimes:

  • Same-day slots fill up fast in spring. Expect to wait 3–7 days in April and May.
  • Wind and snow delay jobs. Pueblo West gets gusts over 40 mph several times a year. On those days we won't load mattresses and loose cardboard onto an open truck.
  • Quotes over the phone are estimates. The final price is set on site once we see the volume. A photo helps, but an estimate can still be off by 25% or more when boxes are stacked to the ceiling.

How to Get the Lowest Possible Quote

You can usually cut 20–40% off a garage cleanout bill with an hour of prep:

  • Group everything that's leaving on one side of the garage. This makes it easy to estimate volume and fast to load.
  • Break down cardboard and recycle it yourself. Flat cardboard takes up a fraction of the space that whole boxes do.
  • Pull out hazardous materials before we arrive so nothing has to be set aside or left behind.
  • Text photos before booking. Two or three wide shots of the garage get you a much closer estimate.
  • Bundle jobs. If the old hot tub, the swing set, or a shed is also going, one visit costs less than two. See our [services](/services) page for what we can combine.
