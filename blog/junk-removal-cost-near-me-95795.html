JUNK REMOVAL COST NEAR ME: $95–$795

In Pueblo West, most junk removal jobs land between $125 and $795, priced by how much space your stuff takes up in the truck. A few boxes and a mattress runs $125–$175. A garage's worth of clutter — roughly a quarter of a 15-cubic-yard dump trailer — runs $225–$295. Half a load (old sectional, box spring, weight bench, 20 bags of yard waste) runs $375–$475. A full load, meaning everything a truck can legally carry, runs $650–$795. Our minimum charge is $95 for a single small item picked up on an existing route day. Those prices include labor, loading, hauling to the transfer station, and disposal fees. No separate "fuel surcharge" line at the end.

Here's what those numbers actually mean, where they go wrong, and when you should skip hiring anybody.

The volume pricing tiers, in plain terms

Nearly every junk removal company in Pueblo County — us included — prices by volume, not by weight or hour. A 15-cubic-yard dump trailer is the unit of measure. For scale: one heaping pickup truck bed is about 2 to 2.5 cubic yards, so a full trailer equals roughly six to seven truckloads in your F-150.

  • Minimum / single item — $95–$125. One recliner, one washer, one mattress. [Mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) typically prices at $85–$110 for the first one, $45–$60 for each additional.
  • 1/8 load (about 2 cubic yards) — $125–$175. A closet purge, 10–15 contractor bags, a couple of small pieces of furniture.
  • 1/4 load (about 4 cubic yards) — $225–$295. The most common Pueblo West job. Half a two-car garage.
  • 1/2 load (about 7.5 cubic yards) — $375–$475. Full garage bay, or a decent-sized yard debris pile.
  • 3/4 to full load (11–15 cubic yards) — $500–$795. Whole-house declutter, [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) first pass, or a shed's contents plus the shed.

Add-ons that are real and should be quoted up front, not sprung on you: $25–$45 for refrigerant recovery on a fridge, freezer, or window A/C unit (federal EPA rules require certified recovery — nobody can legally skip it); $35–$75 for items carried down a full flight of stairs or more than 100 feet from the truck; $65–$95 per ton for anything over the first ton on a heavy load.

Why weight quietly rewrites the volume price

Volume pricing assumes normal household density — roughly 150 to 250 pounds per cubic yard. Break that assumption and any honest company has to reprice, because the transfer station charges by the ton and a trailer will hit its axle rating long before it looks full.

Materials that blow past it:

  • Concrete, brick, flagstone, and river rock — a single cubic yard weighs about 3,000–4,000 lbs. A 4-foot-square pile of broken patio can outweigh an entire full load of furniture. This gets quoted separately as [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo), usually $185–$350 per ton depending on access.
  • Dirt, sod, and gravel — about 2,200–2,700 lbs per cubic yard.
  • Asphalt shingles — a "square" (100 sq ft) of tear-off runs 230–320 lbs, and a 1,600 sq ft roof produces 4,000–6,000 lbs of debris.
  • Wet lumber and treated deck boards — often double the weight of dry framing scrap.
  • Books, tile, and paint cans — the sneaky ones. Twenty banker's boxes of books is roughly 800 lbs in a footprint that looks like nothing.

If a company quotes you a flat "half load, $400" over the phone for a concrete patio without asking a single weight question, that quote will change in your driveway. Ask.

What actually moves the number in Pueblo West specifically

Pueblo West is not Pueblo, and the cost drivers are different in ways that matter:

There is no municipal bulk pickup here. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County served by the Pueblo West Metropolitan District, which handles water, sewer, parks, and roads — not household trash. Trash is a private contract you sign yourself. The "just wait for the city's spring cleanup week" option that Pueblo proper residents sometimes have does not exist for you. That's the single most common wrong assumption we correct on the phone.

Haul distance is real. From most of Pueblo West, a round trip to the disposal site plus scale-in and scale-out time runs 45 to 75 minutes. Companies based in Colorado Springs quoting Pueblo West jobs are absorbing a 90-plus-mile round trip and it shows up in their price — usually $75–$150 more for the same load. Check where the truck is actually coming from.

One-acre lots change the job. A lot of Pueblo West sits on 0.75 to 1+ acre parcels with long gravel drives, detached shops, and debris scattered across a back half-acre rather than stacked at the curb. Scattered material takes roughly 1.5x to 2x the labor time of a stacked pile of the same volume. Pile it before we arrive and you may drop a full tier.

Wind and tumbleweeds. Sustained spring winds here regularly hit 35–50 mph, which means yard debris and open loads get charged for tarping and for chasing material. It also means a pile that sits for three weeks becomes a bigger pile.

> The cheapest quote in Pueblo West is almost always the one that hasn't asked about weight, access, or where the truck is driving from. Those three questions are where the real price lives.

When you should NOT hire a junk removal company

This is the part most of these pages leave out. Plenty of Pueblo West jobs are a bad use of your money, and we'd rather tell you now than argue about it in your driveway.

Skip us if you have a truck, a free Saturday, and under 3 cubic yards. Do the math honestly: a round trip to the transfer station is roughly 20–30 miles from most of Pueblo West. At $3.10/gal and 16 mpg towing, that's about $6 in fuel, plus a disposal minimum that has generally run in the $25–$45 range for small residential loads (call and confirm current rates — they change, and hours are seasonal). Two trips and two hours of your time gets a $250 job done for well under $100. If your time is worth less to you than $75/hour on a Saturday, haul it yourself.

Skip us if you're doing a multi-week remodel. A 10-yard roll-off dumpster rented locally runs about $325–$450 for 7 days, usually with a 2-ton weight cap and $55–$85 per ton over. If you're generating debris continuously for two weeks, the dumpster beats us on cost every time — we're priced for one visit, not for standing by. We'll say so. The crossover point is roughly two loads: past that, rent the box.

Skip us if you want the stuff to go to a good home rather than a landfill. We donate what's genuinely donatable, but be realistic — a cloth sectional that's been in a Pueblo West garage through two summers of 95° heat is not a donation, it's a disposal. If sentimental placement matters more than speed, sell or gift it yourself first, then call us for the remainder. Do not pay us $400 to haul away things you'd have felt better rehoming.

Skip us if the item is worth more than the haul. Working appliances under 8 years old, decent solid-wood furniture, and running mowers often sell locally within 48 hours. Paying $110 to remove a dryer you could sell for $150 is a $260 swing in the wrong direction.

Skip us — and everyone — for hazardous material. We cannot legally take paint (liquid), solvents, motor oil, pesticides, propane tanks, car batteries, medical sharps, or asbestos-containing material. Those go to a household hazardous waste event or a certified abatement contractor. Anyone who says yes to asbestos over the phone is either lying or unlicensed.

Understand the failure modes. Same-day service in Pueblo West is real but not guaranteed — during March–June and September–October we book 2 to 5 days out, and a 2-hour arrival window can slip when a morning job turns out to be double its described size. Phone estimates are estimates; a written on-site quote before we load is the only number that binds. And if a hoard, an evicted tenant's leavings, or a deceased relative's home turns out to be 3x what was described, expect the crew to re-quote rather than eat it. That's the honest version of how this business works.

Getting a quote that doesn't move

Four things make a phone estimate accurate to within about 10%:

  • Send photos with a person or a car in frame for scale. A pile photographed alone is unreadable.
  • Name the heaviest thing in the pile. Concrete, dirt, shingles, tile, or a safe.
  • Describe access. Stairs, gate width, gravel drive, whether a truck can back within 30 feet.
  • Say whether it's stacked or scattered, and whether anything needs disassembly — a [hot tub](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) is a $350–$650 job precisely because it's a demo job, not a lifting job.
  • Ask for the all-in number, including disposal, fuel, and refrigerant fees. Get it in writing by text before the truck rolls.

If you want a straight number for your specific pile, photos texted over usually get a firm quote back within the hour.
