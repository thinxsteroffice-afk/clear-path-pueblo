DO STORES REMOVE OLD FURNITURE?

Yes — most national furniture retailers will haul away your old furniture, but almost always with three strings attached: it happens only on delivery day, it's one-for-one and same-category (a new sofa buys removal of one sofa, not the sofa plus the recliner plus the coffee table), and the piece has to be clean, dry, bug-free, and already disassembled or at least reachable. Some chains do it free, some charge $25–$75 per piece, and some quietly exclude mattresses, sleeper sofas, and anything above the first floor. If you're buying in Pueblo or Colorado Springs and taking delivery out in Pueblo West, there's a fourth string nobody mentions: the crew is working a two-hour window and will not wait while you empty a dresser.

Here's what actually happens, what the fine print says, and the cases where hiring anyone — including us — is a waste of your money.

What "Free Haul-Away" Means in the Fine Print

The policy you care about is almost never on the website's marketing page. It's in the delivery terms, usually a paragraph on the back of the sales order or a checkbox the salesperson clicks at the register. Ask for it in writing before you sign, and ask these four questions by name:

  • Is it one-for-one, or per order? One-for-one is the industry default. Buy a 3-piece sectional, and many stores still remove only one sofa.
  • Does "same category" include mattresses and box springs? Mattress removal is the single most common exclusion, and when it's offered it usually carries a $20–$50 fee per piece.
  • Does the crew go upstairs or into a basement? Plenty of delivery contracts limit removal to the ground floor or to "the room of delivery."
  • What voids it on the spot? Visible bed bugs, mold, pet urine saturation, and standing water in an outdoor piece will get a flat refusal, and the crew is not obligated to tell you in advance.

The honest version: roughly half the time we get called for a single sofa in Pueblo West, the customer had free store haul-away available and lost it — because the sectional counted as three pieces, because the old bed was in the basement, or because the delivery landed two weeks before the new house closed and there was nowhere to put anything.

The Timing Trap: Delivery Day Is the Only Day

Store haul-away is a service bundled to a truck that is already at your curb. Miss that window and the offer evaporates — there is no "come back Thursday."

That matters more here than it does in a dense city. Typical realities for Pueblo West deliveries as of fall 2026:

  • Delivery lead times run 7–14 days for in-stock items and 6–12 weeks for special-order upholstery.
  • Crews quote a two to four hour arrival window and are usually on-site 20–40 minutes.
  • Many retailers charge a $75–$150 redelivery fee if nobody's home or the path isn't clear.
  • A loaded sectional sofa runs 90–150 lbs per piece, and a full one can top 250 lbs — two people, not one.

If your old furniture is still full of clothes, if the new piece is replacing something in a room you haven't painted yet, or if you're mid-move between a Pueblo rental and a Pueblo West house, the store's window and your reality won't line up. That gap is most of our furniture business. See [furniture removal pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) for how we handle the second-trip version of this.

> The retailer's haul-away isn't a disposal service. It's a courtesy attached to a delivery truck, and it expires the moment that truck pulls away.

Where It Actually Goes

Worth knowing before you feel good about it: store haul-away is usually a landfill trip, not a donation or recycling program. Delivery crews are paid per stop. They are not driving your gently-used loveseat to a charity on their own time, and liability policies at most chains forbid it.

Mattresses are the clearest example. Colorado has no statewide mattress recycling fee or take-back mandate — unlike states such as California, Connecticut, and Rhode Island, which fund recycling with a per-mattress fee collected at purchase. With no funded program here, a hauled mattress in Pueblo County is overwhelmingly headed to the landfill, where it's one of the worst items in the waste stream: about 23 cubic feet of mostly recyclable steel, foam, and wood that jams compactors. If diversion matters to you, that's an argument for routing it deliberately rather than handing it to a delivery crew. More on that in [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo).

When This Isn't Worth Paying For — Including Paying Us

This is the part most junk removal sites skip. There are several situations where hiring a hauler, us included, is the wrong call.

Don't pay anyone if the store's free haul-away actually fits. One sofa out, one sofa in, ground floor, clean, nothing inside it? Take the free removal. You'd be paying $75–$150 for something already included in your purchase. We'll tell you this on the phone.

Don't pay us for one small item if you own a pickup. Disposal at a Pueblo County facility typically runs a $25–$45 minimum load charge for a car or small pickup. A single chair or end table is a 35–50 minute round trip from most of Pueblo West plus the gate fee. That's under $50 and an hour of your Saturday against a service minimum several times higher. Our pricing only starts making sense around three or more pieces, or when weight, stairs, or a disassembly job is involved.

Don't pay anyone if the piece is genuinely sellable. A solid-wood dresser, a mid-century piece, a dining set in good shape, a recent-model recliner — these move on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo West in 2–7 days at $50–$300, listing cost $0, and the buyer carries it out. Paying $100 to throw away a $200 asset is a bad trade. The catch is real, though: expect 30–50% no-shows on free and cheap listings, and plan on it sitting in your garage for a week or two.

Don't pay us if a charity will take it and you can wait. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar organizations in the Pueblo area run free pickups for qualifying furniture. Two honest caveats: scheduling is commonly 2–4 weeks out, and standards are strict — no rips, no stains, no pet odor, no particleboard that's swelling, no sleeper sofas at many locations. A declined pickup costs you a month. If you itemize, a donated sofa in good condition is typically valued at $40–$200 in standard charity valuation guides, which may beat our fee outright.

Know our limits, too. We're not a recycling miracle. We separate metal and route usable furniture to donation where it qualifies, but a saturated, torn, or smoke-damaged couch goes to the landfill — ours or anyone's. We don't take hazardous material, and we're not the cheapest option for a single light item: a one-day truck rental at $19.95 plus mileage and fuel will beat us on pure cost every time if you have the back for it. Also, the Pueblo West reality: unimproved driveways, snow, and spring mud occasionally cost us a day. If your deadline is a same-afternoon closing, say so when you call, because sometimes the right answer is that we can't promise it.

A Five-Minute Decision

Run it in this order:

1. Call the store and get the haul-away terms in writing. Count your pieces. If their offer covers everything and the timing works, you're done — spend $0. 2. Anything genuinely sellable? List it today, give it 10 days, keep the cash. 3. Anything donation-grade that you can wait 2–4 weeks on? Book the free charity pickup. 4. Whatever's left — the stained sectional, the basement hide-a-bed, the dresser that's two pieces of water-damaged particleboard — is what a hauler is for. That's where paying someone is honest value, not a convenience tax.
