ESTATE HOUSE CLEAN OUT SERVICES

Estate house clean out services empty a deceased or relocating person's home of everything that isn't staying — furniture, appliances, clothing, decades of garage and shed contents — and leave it broom-clean for listing, rental, or transfer to an heir. In Pueblo West, a full clear-out of a typical 1,600–2,400 sq ft ranch on a one-acre lot runs $1,800–$3,500 and takes a two- or three-person crew one to two working days. Partial jobs (garage only, one bedroom, a shed) start around $180–$250 for a quarter truckload. Most Pueblo West estates need 3 to 5 full truckloads. We can usually be on site within 48–72 hours of a walkthrough, and we sort donate/recycle/dump before anything leaves the driveway.

That's the short version. The rest of this page is the part nobody tells you: what actually drives the price here, where the real money is hiding in a 1970s Pueblo West house, and the several situations where hiring us is a bad financial decision.

What Pueblo West Estates Cost — and Why the Numbers Move

Pueblo West was platted starting in 1969 by McCulloch Properties, and the housing stock reflects it: roughly 35,000 residents now, but the core neighborhoods off Purcell, Joe Martinez, and McCulloch are full of original-owner ranch homes built between 1972 and 1988, sitting on one-acre lots with detached shops, well houses, and 40 years of accumulation behind the fence line.

That acre is the thing national franchises consistently price wrong. A Denver-based quote written off square footage misses the two sheds, the dead pop-up camper, and the 60 feet of railroad tie that's been holding back a berm since the Carter administration.

Here's how our pricing actually breaks down:

  • Volume is the base. A full 15-yard truckload runs $550–$750. Half load $350–$450. Quarter load $180–$250. Most 3-bed/2-bath Pueblo West estates land at 3–5 loads.
  • Disposal is a real cost, not a markup. Landfill tipping runs roughly $45–$65 per ton, and a loaded estate truck weighs 1.5–3 tons. A house full of waterlogged particleboard furniture costs us more than a house full of clothes at identical volume.
  • Regulated items carry surcharges. Refrigerant appliances need EPA Section 608 recovery before scrapping — $35–$50 each. Mattresses run $40–$60 apiece. Tires are $8–$15 each depending on size.
  • Electronics can't be landfilled in Colorado at all. The state's landfill ban on TVs, monitors, and computers took effect July 1, 2013. A garage with six CRT televisions in it is a real line item, not a rounding error.
  • Access drives labor. Second-floor, narrow stairwell, or a shop 200 feet from the driveway across soft ground adds 20–35% to the labor hours.

A same-week turnaround on a house that's already sorted is the cheapest version of this job. A house where the crew has to make a judgment call on every single object is the most expensive, and it's the version most families accidentally buy.

Sort First, Schedule Second — the Single Biggest Lever on Price

The most expensive hour we bill is the one where a crew member stands holding a shoebox waiting for someone to decide.

Do these four passes yourself, in this order, before you call anyone:

  • Documents and keys. Tax returns, deeds, titles, insurance policies, savings bonds, safe deposit keys. Colorado savings bonds and uncashed checks turn up in bookshelves constantly — check inside every book with a spine over an inch thick.
  • The obvious valuables. Jewelry, firearms, coin collections, tools. Detached Pueblo West shops routinely hold $2,000–$8,000 in Snap-on, Lincoln welders, and compressors that a cleanout crew will happily haul as scrap if nobody says otherwise.
  • Sentimental items, physically removed from the house. Not "set aside in the corner." In a box, in someone's car.
  • A quick value triage on the rest. Anything you think is worth more than $100, photograph it.

Then call us and point at what's left. That conversation takes fifteen minutes and routinely cuts a quote by $400–$900.

Where This Intersects Colorado Probate

You generally cannot clear out a house you don't yet have authority over. A few numbers worth knowing before you book anything:

  • Colorado's small estate affidavit threshold sits in the low $80,000s and adjusts annually for inflation. Under that, with no real property, you may avoid formal probate entirely — but you must wait 10 days after the death to use it.
  • A house in the estate almost always means formal probate, and the personal representative's Letters Testamentary are what give you standing to dispose of contents.
  • Once notice to creditors is published, known creditors have four months to file claims. Colorado's outside bar is one year from date of death.

If two siblings disagree about the estate and only one has Letters, the crew is going to stop work when the second sibling shows up in the driveway. We've had it happen. Settle it first.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part that costs us jobs, and we'd rather lose the job than the reputation.

Don't hire a cleanout crew if the contents are worth more than the labor. If the house holds genuine antiques, a mid-century furniture collection, a firearms collection, or shop equipment, an estate sale company or auction house is the better call even at 35–50% commission. Rough rule: if the sellable contents would gross over $5,000, the commission still nets you more than we save you. Below about $2,500 gross, an estate sale isn't worth the setup and most Pueblo-area operators won't take it anyway.

Don't hire us for a light, boxed-up, mostly-empty house. If it's two rooms of furniture and 15 boxes, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster at $450–$600 for a 7-day rental and a couple of weekends with family costs less than full-service removal. You're paying us for labor and decision-making; if there's not much of either, we're the wrong tool. Watch the weight cap — most roll-offs include 3–4 tons and overage runs $55–$85 per ton.

Don't assume we can touch hazardous material. We do not do asbestos abatement. Colorado's CDPHE Regulation 8 triggers permitting and licensed abatement at 160 square feet, 260 linear feet, or 35 cubic feet of asbestos-containing material — and vinyl floor tile, sheet flooring adhesive, and duct tape wrap in Pueblo West homes built before the mid-'80s are common culprits. Same for old fuel drums, pesticides, and unlabeled chemicals in the shop. We'll flag them; you'll need a specialist, and that's a separate $1,500–$6,000 conversation.

Don't call a general cleanout crew for a true hoarding or biohazard situation. Animal waste, rodent infestation, or decomposition requires PPE, containment, and different insurance. We do handle these under a separate scope — see [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) — but pricing runs 2–3x a standard estate job and the timeline stretches to 3–5 days. If someone quotes you a standard rate for that sight-unseen, they haven't seen it.

Don't pay for full-service removal on items that sell themselves. Working appliances, decent sectionals, and running lawn equipment move on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo West within 48–96 hours if priced under $150. Hauling a functional fridge costs you; listing it pays you.

The real failure mode, and the one that hurts: something irreplaceable goes in the truck. Once a load is compacted and tipped, it's gone. No crew, ours included, can reconstruct a family's judgment about what mattered. Every cleanout we've ever done where the family was in a hurry and skipped the sort pass ended with at least one phone call about something nobody meant to lose.

Getting an Honest Quote in Pueblo West

Insist on an on-site walkthrough, not a phone estimate, for anything bigger than a single room. Ask specifically whether the quote includes the outbuildings, the fence line, and anything behind the house — on an acre lot, that omission is how a $1,800 job becomes a $3,200 surprise. Ask what happens if the load runs over. Ask who's insured for the second-story hauling.
