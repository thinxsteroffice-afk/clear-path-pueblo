HOW MUCH DOES HOARDER CLEANUP COST

Most hoarder cleanup in Pueblo West lands between $1,200 and $9,500, and the honest midpoint for a typical 1,200–1,600 sq ft house here is $3,400 to $6,200. Light clutter in one or two rooms runs $900–$2,400. A moderate whole-house job — floors visible, no biohazard — runs $2,800–$6,000. Severe cases with blocked exits, a packed garage, and a yard full of stored items run $7,000–$18,000. Priced by the load, expect $375–$750 per 15-cubic-yard truckload in the Pueblo area, or $95–$150 per hour for a two-person crew when sorting is involved. The single biggest variable is not volume — it's whether someone is standing there deciding what stays.

Why the Same Square Footage Costs Wildly Different Amounts

Two houses on the same street in Pueblo West, both 1,400 sq ft, can quote at $2,900 and $11,000. The difference comes down to five things, roughly in order of how much they move the number:

  • Sorting vs. hauling. Pure haul-out — everything goes — moves at about 8–14 cubic yards per crew-hour. Item-by-item sorting with the homeowner present drops that to 2–4 cubic yards per crew-hour. That's a 3x to 5x labor difference on identical volume.
  • Vertical density. Waist-high clutter across a room is maybe 6–8 cubic yards. Chest-high is 14–18. The room didn't get bigger; the price roughly doubled.
  • Disposal mix. General waste at area landfills typically runs $35–$60 per ton (confirm current tipping rates before you budget). Mattresses add $25–$40 each. Refrigerators, freezers, and AC units need refrigerant recovery at $30–$55 each. Tires are usually $8–$20 apiece. Paint, solvents, and old pesticides go to household hazardous waste and can add $150–$400 to a job.
  • Access. A Pueblo West property with a long gravel drive, a locked gate, or a detached shop 150 feet from the curb adds carry distance. Every 100 feet of carry adds roughly 20–30% to labor time on that portion of the load.
  • Structural surprises. Water-damaged subfloor, rodent nesting in insulation, or a collapsed ceiling turns a cleanout into a demolition job — a separate trade and a separate bill.

What Pueblo West Specifically Changes About the Price

National cost calculators inflate two things and ignore one that matters here.

They overstate mold remediation. At roughly 4,700 feet with average relative humidity often in the 30–50% range and about 12 inches of annual precipitation, hoarded homes in Pueblo West develop far less structural mold than the same house would in Houston or Atlanta. Unless there's been an actual water event — burst supply line, failed swamp cooler, roof leak — you can frequently skip the $2,000–$6,000 remediation line item those calculators auto-add.

They understate rodent exposure. Southern Colorado has persistent deer mouse populations, and Colorado consistently reports among the highest hantavirus case counts in the country. A hoarded home with accumulated paper and food packaging is close to ideal deer mouse habitat. That means no dry sweeping, no vacuuming of nests, wetting down with disinfectant first, N95 or better respirators, and disposable coveralls. Budget $120–$300 in PPE and disinfectant consumables for a moderate job, plus slower work. Any contractor who walks into a rodent-active hoarded home and starts dry-sweeping is telling you something about how they'll handle the rest of it.

And the lot size here is the thing out-of-town estimators miss entirely. A lot of Pueblo West parcels run a half-acre to a full acre. Hoarding rarely stops at the front door — it moves to the yard, the carport, the shed, and the two non-running vehicles. We've had jobs where the interior was $3,100 and the exterior was $4,800. If you're getting a quote over the phone, walk the whole property with your camera, not just the living room. Real Price Bands, With What's Actually In Them

  • Level 1–2 (cluttered but functional), 1–3 rooms: $900–$2,400. Usually 1 crew day, 2–4 truckloads.
  • Level 3 (one blocked room, some odor, heavy surface clutter), whole house: $2,800–$6,000. Typically 2–3 crew days, 6–11 loads.
  • Level 4 (multiple unusable rooms, structural clutter, rodent activity): $6,500–$12,000. 3–5 crew days, 12–20 loads, PPE protocol throughout.
  • Level 5 (blocked egress, no working utilities, biohazard present): $11,000–$25,000+ and usually requires a licensed biohazard remediation firm, not a junk removal company.
  • Add-ons that surprise people: deep clean after haul-out $400–$900; carpet and pad removal $1.10–$2.25 per sq ft; dumpster rental if you're self-performing $350–$650 for a 20-yard with a 2–4 ton limit.

> If a company quotes a hoarded house sight-unseen over the phone, the number is a lead-generation number. Nobody who has actually done this work will commit to a price without walking the property.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Call

This is the part most cleanup pages leave out, and it's the part that saves people money.

Don't hire anyone if the person living there hasn't agreed. Forced cleanouts of an occupied home — adult children hiring a crew while a parent is at a doctor's appointment — fail more often than they succeed. The relapse is fast, the trust damage is permanent, and you've spent $5,000 to make the situation worse. Hoarding disorder is a diagnosed mental health condition; the clutter is a symptom. If there's no treatment plan and no buy-in from the person, our honest advice is to spend $150–$200 on a session with a therapist who treats hoarding disorder before you spend $5,000 on a truck. Untreated, re-accumulation to prior levels within a few years is common — we've been called back to the same address more than once.

Don't hire us if there's biohazard. Human or animal waste beyond incidental, decomposition, needles, or a death in the home is not junk removal. That's a licensed biohazard remediation firm with different insurance, different training, and different disposal chains. We will tell you that on the walkthrough and refer you out rather than take the job. Expect that work to cost 2–4x what standard cleanup does.

Don't hire us if the math doesn't work. If the property is headed to a teardown or a cash-as-is investor sale, a full cleanout may be wasted money. Investors in the Pueblo market routinely buy occupied-condition properties and discount $8,000–$20,000 for cleanout. If your cleanout quote is $12,000 and the discount is $10,000, you're paying $2,000 for the privilege of doing the work yourself. Get one as-is offer before you book anything.

Don't hire us if you have time, a truck, and a strong back. A determined family with a pickup, four weekends, and $600 in landfill fees can clear a Level 2 house. You'll spend roughly 45–60 hours doing it. If your time is worth less than about $40/hour and the emotional load is manageable, DIY genuinely wins. What you're buying from us is speed, disposal logistics, and not being the person who has to throw away your mother's things.

Where phased cleanup costs more. Splitting a job into three visits over two months adds roughly 15–30% to the total, because we're paying mobilization, staging, and re-assessment three times instead of once. Sometimes that's still the right call — it can be the only way a resident tolerates the process. Just go in knowing you're paying a premium for pacing.

How to Get an Accurate Quote Instead of a Range

  • Insist on an on-site walkthrough. Twenty minutes on the property is worth more than an hour on the phone. It should be free.
  • Ask what's excluded. Get disposal fees, hazardous materials, appliances with refrigerant, and post-haul cleaning stated as line items, not folded into a vague "plus dump fees."
  • Ask for proof of insurance, specifically general liability with the property listed. A crew injured in your hallway is your problem otherwise.
  • Ask what happens to usable items. We route serviceable furniture, appliances, and clothing to local donation rather than the landfill where it makes sense — it lowers tonnage and sometimes gives the resident a reason to let go of an item.
  • Get the estimate as a not-to-exceed number with a written change-order trigger, not an open-ended hourly rate.
