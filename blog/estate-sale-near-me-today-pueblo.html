ESTATE SALE NEAR ME TODAY: PUEBLO

If you're looking for an estate sale near me today in Pueblo West, check three places in this order: EstateSales.net and EstateSales.org filtered to ZIP 81007 and a 25-mile radius, the Pueblo Chieftain classifieds, and the Pueblo/Pueblo West buy-sell-trade groups on Facebook. Most Pueblo West estate sales run Friday through Sunday, 8 a.m. to 2 p.m., and the furniture and tools are usually gone by 9:30 a.m. on day one. If today is a Tuesday, there is likely nothing open within 20 miles — new listings post Wednesday and Thursday night. Colorado Springs, 45 minutes north, runs 8–15 sales most weekends versus 1–3 in the entire Pueblo West area.

That's the shopper's answer. The rest of this page is for the other side of the search — the family that owns the house.

Why Pueblo West has so few sales to find

Pueblo West is a Metro District, not a city, and most of it is one-acre-plus lots off Purcell, McCulloch, Joe Martinez, and Platteville. That geography changes the economics of an estate sale in ways that Denver-focused advice never mentions.

Professional estate sale companies take 30% to 45% of gross, and almost none of them will book a sale projected under $5,000 in gross revenue — the staffing math doesn't work. Pueblo's two or three active companies mostly work the city core and Belmont. Getting one to drive out past the Highway 50 split for a sale expected to gross $3,200 is a hard sell, and several will quote a $500–$1,500 minimum guarantee to make the trip worth it.

That leaves most Pueblo West families running the sale themselves, or skipping it.

What a Pueblo West estate sale actually grosses

Real numbers from the kind of houses out here — 1,600 to 2,400 square feet, built between 1978 and 2004, two-car garage, a shed or two, and a 1-acre lot:

  • Full-house sale, professionally run: $4,000–$12,000 gross. After a 35% commission and roughly $150–$400 in advertising and permits, the family clears $2,400–$7,600.
  • Self-run weekend sale: $1,500–$5,000, and it costs you three to five full days of pricing, staging, sitting in a driveway, and negotiating over a $4 casserole dish.
  • Tools and shop equipment: the single best category in Pueblo West. A decent Craftsman rolling chest, a welder, an air compressor, and a chop saw can be $800–$2,000 of the total by themselves.
  • Brown furniture (china cabinets, formal dining sets, entertainment centers): $0–$75 each, if it sells at all. This is the category families overestimate by the widest margin.
  • Outdoor furniture and patio sets: high-desert sun at 4,700 feet destroys resin and cushion fabric in 4–6 years. Expect $10–$40 a set, not the $200 you paid.

> The sale is not the hard part. The 60% to 70% of the house that doesn't sell is the hard part, and nobody budgets for it.

The Monday-after problem

Here is what nobody tells you before the sale: by Sunday at 2 p.m., the buyers are gone and the house still has most of its volume in it. Not its *value* — its *volume*. Mattresses, box springs, particleboard bookcases, twelve boxes of paperwork, a 1990s console TV, the chest freezer in the garage that nobody wants because it's 28 years old, and every single thing in the shed.

If you have a closing date, a renter moving in, or a family member driving back to Phoenix on Monday, that gap becomes an emergency. We get called for it constantly, usually on a Sunday afternoon with a Tuesday deadline.

Typical post-sale volume for a Pueblo West property: 1.5 to 3 trailer loads, or roughly 12–24 cubic yards. Add a shed and it's more. Our [estate cleanout service in Pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) is built around that specific window — we'll schedule the truck for the Monday or Tuesday after the sale and clear whatever's standing, no sorting required from you.

When an estate sale — and hiring us — is the wrong call

This is the part that costs us work, so read it carefully.

Skip the estate sale entirely if the house contents are worth under roughly $2,500. You will spend 30–40 hours of your own labor plus $200+ in signage, tables, and change to gross maybe $1,800. If you value your time at even $25/hour, you lost money. Donate the good items to the Pueblo Rescue Mission or Goodwill on Highway 50, take the receipt, and hire one clearout. The tax deduction on a documented $1,500 donation is often worth more to a family in the 22% bracket than the sale would have netted after the hassle.

Do not hire us if you have time and a truck. A Pueblo West resident with a half-ton pickup and two free Saturdays can haul 4–6 loads to the county landfill for tipping fees in the $25–$60 per load range plus fuel. That's maybe $250 total versus $1,200 to us. We are selling you speed and backs, not access. If you're not on a deadline and you're physically able, you don't need us.

Do not hire anyone before the will is settled. If the estate is in probate and the personal representative hasn't been appointed, hauling the contents can create real legal exposure for whoever authorized it. We've turned down two jobs in the last year for this reason. Get the letters testamentary first. A two-week delay is cheaper than a sibling lawsuit.

We are the wrong call if you need appraisal or resale. We are a junk removal outfit. We do not price antiques, we do not run the sale, and we do not consign. If Grandpa's shop has pre-1960 hand tools, a gun safe, or a coin collection, get an appraiser before anyone with a trailer walks through. We have watched families throw away items an appraiser would have valued at $3,000+. Sell first, call us second — that order matters and it costs us volume to say so.

Scheduling limits, plainly stated. Same-day service in Pueblo West is realistic maybe 60% of the time and almost never on Saturdays between May and September, our busiest stretch. If you tell us Friday afternoon that you need a full house empty by Saturday noon, we will probably say no rather than say yes and fail. Rural properties past the Metro District boundary, toward Colorado City or out by the reservoir, may carry a $40–$75 trip charge.

What we can't take at all: paint and solvents, propane tanks, ammunition, asbestos-containing material (common in Pueblo-area homes built before 1980), and medical sharps. Those need Pueblo County household hazardous waste drop-off, and pretending otherwise would just get our load rejected at the gate.

A realistic three-week sequence

1. Week 1 — Legal and valuables. Confirm authority to dispose. Pull documents, photos, jewelry, firearms, and anything that looks old. Get an appraiser out if you're unsure. 2. Week 2 — Book the sale or decide against it. Call estate sale companies by Monday; the good ones book 3–6 weeks out. Listings should post to EstateSales.net by Wednesday for a weekend sale. 3. Week 3 — Sale Friday through Sunday, clearout Monday or Tuesday. Schedule the haul *before* the sale, not after. Our calendar for Mondays fills by the prior Thursday.
