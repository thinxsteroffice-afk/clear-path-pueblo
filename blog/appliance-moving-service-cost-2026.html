APPLIANCE MOVING SERVICE COST 2026

Moving a single appliance in Pueblo West runs $75 to $175 for most jobs, and that's the number to plan around. A washer, dryer, dishwasher, or standard fridge moved from inside the house to a truck, a garage, or a new spot in the same home lands in that band. Add a second appliance and expect $50 to $90 more, not another full price. Stairs, a basement, or a hallway that needs a door removed pushes a single unit toward $200 to $250. If you want the old unit hauled away instead of relocated, disposal is usually $85 to $150 including the Pueblo County landfill fee. Same-day service in the 81007 area typically adds $25 to $40.

What You Actually Pay, Line by Line

Pricing for appliance moving splits into three things: the labor to get the unit out of where it sits, the difficulty of the path it travels, and whether it's going onto a truck or across a room.

Here's how the common jobs price out in Pueblo West:

  • Washer or dryer, ground floor to garage or truck: $75 to $110. Two people, 20 to 30 minutes on site.
  • Standard top-freezer refrigerator, kitchen to garage: $95 to $140. Doors usually stay on.
  • French-door or counter-depth refrigerator: $130 to $200. These run 28 to 36 inches deep and almost always need doors pulled off both the fridge and the doorway.
  • Electric range or wall oven: $85 to $130 for the range, $150 to $225 for a wall oven, because the oven has to come out of a cabinet cutout without wrecking the face frame.
  • Chest freezer, basement to ground level: $140 to $220. The stair carry is the whole cost.

Multi-appliance jobs get cheaper per unit. A full kitchen set — fridge, range, dishwasher, microwave — moved out during a remodel runs $275 to $425 as a package instead of the $400-plus you'd pay buying them one at a time.

Why Pueblo West Costs More Than Downtown Pueblo

This is the part almost nobody writing about appliance moving costs will tell you, because most of those articles are written for a national audience with no idea what a five-acre lot does to a job.

Pueblo West is spread out. A truck running from the Highway 50 corridor out to the far side of McCulloch or down toward Purcell burns 20 to 35 minutes of drive time each way that a downtown Pueblo job doesn't. Most local outfits either build that into a slightly higher base rate or charge a trip fee of $20 to $45 past a certain radius. Ask which one you're getting, because a company quoting "$65 base" with a $45 trip fee is more expensive than one quoting a flat $95.

Two more Pueblo West specifics that move the number:

  • Gravel and dirt driveways. A 150-foot unpaved driveway with a fridge on an appliance dolly is a genuinely different job than 30 feet of concrete. Wheels sink. Expect $25 to $50 more, or plan to have the appliance staged at the end of the drive before the crew arrives.
  • Detached garages and outbuildings. A lot of properties out here store the old chest freezer or spare fridge in a shop 80 feet from the house. That carry distance is real labor and it's the single most common reason a quote comes in above what someone expected.

> The quote you get over the phone is only as good as the details you gave. Distance from the truck to the appliance is the number most people forget to mention, and it's the one that changes the price most.

Moving vs. Hauling Away: Two Different Prices

People search for "moving appliances service cost" meaning two different things, and they price differently.

Relocation means the appliance survives the trip and works at the other end. You're paying for careful handling, blankets, straps, and someone who won't kink the copper line on the ice maker. Budget $75 to $175 per unit as above.

Removal means it's dead and you want it gone. That's a haul-away job, and it's priced on disposal cost plus labor. In Pueblo County, appliance disposal at the landfill runs roughly $15 to $30 per unit, more if it contains refrigerant that has to be recovered by a certified tech before the metal can be scrapped. Full details on that service are on our [appliance removal Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) page.

The refrigerant piece matters. Any fridge, freezer, window AC unit, or dehumidifier built before roughly 2010 likely contains R-134a or R-22. Federal law requires certified recovery before disposal. That's why a working fridge relocation might cost $120 and a dead fridge haul-away costs $135 — the disposal side has a compliance cost the relocation side doesn't.

When This Is Not Worth Paying For

Here is the section that costs us jobs. Read it anyway.

Don't hire anyone to move a single washer 15 feet on a flat concrete garage floor. An appliance dolly rents for $15 to $25 a day at a Pueblo rental yard, and moving a 180-pound washer across level concrete with one other person takes about ten minutes. Paying $85 for that is paying for convenience, and you should know that's what you're buying.

Don't pay for relocation on an appliance worth less than the move. A 14-year-old top-load washer is worth $40 to $90 used in this market. Spending $110 to move it to a rental property is a bad trade. Sell it on Facebook Marketplace for $75, have the buyer haul it, and buy a used one closer to the destination.

Don't hire a junk removal crew for a job that needs a licensed trade. This is the real limitation. We move appliances. We do not:

  • Disconnect or reconnect a gas line. A gas range or gas dryer needs a licensed plumber or a qualified appliance tech to shut the valve, cap it, and test for leaks. That's a $90 to $180 service call on its own and it is not optional. Black Hills Energy and Colorado plumbing code are both clear on this.
  • Do permanent water supply or drain work. Pulling a dishwasher out means disconnecting a water line and a drain hose. Most crews will do a straightforward disconnect on a shutoff valve that actually shuts off. A corroded 1997 valve that weeps when you touch it is a plumber's problem, not ours, and pretending otherwise floods a kitchen.
  • Guarantee your appliance still works after a move. Compressors in old fridges can fail after being tilted and moved. Reputable crews carry insurance for damage they cause, not for a 16-year-old sealed system that gave out. Nobody can insure against that. If your fridge is on its last legs, moving it may be the event that ends it.
  • Repair drywall, flooring, or trim. Getting a 36-inch fridge through a 32-inch opening sometimes means removing a door and its casing. Good crews minimize this and tell you before they do it. But a scuffed doorjamb on a tight carry is a realistic outcome, not a failure.

If you're already hiring movers, don't hire us separately. A full-service moving company handling your whole household will include appliances in the base estimate. Paying a second company $150 for the fridge when it's already on the mover's manifest is money burned. Where a junk crew makes sense is the opposite case: you're not moving everything, you just need three heavy things relocated or gone.

If your timeline is flexible and your budget is tight, appliance retailers often beat us on price. Lowe's, Home Depot, and Best Buy typically charge $25 to $50 to haul the old unit away when they deliver the new one, and sometimes zero on a promotion. That's less than any independent haul-away price, us included. The catch is they'll only take the appliance the new one replaces, it has to be disconnected and accessible, and they won't touch anything in a basement or a detached shop.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in One Call

Most bad quotes come from bad information, not bad intent. Have this ready:

  • Appliance type and rough age. "2019 LG front-load washer" is useful. "A washer" is not.
  • Where it sits and where it's going. Include the floor, the number of stairs, and the tightest doorway on the path.
  • Distance from the appliance to where a truck can park. Measure it if the answer is more than 40 feet.
  • Whether it's disconnected already. An appliance that's unplugged, drained, and pulled from the wall saves 15 to 25 minutes of labor, which is real money on an hourly-influenced quote.
  • Gas or electric. This determines whether you need a second trade before anyone shows up.

A crew that gives you a firm number on that information and holds to it is doing this right. A crew that quotes $65 sight-unseen and then finds $80 of add-ons in your driveway is not.

What Else Usually Goes With It

Appliance moves rarely happen alone. If you're clearing a kitchen for a remodel or emptying a house, the appliance is one item on a longer list, and bundling drops the per-item cost meaningfully. A crew already on site with a truck and two people will take the old couch for $50 to $75 instead of the $95 to $150 it would cost as a standalone [furniture removal Pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) trip.

The same logic applies to a [garage cleanout Pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) job where the dead chest freezer in the corner is what prompted the call in the first place. One trip, one trip charge, one labor mobilization. Combining a $120 appliance move with a $350 garage cleanout typically lands around $400 to $430, not $470.
