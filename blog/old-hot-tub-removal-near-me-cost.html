OLD HOT TUB REMOVAL NEAR ME: COST

Most old hot tubs in Pueblo West can be drained, cut apart, and hauled off in a single visit for $300 to $650, and the whole job takes a two-person crew about 45 to 90 minutes once the water is out. Price moves with three things: whether a truck and trailer can get within 40 feet of the tub, whether the shell is full-foam insulated, and whether a fence panel or deck section has to come off first. Draining is the slow part, not the demolition. A 400-gallon tub takes roughly 20 to 25 minutes with a 1,500 GPH submersible pump and 90 minutes or more on the built-in drain valve. Book it after the water is already gone and you will usually pay the low end of that range.

What an Old Hot Tub Actually Weighs

Homeowners consistently underestimate this, and it is the reason "I'll just get some buddies and a trailer" goes sideways.

A standard 7' x 7' acrylic spa weighs 700 to 900 pounds dry. Fill it and you add 400 gallons of water at 8.34 pounds per gallon, about 3,300 pounds. A loaded tub sitting on a wood deck is carrying nearly two tons over a handful of joists.

The dry weight varies more than people expect:

  • Thermal-blanket insulated tubs (open cavity, foil-faced wrap) run lighter, often 600 to 750 pounds, and cut apart fast.
  • Full-foam tubs have every inch of the cabinet cavity packed with expanded urethane. Same footprint, but 950 to 1,300 pounds, and the foam bonds to the plumbing so every cut fights you.
  • Rotomolded and older 1980s fiberglass shells are heavy and brittle. They shatter rather than slice.
  • Soft-side inflatable tubs weigh 60 to 90 pounds and fold into a pickup bed. Nobody should be paying for removal of one of these.

Cut into six to eight pieces and stacked, a typical tub occupies 3 to 4 cubic yards of truck space. That is why a hot tub often prices similarly to a small [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) even though it is one object.

The Electrical Step Almost Everyone Skips

A hard-wired spa in Pueblo West is fed by a 240-volt, 50-amp circuit through a GFCI disconnect box, usually mounted on a post 5 to 10 feet from the tub. Cutting into a tub without killing that circuit is the single most dangerous part of this job.

Turn the breaker off at the main panel, not just at the spa disconnect. Then verify with a meter. After the tub is gone, you are left with a live-capable 50-amp feed terminating in a box in your yard. That circuit needs to be either properly capped and labeled or removed back to the panel. An electrician in the Pueblo area typically charges $125 to $250 for that visit, and Pueblo Regional Building Department, which handles permitting for unincorporated Pueblo West, is the office to call if you are unsure whether your specific disconnect removal needs a permit. A junk hauler is not a licensed electrician, and any company that tells you otherwise is a company to hang up on.

Where the Water Goes Matters Here

This is the Pueblo West detail that generic national junk-removal pages never mention: a large share of properties out here run on septic systems with leach fields, especially on the older one-acre lots north and west of McCulloch.

Dumping 400 gallons in 25 minutes into a laundry standpipe or floor drain that feeds a septic tank is a hydraulic overload. It pushes solids into the leach field and can cost you $4,000 to $12,000 in field replacement. Never drain a spa indoors on septic.

Drain it out onto gravel or bare ground instead, and take two precautions first:

  • Stop adding chlorine or bromine at least 5 to 7 days before draining, and let the reading fall below 0.5 ppm. Untreated spa water at 3 ppm will kill turf and stress ornamentals.
  • Run the hose 20 or more feet away from foundations and away from any downslope neighbor. On the caliche-heavy soil common in parts of the district, 400 gallons does not percolate quickly. It ponds and it migrates.

> Four hundred gallons is not a puddle. It is roughly what a Pueblo West household uses indoors in three days, released in under half an hour, in one spot.

When Hot Tub Removal Is Not Worth Paying For

This section will cost us jobs. Read it anyway.

Do not pay us if the tub still runs. A functioning spa from 2016 or newer, with an intact cover and no leaks, is worth $1,200 to $2,500 on the local resale market and moves in a week or two in spring. Even a tired but operational 2010-era tub will find a free-to-hauler taker. You will get no-shows, and you will need a firm rule that the taker brings four people and their own trailer, but selling it beats paying to destroy it. We would rather you keep the money.

Do not pay us if you can access it with a trailer and you have three able bodies. A drained, thermal-blanket tub on a concrete pad with a gate wide enough for a trailer is a genuine DIY job. Budget 2 to 4 hours, a reciprocating saw, 3 to 5 bi-metal 9-inch blades (the foam and fiberglass eat them), safety glasses, and a respirator for the acrylic and fiberglass dust. Your only real cost is the disposal ticket, commonly a $30 to $60 minimum load fee at a Pueblo County transfer facility. You will save around $400.

Do not pay for a separate trip if you have other work queued. If the tub is going out alongside a deck, a shed, or a pool, bundling into one visit avoids a second mobilization charge. See [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) and [above-ground pool removal](/services/above-ground-pool-removal-pueblo).

Do not expect scrap value to offset anything. The steel frame, pump motors, and heater element together scrap for roughly $18 to $45 at current prices. Stripping them takes an hour. The acrylic shell, the urethane foam, and the PVC plumbing are landfill material, full stop. No one in southern Colorado recycles spa shells, and anybody advertising "eco-friendly hot tub recycling" is describing the metal only.

Do not assume the tub is the last of it. Common surprises after removal:

  • The concrete pad stays. A 8' x 8' x 4" slab is about 2,400 pounds of concrete and is a separate job. That falls under [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • The grass underneath is dead, not dormant. Nothing has photosynthesized under that footprint in years. Plan on reseeding or hardscaping.
  • Deck framing under a tub is often rot-damaged, and we do not do carpentry repair. You will need a contractor afterward.
  • Buried conduit or a gas line to a heater can run under the pad. We stop and call you rather than guess.

Cases where we may decline outright: a tub on a second-story deck with no crane access, a tub inside a finished basement or sunroom with a doorway under 34 inches and no removable wall, or a full-foam tub in a backyard reachable only through a 30-inch side gate on a steep grade. Those become crane or partial-demolition jobs at $500 to $1,500 on top, and it is more honest to tell you that up front than to show up and renegotiate in your driveway.

Two Pueblo West Conditions That Change the Timeline

Winter freeze. Overnight lows here regularly sit near 15°F in January, and an abandoned tub still holding water will crack the shell, split the pump housing, and fracture PVC unions. That does not raise the removal price much, but it destroys any resale value and turns a $1,500 asset into a $450 expense. If you have decided the tub is done, get it drained before the first hard freeze, typically late October.

Wind. Cut shell sections are large, curved, and light. Sustained spring winds across open lots will move a 4-foot acrylic panel into your neighbor's fence overnight. If you cut it yourself, do not leave the pieces sitting until the weekend. Load them the same day or weight them down.

One more local advantage worth naming: Pueblo West lots are big, frequently a third of an acre up to a full acre, with side yards and alley access that Denver and Colorado Springs backyards simply do not have. That access is why removal here often lands $100 to $200 under what the same tub would cost in a dense metro neighborhood. Good access is real money, and most homeowners out here already have it.

Booking It Without Overpaying

Get a firm quote, not a range, before anyone touches the tub. Any honest hauler can price it from four photos: the tub from the front, the cabinet side panel removed if possible, the path from the tub to the driveway, and the gate or opening at its narrowest point. Add the make and approximate year off the label inside the cabinet door.

Ask three questions. Is the disconnect box and wiring included or excluded? Is the concrete pad included or excluded? Is disposal included in the quote or billed separately at the scale? Vague answers on any of those are where surprise charges come from.
