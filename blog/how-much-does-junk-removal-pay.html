HOW MUCH DOES JUNK REMOVAL PAY?

Crew pay for junk removal in Pueblo West runs $17–$22 an hour to start, or roughly $35,000–$46,000 a year full-time, with cash tips on residential jobs adding $30–$80 on a good day. Lead drivers who can run a truck solo and quote on-site earn $23–$28 an hour. If you're asking as an owner instead of an employee, a single truck in this market grosses $400–$900 on a booked day and nets 25%–40% after fuel, landfill fees, labor, and insurance — meaning a one-truck operation that stays busy clears $45,000–$85,000 a year for the owner, not the $200,000 the YouTube videos promise. Below is the actual math, with Pueblo West's drive times and dump costs built in.

The three numbers people are actually asking for

"How much does junk removal pay" gets typed by three different people, and the answers aren't close:

  • A job seeker: $17–$22/hr entry, $23–$28/hr as a lead, plus tips. No CDL required for a standard dump trailer or 12–16 ft box truck under 26,001 lbs GVWR.
  • A side-hustler with a pickup: $150–$400 per job gross, but 1–2 jobs a weekend is realistic, so $800–$2,400 a month gross before the dump bill.
  • An owner building a real company: revenue per truck per year of $110,000–$200,000 in a market this size, with owner take-home landing well under that.

The gap between gross and take-home is where almost every estimate online goes wrong. The landfill doesn't care what you charged.

What a crew member earns here, line by line

Colorado's state minimum wage sits just above $15/hour, and Pueblo County has no separate local minimum, so junk removal starts about $2–$7 above the floor. That premium exists because the work is genuinely hard and the turnover is high.

A realistic full-time week for a Pueblo West crew member:

  • 40–45 scheduled hours, Monday–Saturday, with Saturdays common April through September
  • 6,000–14,000 lbs lifted per day across 2–4 jobs
  • Tips on roughly 1 in 3 residential jobs, typically $20–$40, occasionally $100 on an [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo)
  • $0 in tips on commercial, foreclosure, and construction work — those are invoiced, and nobody tips an invoice
  • Overtime past 40 hours at 1.5x, which in Colorado also kicks in past 12 hours in a single day

Add it up: a lead at $25/hr with 4 hours of weekly overtime and average tips lands near $58,000–$62,000 annually. That's a legitimate number for someone with no degree, no license beyond a clean MVR, and two years of experience. It is also close to the ceiling unless you move into dispatch, sales, or ownership.

What an owner grosses — and what's left

Here is a real-shaped job: a full garage cleanout in Pueblo West, priced at $575 for a three-quarter trailer load.

  • Landfill tipping: 2,400 lbs at roughly $45–$70 per ton = $54–$84
  • Fuel: 34 miles round trip from a Pueblo West job to the landfill and back to base, at 11 mpg = $14–$18
  • Labor: two people, 2.5 hours on site plus 1 hour driving and dumping = 7 man-hours at $20 = $140
  • Payroll burden (workers' comp for hauling classifications runs $8–$14 per $100 of payroll, plus FICA and unemployment): add 35%–45% to that labor number = $189–$203 all-in
  • Truck, trailer, insurance, phone, software, ads: allocate $120–$160 per job at 3 jobs a day

Net on a $575 job: roughly $190–$225. That's a 33%–39% margin, which is healthy. The problem is volume. In Pueblo West, with lots spread across 5-acre parcels and a 15–25 minute run to the transfer point, three jobs a day is a full schedule — not the six a Denver crew can stack in a two-mile radius. Multiply $200 by 3 jobs by 5.5 days by 46 productive weeks and you get about $150,000 — before the owner pays themselves, replaces a $6,000 transmission, or covers the two weeks in February when the phone rings four times total.

> The honest version: junk removal pays a solid working wage reliably, and pays an ownership income only after you've survived two winters and bought the second truck.

Why Pueblo West pay differs from Colorado Springs or Denver

Same trade, different economics, and it cuts both ways.

Against you: density. Fewer jobs per mile means more unpaid windshield time. A Front Range operator bills 5.5 hours out of a 9-hour day; out here it's closer to 4.5. That alone is a 15%–20% revenue haircut per truck.

For you: the loads are bigger and the competition is thinner. Pueblo West properties come with outbuildings, corral panels, decades of accumulated yard iron, and a lot of things that require equipment rather than a strong back. A [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) is a $350–$650 job that takes 90 minutes. A [shed demolition](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) runs $600–$1,800. Add [skid steer work](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) and the per-hour billing roughly doubles, because you're renting expertise and iron, not just arms. Operators who can run a machine safely are paid $28–$35/hr here, and there are not many of them.

Also relevant: our dry climate makes yard debris deceptively light. A trailer stacked eight feet high with juniper and tumbleweed might weigh 900 lbs. You get paid for volume and pay the dump for weight, which is why [yard debris jobs](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) are among the better-margin work in Pueblo West — and why hauling wet drywall, tile, or dirt is among the worst.

When this is not worth it — read this part twice

We'd rather you not take the job or start the truck than quit in five weeks. Plainly:

  • If your back or knees are already compromised, don't. This is 200+ lifts a day, at awkward angles, on stairs, in 96° July heat and 18° January mornings. A mattress is 70 lbs of nothing to grab. A cast iron tub is 300 lbs on a stair turn. There is no light-duty version of this job.
  • If you need $60,000 in year one, this is the wrong trade. Entry pay is $17–$22. You get to $28 with two years and a proven safety record, or you don't.
  • If you're starting a truck with under $12,000 in reserves, the math will beat you. A used dump trailer is $6,000–$11,000, commercial auto insurance for a hauling operation runs $2,400–$5,500 a year, and general liability adds $600–$1,500. Then Q1 arrives: January and February typically run 40%–55% below the June peak in this market. Most new operators die in that window, not from lack of skill.
  • If you can't handle unpleasant conditions, skip [hoarding work](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) entirely. It pays the best per hour — sometimes $2,500–$8,000 per project — because of rodent waste, ammonia, needles, and grief-stricken families. Some crew members are simply not suited to it, and pretending otherwise gets someone hurt.
  • If you're a homeowner reading this to decide whether to DIY: a trailer rental at $70–$110 a day plus your own dump fees will genuinely beat hiring us on a single light load. Do that. Where DIY stops making sense is at roughly 2,000+ lbs, anything requiring two people on a staircase, or anything the landfill charges a special rate for.

Two more limitations worth naming. Tips are not income you can budget — a slow week produces zero. And weather takes real days off the calendar: a hard freeze or a March blizzard can cancel 3–6 workdays a month in winter, and hourly crews don't get paid for a canceled Tuesday unless the company guarantees hours in writing. Ask that question before you accept an offer anywhere.

What actually raises your number

Ranked by how much they move pay in this market:

1. Learn to operate a skid steer and mini excavator — the single largest jump, $6–$10/hr 2. Learn to quote on-site accurately — leads who don't underbid are worth $200+ a day to the company 3. Keep a clean MVR — one at-fault accident can price you out of commercial auto coverage entirely 4. Get comfortable with demolition — decks, fences, above-ground pools, and outbuildings bill 40%–70% higher per hour than loading loose junk

Straight talk before you apply or start

Junk removal in Pueblo West pays a dependable working-class wage, faster than most trades, with no schooling required — and it takes payment out of your body. Owning is a real business with real margins, and it is closer to 60 hours a week for two years than to passive income. Anyone telling you a truck prints $200,000 is describing a five-truck operation with a dispatcher, and leaving out the four winters it took to get there.
