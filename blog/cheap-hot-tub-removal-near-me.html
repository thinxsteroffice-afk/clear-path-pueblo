CHEAP HOT TUB REMOVAL NEAR ME

Cheap hot tub removal in Pueblo West runs $250 to $550 for a standard 6-to-8-person acrylic spa on a concrete pad with clear access. ClearPath Junk Removal charges a flat quote in that range, usually same-week, and the whole job takes 90 minutes to 3 hours. If your spa sits behind a 36-inch gate, on a second-story deck, or has a rotted wood cabinet full of standing water, expect $600 to $1,100 instead. Anything under $200 in this market is either a bait quote that grows on site, or someone hauling it to an arroyo off Pueblo Blvd. and leaving it there. Below is what actually drives the number, how to read the reviews you're about to Google, and the four situations where you should not hire anyone at all.

What "Cheap" Actually Costs in Pueblo West, By Job Type

Real quoted ranges from this market, 2025–2026:

  • Standard acrylic spa, ground level, concrete pad, 10+ ft of clear path: $250–$450. Two people, sawzall, roughly 2 hours.
  • Same spa but through a side gate under 42 inches wide: $400–$650. The cabinet gets cut into 4–6 sections, which adds 45–60 minutes.
  • Deck-surrounded or partially sunken spa: $650–$1,100. Deck boards come up, and someone has to eat the cost of putting them back or not.
  • Softside / inflatable spa (Intex, Coleman): $95–$175. Honestly, drain it and cut it up yourself in an afternoon.
  • Swim spa, 12–19 ft: $900–$1,800. These weigh 900–1,400 lbs dry and often need a trailer plus four people.

The line item nobody quotes upfront is disposal. A dry, cut-up hot tub is 30–50 cubic feet of C&D waste. Pueblo County landfill tipping runs in the neighborhood of $40–$65 per ton with minimums, and the fiberglass shell, ABS backing, and closed-cell foam are all non-recyclable in this county. That $60–$110 in dump fees is inside an honest flat quote and mysteriously outside a $149 one.

Why the $149 Quote Turns Into $475

The pattern is consistent enough that you can predict it. A low phone quote is priced against a photo, not a site. Then on arrival:

  • Water still in the shell. A 400-gallon spa holds about 3,300 lbs of water. Nobody moves that. Draining through a garden hose siphon takes 45–90 minutes, and that time gets billed.
  • Access is worse than the photo showed. Six feet of dirt side yard, a swamp cooler line at head height, a gate post that doesn't come out.
  • The pad is glued. Some installs get set into mortar or have anchors drilled into the pad, adding 30–40 minutes of chipping.
  • Electrical is live. See the next section — this is the one that actually stops jobs.

> A flat quote you can hold someone to is worth more than a low quote you can't. Get the number in writing with the words "includes disposal, labor, and cut-down" in it, or assume it doesn't.

How to Read Hot Tub Removal Reviews Without Getting Fooled

Searching "reviews" is the right instinct, but star ratings on junk removal are close to useless because almost every job is a 5 or a 1. Sort by the 3-star reviews — those are the honest ones. Then look for these specifics:

  • Does the review name a final price? "Great service, fair price" is noise. "Quoted $325, paid $325" is signal.
  • Does it mention the yard afterward? Screws, staples, and foam pellets in gravel are the #1 complaint that shows up two weeks later. Foam insulation shatters into thousands of pieces the moment a sawzall touches it.
  • Date density. Twelve 5-star reviews inside nine days is a purchased batch. Reviews that trickle in 2–4 per month over two years are real.
  • Photos in reviews. Homeowners who post an after-photo of a clean pad were happy for a reason worth trusting.
  • The company's replies to bad reviews. A reply that explains the change order ("gate was 34 inches, we added $120, customer approved before cutting") tells you their pricing is at least legible.

One local wrinkle: Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County with a lot of half-acre and one-acre lots, so a big share of reviews mention easy access and low prices. Do not use a Desert Cove Dr. review to predict your price if you're in a newer subdivision with a 5-foot side yard and a block wall.

The Electrical Step 90% of Cheap Quotes Skip

A 240V hot tub is hardwired to a GFCI spa panel on a 50- or 60-amp breaker. Junk removal crews are not electricians and, in Colorado, cutting into that whip is outside our lane. The right sequence:

1. Kill the breaker at the main panel and the spa disconnect. 2. Verify dead with a meter — not by pushing the spa's power button. 3. Have a licensed electrician disconnect the whip and cap or remove the spa panel. Budget $150–$300 for a one-hour call in Pueblo County. 4. If you're not replacing the spa, ask the electrician to pull a permit and properly abandon the circuit. Pueblo Regional Building Department permit fees on a small electrical job typically land around $65–$100.

Removal crews who "just snip it" are creating a live, capped 240V line in a wet outdoor box. It'll pass a walkthrough and fail an inspection. If you're selling the house in the next 18 months, this shows up.

When Hot Tub Removal Is NOT Worth It — Read This Before You Book

We'd rather lose the job than have you spend $400 on the wrong thing. Skip hiring us in these cases:

Your spa is under 8 years old and actually runs. A working 2019-or-newer spa has real resale value — $800 to $2,500 on Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo/Colorado Springs corridor. Listing it "free, you haul" moves it in under 72 hours in the spring, and the buyer does the labor. You've turned a $400 expense into $0, or into money. The tradeoff is real: you're coordinating strangers with a trailer, some will no-show, and it may sit for three weeks. If you need the pad clear by Friday, that's not a plan.

It's an inflatable or softside. Paying anyone $150 to remove an Intex is a bad trade. Drain it, cut it into 3-foot squares with a utility knife, and it fits in a standard 96-gallon trash cart over two pickups. Total cost: an afternoon and one broken blade.

You've got a truck, a helper, and a free Saturday. A dry, disconnected acrylic spa is genuinely a DIY job. Reciprocating saw with a demolition blade, safety glasses, gloves, and two people. Realistic self-cost: $45 for blades, $40–$70 in landfill fees, 4–6 hours, and a sore back. You save roughly $250. If your time is worth more than $50/hour, hire it out. If it isn't, don't pay us.

The spa is one item in a much bigger project. If you're also clearing a shed, a deck, and eight years of garage, booking a standalone hot tub removal is the expensive path. A combined load prices better per cubic yard — often 20–35% less than three separate trips. Bundle it with a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) and ask for one number.

Failure modes you should know about, ours included:

  • Gravel and xeriscape yards hold debris. Foam pellets and staples work down into landscape rock and no magnet sweep gets them all. We do a magnet pass and a hand pick, and we still tell customers to expect to find a screw or two over the following month. Anyone promising a perfect gravel yard is overpromising.
  • The pad underneath will look bad. Ten years of a spa on concrete leaves a discolored rectangle, sometimes with anchor holes and adhesive residue. Removal does not restore concrete. If you want that fixed, that's a separate [concrete removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) or resurfacing job.
  • Deck damage is often unavoidable. If the spa was set before the deck was built — common on Pueblo West builds from the mid-2000s — boards get cut. We'll show you the cut line before we touch it, but there is no version where the deck comes out unmarked.
  • Wind days. Pueblo West sees 30+ mph gusts often enough that cutting a shell into panels becomes genuinely unsafe. We'll reschedule rather than chase panels across your neighbor's lot. That costs you a day.
  • We are not a spa repair or relocation service. Moving a working spa to a new pad 40 feet away is a rigging job, not a junk job. Different equipment, different insurance. We'll decline it.

What Makes a Quote Trustworthy in This Market

Ask these four questions on the phone. The answers separate a real operator from a lead-broker:

  • "Is that price all-in, including dump fees?" A yes that hesitates is a no.
  • "What would change the price on arrival?" An honest answer names access width, water, and electrical. A dishonest one says "nothing."
  • "Are you insured, and can you send the certificate?" Should arrive in five minutes, not "later."
  • "Who's actually showing up?" National-brand phone numbers often sell your lead to whoever's closest for a 15–25% cut — which comes out of the crew's margin, which comes out of your job.
