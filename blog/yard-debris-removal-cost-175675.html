YARD DEBRIS REMOVAL COST: $175–$675

Most Pueblo West yard debris removal jobs run $175 to $675. A small pile — a few tumbleweed bales, some pruned elm limbs, a couple bags of leaves — lands at $175–$250 (about a quarter of a truck). A half-truck load of branches and brush is $300–$425. A full 15-cubic-yard truck, packed, is $500–$675. There's a $95–$145 minimum for anything smaller, because the drive and dump fee cost the same whether we haul two yards or six. Loads that are heavy rather than bulky — sod, wet dirt, landscape rock — get priced by weight instead, typically $85–$140 per ton over the first ton. Below is what actually moves those numbers in Pueblo West, plus the honest list of jobs you should not hire out.

Why yard debris is priced by volume, not by the pile

Junk haulers price on truck space because that's the scarce thing. A standard dump trailer holds roughly 15 cubic yards. A pickup bed, loaded level, holds about 2.5 yards. So the mental conversion is: *your pile, if it were stacked in a box, how many pickup beds is it?*

That conversion is where homeowners consistently guess wrong, in both directions:

  • Branches lie. A brush pile is 60–80% air. Six feet of piled Siberian elm limbs compresses to about 2 yards once it's stacked and cut. People expect a full-truck price and get a quarter-truck price.
  • Tumbleweeds lie harder. A Pueblo West fence line after a windy March can produce 8–10 cubic yards of Russian thistle that weighs under 300 pounds total. That's a $400+ volume charge for a load lighter than a single pallet.
  • Sod and rock lie the other way. One cubic yard of wet sod runs 1,500–2,200 lbs. Three yards of it maxes out a trailer's legal weight before it looks half full. That's why it flips to weight-based pricing.

The practical takeaway: cut your brush into 4-foot lengths and stack it before you get a quote. On a typical Pueblo West half-acre cleanup, that alone has moved quotes down a tier — a $425 job becoming a $300 job — because the same material now occupies 40% less truck.

What a Pueblo West yard cleanup actually costs, by job type

Real ranges for this market, assuming curbside or driveway-adjacent access:

  • Spring tumbleweed and windblown debris clear (fence lines, side yards, under decks): $175–$350
  • Tree trimming debris, homeowner-cut, one to three trees: $250–$475
  • Full seasonal cleanup on a 1-acre Pueblo West lot — brush, leaves, dead xeriscape plants, a few fence pickets: $450–$700
  • Sod removal, 500 sq ft strip-out (about 4 tons): $400–$650, weight-driven
  • Landscape rock removal, 200 sq ft at 3 inches deep (roughly 2 tons): $300–$500 — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo)
  • Stumps, already ground or dug out: $75–$150 each, more if root balls still carry dirt
  • Mixed load (yard debris plus an old grill, patio furniture, a broken swing set): priced as general junk, usually 10–20% above clean green-waste rates

Add-ons that legitimately raise the price: a $50–$125 access surcharge when the pile is in a fenced backyard with a 36-inch gate and everything moves by wheelbarrow; $60–$100 per additional hour if we're doing the raking and cutting rather than hauling a pile you've already made; and a $40–$90 disposal differential when green waste is contaminated with plastic, treated lumber, or bagged soil, because it can no longer go to the cheap green-waste stream.

The Pueblo West factor most price guides miss

Pueblo West is not Pueblo, and the difference shows up in the invoice.

Lots here run 1 to 2.5 acres where in-town Pueblo lots run a fifth of that, so "a yard cleanup" here means five times the material. Round-trip mileage from the Pueblo West Metro District to disposal is roughly 20–30 miles depending on the site, which is real fuel and real drive time baked into the minimum charge. And the vegetation mix is specific: Siberian elm suckers, Russian olive with thorns that flat tires, Russian thistle, and goathead vines. Thorned material is slower to load and gets handled with more care, which is part of why brush here prices above what a Denver-suburb calculator will tell you.

There's also a genuinely free option that most national pricing pages will never mention, because they don't operate here: the Pueblo West Metro District runs seasonal cleanup and slash/mulch drop-off events, typically in spring and fall. If your debris is clean woody material and you own a trailer, that's a $0 disposal path. Call the Metro District for current dates and eligibility before you assume it covers your pile — the rules change year to year and mixed junk is usually excluded.

When hiring us is the wrong call

This is the part we'd rather skip, so here it is plainly.

Don't hire a hauler for tumbleweeds if you own a truck. Tumbleweeds are the worst dollar-per-pound value in this business. You're paying a volume rate for material that weighs nothing and compresses to 20% of its size if you drive over it a few times. Two hours, one pickup, two trips, and you've saved $300. Wear gloves and long sleeves.

Don't hire out a pile under about 1.5 cubic yards. That's smaller than a pickup bed. You'll hit the $95–$145 minimum, and the minimum on a tiny pile is a bad deal for you. Either wait until you've accumulated more, or split a load with a neighbor doing the same cleanup — we'll do one stop, two piles, one charge, and it's still cheaper for both of you than two separate calls.

Don't hire out grass clippings and leaves alone. Bagged leaves are the cheapest thing you can handle yourself, and a compost pile or a mulching mower makes them disappear for free. Paying $200 to remove leaves is paying for convenience only. Say that out loud and see if it still sounds right.

Don't call us for standing trees or live limbs. We haul cut material. We are not arborists, we don't climb, and we don't drop limbs over a roofline. If the tree is still vertical, you need a tree service first — expect $400–$1,800 for removal depending on size and proximity to structures. Some tree companies include haul-away in that number, which makes hiring us afterward pure duplication. Ask them before you ask us.

Don't call us if a burn permit is a real option for you. Pueblo County issues open burn permits, and on a rural-ish Pueblo West parcel with a legal burn pile, that's a $0–$40 solution against a $400 haul. It's weather-dependent, it's restricted during fire bans, and burning near structures in a high-wind county carries obvious risk — but it's a legitimate alternative and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

> If your debris is light, clean, woody, and you own a trailer, you are the customer we cost too much for. Use the Metro District drop-off and keep your money.

The limitations worth naming: we can't take dirt or concrete on a standard brush load — those are separate weight-based jobs, sometimes needing equipment rather than a trailer, which is what [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) covers. We don't handle hazardous material: no fuel cans, no pesticide containers, no railroad ties in bulk. We won't scale a slope to reach a canyon-side pile. And in peak season — late April through June, and again in October — same-day scheduling is often 3 to 7 days out. If you need it gone tomorrow because a buyer's inspection is Friday, tell us at the first call so we don't waste your week.

How to get an accurate quote instead of a range

Ranges exist because the pile is unknown. Collapse the range like this:

  • Send photos with a scale object — a trash can, a person, a truck tailgate in frame. A pile photographed alone is unquotable.
  • Say what's in it. "Elm limbs and tumbleweeds" prices differently than "sod, rock, and an old hot tub cover."
  • Say where it is. Driveway vs. backyard behind a gate is a $50–$125 swing.
  • Stack and cut first if you have the time. It's the single highest-return hour of labor you can spend on this job.
  • Bundle. If a garage cleanout or an appliance is also on the list, one trip beats two — combining typically saves 15–25% against separate visits.

Most quotes come back within a few hours, and the number we quote from clear photos is the number you pay unless the pile grew.
