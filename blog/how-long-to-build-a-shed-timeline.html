HOW LONG TO BUILD A SHED: TIMELINE

If you're building a 10x12 shed from a kit in Pueblo West, plan on 16 to 24 hours of actual build time — two solid weekends with a helper, or 2 to 3 days for a two-person pro crew. A stick-built shed on a poured slab runs 40 to 60 labor hours. But the honest answer to "how long to build a shed" is that construction is the short part. Start to finish — including Metro District architectural approval, county permitting if you need it, site leveling, and getting the old shed out of the way — most Pueblo West projects take 3 to 6 weeks of calendar time. The build is a weekend. The clearing, approving, and hauling is what eats the month.

The Real Timeline, Broken Into Phases

Here's how a typical Pueblo West shed project actually spends its calendar days. We see this sequence constantly because we're usually the ones hauling out whatever was standing there before.

  • Design and ordering: 3 to 14 days. A big-box kit sitting on the lot is same-day. A custom order or a delivered pre-built from a regional builder is commonly 2 to 8 weeks out, longer in spring.
  • Pueblo West Metropolitan District architectural review: 2 to 4 weeks. Pueblo West property is subject to Metro District covenants, and accessory structures generally need written approval before you start. Submitting a site plan and getting it back is the single most-ignored delay in this whole process. Confirm current submittal deadlines and meeting dates with the District directly — they are not the same as the county's.
  • Pueblo County building permit: 1 to 3 weeks. Under the IRC, detached accessory structures under 200 square feet typically don't require a building permit, which is exactly why 8x10, 10x12, and 12x16 sheds are so popular — a 12x16 is 192 square feet and lands just under. Over that threshold, expect plan review, fees, and inspections. Verify the current threshold with the Pueblo County Department of Planning and Development before you order; a 200+ sq ft shed built without a permit becomes a title problem the day you sell.
  • Site clearing and old shed demolition: 2 to 6 hours. A rotted 8x10 wood shed comes down in an afternoon. A 12x20 with a concrete slab and a pack of stored junk inside is a full day.
  • Foundation: 1 to 4 days. Gravel pad on compacted base is a half-day. A poured slab is 1 day of forming and pouring, then 3 to 7 days of cure before you set anything heavy on it. Concrete cure time is not negotiable and it is where DIY schedules die.
  • Assembly: 1 to 3 days for a kit, 4 to 7 days for stick-built with siding, trim, roofing, and a door that actually closes.

Why Pueblo West Adds Time Other Shed Articles Don't Mention

Generic shed-timeline articles are written for flat, graded suburban lots. Pueblo West is not that. Lots here are often half-acre to 2.5 acres, frequently unimproved, and the dirt is caliche-hard clay and rock that does not shovel.

Three local realities change the schedule:

Wind design. The Front Range and southern Colorado plains see design wind speeds in the 105 to 115 mph range, and Pueblo West gets real gusts every spring. A shed that isn't anchored — ground anchors into undisturbed soil, or anchor bolts into a slab — is a kite. Proper anchoring adds 2 to 4 hours and roughly $80 to $250 in hardware. Skipping it is how we end up hauling a shed out of someone's fence line in April.

Frost depth and grade. Local frost depth is commonly figured around 30 inches. Shallow skid foundations on a lot that slopes even 6 inches across 12 feet will rack the door frame within two seasons.

Rock. Hand-digging a level pad in Pueblo West clay and cobble is realistically 8 to 16 hours of brutal work for a 12x16 footprint. A skid steer does it in 1 to 2 hours. If your lot has more than about 4 inches of fall across the pad, machine work isn't a luxury — it's the difference between a 3-week project and a 3-month one. That's most of what our [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) and [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) calls are: somebody started hand-digging on a Saturday and quit by noon.

> The shed goes up in a weekend. The month is spent on approvals, cure time, and getting the old structure and its contents off the property.

The Part That Blindsides People: Clearing the Old Shed

Roughly speaking, when someone calls us about a shed, they're not replacing an empty one. The old shed is full. Eighteen years of paint cans, a dead mower, half a swing set, three bags of concrete that turned to stone, and a mattress someone stored "temporarily" in 2014.

Budget realistically:

  • Emptying the old shed: 2 to 5 hours. More if you sort instead of toss.
  • Demolition of an 8x10 to 12x16 wood shed: 2 to 6 hours with the right tools.
  • Debris volume: 1 to 3 trailer loads. A 10x12 wood shed with shingles generates roughly 1.5 to 3 tons of material.
  • Disposal: Shingles, treated lumber, and old siding are construction debris, not curbside trash. Pueblo-area landfill tipping is charged by weight or volume, and a single pickup load of mixed C&D commonly runs $40 to $90 per trip in gate fees before you count fuel and your Saturday.

A DIY teardown-and-haul on a 10x12 shed is usually 6 to 12 hours across two days, four or five truck trips, and $150 to $300 in dump fees and gas. A crew with a dump trailer does it in two to four hours, one trip. That's the honest comparison — see [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) and [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) for how that side of the job gets scoped.

When Hiring This Out Is a Waste of Your Money

This is the part most contractors leave off their websites. We'd rather you not call us when calling us is dumb.

Don't pay anyone — including us — if:

  • Your shed is small, empty, and already falling apart. An 8x8 rotted wood shed with no floor and nothing inside is genuinely a 3-hour DIY job with a sledge, a pry bar, and a borrowed truck. Two trips to the landfill. You'll spend $60 and a morning. Paying a crew for that is paying for convenience you don't need.
  • You own a truck, a trailer, and a free weekend. Our value is speed and your back, not magic. If all three of those are available to you and the structure is under about 100 square feet, DIY wins on cost every time.
  • You're not actually ready to build. We've had people clear a pad in March and not order the shed until July. Now there's a bare dirt rectangle growing tumbleweeds and the grade has washed. Clear the site 5 to 10 days before your foundation goes in, not five months.
  • Your real problem is a 200+ sq ft permit you haven't pulled. Removal and site prep don't fix that. Call the county first. If plan review comes back wanting engineered anchoring or a setback change, every hour of prep work done beforehand may need redoing.
  • You're hoping demolition pays for itself in salvage. It won't. Old shed lumber is nail-riddled and sun-cooked. Shingles are worthless. Occasionally a usable metal roof panel or a good door is worth pulling, but assume $0 recovered value and you'll never be disappointed.

The failure modes worth naming plainly:

Site prep estimates slip. If we hit buried concrete, an old slab nobody mentioned, or an unmarked irrigation line, a 2-hour grading job becomes 5. Spring and early summer are the busiest window in Pueblo West — scheduling in April or May can mean 5 to 10 business days of lead time rather than next-day. Wet clay after a storm can't be graded properly; we will tell you to wait 2 to 3 days rather than compact mud and watch your pad fail. And we don't build sheds. We clear, demo, haul, grade, and prep. If you want one entity owning the entire project end to end, you want a general contractor, and that's a fair thing to want.

How to Compress the Timeline

If 3 to 6 weeks is too long, here's where the real time is recoverable:

  • Submit for Metro District approval the same day you decide, before you've picked a model. This one move saves 2 to 3 weeks more than anything else on this list.
  • Stay under the permit threshold if a 12x16 meets your needs. Confirm the current square-footage cutoff with the county yourself.
  • Choose a gravel pad over a slab where the shed design allows it. You skip 3 to 7 days of cure.
  • Do demolition and grading in one mobilization. Teardown, haul-off, and pad prep in a single visit eliminates a scheduling gap that routinely costs a week.
  • Order the kit before you clear the site, so the delivery window drives the schedule instead of the pad sitting open.

Done in that order, a 10x12 shed on a gravel pad with an old structure to remove is a realistic 12 to 18 day project in Pueblo West. Done in the wrong order, the same shed takes two months.
