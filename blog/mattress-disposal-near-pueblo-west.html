Why "Mattress Recycling" Barely Exists in Southern Colorado

A mattress is about 95% recyclable by weight. Steel springs, polyurethane foam, cotton batting, and a wood frame all have downstream buyers. That statistic gets repeated constantly, and in Pueblo County it is close to meaningless.

Only a handful of states run funded mattress recycling programs paid for by a fee at the point of sale. Colorado is not one of them. Without that fee, someone has to eat the cost of dismantling a mattress, and dismantling one by hand takes 20 to 30 minutes of labor. The nearest facilities that will actually process bedding are in the Colorado Springs and Denver corridors, roughly 45 miles and 115 miles from Pueblo West respectively. A dedicated round trip to Colorado Springs is about 90 miles, two and a half hours, and $18 to $25 in fuel, to divert one mattress.

> The honest version: most mattresses that leave Pueblo West go into a landfill, and any hauler telling you otherwise on a single-item job is either running a route you should ask to see, or telling you what you want to hear.

We will not claim your mattress gets recycled. What we do is real but smaller: metal frames, bed rails, and springs from stripped units go to scrap, and anything genuinely clean and current gets offered locally before it gets loaded. That is maybe one mattress in fifteen.

Donation is a dead end more often than people expect. Colorado regulates the resale of used bedding, and the practical result is that Goodwill, the ARC, and most Pueblo-area thrift operations decline used mattresses outright. Calling four charities before calling a hauler costs you an afternoon and usually ends the same way.

The DIY Route, With the Numbers Nobody Mentions

Hauling it yourself is legitimately cheaper. It is also more work than it looks, and the failure point is almost always the truck.

  • A queen mattress is 80 inches long. A standard short pickup bed is 68 inches and a standard bed is about 78 inches. A queen does not lie flat in either one with the tailgate up.
  • An innerspring queen runs 60 to 120 pounds. A memory foam or hybrid king can hit 180. These are two-person lifts, not one-person lifts, no matter how it looked coming up the stairs ten years ago.
  • Colorado requires loads to be secured. A mattress that leaves your truck on Highway 50 is a citation and a liability problem, and Pueblo West sees spring gusts over 40 mph that will lift a foam mattress out of a bed like a sail.
  • Expect a minimum load charge at the landfill even for one item, plus a possible per-mattress surcharge. Mattresses wrap around compactor drums, so many facilities price them separately. Confirm the current rate and gate hours by phone the morning you go.
  • Budget 90 minutes to two hours door to door, plus rope, a tarp, and a second person.

If you own a truck, have help, and the landfill is open, do it yourself. You will spend $30 to $45 and an afternoon instead of $120. That is a fair trade and we will not pretend otherwise.
