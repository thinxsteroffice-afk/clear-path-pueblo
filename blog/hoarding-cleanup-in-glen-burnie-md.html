Why a Colorado Company Is Telling You This

Search engines sometimes send out-of-state searches to local service pages. Most companies would let that slide and take the phone call anyway. We'd rather tell you now than waste 20 minutes of a hard week finding out on the phone.

Whatever company you hire, in Maryland or Colorado, ask these three questions before you book:

  • "Do you sort, or do you only haul?" Many junk haulers price by truckload and toss everything. A real hoarding cleanout means going through items by hand for documents, cash, medications, firearms, and keepsakes.
  • "What do you do if you find biohazards?" Animal waste, needles, or decomposition need a licensed biohazard remediation company. That is a separate trade from junk removal. A company that says it does both without naming its certifications is guessing.
  • "Is the price a written cap or an estimate?" Hoarding jobs regularly run past the first estimate by 25–50% once crews find out how dense the rooms really are. Get the maximum in writing.

How a Cleanout Actually Runs

Walkthrough (30–60 minutes). We walk every room, the garage, and the yard. If the person living in the home is present, we talk with them, not past them. We ask what must be kept and what must be searched for: wills, titles, photos, a specific piece of jewelry.

Sorting plan. Before any truck is loaded, we agree on three categories: keep, donate, discard. Anything uncertain goes in a fourth pile, "decide later," which the family reviews at the end of each day.

Removal. We work one room at a time, usually starting with the room that restores a safe exit path or a working bathroom. In homes at Level 4 or 5 on the Institute for Challenging Disorganization's 5-level Clutter-Hoarding Scale, clearing exits comes first for fire safety.

Disposal. Usable furniture and household goods go to local donation centers when they're in good condition. Metal goes to scrap recycling. Everything else goes to the landfill. Expect 10–25% of a typical hoard to be donatable. People often hope for more, but water damage, odor, and pests rule most of it out.

Final sweep. We broom-sweep and remove loose debris. We are not a deep-cleaning company. If you need carpets pulled, odor treatment, or sanitizing, that's a separate contractor, and we'll tell you so up front.

When Hiring Us Is NOT Worth It

This is the section most cleanout companies leave out. Read it before you call anyone.

1. When the person living there hasn't agreed. Hoarding disorder is a recognized mental health condition, estimated to affect about 2–6% of adults. A forced cleanout while the person objects often ends in anger and broken trust, and the home tends to fill back up within 6–18 months. We'll still do the job if a landlord, court order, or code enforcement requires it, but a family that clears a parent's house over their objections is usually paying for a temporary fix. If you have time, spend it on a therapist who treats hoarding with cognitive behavioral therapy before you spend it on a crew.

2. When you need biohazard remediation, not junk removal. If there's been an unattended death, heavy animal waste soaked into the subfloor, or sewage backup, you need a licensed biohazard company first. We can haul the remaining contents afterward. Hiring us first means paying twice and exposing our crew to risks we aren't certified to handle.

3. When the volume is small and you have able-bodied help. If it's one room of boxes and clothes, renting a 10-yard dumpster for about a week and doing it with family over a weekend can cost 40–60% less than hiring us. You trade money for your own labor and back strain, and for some families that trade makes sense.

4. When the house is being sold as-is to an investor. Some cash buyers in Pueblo County will take a property full of contents and price the cleanout into their offer. Get that quote before paying $5,000 to empty a house the buyer would have taken full. The investor's discount may be smaller than our bill.

5. When you're expecting us to find valuables for you. We sort carefully, and we've turned up cash in cereal boxes and savings bonds in coat pockets. But we are not appraisers or estate liquidators. If the home may hold real antiques, coins, or collectibles, have an estate sale company or appraiser go through it before we arrive. 6. When you're outside our service area. That includes Glen Burnie, MD, and anywhere more than about 50 miles from Pueblo West. Check [areas](/areas) for where we actually go.

Limits and Failure Modes to Expect

Even a well-run hoarding job has problems. Here's where they usually happen:

  • Estimate creep. Clutter packed floor to ceiling often hides more than it shows. Our written quotes include a cap, but if we find a second layer the walkthrough couldn't reveal, such as a basement or a locked room, we stop and requote rather than surprise you on the invoice.
  • Decision fatigue. Families who insist on reviewing every item can turn a 3-day job into a 9-day job, and since our pricing is partly labor-based, that costs real money. Decide in advance which categories you'll trust the crew to discard on sight, like food containers, newspapers, and broken plastics.
  • Hidden damage. Clearing a room often exposes rotted flooring, roof leaks, mold, or pest damage. Budget an extra 10–20% of the cleanout cost for repairs you can't see yet.
  • Emotional collapse mid-job. It's common for a family member to ask us to stop on day two. We'll pause, and you only pay for time worked, but rebooking can push the finish date back 1–2 weeks during our busy spring season.
