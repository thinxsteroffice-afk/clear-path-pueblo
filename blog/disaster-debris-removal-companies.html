DISASTER DEBRIS REMOVAL COMPANIES

If a hailstorm, windstorm, grass fire or burst pipe has just left a mess on your Pueblo West property, a disaster debris removal company hauls it away. That means broken fencing, shredded siding, downed limbs, soaked drywall, ruined furniture and collapsed sheds. ClearPath Junk Removal does this work for homeowners, landlords and small businesses in Pueblo West. We can usually be on site within 24 to 72 hours after a storm, and a typical load is priced by how much of the truck it fills. We aren't the right call for every disaster, though. Fire ash, suspected asbestos and debris from a federally declared disaster often need a different contractor or a government program. This page explains who should handle what, what it costs, and how to keep your insurance claim intact.

What disaster debris removal covers in Pueblo West

Pueblo West gets high plains weather. Spring and summer hail runs from about April through September. Winter and spring bring strong downslope winds, and dry grassland can burn fast. The debris from those events usually falls into a few groups:

  • Storm and wind damage: broken fence panels, torn shingles, twisted carports, trampolines blown into the neighbor's yard, and sheds lifted off their pads.
  • Vegetative debris: limbs, whole trees and brush, including piles left after you or a tree crew cut up a fallen cottonwood or elm. See our [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) service.
  • Water-damaged contents: carpet, pad, drywall, mattresses and upholstered furniture that got soaked by a burst pipe or roof leak and can't be dried in time. Mold can start growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, so speed matters here more than anywhere else.
  • Structural pieces: partially collapsed decks, pergolas, sheds and outbuildings. For these, our [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) and [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) crews take the structure down and haul it off in the same visit.
  • Heavy or bulk material: concrete, dirt, rock and large debris spread across a big lot. On Pueblo West's typical half-acre to multi-acre parcels, our [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) often clear it faster and cheaper than hand-loading.

What disaster cleanup costs

Pricing depends on volume, weight and how hard the material is to reach. These are the ranges we see most often for residential jobs in Pueblo West. Your written quote is the number that counts.

  • Minimum or small load (a few fence panels, one soaked mattress): roughly $100–$200
  • Quarter to half truck (a storm-damaged carport, a room of wet carpet and pad): roughly $250–$450
  • Full truckload (about 15 cubic yards, the size of 6 to 8 pickup loads): roughly $550–$750
  • Shed or deck teardown plus haul-off: commonly $400–$1,500, depending on size and materials
  • Skid steer work on large lots: usually quoted by the half day or full day, not by volume

Heavy materials cost more because landfills charge by weight. Wet drywall and saturated carpet can weigh two to three times as much as the same materials dry. Concrete and roofing shingles are heavy too. A load of wet material that looks small can still cost as much as a full load of dry furniture.

> The cheapest disaster cleanup is the one you photograph first. Debris that goes to the landfill before your adjuster sees it or has proof of it can cost you more than the haul-off ever will.

How to keep your insurance claim intact

This is the part most homeowners never hear about until it's too late. Most standard homeowners policies, including the common HO-3 form, have specific debris removal rules:

  • The extra 5%: Many policies add up to 5% of your dwelling coverage limit for debris removal once the main limit is used up. On a home insured for $400,000, that's up to $20,000 in extra debris money. Most people never learn it exists.
  • Tree limits are low: Many standard policies cap removal of fallen trees at about $1,000 per event, and often no more than $500 per tree. Coverage usually applies only if the tree hit a covered structure or blocks a driveway or accessibility ramp. A tree that fell in an open field and hit nothing is often not covered at all.
  • Flood is excluded: Regular homeowners insurance doesn't cover surface flooding. A flood policy through the National Flood Insurance Program usually has a 30-day waiting period, so buying one after the forecast turns bad won't help.

Before anything leaves your property, work through this list:

1. Photograph and video every pile, every damaged structure and the property as a whole. Include wide shots and close-ups. 2. Call your insurance company and get a claim number. 3. Ask your adjuster whether you may remove debris before the inspection. In most cases you're allowed and expected to prevent further damage. Get that permission in writing or note the date and name. 4. Keep every invoice. Our receipts itemize what we hauled so your adjuster can match it to the claim.

When hiring us isn't worth it (or isn't allowed)

A lot of cleanup jobs in Pueblo West don't need us, and a few shouldn't involve us at all. Here's where we'd tell you to look elsewhere.

1. The disaster is federally declared and the county is collecting debris at the curb. After major declared disasters, FEMA's Public Assistance program can pay local governments to collect debris set out at the curb. Residents are usually asked to sort it into categories such as vegetation, construction debris, appliances, electronics and household hazardous waste. If Pueblo County announces a program like that, set your debris out by the deadline and pay nothing. Paying a private hauler for the same material wastes your money. After the 2021 Marshall Fire, Boulder County ran a coordinated debris removal program for burned properties, and many owners who joined paid far less out of pocket than those who hired private cleanup on their own.

2. It's fire ash or a burned structure. Ash from a burned home can contain heavy metals, asbestos and other toxic residue. It's not a junk removal job. You need a contractor who handles hazardous materials, with proper protective gear, wetting of the ash and approved disposal. We can still help with undamaged contents, outbuildings and yard debris farther from the burn area, but we won't shovel ash.

3. You suspect asbestos. Older buildings may contain asbestos in things like popcorn ceilings, vinyl floor tile and some siding. Colorado requires an inspection by a certified asbestos inspector before renovation or demolition disturbs more than trigger amounts (for single-family homes, 32 square feet, 50 linear feet of pipe, or the volume of a 55-gallon drum). If the inspection finds asbestos, a licensed abatement contractor removes it first. We come in after that.

4. The pile is small and you have a truck. Three or four broken fence panels and a few branches fit in a pickup. If you can safely load and drive it yourself, a landfill trip usually costs a fraction of our minimum charge.

5. It's a full rebuild with a general contractor. If a roofer or restoration company is already on the job, their dumpster is often built into the insurance estimate. Hiring us too means paying twice for the same work. We make sense when you want debris gone *before* a contractor arrives, or for contents and outbuildings their scope doesn't include.

6. You need it gone the same day during a regional storm. After a big hail or wind event, every hauler on the Front Range gets swamped at once. We aim for 24 to 72 hours, but after a widespread storm the wait can run longer. If something is blocking your driveway or creating a hazard right now, call emergency services or a tree service first.

How to vet any disaster debris removal company

Out-of-area "storm chasers" show up in southern Colorado after big hail events. Whoever you hire, check these things:

  • Get a written quote that says what's included: labor, loading, disposal fees and any extra charge for heavy materials.
  • Don't pay the full amount upfront. A legitimate hauler charges when the work is done, or takes a reasonable deposit on large demolition jobs.
  • Ask where the debris goes. Illegal dumping on open land around Pueblo County is a real problem, and material traced back to your property can come back to you.
  • Confirm insurance. Ask for proof of liability coverage before anyone brings equipment onto your lot, especially near septic systems, which are common in Pueblo West.
  • Ask for itemized receipts you can send to your insurance company.
