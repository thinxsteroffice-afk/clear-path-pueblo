MATTRESS DISPOSAL COST, PUEBLO WEST

In Pueblo West, expect to pay $75–$150 to have a single mattress hauled away by a full-service junk removal company, with each additional mattress or box spring running $30–$60. Hauling it yourself to the landfill in Pueblo costs roughly $25–$45 in minimum-load tipping fees, plus about 30 miles round trip and an hour of your time — call $45–$70 all-in if you already own a truck. Your private trash hauler may take it as a scheduled bulky item for $25–$50, but only with advance notice and only if it's bagged. Below is the honest math on all three, including the cases where paying us is a waste of your money.

What a Mattress Actually Costs to Dispose of in Pueblo West

Three real paths, with real numbers:

  • Full-service pickup (we come get it): $75–$150 for one mattress or one mattress + box spring set. Two-piece king sets and stairs push toward the top of that. Add-ons run $30–$60 each. Most companies, us included, have a $75–$95 minimum charge — you can't buy a $40 job.
  • DIY to the landfill: $25–$45 minimum load fee at the transfer station, ~$8–$12 in gas for the round trip from Pueblo West, 45–75 minutes door to door. No truck? A pickup rental runs about $19.95/day plus $0.99/mile — that mileage charge alone adds ~$30 on a 30-mile round trip, so a "free" DIY trip becomes $70–$90 the moment you rent.
  • Curbside bulky item with your trash hauler: $25–$50, scheduled 3–14 days out depending on the company and the route. Cheapest option by far — if you can wait and if you follow their bagging rules exactly.

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no municipal bulk-trash day here the way Pueblo city residents sometimes get through cleanup events. Whatever your private hauler offers is your only curbside option, and their bulky-item policy is a line item in your contract, not a courtesy.

Why Landfill Fees Don't Work the Way You Think

Transfer stations price by weight — typically $40–$60 per ton in southern Colorado. A queen innerspring mattress weighs 60–120 lbs. A memory foam queen is lighter, often 50–80 lbs. A box spring adds 30–50 lbs.

Do the math: 150 lbs of bedding is 0.075 tons, or about $3.75 of actual tonnage. You will not pay $3.75. You'll pay the minimum load charge, which is where the $25–$45 comes from.

The practical consequence: the second, third, and fourth mattress are nearly free at the landfill. If you're clearing out a rental or a parent's house with four beds, one DIY trip handles all of them for the same minimum fee — the tonnage on four full sets still lands under a quarter ton. That's the single biggest cost lever most people miss, and it argues against hiring anyone.

Some facilities also charge a $5–$15 per-item surcharge specifically for mattresses, because they jam compactors and shredder equipment. Confirm by phone before you load. Fees and hours at Pueblo-area facilities change, and a wasted 30-mile trip costs more than the call.

Colorado Has No Mattress Recycling Program — This Matters

California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon run mattress stewardship programs funded by a recycling fee added at the point of sale, which pays for free public drop-off. Colorado has none of this. There's no state-funded recycling site, no prepaid disposal, no free drop-off window.

What that means for you in Pueblo West:

  • Nobody local is legally obligated to recycle your mattress, and almost nobody does it for free.
  • Roughly 75% of a mattress by weight is recoverable material — steel springs, foam, cotton batting, wood frame — but recovering it takes 20–30 minutes of labor per unit. At Colorado scrap prices, the steel in a queen innerspring is worth maybe $4–$8. The labor costs more than the steel. That's why it goes to the landfill.
  • Any company that markets "eco-friendly mattress recycling" in this market and charges the same $99 as everyone else is either eating that cost or not doing it. Ask them where the mattress goes. A straight answer is a good sign.

> The honest version: in Pueblo County, your mattress is almost certainly going in a hole in the ground. What you're paying for is the truck, the labor, and not throwing your back out — not a green outcome.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal sites leave off. Skip full-service pickup when:

  • You own a truck and one mattress. A single mattress in a pickup bed with two ratchet straps is a $35 problem, not a $95 problem. Hiring us for that is paying $60 for an hour you could spend yourself. Take it yourself.
  • You have 3+ mattresses and any truck access. The minimum-load pricing at the landfill means four mattresses cost the same to dump as one. We'd charge $75 for the first and $30–$60 each after — call it $165–$255 for four. Your DIY cost is still $45–$70. That's a $100–$185 penalty for convenience on a job that takes 90 minutes.
  • Your mattress is under 5 years old and clean. Donation is genuinely viable here — but only if it's stain-free with no rips, and only if you can deliver it. Most thrift operations in Pueblo County will not pick up mattresses and many won't accept them at all due to state health regulations on bedding resale. Call first; don't drive over hoping. If it's a name brand in good shape, a listing at $50–$150 on Facebook Marketplace often moves in 48 hours, and the buyer hauls it. You come out ahead by $150–$250 versus paying us.
  • You're within your mattress retailer's delivery window. Most retailers haul the old set free with delivery of the new one, or charge $25–$50. That is always cheaper than calling us afterward. Set it up when you order, not after the truck leaves.
  • Your budget is genuinely tight and the mattress can wait. A mattress leaning in a garage costs nothing this month. Bulky-item curbside at $25–$50 next month beats $95 today. We'd rather tell you that than take the job.

Where It Goes Wrong — Real Failure Modes

Bed bugs change everything. If there's any suspicion of infestation, the mattress must be sealed in plastic *before* it leaves the bedroom. Most haulers — including us — will refuse an unbagged suspected-infested mattress, and some charge a $25–$50 handling surcharge even when it is bagged. Heavy-duty mattress disposal bags cost $8–$20 at hardware stores. Buy one before you call anybody. Dragging an infested mattress through the hallway to the curb is how a one-room problem becomes a whole-house problem and a $1,000–$2,500 pest treatment.

Wet or moldy mattresses. A mattress left in a Pueblo West yard through a monsoon week can double in weight and gain 60+ lbs of water. Some facilities reject saturated bedding outright. Expect a surcharge or a refusal.

Unsecured loads. Pueblo West sees 40+ mph gusts regularly in spring. A mattress flying out of a pickup bed on Highway 50 is a genuine hazard and an unsecured-load citation — and if it causes a wreck, that's a liability claim, not a ticket. Two straps minimum, over the top and through the bed anchors. Not bungee cords.

Dumping it on BLM land or a vacant lot. Illegal dumping in Colorado carries fines that start in the hundreds and climb past $1,000, plus cleanup costs and possible community service. Rural Pueblo County parcels are watched more closely than people assume, and a mattress with a delivery tag on it identifies you by name. The $95 is cheaper.

Same-day expectations. Most local outfits book 1–3 days out. Same-day service, when available, typically carries a $25–$50 premium. If you need it gone before a Saturday closing, call Wednesday.

The Decision, Compressed

Hire it out if you don't have a truck, can't lift 100 lbs safely, have stairs or a tight stairwell, or your time is worth more than $50/hour. In those cases the $75–$150 is fair value — it's roughly 45–60 minutes of two-person labor plus the disposal fee, and about 35–45% of what you pay is the tip fee and fuel, not profit.

Handle it yourself if you have a truck, one or two items, and a free Saturday morning. You'll save $50–$100 on a single mattress and $100–$185 on a load of four.

If you're already clearing a house — old bed sets plus a couch plus 20 years of garage — the per-item math stops mattering and volume pricing takes over.
