What Drives the Price, Specifically

Square footage matters less than three other things: volume, weight, and how much sorting you want.

  • Volume. We price by trailer load. A 16-yard dump trailer holds roughly a packed 10x12 room. A severe whole-house hoard in Pueblo West typically produces 8 to 14 loads, counting the garage and any sheds.
  • Weight. Books, magazines, tile, and water-damaged carpet are the expensive ones. A trailer of clothing might weigh 900 lbs; the same trailer of soaked paper hits 3,500–4,000 lbs, and disposal is billed by the ton.
  • Sort level. "Take everything" is the cheapest option. "Sort every container and set aside anything that looks like a document, photo, or cash" can add 40–60% to labor hours because a crew moves at maybe a quarter speed.
  • Access. A walkout basement with a 30-inch stairwell doubles handling time versus a garage we can back a trailer up to.
  • Trip distance. Most Pueblo West addresses sit 12 to 20 miles from where the load ends up. Rye and Beulah jobs add drive time and we say so in the quote rather than hiding it.

Roughly 20–35% of what we pull out of an average hoard is donatable or recyclable — metal especially. Scrap metal offsets part of the disposal bill, and we credit that back on the invoice rather than pocketing it quietly.

Why Pueblo West Jobs Aren't Like City Hoarding Jobs

Most hoarding-cleanout content on the internet was written for apartments and row houses. Pueblo West is a different animal, and it changes the job in ways that cost real money.

Lots here commonly run half an acre to a full acre. Accumulation doesn't stop at the door — it spills into detached garages, carports, three or four sheds, a dead camper, and a fence line of stacked pallets and tires. On more than half the residential-hoard estimates we write out here, the exterior is a bigger job than the interior.

Then there's wind. Spring gusts through Pueblo West regularly hit 40–60 mph. Sorting outdoors on a windy day means paper and insulation end up in three neighbors' yards, and you own that. We tarp and load into enclosed or netted trailers, and on genuinely bad days we push exterior work to the following morning rather than create a debris field.

Many properties out here run on septic and a well. Years of stacked material over a leach field compacts the soil and, occasionally, hides a collapsed lid. Before we bring heavy equipment across a yard, we ask where the tank is. If nobody knows, that's a locate call before, not after. Related work like grading or hauling off buried debris falls under [dirt work and excavation in Pueblo](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo).

The Rodent Risk Specific to Arid Colorado

Humid states worry about black mold. Southern Colorado's dry air means mold is often less severe than expected — but our low humidity does nothing to reduce the real hazard here, which is deer mice and hantavirus. Sin Nombre virus is present in Colorado rodent populations, and the exposure route is inhaling aerosolized dust from dried droppings and nesting material — exactly what a hoarded, mouse-occupied storage shed produces when you start moving boxes.

> If there are droppings, do not sweep and do not vacuum. Dry sweeping is the single most dangerous thing an untrained person does in a hoarded space.

The standard approach: ventilate the space for at least 30 minutes before entry, then wet the material down with a bleach solution — 1 part household bleach to 10 parts water — let it sit 5 minutes, and wipe or bag it wet. Our crews wear P100 respirators and Tyvek for any job with visible nesting. If you're doing part of the work yourself, do that part this way or don't do it at all.

What We Won't Haul, and What To Do Instead

Being direct saves you a wasted appointment:

  • Ammunition and firearms. Extremely common in rural Pueblo County homes. We leave them in place. Live ammo goes to the Pueblo County Sheriff's Office or a licensed dealer; unsecured firearms are a family or law-enforcement matter, not a junk crew's.
  • Propane cylinders, paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection — call the county to confirm current dates and limits before you haul anything anywhere. Budget $150–$400 if you'd rather we coordinate it.
  • Medical sharps and prescription medications. Sharps need a rigid sharps container. Meds go to a pharmacy or law-enforcement take-back box.
  • Suspected asbestos. Homes built before roughly 1980 may have asbestos in floor tile, mastic, and pipe wrap. Colorado requires a licensed abatement contractor for anything above the threshold. We stop and refer out; we do not "just bag it."
  • Biohazard — human or animal waste, decomposition, needles in volume. That's remediation work under a different license category, not junk removal.

When a Hoarding Cleanout Is Not Worth It — and Who Should Not Hire Us

This is the part that costs us jobs, and we'd rather lose the job than take a bad one.

Do not hire anyone for a surprise cleanout. The most common call we get is an adult child asking us to clear a parent's house while the parent is at a medical appointment. We decline these. Not because it's difficult — because it reliably backfires. Forced cleanouts done without the person's participation produce severe distress, permanent family estrangement, and re-accumulation that is frequently visible within 3 to 6 months. You will spend $5,000 to arrive back where you started, minus the relationship. If the person has capacity to make their own decisions, they have to be part of it.

A cleanout does not treat hoarding disorder. We move objects. That's the entire service. Without ongoing clinical support — hoarding responds to a specific form of CBT, and treatment is typically measured in months, not weeks — relapse is the default outcome, not the exception. Anyone selling you a cleanout as a cure is selling you something they can't deliver. Health Solutions in Pueblo is a starting point for local behavioral health referrals.

Skip the full-service option if you have time and no hazard. If the accumulation is ordinary clutter — no rodents, no rot, no structural loading — and you have three able-bodied weekends, a rented roll-off container plus your own labor beats us on price by a wide margin, often $800–$1,500 on a garage-scale job. We'll tell you that on the estimate. See [garage cleanout Pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) if that's really what you have.

Don't pay us to sort for value. People ask us to look for cash, coins, jewelry, and deeds. We do a reasonable-care pass and set aside anything obviously valuable, but a crew billing hourly is a terrible instrument for finding a $40 silver dollar in a box of receipts. If the estate genuinely has value, hire an estate liquidator or an appraiser first, then call us for the remainder. That sequencing is also why [estate cleanout Pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) is a separate service.

Don't count on insurance. Homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental, covered perils. Accumulated hoarding is treated as neglect and maintenance, and claims are routinely denied. If there was a burst pipe under the pile, that water damage may be covered — the hoard itself almost never is. Get the denial in writing before you plan around it.

Landlords and lienholders: if the occupant hasn't been legally removed, we can't enter. Colorado eviction procedure has to complete first, and abandoned-property notice periods apply. That's [foreclosure and eviction cleanout Pueblo](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo), and it moves at the court's pace, not ours.

How the Work Actually Runs

Day one, hours 1–2: walk the property, tape off a staging zone, establish one clean pathway in and out, and set the keep/donate/dispose stations. Hours 3–8: bulk removal of obvious dispose material — the fastest and most visibly satisfying stretch, usually 50–60% of total volume.

Day two onward: the slow half. Sorting layers, pulling appliances (refrigerant units need proper handling — see [appliance removal Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo)), mattresses, and the exterior. Final pass is a broom-clean sweep and a walkthrough with you before we invoice.

Two practical requests that speed everything up: designate one decision-maker, and pre-mark five to ten items as absolute keeps. Crews stall out when four family members give four different answers about the same box.
