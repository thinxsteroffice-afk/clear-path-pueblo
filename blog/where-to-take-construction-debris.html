WHERE TO TAKE CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS

Pueblo West doesn't have its own construction debris facility. Your three real options, all in Pueblo proper, are the Southside Landfill on Lime Road, the City of Pueblo Transfer Station on Erie Avenue, and Waste Connections' Pueblo Landfill on Highway 47 — expect roughly $45–$75 per pickup load, $18–$32 per ton for clean fill and mixed C&D, and a 20–35 minute drive each way from the Purcell/Highway 50 area. Clean concrete, brick, and asphalt go to a different pile than shingles and drywall, and mixing them can double your gate price. Everything below is the detail those gate attendants won't have time to explain while there are six trucks behind you.

The Actual Facilities Within Driving Distance of Pueblo West

You have no in-district option. Pueblo West Metro District runs a residential trash contract and a seasonal slash/brush drop-off, and neither accepts construction and demolition debris. The moment you tear out a bathroom, you are hauling into Pueblo County's system.

  • Southside Landfill (Lime Road, south Pueblo) — the workhorse for mixed C&D. Roughly 22 miles from the Pueblo West Sheriff's substation, about 28 minutes via Highway 50 East to I-25 South. Minimum charges typically start near $35 and scale by weight past the first ton.
  • City of Pueblo Transfer Station (Erie Avenue) — closer for East Pueblo West residents, but it is a transfer station, not a landfill, so per-ton rates run higher and load restrictions are tighter. Good for a single pickup load of drywall and trim. Bad for a 6,000-pound concrete load.
  • Waste Connections Pueblo Landfill (Highway 47 corridor) — the one most contractors use for volume. Commercial accounts get scale-weight billing; cash walk-ups get a flat minimum that punishes small loads.
  • Concrete/asphalt recyclers along the Highway 50 and Santa Fe corridors — several aggregate yards accept clean concrete, brick, and asphalt at $8–$15 per ton, sometimes free if the load is large and truly clean. "Clean" means no rebar over 1/2 inch, no dirt caked on, no wire mesh, no wood.

Call ahead every single time. Pueblo-area gate hours shift seasonally, and the C&D pile at more than one of these has closed early for wind — a genuine issue when the gusts come off the mesa at 40+ mph and a shingle load starts flying.

What It Actually Costs to Haul It Yourself

Here is the arithmetic most people skip. A half-bath demo produces roughly 1,100–1,600 pounds of debris: cast iron tub at 250–350 lbs, tile and backer at 400 lbs, drywall at 1.6 lbs per square foot, plus vanity, trim, and subfloor.

  • Gate fee: $45–$60 for that load at the landfill, more at the transfer station.
  • Truck rental if you don't own one: $19.95/day plus $0.99–$1.29 per mile at the Pueblo West and Pueblo Home Depot locations. A round trip to Lime Road runs 44–50 miles, so figure $65–$85 all-in after fuel surcharge.
  • Your time: 45 minutes loading, 55 minutes round-trip driving, 15 minutes in the scale line. Call it 2 hours. Two trips is a Saturday morning gone.
  • Tarp and straps: Colorado's unsecured-load statute carries fines that start around $65 and climb sharply if debris actually leaves your vehicle. Highway 50 at 65 mph will strip an untarped drywall load in under three miles.
  • Tire and suspension math: a half-ton pickup rated for 1,500 lbs payload is over its rating with 40 sheets of wet drywall or 14 square feet of poured concrete. People do it anyway. It's how you replace $400 worth of leaf spring.

Honest total for a one-load DIY job: $110–$145 and about two hours. For that same load, a hauler quote in this market lands around $250–$400. The DIY math genuinely wins on small jobs.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Call

This is the part a brochure leaves out, so here it is straight.

Don't hire a junk removal company if your load is under about 1/4 truck and you own a pickup. One vanity, a toilet, and three bags of tile is a $45 dump run. Any hauler in Pueblo County, us included, has a minimum around $125–$175 because we're paying for a truck, two people, insurance, and the same tipping fee you'd pay. Paying $150 to avoid a $45 errand is a bad trade unless your time is worth more than $50/hour or you physically can't lift.

Don't hire us for clean concrete, brick, or dirt if you can find a recycler yourself. Concrete is heavy and cheap to tip — $8–$15 per ton at an aggregate yard. Our price on a concrete load reflects weight, and weight is brutal. If you have a clean 6-yard pile of broken patio with no rebar, calling three aggregate yards will beat our number most of the time. We'll still do it, and [concrete rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is a real service line for us, but you should know the alternative exists before you book.

Don't hire anyone if you're mid-project with debris still accumulating. Paying for two or three pickups on a kitchen remodel that's spread over five weeks costs more than a 10-yard roll-off sitting in your driveway for $340–$480 for the rental period. Roll-offs beat on-demand haulers on any job producing more than about 4 cubic yards over multiple weeks. We are an on-demand service, and on-demand is the wrong tool for a slow-drip remodel.

Don't expect us to touch certain materials. Asbestos-suspect flooring, popcorn ceiling scrapings from a pre-1980 house, and friable pipe insulation require a CDPHE-certified abatement contractor, not us. Testing runs $75–$250 per sample through a Colorado lab, and every legitimate hauler in the state will refuse the load without a clear result. Same with wet paint, solvents, treated pole ends, and anything that was once a fuel tank. If a hauler tells you they'll "just take it," you've found the one who'll dump it in an arroyo off Burnt Mill Road and leave your name on the paperwork.

Our pricing has a soft spot on tight-access jobs. Pueblo West lots are big, but a lot of them have 200-foot driveways, gravel that turns to soup after a monsoon afternoon, and gates too narrow for a 15-foot box truck. If we have to hand-carry debris 150 feet, the labor line goes up and you may feel the price is unfair for the volume. Say so on the phone before we roll — a wheelbarrow path is a pricing fact, not a surprise.

> The cheapest debris removal in Pueblo West is the load you sort before anyone quotes it. Separating clean concrete from mixed C&D on the ground, before a truck shows up, moves material from the $32/ton pile to the $12/ton pile. Nobody does this and it's the single biggest lever on the bill.

Sorting: The Step That Changes Your Price by 40%

Gate attendants price by what they see. A pile that's 80% clean concrete with a few 2x4s thrown on top is priced as mixed C&D — the expensive category. Pull the wood off and you've reclassified the whole load.

  • Clean fill (concrete, brick, block, unpainted masonry, rock, dirt): cheapest tier, $8–$15/ton at recyclers, sometimes accepted free as fill.
  • Clean wood (untreated dimensional lumber, pallets): some yards grind it, occasionally at a reduced rate.
  • Mixed C&D (drywall, shingles, insulation, carpet, laminate, treated wood): $22–$32/ton, no way around it.
  • Metal (rebar, ductwork, copper, appliances, siding): pull this out entirely. Pueblo-area scrap yards paid roughly $0.08–$0.12/lb for shred steel and $2.90–$3.60/lb for #1 copper through 2025–2026 pricing cycles. A gutted HVAC system and 60 feet of ductwork can offset a full dump run.
  • Shingles: some facilities charge a surcharge for asphalt shingles because of the tonnage. A single-layer tear-off on a 1,800 sq ft Pueblo West ranch is 5.5–7 tons — expect $150–$230 in tip fees alone.

Shingles catch people out more than anything else here. Homeowners see one flat roof surface and imagine a couple of pickup loads. It's five to seven pickup loads at legal payload, which is why the roofers all use dumpsters.

Pueblo West Specifics Nobody Puts in Writing

The high desert changes the calculus in ways a generic guide won't cover.

Wind. Gusts across the mesa routinely hit 40–55 mph in March and April. An untarped load of insulation or shingles isn't just a fine risk — it's your neighbor's yard. Tarp everything, and if the forecast shows sustained 35+, move the run to a different day. More than one Pueblo West dump run has ended with someone chasing OSB across Highway 50.

Covenants and timelines. Pueblo West Metro District nuisance rules mean a debris pile visible from the road can generate a complaint faster than most people expect. Neighbors call. If your remodel is going to sit for six weeks, keep the pile behind the house or in a container, not stacked at the end of a 1-acre lot where every passing car sees it.

Caliche and soil. Excavation spoil out here often includes caliche hardpan — heavy, dense, and priced as dirt rather than fill at some gates. If you're digging footings or a septic line, get a weight estimate first. A 10-yard load of local soil runs 13–15 tons wet, well beyond what most homeowners picture. That's [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) territory, with equipment sized for it.

Distance penalty on repeat trips. Everything in Pueblo West costs an extra 20–30 minutes each way versus a Pueblo city address. That's a rounding error on one trip and a full workday across six. The break-even against hiring a hauler arrives faster out here than it does for someone living on Northern Avenue.

Where Hiring Actually Pencils Out

  • Post-demo cleanup over 3 cubic yards — the point where DIY becomes four or more trips.
  • Heavy material with no truck — a cast iron tub, a 400-pound hot tub shell, or slab sections you can't lift alone.
  • Deadline pressure — closing, inspection, or a rental turnover where losing a Saturday costs more than the fee.
  • Anything requiring machinery — collapsed sheds, buried concrete, footings. Hand tools stop working fast, and [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) exist for exactly that.
  • Mixed loads you don't want to sort — you're paying for someone else to do the sorting math, which is legitimate value if you'd rather not.

For jobs that fit that profile, [construction debris removal in Pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) covers the haul, the sorting, and the tipping fees in one number, and our [service area](/areas) includes all of Pueblo West with no travel surcharge inside the district.

Quick Decision Rule

Under 1/4 truck and you own a pickup: drive it yourself. Clean concrete only, and you have time to call around: find a recycler. Multi-week remodel producing steady debris: rent a roll-off. Over 3 yards, mixed material, or heavy items you can't safely lift, or you need it gone this week: that's when a call makes sense.

Whichever way you go, weigh your load before you load it, tarp it before you drive it, and separate the concrete from everything else. Those three habits will save more money than any single phone call.
