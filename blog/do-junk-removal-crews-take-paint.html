DO JUNK REMOVAL CREWS TAKE PAINT?

Yes, but only under specific conditions, and the answer depends entirely on what kind of paint you have. ClearPath Junk Removal and most reputable haulers in Pueblo West will take fully dried latex paint in its can, and empty cans of any type. We will not take liquid paint of any kind, oil-based paint, stains, thinners, or aerosol spray cans, because those are classified as household hazardous waste and Colorado landfills reject them at the gate. The good news: latex paint is the easy case, you can dry it out yourself for about $8, and Colorado runs a free statewide paint recycling program that covers the rest.

Here is the part most articles on this topic skip: the decision is not really ours. It is made at the landfill scale house.

What "Paint" Means to a Junk Hauler

When you tell us you have paint in the garage, we hear four different problems:

  • Latex or acrylic, fully dried and solid. Yes. Goes on the truck as ordinary solid waste. No surcharge.
  • Latex or acrylic, still liquid or gooey in the middle. No. Even a can that looks skinned over on top is usually liquid underneath.
  • Oil-based, alkyd, enamel, stain, varnish, shellac, lacquer. No. These are ignitable hazardous waste and never become "safe to landfill" by drying.
  • Aerosol spray cans, thinners, strippers, two-part epoxies, roof tar. No, and these are the ones that get haulers fined.

An empty can is different from an empty-looking can. If you can tip it and nothing moves, and a putty knife scrapes a hard surface rather than a skin, it is dry.

The Real Gatekeeper Is the Scale House

Colorado solid waste regulations prohibit permitted landfills from accepting free liquids. The standard the facility uses is the EPA Paint Filter Liquids Test, Method 9095B: a sample goes into a mesh filter, and if any liquid passes through in five minutes, the material fails and the load is rejected.

That test is why "just throw it in the truck, nobody will notice" is bad advice. A rejected load means we haul 15 cubic yards of your neighbor's garage back out, sort it on the ground, and pay a second tipping fee. Some facilities issue a written violation for the hauler's account. A single half-gallon of wet Behr semi-gloss can cost a small company $200 to $400 in wasted time and fees.

> We are not being fussy about your paint. We are being fussy about the one thing that can get an entire truckload turned away at the gate.

This is also why you should be skeptical of any hauler who cheerfully says "sure, we'll take anything." They are either charging you for a hazardous waste service they are not licensed to perform, or they are planning to hide it in the load.

Drying Latex Paint Yourself, Pueblo West Edition

Our climate is genuinely an advantage here. Pueblo West sits around 4,700 feet, gets roughly 12 inches of precipitation a year, and sees 300-plus days of sun. Summer afternoon humidity regularly drops under 25 percent. Paint that would take two weeks to cure in Houston sets up fast here.

Three methods, cheapest first:

  • Air dry. Pull the lid off, set the can in a shaded, ventilated spot in the garage or under a covered patio, out of reach of kids, pets, and wind. A can that is one-third full or less will usually be solid in 3 to 5 days from May through September. Do not do this in direct sun on a 95-degree day, which skins the surface and traps liquid underneath.
  • Kitty litter. A 20-pound bag of cheap clay litter runs $6 to $10 at any Pueblo West grocery or hardware store and will solidify 3 to 4 gallons of paint. Stir in litter until the mix has the texture of oatmeal, then leave the lid off for a few hours.
  • Commercial paint hardener. Packets cost $3 to $5, handle up to one gallon each, and set in about 10 minutes. Worth it if you have two or three cans and no patience.

Leave the lid off when you set the can out for pickup. That is the visual cue that tells our crew, and the landfill, that the contents are cured.

If you have more than about eight gallons of liquid latex, skip all three. Drying that much paint is a multi-week project and you will run through $40 of litter.

Colorado's PaintCare Program Is Free, and Underused

Colorado has had a paint stewardship program since 2015. You already paid for it. Every container of architectural paint sold in the state carries a recovery fee at the register, historically around $0.45 for containers between a half pint and a gallon, $0.95 for a one-gallon can, and $1.95 for containers over one gallon up to five gallons. Fees and site lists change, so confirm current numbers at paintcare.org before you load the truck.

What that fee buys you: free drop-off, usually at participating paint and hardware retailers. The standard limit is five gallons per visit, per site, and it is for latex and oil-based paint, stains, varnishes, shellacs, and sealers, in original containers with legible labels.

Practical notes for Pueblo West:

  • The drop-off sites are in Pueblo proper, roughly a 10 to 15 minute drive east on Highway 50 from the McCulloch and Purcell area. Call the specific store first. Retail sites set their own hours for paint intake and can be temporarily full.
  • PaintCare does not take aerosol spray cans, solvents, thinners, paint strippers, caulk, adhesives, roof coatings, or two-component industrial coatings. Those go to a Pueblo County household hazardous waste collection event, which are held periodically. Check with the county before you drive anything over.
  • Leaking, unlabeled, or rusted-through cans are refused. Repackaging into a labeled, sealed container of the same paint type is acceptable at most sites.

When Hiring Us for Paint Is a Waste of Your Money

This is the section that costs us the job, and you should read it before you call.

Do not book a junk removal truck if paint is all you have. If your entire problem is nine cans in a garage corner, a minimum-load junk removal charge in the Pueblo market generally starts somewhere in the $100 to $175 range depending on the company and the drive. Nine cans fit in your trunk. Two PaintCare trips cost you an hour and a tank of gas that you were burning anyway on a Costco run. Paying a truck to move dried paint is paying truck rates for a task a hatchback solves.

We cannot solve your oil-based problem at any price. ClearPath is not a licensed hazardous waste transporter. There is no upcharge that changes that. If you have five gallons of old alkyd enamel and a coffee can of lacquer thinner, no amount of money gets it on our truck. You need PaintCare for the paint and a county collection event for the solvents.

Drying is on you, and it is the failure mode that costs a return trip. The most common way a paint job goes sideways is a homeowner who dried the top two inches of a mostly full five-gallon bucket. Our crew tests it with a stir stick, finds liquid, and leaves it. You then wait for the next appointment. Give partial five-gallon buckets two to three weeks, or split the contents across shallow cardboard flats to speed it up.

Pre-1978 homes need a different conversation. Pueblo West's original McCulloch-era subdivisions date to 1969, so early homes here fall inside the lead paint window. Sealed cans of old lead-based paint are a PaintCare or hazardous waste question. Lead paint chips, dust, and debris from sanding or demo are a regulated abatement issue, not a junk removal issue, and you want an EPA RRP-certified contractor rather than us.

Never pour it out. Much of Pueblo West runs on septic. Latex paint solids will foul a leach field, and repairs run into the thousands. Paint poured on the ground or into a street inlet reaches the arroyos and the Arkansas River. This is the single worst option available to you and it is also, quietly, the most common.

Ninety-Second Checklist

  • Sort by type first. Latex on the left, oil-based, stains, and aerosols on the right.
  • Latex, mostly empty: lid off, shaded garage, 3 to 5 days in summer. Then it rides with us.
  • Latex, mostly full: kitty litter or a $4 hardener packet today, or drive it to a PaintCare site.
  • Oil-based, stains, varnish: PaintCare, five gallons per visit, original labeled cans.
  • Aerosols, thinners, strippers, roof tar: county household hazardous waste event only.
  • Nothing goes down a drain, into a septic system, or onto the ground.

Handle paint the cheap way and save the truck for the things that actually need one.
