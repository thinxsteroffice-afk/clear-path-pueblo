SHED DEMOLITION COST: $275–$1,400

Shed demolition in Pueblo West runs $275 to $1,400 for most residential jobs, fully hauled away. A small metal or vinyl shed (8x8 to 8x10) with nothing inside and no slab under it lands at $275–$450. A standard 10x12 wood shed with asphalt shingles runs $450–$750. A 12x16 or 12x20 lofted barn-style building runs $800–$1,400. Two-story or garage-sized outbuildings (16x24 and up) start around $1,500 and climb past $3,000. Removing a concrete slab underneath adds $3–$7 per square foot — usually $350–$900 on a typical shed pad. Those numbers assume driveway access, which most Pueblo West lots have. Below is what actually drives the price, and when you shouldn't hire anyone at all.

What You're Actually Paying For

The shed itself is rarely the expensive part. A 10x12 wood shed is maybe 900–1,300 pounds of lumber, shingles, and hardware. Two people with a reciprocating saw and a pry bar can flatten it in 90 minutes. What you're paying for is the other three-quarters of the job:

  • Disposal by weight. Construction and demolition debris in the Pueblo area typically tips at $40–$65 per ton, with a minimum charge that means a half-empty truck still costs you. Call ahead for current rates — they change.
  • Truck trips. A 12x16 shed with shingles and a plywood floor usually fills a 14-foot dump bed to the rails. Bigger buildings mean two trips, and the second trip is drive time plus another gate fee.
  • Shingles. Three-tab asphalt weighs roughly 230 pounds per square (100 sq ft). A 12x20 shed with two layers of shingles is carrying 550+ pounds of roofing alone — pure disposal weight with no scrap value.
  • Nails, screws, and the pad prep. Somebody picks up the hardware afterward. A shed floor sheds several hundred fasteners into the dirt, and a flat tire on your mower next spring costs more than the cleanup.
  • The slab. This is the swing factor, and it deserves its own section.

The Concrete Slab Is the Real Number

Homeowners quote us the shed dimensions and forget the pad. A 10x12 slab poured 4 inches thick is 1.5 cubic yards of concrete — roughly 5,900 pounds. If it's wire-mesh reinforced or has a thickened edge (common on Pueblo West pours because of the caliche and expansive clay), figure closer to 7,000 pounds.

That's a jackhammer or a hydraulic breaker on a skid steer, plus a truck rated to haul it. Clean concrete can sometimes go to a recycler cheaper than mixed debris, which is why we separate it on site instead of throwing it in with the lumber — it's usually a $75–$200 difference on a single slab. We break out slabs as part of [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo), and on larger pads we bring [skid steer equipment](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) rather than swinging a breaker for six hours.

> If you're going to rebuild in the same footprint, leave the slab. Paying $600 to remove a pad and then $1,400 to re-pour one the same size is the most common money-burn we see in Pueblo West.

Pueblo West Specifics That Change the Price

This market has quirks that don't show up on national cost calculators.

Access is usually good, and that saves you money. Most Pueblo West lots run a half-acre to a full acre with wide frontage and no alley bottleneck. We can back a truck within 20 feet of the shed on maybe 8 out of 10 jobs. In older Pueblo neighborhoods with 30-foot lots and narrow alleys, the same shed costs $150–$250 more because everything gets carried. If your shed sits behind a locked side gate narrower than 36 inches, tell us — that's a hand-carry job.

Wind dictates the schedule. Spring gusts here regularly hit 45–60 mph. We don't start a demo we can't finish, because a half-stripped shed with loose siding is a projectile aimed at your neighbor's truck. If the forecast is ugly, we'd rather move you to the next day than leave a structure open overnight.

Posts are set deep. Anything post-framed here is typically sunk 24–36 inches into caliche and backfilled with concrete. Each post footing is a 60–120 pound chunk that has to come out or get cut off below grade. On a 12x20 pole-framed building with six posts, that's an extra 45 minutes and several hundred pounds.

Check with Pueblo County first, not the City. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, so demolition questions go to Pueblo County Planning and Development — not City of Pueblo permitting. Small detached accessory structures often don't require a demo permit, but if your shed has electrical service run to it, that circuit needs to be killed at the panel and capped properly, and that may require an electrical permit. One phone call, five minutes, before demo day. Also check your Pueblo West Metro District covenants if you're planning what replaces it.

Mice and snakes are not a joke. Old sheds here are deer mouse condos, and hantavirus is a genuine Colorado risk. Don't dry-sweep droppings — wet them down with a 1:10 bleach solution, let it sit five minutes, and wear a mask. Rattlesnakes den under slabs and wood floors from about April through October. We move slow on the first lift for a reason.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the section most contractors leave out. Here are the cases where calling us is the wrong move:

  • Small, empty metal shed and you own a truck. An 8x8 metal shed is roughly 40 sheet-metal panels and a frame, held together with a few hundred bolts. Two people, a nut driver, gloves, and three hours. Disposal might run you $40–$70. Paying us $300 for that is paying for convenience, not capability. Be honest about whether you'll actually do it — but if you will, do it.
  • The shed is structurally sound and you're just tired of it. Sell it instead. A solid 10x12 Tuff Shed or similar resells on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo County for $600–$1,500, and shed-moving outfits will relocate one for $400–$800. Instead of paying $650 to demolish it, you can net $400+. Test it first: kick the bottom plate at all four corners. If the sill is punky or the floor flexes, it can't be lifted and demo is your only option. If it's solid, list it before you call us.
  • You're filing a wind or hail claim. Demolish before the adjuster documents it and you've likely torched the claim. Photos, adjuster, claim number, *then* demo. We've watched people lose $3,000+ in coverage to save four days.
  • You suspect asbestos. Cement-board (transite) siding and some old roofing panels on structures from the 1960s and 1970s can contain asbestos. Colorado's rules treat demolition differently than a repair, and a certified inspection typically runs $300–$600. We are not an abatement contractor. If the panels are gray, brittle, and cement-like, stop and call a licensed abatement firm — hiring us would be the wrong call and we'll tell you so on the phone.
  • Your budget is under $200. Our practical floor for a two-person crew, a truck, fuel, and a dump run is around $250. If that doesn't fit, rent a dump trailer for $80–$120 a day and do it yourself. We'd rather say that than pretend.
  • The shed is packed to the ceiling. Demo pricing assumes an empty structure. Twenty years of paint cans, tires, and boxes is a separate job that can cost more than the demolition — sometimes $400–$900 on top. Tires and paint have their own disposal rules and fees. Empty it yourself if you can, or price it as a [garage and shed cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) up front so nobody's surprised.
  • A shared fence or a gas line runs along the wall. Mechanical demo isn't safe there. Hand demolition roughly doubles the labor hours, and if the numbers don't work for you, that's a legitimate reason to walk away.

The Cost Nobody Quotes You

After a 12x16 shed comes out, you own 192 square feet of bare, compacted dirt. In Pueblo West that patch grows goatheads and kochia by the following July unless you do something with it. Budget for it now:

  • Gravel: about 3 cubic yards for a 2-inch layer over that footprint, roughly $45–$70 per yard delivered.
  • Landscape fabric plus mulch: cheaper up front, but expect to redo it in 3–5 years under this UV load.
  • Grading: if the slab held water against your foundation, fixing the slope matters more than the cover. That's [dirt work and grading](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo), usually a 1–2 hour add.

How Long It Takes

  • 8x10 metal shed, no slab: 1.5–2 hours, one trip.
  • 10x12 wood shed, shingled, wood floor: 3–4 hours, one trip.
  • 12x20 lofted barn: 5–7 hours, two trips.
  • Slab removal: add 3–5 hours and a separate concrete haul.
  • Scheduling: most Pueblo West shed jobs go out 2–5 days from the call. Same-week is normal; same-day happens when a cancellation opens up.

Scrap value doesn't move the needle, so don't let anyone tell you it does. A 10x12 metal shed is maybe 350 pounds of light steel — at typical regional scrap rates that's $15–$25, less than the fuel to the yard. We take it into account, but it's a rounding error, not a discount.
