MATTRESS DISPOSAL & RECYCLING SITES

There is no mattress recycling drop-off site in Pueblo West, and there isn't one in Pueblo either. The nearest facility that actually dismantles mattresses and sells off the steel, foam, and wood is in the Denver metro, roughly 115 miles north, which is a 230-mile round trip. So your real choices here are four: the Pueblo County landfill or a transfer station for a bulky-item fee of about $20 to $35 per piece, free haul-away from the retailer delivering your new bed, a self-haul to Denver if recycling matters more than money, or a pickup from a local hauler like us at roughly $75 to $125 for the first mattress. This page explains which one fits, including the times you should not call us.

Why Colorado Has No Free Mattress Recycling Drop-Off

Four states run producer-funded mattress recycling programs where you drop a mattress at a participating site and pay nothing: California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon. Those programs are paid for by a recycling fee added to every mattress sold in the state, usually $10.50 to $23, collected at the register. Colorado has never passed one.

That single fact explains almost everything confusing about mattress disposal down here. Colorado does have a producer responsibility law for packaging and paper, passed in 2022, but mattresses are not covered by it. No fee at the register means no funded network of drop-off sites, which means every mattress recycler in this state has to survive on scrap revenue and gate fees alone.

Scrap revenue is thin. A queen innerspring holds about 25 pounds of steel. At the shredded scrap prices Front Range yards have been paying, that coil is worth somewhere around $2 to $4. Stripping the mattress to get it takes a worker 10 to 20 minutes with a box cutter. The math never closes on scrap alone, which is why every recycler charges an intake fee of roughly $15 to $40 per unit even though 75 to 80 percent of the mattress by weight leaves their building as a sellable commodity.

> If a recycling site near you is free, someone else paid for it. In Colorado, nobody did.

The Pueblo West Wrinkle Most People Miss

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no city sanitation department and no municipal bulk-item pickup day. Trash service here is a private subscription you arrange yourself, and almost none of those residential contracts include mattresses. Your hauler will leave a queen set at the curb and tag it, and it will sit there.

That is different from how it works in a lot of the country, and it is why the "check with your city" advice on national mattress disposal pages is useless in the 81007 zip code. There is no city to check with. Trying to sneak a mattress into a curbside cart is also a non-starter because a folded queen is roughly 23 cubic feet of compressed volume, more than most 96-gallon carts hold.

Dumping it out on the mesa is the expensive version of this mistake. Colorado's littering statute starts around $500 for a first offense and climbs from there for repeat or commercial dumping, and Pueblo County pursues cleanup costs on top of the fine. A mattress with a delivery tag or a moving label on it is not hard to trace back.

Your Four Options, With Honest Numbers

Option 1: Self-haul to the landfill. Load it, drive it, pay the gate. Budget a bulky-item charge in the $20 to $35 range per mattress and per box spring, plus a minimum load fee if you show up with almost nothing. Hours are limited and most local disposal sites are closed Sunday, so call the scale house the morning you go. Cheapest real option if you already own a truck.

Option 2: Retailer haul-away. If a new bed is being delivered, ask about removal of the old set at the point of sale, not on delivery day. Most mattress retailers serving Pueblo will do it for free or for $25 to $50 when it is written on the order. Delivery crews frequently refuse to take a set that was never added to the ticket, and once the truck leaves, that deal is gone.

Option 3: Drive it to Denver. A commercial mattress recycler in the metro will take it for an intake fee, usually $15 to $40, and actually break it down. The round trip is about 230 miles. In a half-ton truck getting 15 miles per gallon, that is roughly 15 gallons of fuel, call it $45 to $55, plus 4 to 5 hours of your day. You are spending about $70 to $95 all-in to keep 25 pounds of steel and some foam out of a landfill cell.

Option 4: Call a hauler. We handle the lifting, the stairs, the truck, and the disposal fees. See [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for what that covers.

When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out, so here it is plainly.

  • You own a truck and a free Saturday. One mattress, one box spring, a landfill fee under $70 total, and about 90 minutes of your time beats paying us. Hire this out when you have four mattresses, a second-story staircase, a bad back, or a closing date. Do not hire it out for a single twin you could throw in the bed of an F-150.
  • A new bed is being delivered next week. Wait for it. Free retailer haul-away is better than anything we can quote you, and it is the single most common reason we tell someone on the phone not to book.
  • You want a guaranteed recycling certificate. We cannot promise every mattress gets dismantled. With no recycler closer than the Front Range, the economics of a dedicated 230-mile run for two or three units do not work, and any Pueblo hauler telling you otherwise on a $95 job is telling you a story. We route what we can when we have a load and a run heading north, and we are straight with you when a piece is landfill-bound. If certified recycling is non-negotiable, drive it yourself to Denver and get the receipt in your hand.
  • You have one item and a $40 budget. Our minimum exists because a truck, fuel, insurance, two people, and a disposal ticket cost the same whether we pick up one mattress or six. Nobody in this market can put a crew in your driveway for $40. A page that hints otherwise is baiting you into a phone call.
  • The mattress is soaked, moldy, or has been outside since spring. Water changes this from a mattress problem to a hazmat-adjacent problem. Recyclers reject wet units outright because mold contaminates the foam bale, and even a landfill run gets heavier and messier. A queen that sat through a Pueblo West monsoon season can gain 40 to 60 pounds of water weight. We will still take it, but expect a surcharge and no recycling path.
  • You are already renting a dumpster for a bigger project. If a 20-yard roll-off is sitting in your driveway for a remodel, the marginal cost of throwing a mattress in it is close to zero, as long as your dumpster company allows mattresses. Many charge $25 to $40 extra per unit. Ask first.

The Condition Rules That Decide Everything

Two conditions change your options more than anything else.

Bed bugs. Wrap the mattress in plastic before it leaves the bedroom, not after it is in the hallway. A dedicated mattress bag runs $10 to $20 at any hardware store. Sealed units go straight to disposal and are never recycled or donated. Tell your hauler on the phone. A crew that finds out in your doorway may decline the job, and reputable companies charge more because the truck needs treatment afterward.

Donation. Most Colorado thrift operations, including the majority of Goodwill and Habitat ReStore locations, will not accept a used mattress at all, and state health rules make resale of used bedding tightly restricted. A near-new mattress in a sealed condition sometimes finds a home through a local shelter or a Pueblo West buy-nothing group within 48 hours. Anything with a stain, a sag, or a smell will not, and listing it for a week only delays the inevitable while it sits in your garage.
