JUNK REMOVAL AND MOVING SERVICES

Short answer: ClearPath Junk Removal handles the junk side of a Pueblo West move. We haul off what you're leaving behind: the garage overflow before you pack, the furniture that won't fit the new place, and everything left in the house after the moving truck drives off. We don't load your belongings and drive them to your new home. In Colorado that job belongs to a household goods mover registered with the Public Utilities Commission (PUC), and you should hire one. Most Pueblo West moves go best with both: we clear out the stuff you aren't keeping, often 2–5 days before the movers come, so they're only paid to move what you want. A typical pre-move cleanout here runs about $350–$800, depending on how much of our truck it fills.

Why junk removal and moving are two different jobs

People search "junk removal and moving services" hoping one company can do it all. A few try, but there's a real legal and practical line:

  • Movers take your belongings from Point A to Point B. In Colorado, anyone moving household goods for pay needs a PUC permit, plus the insurance and liability rules that come with it. That protection matters when your dresser is riding in someone else's truck.
  • Junk removal takes things *out* of your life. Items go to donation, recycling, or the landfill. Nothing gets delivered to your new address.

When one outfit claims to do both, ask for its PUC household goods permit number. If it can't give you one, it's a hauler offering to move your stuff without the coverage a licensed mover carries. We're a junk removal company and say so plainly. What we're very good at is making the move cheaper and faster.

How a pre-move purge cuts your moving bill

Movers charge by time, by weight, or both, so everything you don't move is money you keep.

  • Local moves (Pueblo West to Pueblo, Cañon City, or Colorado Springs) usually bill by the hour. A 2–3 person crew in southern Colorado commonly runs $120–$200 per hour. Taking 3–4 hours of loading and unloading off the job saves roughly $360–$800.
  • Long-distance moves bill mostly by weight. A common rule of thumb is about 1,000 pounds per furnished room, so a 3-bedroom house often weighs in at 7,500–10,000 pounds. An old sectional (150–250 lb), a treadmill (200–300 lb), and a garage full of boxed "someday" items can add up to 1,000+ pounds you'd pay to ship and then throw out at the other end.
  • Military PCS moves: plenty of Fort Carson families live in Pueblo West, about 45 miles south of post. Your household goods move is capped by a weight allowance set by rank and dependents. Going over the cap gets billed to you as excess weight. If your last move came in near the limit, purge before the packers show up, not after.

What it costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by volume: how much of the truck your items fill. Labor, loading, and disposal are included. These are typical ranges for our area. Your actual price is set when we see the items, before any work starts.

  • Single item or minimum load: about $100–$175
  • Quarter truck (a room's worth of furniture): about $200–$325
  • Half truck (a packed one-car garage bay): about $350–$475
  • Full truck (roughly 15 cubic yards, a large garage or small-house cleanout): about $550–$800
  • Add-ons: refrigerators and freezers often add $25–$75, because refrigerant has to be recovered under EPA rules before disposal. Mattresses can add a disposal fee, and TVs and monitors can too. Colorado has banned TVs, computers, and monitors from landfills since July 1, 2013, so they have to go to an electronics recycler.

Timing: leases and closings cluster at the end of the month, so the last 3–4 days of any month book up first. If your move-out date is the 31st, call by the 20th. Midweek slots early in the month are the easiest to get on short notice.

When this isn't worth it, and who shouldn't hire us

This is the part most junk removal pages skip. These are the cases where paying us is the wrong call:

  • You only have a pickup bed's worth and you own a pickup. If it's one couch and a few bags, an afternoon trip to the landfill yourself costs far less than our minimum. You're paying us for labor and a truck you already have.
  • Your stuff has real resale value. Usable furniture, tools, a riding mower, a newer washer and dryer: if you have 2–3 weeks before the move, selling on Marketplace or holding a garage sale can put money in your pocket instead of costing you. We donate usable items when we can, but donation doesn't pay you.
  • You need your belongings moved, not removed. We won't transport your things to your new house. Doing that without a PUC household goods permit and the right coverage is the wrong way to move a household, and if something breaks you'd have little recourse. Hire a licensed mover.
  • Hazardous materials. We can't take liquid paint, solvents, pesticides, motor oil, propane tanks, pool chemicals, or anything that might contain asbestos (old popcorn ceilings, some pre-1980s flooring and siding). Those need household hazardous waste drop-off or a licensed abatement contractor. If a big share of your garage is chemicals, we only solve part of the problem.
  • Biohazard situations. We do [hoarding cleanouts](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo), but if there's human or animal waste, mold, or sharps, you need a remediation company first. We can come in after they're done.
  • You're calling the day before closing at month's end. We work hard to fit people in, but the last 72 hours of a month are the hardest window to get. If we can't make your deadline, we'll tell you up front rather than promise and miss.
  • You haven't decided what to keep. Our crew loads quickly, often clearing a full truck in under 2 hours. If the family is still sorting, you'll either rush decisions you regret or pay for a return trip. Sort first, then book.

One more limitation to name: volume pricing is an estimate until we see the pile. If you describe "a few things in the garage" over the phone and we find a full truckload, the on-site price will be higher. Photos sent ahead of time are the best way to get an accurate number.

How to run a move-plus-cleanout in Pueblo West

The order that saves the most money and stress:

1. 4–6 weeks out: walk every room, the garage, and any sheds or outbuildings. Mark items as keep, sell, donate, or haul. 2. 3–4 weeks out: list what you're selling. Get moving quotes, and ask each mover for their PUC permit number and whether they bill by hour or weight. 3. 1–2 weeks out: book the junk pickup for 3–7 days before move day. Unsold sale items can go on the same load. 4. Move day: movers take only what's going with you. 5. 1–3 days after: book a second, smaller pickup for leftovers if you need one. This is often a minimum or quarter load.

If you're also leaving behind yard junk, a dilapidated shed, or an old hot tub, which are common on Pueblo West's larger lots, bundle it into the same visit. One trip usually costs less than two. If you're only getting rid of a little, have a truck of your own, or have things worth selling, skip us and do it yourself.
