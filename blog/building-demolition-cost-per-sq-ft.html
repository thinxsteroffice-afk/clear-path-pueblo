BUILDING DEMOLITION COST PER SQ FT

Building demolition in Pueblo West runs $5–$15 per square foot for residential structures and $6–$12 per square foot for small commercial buildings, which puts most real jobs in these brackets: a 10x12 shed at $350–$900, a detached two-car garage at $3,000–$6,500, a 30x40 pole barn or metal shop at $4,500–$9,000, a single-wide mobile home at $4,000–$8,000, and a 1,200 sq ft slab-on-grade house at $9,000–$18,000. Add $5,000–$12,000 if there's a basement to remove and backfill. Asbestos survey and abatement, when required, adds $450–$1,200 for the inspection and $3–$12 per square foot for the removal — this is the line item that blows up budgets.

Below is where those numbers actually come from, and when demolition isn't worth paying anyone for.

The Real Cost Driver Isn't Knocking It Down — It's Tonnage

Most people picture the excavator and assume that's the bill. It isn't. A mini excavator with an operator runs $650–$1,100 per day in this market, and a skid steer runs $450–$800. A detached garage takes a machine about three hours to flatten.

The money is in what's left on the ground. Pueblo-area landfill tipping fees for mixed construction and demolition debris run roughly $45–$70 per ton (call ahead — rates change and some facilities have minimums). A wood-frame house generates about one ton of debris per 100 square feet, so a 1,200 sq ft house is 45–70 tons once you count roofing, drywall, and framing. That's $2,000–$4,900 in dump fees alone before anybody gets paid for their time.

This is why per-square-foot quotes vary so widely. Two identical-footprint buildings can differ by $6,000 based on what they're made of:

  • Wood frame with asphalt shingles — heavy, non-recyclable, full tipping rate
  • Metal building — light, and the steel is worth $90–$180 per ton at Pueblo scrap yards. A 30x40 steel shop yields 4–7 tons, a $400–$1,200 credit against your bill
  • Masonry or block — heaviest per square foot, and mixed masonry doesn't get the clean-concrete recycling rate
  • Clean concrete separated out — often $0–$20 per ton to recycle versus $50+ mixed, which is why separating pays
  • Plaster and lath (pre-1955 homes) — roughly double the debris weight of drywall for the same wall area

> Anyone who quotes you a demolition price without asking what the roof is made of and whether the slab is staying is guessing. Ask them what tonnage they assumed. If they can't answer, the number will move.

Pueblo West Line Items Other Towns Don't Have

Pueblo West sits on large unincorporated Pueblo County lots, and that geography creates costs that generic national demolition calculators miss entirely.

Well and septic abandonment. If the structure you're removing was served by a private well or septic system that's going out of service, Colorado requires proper abandonment. Plugging a well through a licensed contractor typically runs $800–$2,500. Pumping, crushing, and backfilling a septic tank runs $600–$1,800. Leaving either one open is both a code violation and a genuine safety hazard on a property where kids or livestock roam.

Caliche and hardpan. A lot of Pueblo West sits on cemented hardpan layers. Foundation and footing removal that would take four hours in sandy soil can take a full day here, and the excavated material often can't be reused as clean backfill.

Backfill isn't free. A 1,200 sq ft basement hole needs roughly 350–450 cubic yards of fill. Imported fill delivered runs $18–$30 per cubic yard, and compaction in lifts adds labor. That's how a $12,000 house demo becomes a $22,000 project. Our [dirt work and excavation crew](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) handles this side, but it has to be budgeted from day one, not discovered at the end.

Wind. Sustained 40+ mph gusts are routine here. A partially demolished structure left standing overnight is a liability. That constrains sequencing — a crew can't tear open a roof on Friday and come back Monday — which sometimes means paying for a compressed, fully-staffed day instead of a stretched-out cheap one.

Burning is not an option. Pueblo County open-burn rules do not permit burning demolition debris, and treated lumber, shingles, and painted wood are illegal to burn anywhere in Colorado. If a bidder mentions burning the pile to save on dump fees, walk away — the fine will exceed the savings.

Permits, Asbestos, and the 10-Day Clock

Demolition permits go through the Pueblo Regional Building Department. The permit fee itself is minor — generally under $250 depending on valuation — but it is not the thing that sets your schedule.

Colorado's asbestos rules (CDPHE Regulation 8, Part B) require an asbestos inspection before demolition on most structures, with narrow exemptions for certain single-family residential situations. Commercial, multi-unit, and most outbuildings on commercial property do not get a pass. Two numbers matter:

  • Inspection: $450–$1,200 depending on building size and number of samples
  • Notification window: typically 10 working days before demolition begins — confirm current timing with CDPHE directly, since it varies by project type

If the survey comes back positive, abatement runs $3–$12 per square foot of affected material. Popcorn ceiling and 9x9 floor tile abatement in a small house commonly lands $2,500–$9,000. That work must be done by a state-licensed abatement contractor — not by us, not by your general contractor, not by you.

Utility disconnects are the other schedule-setter. Gas disconnect through Black Hills Energy and water shutoff through the Pueblo West Metro District each take their own lead time, commonly 1–3 weeks from request. Nobody demolishes a building with a live gas line to it. Start these calls before you start collecting bids.

When Demolition Is Not Worth Paying Us For

This is the section most contractors skip. Here's where hiring a demolition crew — ours included — is the wrong call.

The building is worth more than the dirt under it. In Pueblo West, a solid detached shop or barn is a selling feature. Buyers here want a place for a truck, a tractor, and a workbench. Removing a functional 30x40 shop to gain lawn can cost you $7,000 in demolition and another $10,000–$25,000 in resale value. Get an agent's opinion before you get a demolition quote.

It's small enough to DIY. A 8x10 or 10x12 shed with an asphalt-shingle roof is a two-weekend job with a sawzall, a pry bar, and a pickup. You'll spend $150–$350 in dump fees and save $400–$600. If you own a truck and a Saturday, take the Saturday. Our [shed demolition and removal service](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) exists for people who don't have the truck, the time, or the back — not because the job is technically hard.

You have friable asbestos and no abatement contractor yet. We will not touch it, and any junk removal company that says they will is either lying or putting your family and their crew at risk. Sequence: survey first, licensed abatement second, demolition third. Calling us first just wastes both our time.

The project needs an engineered demolition plan. Multi-story commercial, structural steel over roughly 15,000–20,000 sq ft, anything with shared walls, anything requiring shoring of an adjacent structure, or anything where controlled collapse is part of the plan — that is a licensed demolition contractor with a structural engineer, not a junk removal and skid steer outfit. We'll tell you that on the phone rather than after you've paid a deposit.

The insurance claim isn't settled. On fire and storm losses, adjusters need to document the loss. Demolishing before the settlement is final can cost you far more than the demolition. Wait.

There's still a lien or mortgage on the structure. You generally cannot demolish a financed building without lender consent. Check the loan documents before you check prices.

Your budget is under $1,500 and the job is a garage. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster plus your own labor over two weekends will beat any crew's price. It'll be miserable, but it'll be cheaper, and you deserve to hear that.

How to Compare Bids Without Getting Burned

Cheap demolition bids are usually cheap because something is excluded. Make every bidder answer these in writing:

  • Is the slab or foundation included, or does it stay? Slab removal alone runs $2–$6 per square foot — a 400 sq ft garage slab is $800–$2,400. This is the single most common exclusion. [Concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is priced separately for exactly this reason.
  • Is disposal included, or billed at cost plus? "Plus dump fees" on a 60-ton house is a $3,000+ open line item.
  • Is backfill and rough grade included? A hole is not a finished job.
  • Who pulls the permit and who files the CDPHE notification?
  • What's the scrap credit policy? If they're keeping the steel, that should be reflected in the price.
  • Are they insured for demolition specifically? General liability for hauling is not the same coverage.

A useful sanity check: for a straightforward detached structure, disposal typically accounts for 30–45% of the total, labor and machine time 35–50%, and permits, mobilization, and profit the rest. A bid where disposal appears to be under 20% of the total on a wood-frame building is either mispriced or planning to leave material behind.

Realistic Timelines

  • Shed, 10x12: half a day on site
  • Detached two-car garage with slab: 1–2 days
  • 30x40 pole barn or metal shop: 2–3 days
  • 1,200 sq ft house, slab-on-grade: 3–5 days
  • Same house with basement removal and backfill: 6–10 days
  • Add to all of the above: 10 working days for asbestos notification and 1–3 weeks for utility disconnects

Winter changes the math both directions. Frozen ground in January and February makes foundation removal and backfill compaction meaningfully slower and more expensive, but crew availability is better and scheduling is faster than the April–September rush.
