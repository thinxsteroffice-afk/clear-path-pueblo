What Furniture Removal Costs in Pueblo West

Most junk haulers in Colorado price by volume: how much of the truck your items fill. A standard junk removal truck holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly the space of six to eight pickup truck beds. These are typical price ranges for the Pueblo market in 2026:

  • Single small item (end table, office chair, nightstand): $75–$100, usually the minimum charge
  • Standard sofa or loveseat: $100–$175
  • Sectional (3–5 pieces): $175–$300, depending on piece count and weight
  • Mattress and box spring set: $85–$150 (see our [mattress removal Pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page for details)
  • Recliner or lift chair: $90–$150. Power recliners with steel mechanisms can weigh 120–180 lbs each.
  • Quarter truckload (a bedroom set, for example): $175–$275
  • Half truckload: $300–$425
  • Full truckload (a whole-house furniture clearout): $500–$700

These ranges include loading, hauling, and dump or recycling fees. They assume the items can be reached without disassembly. If your quote is much higher than this, ask why. Legitimate reasons exist, like stairs, weight, or taking things apart, but a vague "handling fee" isn't one of them.

What Actually Changes Your Quote

Two couches can cost very different amounts to remove. These are the things that move the price in Pueblo West:

Access. A couch in a ground-floor living room with a straight path to the front door takes about 10 minutes to load. The same couch in a basement with a tight turn at the bottom of the stairs can take 30–45 minutes and sometimes has to be taken apart. Many Pueblo West homes on larger lots have long gravel driveways, so there's also carry distance to the truck. That's why we ask where the item is, not just what it is.

Weight, not just size. A hide-a-bed sleeper sofa weighs 150–250 lbs because of the steel frame inside. A regular sofa of the same size weighs about 80–120 lbs. Solid oak dressers and china hutches built before about 1980 can top 200 lbs. Heavy items take extra crew time and extra landfill tonnage.

Disassembly. Bunk beds, adjustable bed bases, glass-top desks and large entertainment centers often need to come apart before they fit through a doorway. Plan on 15–30 extra minutes for each large piece that needs tools.

How many pieces go at once. This is where you have the most control. Our truck is already making the trip from Pueblo, so a second item adds far less than the first. Removing one couch might cost $125. A couch, recliner, coffee table and mattress together might run $275–$325 instead of the $450+ you'd pay booking them one at a time.

> A common expensive mistake is booking furniture removal three times over six months. Walk through the whole house first, including the garage and the shed, and do it all in one visit.

When Furniture Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

A junk removal page won't usually tell you this, but in a lot of situations hiring us, or any hauler, is a waste of your money. Here they are.

You own a pickup and the item goes in one trip. If you have a truck and someone to help lift, one couch or dresser costs you some gas and whatever the landfill charges at the gate. That's usually a fraction of a $100+ hauler minimum. Paying a crew only makes sense for single items if you physically can't lift them, have no truck, or your time is worth more than the difference.

The furniture is in good shape and you're not in a hurry. A clean couch listed free on Facebook Marketplace or Nextdoor in the Pueblo area often gets picked up within 24–72 hours. Charities with pickup service, including thrift stores and home-goods reuse stores, will take usable furniture for free. Two limits apply to that route:

  • Charities almost always refuse stained, torn, pet-damaged or smoke-exposed upholstery, and most won't take mattresses at all.
  • Charity pickup windows are often scheduled 1–3 weeks out. That's too slow if you're closing on a house on Friday.

If you have time and the item is clean, try the free options first. If they don't work, call us.

You're relocating the furniture. We're not a moving company. We take furniture *away* to donation, recycling or disposal. We don't carry your dining set to your new house in Cañon City or pad-wrap an heirloom armoire. For that you need a licensed mover, and they'll charge by the hour, commonly $100–$180/hour for a two-person crew in southern Colorado.

Specialty-weight items. Upright pianos weigh 300–500 lbs, and grand pianos can go well over 600 lbs. Gun safes often run 500–1,000+ lbs. Most junk crews, including ours, will quote these individually, and some require a specialist with a piano board or stair-climbing dolly. If a company quotes a gun safe the same as a dresser without asking questions, be wary.

Bed bug infestations. If upholstered furniture has an active infestation, we need it sealed in plastic before loading, and we may decline items that can't be contained. Sealing them yourself matters, because loading infested furniture through a house can spread bugs to rooms that were clean. Treat first or wrap tightly, then call.

Cheap flat-pack furniture that comes apart easily. A particleboard bookshelf takes about 10 minutes with a screwdriver to break down. The flat panels then fit in most car trunks. Paying $75 for someone else to do that is hard to justify.

Where Your Furniture Goes After We Haul It

People ask us this often, and they should ask any company they're considering. Here's how we handle it in Pueblo West:

  • Usable pieces (solid wood tables, clean dressers, working lamps) go to local donation partners when condition allows. Realistically, that's often 20–35% of a typical household furniture load. Most old sofas are too worn for any charity to accept.
  • Metal (bed frames, recliner mechanisms, steel office desks, patio furniture) is separated for scrap recycling.
  • Mattresses are sent to recycling when possible. A mattress's steel springs and foam can be broken down and reused, unlike most upholstered pieces.
  • Everything else goes to a licensed landfill. We don't dump on the prairie. Illegal dumping on open land around Pueblo West is a real, recurring problem. A suspiciously cheap cash hauler who can't tell you where the load goes may be part of it, and in some cases the dumped item can be traced back to you.
