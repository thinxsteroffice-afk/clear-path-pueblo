HOW MUCH DOES DEMOLITION COST?

Demolition in Pueblo West runs roughly $1.50–$6 per square foot for interior tear-outs and $4,000–$18,000 for a full single-family house teardown, depending on size, foundation, and whether asbestos is present. Smaller jobs — the ones most Pueblo West homeowners actually need — price by the piece: a 10x12 wood shed is typically $450–$900 removed and hauled, a 12x16 deck $700–$1,600, a 24-foot above-ground pool $600–$1,400, and a chain-link or cedar fence line $4–$9 per linear foot. Concrete slab removal (patios, old driveways) runs $3–$8 per square foot because disposal is priced by weight, not volume. Those numbers include labor, equipment, and dump fees. What follows is where each figure comes from and where it breaks.

What Actually Drives the Price in Pueblo County

Demolition quotes look arbitrary until you know the four levers. Every one of them is measurable before anyone shows up.

  • Disposal weight. The Pueblo County landfill charges by the ton, and construction debris runs roughly $40–$60 per ton depending on the material stream. A 12x16 wood deck with railing is about 1.5–2.5 tons. A 300 sq ft concrete patio 4 inches thick is about 6 tons — the same visual footprint, four times the disposal cost. This single fact explains why concrete jobs quote higher than jobs that look bigger.
  • Access. A backyard shed reachable through a 10-foot gate gets machine help. A shed behind a 36-inch side gate with a mature elm in the way becomes hand demolition and hand-carry, which adds 40–70% to labor. Pueblo West's larger lots usually help here; the older Pueblo neighborhoods off Northern Ave usually don't.
  • Separation requirements. Clean concrete and clean metal divert cheaply or free. Mixed loads — wood, shingles, insulation, drywall in one pile — pay full mixed-debris rates. On-site sorting costs labor but often saves more in tipping fees on loads over 3 tons.
  • Structure type. Stick-built comes apart. Anything with a poured footing, buried post concrete, or a slab under it means excavation, not demolition, and that's a different line item — usually $400–$1,200 extra for a small structure with buried piers.

> A 200 sq ft concrete patio can cost more to remove than a 400 sq ft wood deck. Demolition is priced by the pound, not by the picture.

Real Price Ranges by Job Type

These are Pueblo West-area ranges for 2026, assuming reasonable access and no hazardous material.

  • Shed demolition and haul-off: $350–$1,400. An 8x10 metal shed on gravel is the low end. A 12x20 wood shed with a concrete slab is the high end, and the slab alone can be $500–$1,100 of it. See [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Deck removal: $700–$1,600 for a typical 12x16 attached deck. Add $150–$400 if the ledger board is bolted to stucco or brick and needs careful separation to avoid a repair bill.
  • Fence removal: $4–$9 per linear foot. A 150-foot cedar privacy fence with concrete-set posts lands around $900–$1,300. Pulling posts adds roughly $8–$15 each — a 20-post line is $160–$300 of that total.
  • Above-ground pool removal: $600–$1,400 for a 24-foot round, including cutting the wall, rolling the liner, and hauling the frame. Draining 13,000+ gallons is on you or adds a day.
  • Hot tub removal: $350–$700. Fiberglass shells get cut into sections; the disposal weight is low but they're awkward and often need a crane assist over a fence if the deck was built around them. See [hot tub removal pueblo](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo).
  • Interior gut (kitchen, bath, flooring): $1.50–$4 per sq ft. A 10x12 bathroom down to studs is typically $900–$1,800.
  • Detached garage teardown: $2,500–$6,500 for a single-car, more if the slab goes too. A 20x20 slab is roughly 12–15 tons of concrete — figure $1,800–$3,500 for the slab portion alone.
  • Concrete driveway or patio: $3–$8 per sq ft. Rebar-reinforced concrete sits at the top of that range because it can't simply be broken and scooped; it has to be cut. See [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Full house demolition: $4,000–$18,000 for 1,000–2,000 sq ft, before abatement, permits, and utility disconnects.

The Costs Nobody Puts in the Quote

Roughly a third of demolition budget overruns in residential work come from things that aren't demolition at all.

  • Asbestos and lead. Anything built before 1980 in Pueblo County is a real candidate for asbestos in floor tile, mastic, popcorn ceilings, siding, and duct wrap. Colorado's Regulation 8 requires an inspection by a certified building inspector before demolition of most structures, and abatement is licensed work. Testing runs $300–$800. Abatement runs $8–$25 per sq ft and can double the cost of a small interior job. No legitimate demo contractor will proceed without a clear report on a pre-1980 structure, and that isn't upselling.
  • Utility disconnects. Black Hills Energy gas disconnect and Pueblo West Metro District water shutoff have to be scheduled, and lead times of 5–15 business days are normal. A job can sit idle waiting on a meter pull.
  • Permits. Pueblo County demolition permits for structures typically run $75–$250. Sheds under 120 sq ft usually don't need one, but "usually" is not "never" — confirm with Pueblo County Planning and Development before swinging anything.
  • Septic and wells. Plenty of Pueblo West properties have them. Abandoning a septic tank properly means pumping and either crushing-and-filling or removing it: $1,200–$3,000. Skipping it is a title problem later.
  • Repair to what stays. Tearing a deck off a house exposes whatever the deck was hiding. Budget $300–$1,500 for siding, stucco patch, or paint on the newly visible wall. This is the most commonly missed line item on deck and pool jobs.

When Demolition Is Not Worth It — And Who Should Not Hire Us

This is the part most contractor pages leave out, so read it carefully.

Don't hire anyone if the structure is under about $400 of material and you have a truck and a weekend. A 6x8 metal shed on gravel is four hours of work with a reciprocating saw and a $40 dump run. Paying $350 for that is paying for convenience, and if convenience isn't worth $310 to you, do it yourself. Same for a single hot tub if you own a sawzall and can recruit three people — the cut-and-haul is genuinely doable, just miserable.

Don't demolish a structure you might be able to repair. A 12x16 deck with 4 rotten boards and a solid frame is a $400–$800 repair. Removing and rebuilding it is $700 to demo plus $6,000–$14,000 to rebuild. Get a framing carpenter's opinion before you call a demo crew. We have talked people out of teardowns and we'd rather do that than take a job you'll regret.

Don't hire a junk removal company for structural demolition. Load-bearing walls, second stories, anything over 12 feet, anything with commercial steel, and any structure with a compromised roof you'd have to work under — that's a licensed structural demolition contractor with engineered plans and different insurance. If a company quotes you for removing an interior load-bearing wall without asking about a beam and post schedule, that's a red flag regardless of who it is. We turn these down.

Don't expect us to touch abatement. We are not an asbestos or lead abatement contractor. On a pre-1980 structure, that means a testing delay and possibly a second contractor before we can start. If you need it gone by Friday and the house is from 1962, our answer may honestly be no.

Where our pricing is genuinely uncompetitive:

  • Very large concrete volumes. Above roughly 1,500 sq ft of slab, a dedicated concrete recycler with their own crusher and trucking will usually beat us by 15–30%. We'll tell you that instead of quoting it.
  • Long-duration jobs. If a project needs a 30-yard dumpster on site for three weeks and the owner is doing the labor, a $500–$800 roll-off rental beats paying a crew. Dumpster rental wins whenever your own labor is free and the timeline is loose.
  • Nothing-but-metal jobs. A large metal building or scrap-heavy structure sometimes has enough scrap value that a metal recycler will do it cheap or free. Check that first.
  • Rural properties well outside the district. Past about 25 miles from Pueblo West, travel and disposal-run time start adding $100–$250 per day of the job, and a closer outfit will usually win.

Failure modes to plan for. Weather stops demolition — Pueblo West's spring wind events routinely make partial-tear-out sites unsafe, and a job can slip 2–4 days. Buried surprises change quotes: old foundations, an unmarked irrigation line, or a slab under a slab. A fixed quote given without walking the site is a quote that will get revised. And once demolition starts, you're committed — half a shed is worse than a whole shed.

How to Get an Accurate Number in One Visit

The quotes that don't change are the ones built from measurements, not photos.

  • Measure the footprint and note the material: wood, metal, vinyl, concrete, or mixed.
  • Photograph the access path from the street or alley all the way to the structure, including gate width.
  • Find out the build year. A county assessor record takes two minutes and determines whether asbestos testing enters the picture.
  • Decide up front whether the slab, footings, and posts go too. This is the single largest swing factor and the most common source of "that's not what you quoted me."
  • Know where the debris is allowed to sit. If a pile can stage on the driveway for 24 hours, the job costs less than if everything must leave in the same trip.

The Short Version

For a Pueblo West homeowner, most demolition questions land in one of three buckets. Under $500 of work and a free weekend: do it yourself. $500–$8,000 of non-structural tear-out with disposal included: that's the range where hiring a crew with a truck, a trailer, and a skid steer makes financial sense, and it's most sheds, decks, pools, patios, fences, and interior gut jobs. Over that, or anything load-bearing, over 12 feet tall, or with a pre-1980 asbestos question: get a licensed structural demolition contractor and a testing report first, and expect the total to be 30–60% higher than the demolition line item alone once permits, abatement, and utility work are counted.

Bring measurements, a build year, and a decision about the slab, and any honest contractor in Pueblo County can give you a number that holds.
