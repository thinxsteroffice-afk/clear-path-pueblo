CO SPRINGS JUNK REMOVAL REVIEWS

Junk removal in Colorado Springs runs $150–$250 for a single-item pickup, $250–$450 for a quarter truckload, and $600–$900 for a full 15-cubic-yard load, and the review scores across the big Springs operators cluster tightly — most sit between 4.6 and 4.9 stars on Google, which means star ratings alone won't tell you much. What the reviews actually reveal, if you read past the first page, is a consistent pattern: complaints center on quoted-price drift, arrival windows blowing past four hours, and crews declining items at the curb. If you're in Pueblo West, there's a second thing those reviews won't tell you — most Colorado Springs companies either won't come 45 miles south or will charge you a travel surcharge to do it.

What Colorado Springs Reviews Actually Say (Beyond the Star Count)

We've read a lot of them, because our customers quote them at us. Here's what recurs:

  • Price changed on site. The phone quote was "$175 for a couch," the crew arrived and it became $310 once they saw the sectional was three pieces. This is the single most common one-star theme, and it's almost always a volume-vs.-item pricing mismatch rather than dishonesty.
  • The window was too wide. "Between 8 and 12" turning into 2:15 p.m. shows up constantly. Franchise dispatch systems route trucks across a 60-mile metro; a Fountain job running long pushes your Briargate job past lunch.
  • Refused items at the curb. Paint, tires, e-waste, and anything with refrigerant get declined after the truck is already there. Reviewers read that as a bait-and-switch; it's usually a landfill-acceptance issue nobody explained up front.
  • Great crews, universally. The four- and five-star reviews almost never praise the price. They praise the two guys who showed up. That's real, and it's the same in Pueblo.
  • Nobody reviews the disposal. Fewer than 1 in 50 reviews mentions where the stuff went. Which is a shame, because that's where the cost differences actually live.

> A 4.8-star average across 400 reviews tells you the crews are polite. It tells you almost nothing about whether the price you're quoted is the price you'll pay.

Why Colorado Springs Pricing Doesn't Transfer to Pueblo West

The Springs and Pueblo West are two different disposal markets, and the gap is bigger than the 45 miles between them.

Springs haulers typically tip at the Midway Landfill area facilities, where transfer-station rates for mixed municipal solid waste run in the $45–$60 per ton range depending on the load and the contract. Pueblo-area disposal for us runs closer to $35–$50 per ton on standard MSW. That's a real difference on a heavy load — a 2.5-ton concrete-and-shingle job can swing $40–$60 in tipping fees alone.

Then there's drive time. A Colorado Springs company quoting a Pueblo West address on Purcell Blvd is looking at roughly 90 minutes round trip on I-25 before they touch a single item, plus around $25–$35 in fuel for a loaded box truck at 8–10 mpg. Some absorb it. Most add a travel fee of $50–$125, and a few just decline the job. If you got a great quote from a Springs company and they're now not calling you back, that's why.

When You Should Absolutely Not Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal pages skip. Read it before you call.

Rent a dumpster instead if your project runs more than two days. A 20-yard roll-off in the Pueblo area runs about $375–$525 for a 7-day rental with 2–4 tons included. If you're gutting a bathroom over a long weekend and generating debris continuously, that dumpster beats us on cost by a wide margin. We're competitive at one visit; we're expensive at four. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling.

Haul it yourself if you have a truck and a free Saturday. Pueblo County transfer station drop-off for a pickup-bed load of household waste is typically $25–$60. If you own a half-ton, your back works, and the item fits, you're paying us $150 to save you 90 minutes. Sometimes that's a great trade. Often it isn't. A single mattress you can strap down yourself is the clearest example — don't pay us $110 for that unless lifting it is genuinely a problem.

Don't call us for these — we'll decline or have to redirect you: - Hazardous waste. Paint (unless fully dried), solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil, propane tanks. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events; that's your route, and it's usually free or under $20. - Tires. We can sometimes take them, but disposal is $8–$20 each, which makes a set of four an annoying line item you'd rather handle at a tire shop for less. - Asbestos-containing material. Popcorn ceiling, old floor tile, pipe wrap on a 1960s furnace. That requires a licensed abatement contractor and CDPHE notification. We are not that, and any hauler who says "sure, we'll grab it" is putting your liability and their crew at risk. - Anything requiring us to enter a structurally unsound building. Fire-damaged, partially collapsed, or condemned without an engineer's sign-off.

We're a poor fit if the price is the only thing you care about. There is almost always someone with a trailer and a Facebook Marketplace ad who will do it for 30–40% less. Sometimes they're excellent. Sometimes the stuff ends up in an arroyo off Highway 78 and the illegal-dumping citation traces back to a document with your name on it. Colorado illegal dumping penalties start around $500 and scale with volume. Ask any hauler — including us — for their disposal facility name. If they can't answer in one sentence, that's your answer.

Our failure modes, plainly: our arrival windows are 2 hours, not 30 minutes, and about 1 job in 12 slips past that window when a prior cleanout turns out to be twice the estimated volume. We call when it happens, but we do slip. On-site estimates can move 10–20% from a phone quote when the load is bigger than described — we'll show you the truck and the fill line before we load, and you can walk away at that point at no charge, but we're not going to pretend a sight-unseen phone number is a binding contract.

How to Read Any Hauler's Reviews Like a Pro

Whether you're evaluating a Springs company or us:

  • Sort by "lowest" and read 10. The 1-star reviews are where the operating model shows. If four of ten mention price changes, that's structural.
  • Look at review recency, not volume. A company with 600 reviews averaging 4.7 but nothing new in eight months has probably changed hands or lost its crew.
  • Ignore reviews that only praise politeness. Nice guys are table stakes.
  • Check whether the owner responds to bad reviews with specifics or with a copy-pasted "please contact us." Specifics mean somebody's actually reading.
  • Search the reviews for your item. "Piano," "hot tub," "shed," "concrete." A company great at couches may have never done a [shed demolition removal Pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) job in its life.
