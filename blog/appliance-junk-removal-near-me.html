What "Appliance Removal" Actually Includes Out Here

Pueblo West isn't Pueblo proper, and the job is different. Lots run half an acre to five acres. Driveways are long, often gravel, and frequently soft after a monsoon afternoon. Detached garages sit 60–100 feet from the road. A crew quoting from a Denver price sheet doesn't account for a 300-pound side-by-side that has to cross that gravel on a dolly with 2-inch wheels.

Here's what a real appliance haul covers:

  • Disconnect on non-hardwired units. We pull washer hoses, dryer vent, and a plug-in range. We do not cut gas lines or hardwired 240V circuits — that's a licensed trade, and we'll say so on the phone.
  • Water heater draining. A 40-gallon tank holds about 8.3 pounds per gallon, so a full one weighs roughly 330 pounds. Draining takes 15–25 minutes through a garden hose, and that time is in your quote, not a surprise line item.
  • The carry. Stairs, tight hallways, and mobile-home doorways. A lot of manufactured homes in Pueblo West have 30–32 inch interior doors; a modern French-door fridge is 35–36 inches wide. Sometimes the door comes off the hinges. That's part of the job.
  • Cleanup. Twenty years of dryer lint and mouse nest behind the unit gets swept, not left for you.
  • Documented disposal. Scrap steel to a metals yard, refrigerant recovered by a certified tech, and the rest to the transfer station.

If the appliance is one item in a bigger mess, it's usually cheaper to fold it into a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or a general [junk removal](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) load than to book it standalone.

The Refrigerant Problem Nobody Mentions in Their Ad

This is the part that separates a real hauler from a guy with a trailer, and almost no competing page explains it.

Any appliance with a sealed cooling system — refrigerator, freezer, window AC, dehumidifier, wine fridge — contains refrigerant under pressure. Under EPA Section 608, that refrigerant has to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped or crushed. Venting it is a federal violation, and the liability doesn't magically vanish because you handed the fridge to someone else. Most Front Range scrap yards will refuse a sealed-system unit without a recovery tag, which is exactly why the "free appliance pickup" guy from the classifieds sometimes dumps them on county road shoulders instead.

The chemistry matters, too:

  • Units built before roughly 1995 often run R-12, an ozone-depleting CFC with the strictest handling rules.
  • Anything from about 1995–2015 is usually R-134a.
  • Newer compact fridges and freezers increasingly use R-600a (isobutane) — which is flammable. Puncturing one in a truck bed is a genuine fire risk, not a technicality.
  • Appliances made before about 2000 may also contain mercury tilt switches in the door light, and pre-1979 units can carry PCB-containing capacitors.

> If someone offers to take your refrigerator for free and can't tell you where the refrigerant goes, you're not saving $95. You're buying a small share of somebody else's problem.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This section costs us money. Read it anyway, because half the people who call us for a single appliance shouldn't be paying us at all.

Don't pay us if the appliance still works. A functioning washer or fridge is worth $100–$300 on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo County, and it usually sells in under a week if you price it at half of retail and say "you haul." Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar nonprofits will often pick up working appliances at no charge — that's a free removal *and* a receipt. Calling us on a working unit means you paid $95 to throw away $200.

Don't pay us if you're already buying a replacement. Lowe's, Home Depot, and most local appliance dealers will haul the old unit when they deliver the new one, typically for $25–$50 or bundled free during promotions. That is cheaper than we can ever be, and you should take it. The catch: delivery crews generally won't take an appliance that isn't the one they're replacing, won't go down basement stairs, and won't take a unit that's still connected. If any of those apply, call us. If none do, use them.

Check your utility's recycling program first. Programs like the ones Black Hills Energy has run in the past have paid customers around $50 to haul away a working, plugged-in secondary refrigerator or freezer. Terms and availability change year to year, so verify before you assume — but if the program is active and your unit qualifies, you get paid instead of paying. Note the usual restrictions: it must be *working*, it must be a *secondary* unit, and there's typically a limit of 2 units per household per year.

Don't pay us if you have a truck and a free Saturday. The Pueblo County landfill and transfer facilities are roughly 10–14 miles from most of Pueblo West. Disposal fees for a couple of appliances are modest, and a metals yard will pay you scrap value — at steel prices in the $100–$160 per ton range, a 200-pound dryer is worth maybe $10–$20. That's not a payday, but it beats a $95 invoice. If you own the truck, own the straps, and have a second set of hands, doing it yourself is the correct financial answer.

Where we're genuinely a bad fit:

  • Gas appliances that need a line capped. We can disconnect an appliance-side flex connector, but if the shutoff is corroded or the line needs to be capped and pressure-tested, you need a plumber first. We'll reschedule rather than improvise on a gas line.
  • Hardwired 240V ranges and cooktops. Same logic — that's an electrician's disconnect.
  • Built-in and cabinet-integrated units. A panel-ready Sub-Zero framed into custom cabinetry is a cabinet demolition job wearing an appliance costume. We'll quote it, but it isn't the $95 job you were expecting.
  • Single small items. One microwave or one dehumidifier still triggers a minimum charge, because the truck, fuel, and two people's hour cost the same regardless. If it fits in your trunk, take it yourself.
  • Soaked or hoarded-adjacent units. A fridge that's been unplugged and closed for 6+ months in a Pueblo West summer is a biohazard by the time we get there. We'll still do it, but expect a surcharge and expect us to tape it shut before it moves. Open it, bag the contents, and hose it out first and you'll pay less.

Straight Pricing Logic, So You Can Predict the Number

Appliance quotes come down to four variables, and none of them are mysterious:

  • Weight and count. A stacked laundry pair is two units, not one. Front-load washers carry 200–250 pounds of concrete counterweight alone.
  • Distance to the truck. Curbside is the cheapest thing you can do. Every 50 feet of gravel adds time.
  • Stairs and obstacles. A basement pull with a 90-degree turn at the landing can double crew time versus a garage pull.
  • Sealed system or not. Refrigerant recovery adds real cost. A dryer is simply cheaper to dispose of than a fridge of the same weight.
  • Whether it's a standalone trip. Two appliances plus a couch and a mattress ride in one load; separately, that's three service calls' worth of drive time.

The single best thing you can do for your own quote: send 2–3 photos — the appliance, the path to the door, and the driveway — and say whether it's still connected. That turns a range into a firm price on the first call.

Swamp Coolers, Water Softeners, and the Pueblo West Specials

Two items come up constantly here and rarely appear on generic appliance-removal pages.

Evaporative coolers. Half the older homes in Pueblo West run a roof-mounted swamp cooler. A down-draft unit weighs 60–80 pounds dry, more with a rusted-out pan, and it lives on a pitched roof with a water line and a 120V feed running through the curb. Removal means capping the water line, cutting power, lowering the unit safely, and covering the roof opening so the next monsoon doesn't come inside. That's a different job than wheeling a dryer out of a garage, and it should be quoted as one.

Water softeners and whole-house filtration. Well water is common on the outskirts, and a spent softener brine tank full of salt can hit 150–200 pounds. Drain the brine tank a day before we arrive and you'll save real money on the ticket.
