ESTATE SALE CLEAN OUT SERVICES COST

An estate sale clean out is the haul-away that happens after the sale ends — the 60% to 80% of a household nobody bought. In Pueblo West, ClearPath clears a 1,600–2,000 sq ft home after the sale for $600 to $1,800, in a single 5-to-9-hour visit, filling 2 to 3 trailer loads. A garage or single-room leftover runs $250 to $450. We load everything ourselves — no curb staging, no dumpster sitting in the driveway for a week — sort donate/recycle/scrap on the truck, and leave the house broom-swept for a realtor walkthrough. Same-week scheduling is normal; 48-hour turnarounds happen when a closing date is stacked against you. Below is the pricing logic, the Pueblo West specifics, and the cases where you genuinely should not hire us.

What the clean out actually covers — and what it costs here

Pricing is volume-first, weight-second. A 16-foot dump trailer loaded to the rails is roughly one "full load." Our Pueblo West rates in practice:

  • Single item or small pickup (one couch, one fridge, a mattress pile): $150–$250
  • Quarter load — a bedroom's leftovers, closet contents, a few boxes: $250–$400
  • Half load — a typical garage after the sale picked it over: $425–$650
  • Full load — 2 to 3 rooms plus garage overflow: $750–$950
  • Whole-home post-sale clear (2–3 loads, 2-man crew, 5–9 hours): $1,400–$2,400 for anything over about 2,200 sq ft with a detached shop

Surcharges are the part most quotes hide. Mattresses and box springs carry a $35–$40 per-piece disposal fee because they can't be compacted. Sealed-system appliances with refrigerant — fridges, freezers, window A/C units, dehumidifiers — cost $30–$50 each to recover and certify. Paint, motor oil, pesticides, and pool chemicals are household hazardous waste and go to a county collection event, not the landfill; we either coordinate that or price it separately. Tires are typically $8–$15 apiece.

The sequence that saves money: sale first, haul second

Most estate sale companies in Pueblo and Pueblo West take 30% to 40% of gross, and most won't take a job projecting under about $3,000 in sales. Their contract almost always says the house must stay as-is until the sale ends — you can't pre-clean, because a picked-over house sells worse.

That creates a gap most families don't plan for: the sale ends Sunday afternoon, and the contract gives them 24 to 72 hours to clear their own tables and signage. If your closing is Friday, you have three usable days for a whole-house haul. Book the clean out the same week you sign the estate sale contract, not after. We hold a date without a deposit.

If your estate sale company offers a "leftovers buyout," run the math: a $400 buyout offer against an $1,100 clean out is a $700 swing in your favor, and it's often the better deal. We'll tell you that on the phone. We'd rather lose one job than have you feel worked over.

> The single most expensive mistake we see in Pueblo West is hauling before the personal representative is appointed. Once it's in the trailer, you can't un-dispose it — and a sibling who shows up in month three has nothing to look at but your word.

What Pueblo West properties add to the bill

Pueblo West isn't Pueblo, and the pricing reflects it. The Metro District covers roughly 49 square miles with about 32,000 residents, and a large share of the original McCulloch-era lots are a full acre or more. That changes three things:

  • Outbuildings. Close to half our Pueblo West estate jobs include a detached shop, barn, or shed the family forgot to mention. A 12x20 shop full of 40 years of tools and lumber is its own load. See [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Drive time. From the west end of Pueblo West, a landfill round trip is 35 to 55 minutes. That's real cost baked into every load, and it's why a 3-load job isn't 3x a 1-load job — it's closer to 3.4x.
  • Wind. Gusts over 30 mph are routine out here. Open loads have to be tarped and strapped, which adds 10–15 minutes per load and is non-negotiable — untarped debris on Purcell or McCulloch is both a ticket and a hazard.
  • Covenants. District covenants don't allow indefinite outdoor storage of junk piles or a dumpster parked long-term. A staged "we'll get to it" pile can draw a compliance letter in a few weeks.
  • Well, septic, and propane. Leased propane tanks belong to the supplier and must be scheduled for pickup, not hauled. We don't touch septic components.

Where the stuff actually goes

Roughly 50% to 65% of a post-estate-sale load is not landfill material, and separating it is where a good crew earns the fee:

  • Donation: clothing, linens, usable furniture, kitchenware, books. We deliver to Pueblo-area charities and bring back the receipt. A carload of household goods might support a $200–$600 non-cash charitable deduction — your CPA determines the number, not us.
  • Scrap metal: appliances, steel shelving, bed frames, water heaters. At $0.06–$0.12/lb for shred steel, a garage's worth of metal might offset $40–$90. We credit that against the invoice when it's meaningful.
  • E-waste: CRT televisions are the worst offender. A 32" tube TV weighs 90–150 lbs and carries its own recycling fee.
  • Landfill: particleboard furniture, stained mattresses, contaminated textiles, broken plastics. Honestly, most of it.

When an estate sale clean out isn't worth paying for

This is the section that costs us money, so read it carefully.

Skip us if the volume is under half a trailer and you have time. A one-day U-Haul pickup runs about $20 plus $0.99/mile, and a Pueblo County landfill minimum load is roughly $30–$40. Two trips, one Saturday, maybe $150 all-in against our $400. If you're local, able-bodied, and not on a deadline, do it yourself. We'd rather you call us for the big one next year.

Don't hire anyone until you know what's in the house. We are haulers, not appraisers. Our crews flag obvious cash, jewelry, firearms, coins, and documents, and we've set aside plenty of envelopes over the years — but we do not guarantee it, we don't open every box, and once a load compacts at the landfill it's gone. Antique furniture, Native American pieces, tools, and old firearms in this part of Colorado are routinely worth more than families assume. Pay an appraiser $200–$400 before you pay us anything.

Don't clean out before you have legal authority. Colorado's small-estate affidavit path only applies below an indexed threshold — $82,000 for 2025 deaths, adjusted annually, so confirm the current figure — and requires a 10-day wait after death. Above that, you need appointment as personal representative through Pueblo County District Court, and creditors have four months from published notice to file claims. Disposing of estate property before appointment can expose you personally. This is not legal advice; ask the estate's attorney.

Don't hire us for a contested estate. If two heirs disagree about the contents, no hauler should be in that house. Get a written, signed authorization from every interested party, or get a court order. We will decline the job without one.

Don't expect an itemized inventory. We do not photograph, log, or value items for a court accounting. If the probate judge wants an inventory, hire an inventory service first; we come after.

What we won't do

  • Biohazard remediation. Bodily fluids, unattended-death scenes, heavy rodent infestation, and black mold require a licensed remediation contractor. Severe accumulation short of that is a different service — see [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo).
  • Storage. We haul out, we don't hold. If the family needs a 30-day decision window, rent a unit.
  • Disconnecting gas or hardwired appliances. We pull a plug and a shutoff valve; we don't cap gas lines. Details on [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Firearms and ammunition. Those go to a licensed dealer or the sheriff, never in our trailer.
  • Asbestos-containing material. Pre-1980 floor tile, duct wrap, and popcorn ceiling need a licensed abatement contractor first.
