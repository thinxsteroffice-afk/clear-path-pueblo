ESTATE HOUSE CLEANOUT COMPANIES

Companies that clean out estate houses in Pueblo West fall into three groups: estate sale companies, which sell the contents and take a 30–40% commission; junk removal and cleanout crews like ClearPath, which sort, donate, recycle and haul everything else; and full-service estate liquidators, which do both and usually charge the most. For a typical 3-bedroom Pueblo West home, a full junk-removal cleanout usually costs $1,200 to $4,500. It takes a crew of 3–4 people about 1 to 3 days, depending on how full the house is and how many outbuildings are on the lot. Before hiring anyone, you need to decide which type of company fits the estate. This page explains how to decide, what the job involves, and when hiring us is the wrong call.

The three types of estate cleanout companies

Families often call the wrong type of company first. Each one is built to solve a different problem:

  • Estate sale companies price, stage and sell the contents over a weekend sale. Most want at least $8,000–$15,000 in expected sales before they'll take a job. They keep 30–40% of what sells. They generally don't remove what's left over.
  • Junk removal and cleanout companies clear the house down to empty rooms. You pay for the volume and labor, not a share of sales. Donatable items go to local charities, metal and appliances go to recyclers, and the rest goes to the landfill.
  • Estate liquidators and buyout companies make one offer for the entire contents, or run a sale and then clear out what doesn't sell. It's convenient, but you give up control over pricing.

In practice, many Pueblo West families use two of them: an estate sale company goes through the house first, and then a cleanout crew like ours clears what's left. If the house doesn't have enough value for a sale, you can skip the first step and call us directly through our [estate cleanout service](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo).

What an estate cleanout costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal pricing is based on truck volume, and a truck holds roughly 15 cubic yards. Here are typical ranges for this area:

  • A single full truckload: about $550–$750
  • A 2-bedroom house with average contents: 2–3 loads, often $1,200–$2,200
  • A 3–4 bedroom house with a full garage: 3–5 loads, often $2,000–$4,500
  • A heavily packed or hoarded home: add 50–100% to the time and volume, sometimes more. See our [hoarding cleanout page](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo).

Pueblo West lots drive up the price. Many properties here sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots. They often have detached shops, sheds, horse setups, trailers and decades of material stored outside. The house might hold 2 loads while the property holds another 4. If there's a shop full of scrap metal, lumber or old vehicles' worth of parts, count it separately when you estimate. We also handle [garage cleanouts](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) and [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) when the structure itself has to go.

> The house is rarely what blows the budget in Pueblo West. The outbuildings and the back acre are.

Several things push a quote up: pianos and gun safes (often a $75–$200 surcharge each because of weight), heavy upright freezers, many mattresses, tires, and any job where we have to carry everything up a basement stairway. Things that bring a quote down include pre-sorting, a clear driveway for the truck, and doing the job in one visit instead of three.

How a cleanout actually runs, step by step

Most estate cleanouts follow the same sequence. Knowing it helps you avoid paying for the same work twice.

1. Walkthrough and written quote. We look at every room, the garage and each outbuilding. A quote over the phone or from photos is only an estimate until someone has been through the property. 2. The family pulls keepers first. Mark or remove everything you want to keep before the crew arrives: photos, documents, jewelry, firearms, heirlooms. Once a crew is loading at a pace of 1–2 truckloads per half day, sorting items out on the spot costs you labor time. 3. Document search. Before loading, we ask families to check drawers, freezers, books, coat pockets and mattress edges. Older owners often hid cash, bonds, titles and wills. Crews find these things all the time, but a thorough check by the family is more reliable than hoping the crew spots them. 4. Sort and load. Items get split into donation, recycling (metal, appliances, electronics) and landfill. 5. Final sweep. The rooms are broom-swept and empty. That's the realistic standard, not move-in clean.

Most houses are finished in one to two working days. A packed house with a shop can take three.

Colorado estate details families overlook

Estate cleanouts involve more than hauling. A few legal and practical points come up regularly in Pueblo County:

  • Get authorization before anything leaves the house. In Colorado, the personal representative (executor) has authority over estate property. If one sibling hires a crew without the others agreeing, you can end up with a family dispute that's much harder to fix than a messy house. Get written sign-off from the personal representative before the truck arrives.
  • Keep your receipts. Cleanout costs are generally an estate administration expense. Save the invoice, and ask for itemized donation receipts if you want them for the estate's records.
  • Check the insurance clock. Many homeowner policies change or limit coverage once a house has been vacant for 30–60 days. That's a good reason not to let an empty-but-full house sit for a whole season.
  • Firearms and ammunition need to be handled by the family or a licensed dealer. A junk crew should not take them.
  • Prescription medications belong in a pharmacy or law enforcement take-back box, not in the trash.
  • Paint, pesticides, pool chemicals and propane cylinders are household hazardous waste. Most junk haulers, including us, can't put them in a standard load. Pueblo County holds household hazardous waste collection, so check the county's current schedule.

When hiring a cleanout company is NOT worth it

This section will cost us some jobs. It's still worth reading before you call anyone, including us.

1. The contents are worth more than the cleanout. If the house has mid-century furniture, collectible firearms, coins, western art, tools with resale value or a good vehicle, don't call a junk removal company first. Call an estate sale company or an appraiser. A hauler that shows up first can cause you to throw away thousands of dollars. We'll tell you if we see something that looks valuable, but we're not appraisers, and you shouldn't rely on us for that.

2. There isn't much left. If the family has already taken most of the contents and only a room or two remains, a borrowed pickup and a trailer may be enough. A trip to the landfill with a small load often costs under $50. If you have the time, the physical ability and two free weekends, paying $600 or more for one truckload doesn't make sense.

3. You're selling the house as-is to an investor. Some cash buyers in Pueblo County will take a house with the contents inside. If you've already accepted that kind of offer, a $3,000 cleanout just gives the buyer money back. Ask the buyer before you book anything.

4. The heirs haven't agreed yet. If there's a disagreement over who gets what, a cleanout crew should not be in the house. Items we haul away can't be brought back. Wait until you have the personal representative's written decision.

5. There was an unattended death or a biohazard. If someone passed away and wasn't found for some time, or there's significant animal waste, mold or sewage, you need a certified biohazard or trauma cleanup company first. We are a junk removal crew. We can remove the contents after remediation, but we are not the right first call, and any company that says it can handle both without certification should be a concern.

6. You need everything sold. We donate what we can and recycle what we can. We don't run estate sales, list items online or split sale proceeds with families. If you need to get money out of the contents, that's a different business.

7. Common ways cleanouts go wrong with any company:

  • A quote that's much lower than the others, followed by a much higher total "once we saw the attic"
  • No written quote, or no stated volume included in the price
  • A crew that won't give you proof of insurance
  • A "free cleanout in exchange for the contents" offer, where the company keeps everything valuable without disclosing it
  • Items left in the yard or driveway once the truck is full

How to vet companies that clean out estate houses

Ask every company, including us, these questions before you sign:

  • Is the price based on volume, time or a flat rate, and what happens if the job is bigger than expected? Get the answer in writing.
  • Are you insured for property damage and worker injury? A crew member hurt on the stairs at your parent's house shouldn't turn into a claim against the estate.
  • Where do the items go? A good company can tell you which charities and recyclers it uses.
  • Do you handle outbuildings, yard debris and scrap? On a Pueblo West property, you want one company handling the whole lot, not three separate companies.
  • How soon can you start? Around here, 3–7 days from the walkthrough to the cleanout is normal. Faster is sometimes possible.
