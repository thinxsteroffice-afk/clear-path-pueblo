MATTRESS DISPOSAL COST NEAR ME

Expect to pay $75 to $125 to have a single mattress picked up and hauled away from a Pueblo West address, and $95 to $165 for a mattress and box spring together. Hauling it yourself to the Pueblo-area landfill runs roughly $25 to $45 in tip fees plus about $6 to $9 in gas for the 24–34 mile round trip, so the DIY route saves real money — call it $50 to $90 — in exchange for 60 to 90 minutes and a truck. Retailers delivering a new bed often haul the old one for $0 to $150, but only at delivery. Below is the honest math on all three, including the cases where you should not hire us.

What Mattress Disposal Actually Costs in Pueblo West, CO

Pricing in this market falls into four buckets. These are 2026 ranges for Pueblo West and the surrounding Pueblo County addresses — always confirm before you book, because fuel and tip fees move.

  • Full-service pickup, one mattress: $75–$125. Includes two people, the walk-out, the truck, and the disposal fee.
  • Mattress + box spring (a "set"): $95–$165. The second piece is cheaper because the drive and the disposal trip are already paid for.
  • Each additional mattress on the same stop: $35–$55. A four-bedroom cleanout with five mattresses should land near $240–$330, not 5 × $95.
  • Self-haul to the landfill: a minimum load charge around $25–$45 covers most pickup loads; over the minimum you're on scale weight at roughly $45–$60 per ton.
  • Curb-only "we'll grab it from outside" rate: typically $15–$25 less than an inside-the-house pull, because stairs and doorways are the labor.

A queen pillow-top weighs 85 to 120 pounds. A king runs 130 to 160. That matters twice: it's why one person cannot safely do this alone down a staircase, and it's why a rain-soaked mattress costs more — waterlogged foam can gain 30 to 60 pounds, and at scale weight that's a real line item.

Why Pueblo West Has No Free Option (And Pueblo City Sort Of Does)

This is the part most cost pages skip. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no municipal government running a bulky-item amnesty day for you. Trash service here is a private subscription — Waste Connections and the other haulers serving the metro district area — and standard residential carts do not cover a mattress. Most haulers will take one as a scheduled bulky pickup for an added fee, commonly $30 to $60, and often only on a specific route day with 3 to 7 days' notice.

Residents inside Pueblo city limits sometimes have access to seasonal cleanup events. Pueblo West addresses generally do not. If a search result promised you a free city pickup, check whether it was written for Pueblo proper.

Recycling: The Colorado Reality Nobody Tells You

A mattress is 75% to 80% recyclable by weight. An innerspring queen holds 450 to 600 coils — that's 25 to 40 pounds of scrap steel. The foam becomes carpet padding. The wood frame becomes mulch or biomass.

Here's the catch: Colorado has no mattress stewardship program. Only a handful of states — California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon — collect a recycling fee at the point of sale and fund free drop-off recycling with it. Colorado collects nothing, funds nothing, and has no free recycler network. Every mattress recycling claim you read from a national brand is either (a) a facility in another state, or (b) a small-volume partnership.

What that means for your wallet: dedicated mattress recycling in southern Colorado, where you can find it, usually costs more than landfilling, not less — often $20 to $40 per unit at a processor, plus your drive to Colorado Springs or the Front Range. Anyone quoting you a *lower* price because "we recycle it" is quoting you a landfill price with better marketing.

> If a hauler charges you extra for "eco-disposal" in Pueblo County, ask which facility. If they can't name it, you're paying a premium for a landfill trip.

Contamination kills recycling entirely. A mattress with bed bugs, mold, or heavy urine saturation gets rejected at every processor and goes straight to the landfill regardless of what you paid. Tell your hauler up front — see the [FAQ](/faq) — because a surprise bed bug mattress means our truck needs treatment before the next stop, and that's a legitimate surcharge of $25 to $75.

Do not expect to donate it. Goodwill, ARC, Habitat ReStore, and essentially every legitimate thrift operation in southern Colorado refuse used mattresses on liability grounds. The rare exception is a nearly-new mattress with a documented single owner and no stains — and even then, call ahead.

The Failure Modes Worth Knowing About

  • Same-day is not guaranteed. A single-mattress stop is a $95 job on a truck that costs several hundred dollars a day to run. It gets slotted around larger work. Realistic booking is next-day to 3 business days in Pueblo West. If someone promises 2-hour service on one mattress, expect a premium.
  • Phone quotes are estimates. Stairs, a narrow hallway, a locked side gate, or a mattress that's been rained on for a week can move the price. Send a photo and you'll get a number that holds.
  • Unsecured loads are a citation risk. A queen in a 6.5-foot short bed overhangs by roughly two feet. Colorado enforces load securement, and a mattress that launches onto Purcell Blvd is your liability, not the wind's. Two ratchet straps, minimum — through the bed, not over the tailgate.
  • Illegal dumping is prosecuted here. Dumping a mattress on vacant prairie land in Pueblo West carries fines that dwarf every number on this page, and abandoned mattresses on rural lots get reported and traced. It is never the cheap option.
  • Mattress covers cost $8–$15 and are worth it. If you're moving one through a carpeted house, a $10 plastic mattress bag prevents the debris trail and keeps mites contained. Buy it before, not after.
