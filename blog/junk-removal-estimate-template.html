JUNK REMOVAL ESTIMATE TEMPLATE

A good junk removal estimate fits on one page and covers six things: load volume (as a share of the truck), item surcharges (appliances, mattresses, tires, electronics), access and labor (stairs, long carries, demolition), disposal and recycling fees, a price range with a firm cap, and how long the quote is valid. Below is the template we use for Pueblo West jobs, with the line items filled in using typical southern Colorado price ranges. You can copy it to price a job, compare two quotes, or check that a hauler isn't hiding fees. These are typical ranges, not a quote. The only firm number is the one written down after someone sees the pile.

The Template (Copy This)

``` JUNK REMOVAL ESTIMATE Customer: ____________ Address: ____________ (Pueblo West, CO) Date: ________ Estimate valid until: ________ (14 days)

1. VOLUME Truck size: 15 cubic yards Estimated load: [ ] Minimum [ ] 1/4 [ ] 1/2 [ ] 3/4 [ ] Full Volume price: $______

2. ITEM SURCHARGES Refrigerant appliances (fridge, freezer, AC): ___ x $______ Mattresses / box springs: ___ x $______ Tires (off rim / on rim): ___ x $______ TVs / monitors / computers: ___ x $______ Hot tub / piano / safe: ___ x $______

3. ACCESS & LABOR Stairs (flights): ___ Carry distance over 50 ft: [ ] yes Disassembly / demolition required: [ ] yes Est. hours: ___ Labor add-on: $______

4. HEAVY MATERIAL (priced by weight, not volume) Concrete / rock / dirt / shingles: ___ tons x $______

5. TOTAL Low estimate: $______ High estimate (cap): $______ Not-to-exceed without customer sign-off: [ ] yes

6. EXCLUSIONS Paint, solvents, fuel, propane tanks, asbestos, medical waste ```

Every section is there because a hauler can hide money in it. The sections below explain what goes on each line and what a normal number looks like.

Section by Section: What Goes on Each Line

### 1. Volume pricing

Most haulers price by how much of the truck your junk fills. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly the size of a one-car garage stacked 4 feet high. Typical southern Colorado ranges:

  • Minimum load (1–2 bulky items): $95–$150
  • Quarter load (~4 cubic yards, a small room's worth): $175–$275
  • Half load (~7.5 cubic yards, a crowded single-car garage bay): $300–$425
  • Full load (15 cubic yards): $550–$750

If a quote gives you one flat number and no load size, ask for the load size. Otherwise you can't check the bill afterward: if a half load turns into "full truck" pricing, you have nothing to point to.

### 2. Item surcharges

Some items cost the hauler extra to dispose of, so they show up as separate line items:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units: federal EPA rules (Clean Air Act, Section 608) require the refrigerant to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped. Expect $25–$75 per unit on top of volume.
  • TVs, monitors, and computers: Colorado has banned most electronics from landfills since 2013, so they go to an e-waste recycler that charges by the piece or pound. Expect $20–$50 per screen.
  • Mattresses and box springs: $20–$50 each, depending on whether they're recycled or landfilled.
  • Tires: $10–$25 each, more if they're still on the rim.

For the big ones, like a hot tub, piano, or gun safe, a separate line is normal because they take extra crew time and often cutting. Our [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) jobs are quoted that way.

### 3. Access and labor

In Pueblo West this line moves the price more than people expect. Many lots are a half acre to several acres, and junk often sits in a back shed or along a fence line 100–300 feet from where the truck can park. A good estimate states the carry distance up front. Common add-ons are $25–$50 per flight of stairs for heavy items and $50–$100 per crew hour for disassembly or light demolition.

### 4. Heavy material

Concrete, rock, dirt, brick, and roofing shingles fill only a small part of the truck but can max out its weight limit. A truck that holds 15 cubic yards of couches might only legally carry 2–3 cubic yards of broken concrete. Any honest estimate prices these materials by weight or a separate rate, never as a share of the truck. If a quote prices a pile of patio slab at "half a truck," either the hauler hasn't done the math or they're planning to change the price on the day. Our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) quotes are written by weight for exactly this reason.

### 5. The range and the cap

A photo or phone estimate should always be a range, such as $300–$400, because nobody can judge volume exactly from a picture. What protects you is the cap: a written promise that the final bill won't go over the high number unless you approve the change before work starts. If an estimate has no cap, treat it as a guess.

### 6. Validity window

Disposal fees and fuel costs change, so estimates usually expire in 7–30 days. Fourteen days is a reasonable default. If you're planning a move-out or [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) more than a month away, get the estimate re-confirmed the week before.

When Hiring a Junk Removal Company Isn't Worth It

This section loses us some jobs, but these are the cases where we'd tell you not to hire us:

  • You have a truck and only a few items. If you own a pickup and you're getting rid of a recliner and some boxes, hauling it yourself to a Pueblo-area transfer station or landfill will usually cost you a $20–$50 gate fee plus an hour of your time. Our minimum load runs $95–$150. For one trip's worth of non-hazardous stuff, do it yourself.
  • The furniture is in good shape. Charities and resale stores often pick up usable furniture for free. Paying us to haul a solid dresser to a donation drop is paying for something you could get done at no cost. Call them first and call us for whatever they turn down.
  • The project runs for weeks. In a kitchen remodel or roof tear-off, debris piles up over 2–4 weeks. A roll-off dumpster you fill on your own schedule usually beats paying for three or four separate truck visits. We're the better choice when the debris is already sitting there and you want it gone in one afternoon.
  • Most of it is hazardous material. We can't take paint, solvents, motor oil, pesticides, propane tanks, or anything that might contain asbestos. No reputable hauler can. If the garage is mostly old chemicals, take them to Pueblo County's household hazardous waste program, not a junk truck.
  • You need a fixed price today but won't send photos or allow a walkthrough. Without photos or a walkthrough we can only give a wide range. If you need a firm number before anyone has seen the job, any estimate, ours included, can be wrong by 25% or more in either direction.

> An estimate without a load size, a surcharge list, and a not-to-exceed cap is not an estimate. It's an opening bid.

### Where estimates go wrong

Even with a good template, these are the most common ways a quote misses:

  • Hidden junk: things behind the shed, in the attic, or under a tarp that weren't in the photos. This is the cause of most price changes on the day.
  • Compressed piles: bagged trash and broken-down furniture take up more truck space once loose than they did packed tight. Expect the real volume to come in 10–20% higher than a photo suggests.
  • Wind and access: in Pueblo West, spring gusts over 40 mph can make carrying loose panels or roofing unsafe. That can push the job to another day, though it shouldn't raise the price.

How to Use the Template to Compare Two Quotes

If you have estimates from two haulers, fill in one blank template per company using what each one told you. Any line one hauler left empty is a question to ask them. Line up these four numbers:

1. Load size: are both quoting the same fraction of the truck? If one says a quarter and the other says a half, one of them is wrong, and the cheaper quote may simply be the one that underestimated. 2. Surcharges: does one quote leave out the fridge or the mattresses that the other lists? 3. High-end cap: compare the caps, not the low numbers. A $250–$500 quote is riskier than a $325–$375 quote. 4. Exclusions: does either one say they'll take the paint cans? If so, ask where those cans end up.

The cheaper quote isn't always the better one. A hauler who leaves out the e-waste fee and then charges it at the curb has quoted you a number they don't intend to stick to.

For Pueblo West Jobs Specifically

A few local factors belong in any estimate written for this area:

  • Distance to disposal. Most material goes to facilities on the Pueblo side, about 15–25 minutes from most Pueblo West addresses. A hauler based in Colorado Springs adds about 45 minutes each way, which tends to show up in the price as a trip charge.
  • Outbuildings and acreage. Sheds, carports, and old animal pens are common here. If any part of the job means tearing down a structure rather than just hauling, it belongs on its own line. See [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Yard and brush debris. Tumbleweeds, dead juniper, and fire-mitigation brush are bulky but light, so they price by volume and usually come in near the low end of each range. That's covered under [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo).
