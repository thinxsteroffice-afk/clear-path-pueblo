JUNK CAR REMOVAL WITHOUT TITLE

Yes — you can have a car hauled off without a title in Colorado, but not the same way you'd sell one. In Pueblo West, a licensed hauler or salvage yard can legally take an untitled vehicle only if the paperwork gap gets closed some other way: a Colorado Bill of Sale plus a DR 2173 (Application for Duplicate Title), a DR 2426 (Statement of Fact), or a Public Trustee/abandoned-vehicle process through the Pueblo County Sheriff. ClearPath Junk Removal handles the removal side — winching, towing, and disposal from anywhere in the 81007 area, typically within 24 to 72 hours — for a flat $150 to $350 depending on access and whether the car rolls. What we cannot do is manufacture a title. Here's exactly what works, what doesn't, and when you should not call us at all.

The Three Legal Paths for an Untitled Car in Pueblo County

Colorado is stricter than New Mexico or Texas on this. C.R.S. 42-6-206 makes it a misdemeanor to transfer a vehicle without a title assignment, and salvage yards operating under a Colorado Motor Vehicle Dealer or Salvage license get audited on exactly this. So the yards in Pueblo — the ones on Santa Fe Ave and out toward the Blende area — will not hand you cash for a titleless car the way a Craigslist buyer would.

Your realistic options:

  • Duplicate title (DR 2173). If the car is in *your* name in the Colorado DMV system and you simply lost the paper, this is the answer. Cost is $8.20 at the Pueblo County Clerk & Recorder on Bonforte Blvd, and mail turnaround runs 10 to 20 business days — or same-day if you walk in with ID and the VIN. This is by far the cheapest path and the one most people qualify for without realizing it.
  • Bill of Sale + VIN verification. For vehicles 1985 or older, Colorado allows a bonded-title-free path in some cases using a DR 2704 bond or a DR 2426 Statement of Fact. Surety bonds run about 1.5x to 2x the vehicle's appraised value, with a typical minimum premium of $100. For a $400 parts car, that math almost never works.
  • Abandoned vehicle process. If the car isn't yours — a tenant left it, an ex left it, it appeared on your acreage — Colorado's abandoned-vehicle statute (C.R.S. 42-4-2103) is the route. On private property you file with the Pueblo County Sheriff's Office, and the statutory notice period is 30 days before title can transfer to the towing operator or property owner. Plan on 4 to 8 weeks end to end.

> If the car is in your name and you just can't find the paper, stop reading about junk haulers and go get an $8.20 duplicate title. You'll net more money selling it properly than any no-title removal will ever pay you.

Why Pueblo West Specifically Is a Hard Market for This

Two local realities change the numbers here versus Denver or Colorado Springs.

Lot sizes. A huge share of Pueblo West parcels are 0.75 to 2.5 acres, and cars sit far off the road — 200, 400, sometimes 600 feet back on dirt. A flatbed with a 50-foot winch line can't reach that. We end up dragging the car to the road on skates or pulling it with our skid steer, which is the single biggest driver of cost. Measure the distance from the vehicle to a spot a 30-foot truck can back into. Under 60 feet, expect the low end of our range. Over 200 feet, expect the high end.

Caliche and clay. A car that's sat five winters on Pueblo West ground has usually sunk 3 to 8 inches at the tires, and after a monsoon week the ground won't hold a tow truck's rear axle. We schedule these for dry stretches, which in practice means April–June and September–October are our easiest months. July and August are our worst. If the car has been there since 2019 and there's grass growing through the floor pan, expect us to bring the skid steer, and expect it to cost more.

The Part That Might Cost Us Your Business

I'd rather lose the job than have you pay us for something that was a bad idea. Here's when you should not hire ClearPath for an untitled car:

  • The car is in your name and you lost the title. Get the duplicate for $8.20. Then sell it to a salvage yard for $180 to $400, or list it as a parts car and get $500 to $1,500. Hiring us costs you $150–$350 instead of earning you a few hundred. That's a $400 to $1,800 swing against you. Don't do it.
  • The car is a 1967–1974 anything, a square-body Chevy, a 4x4 Toyota pickup, or a Fox-body Mustang. Even rough, even titleless, these have real bonded-title-worth-pursuing value. A rusted-out 1972 C10 shell brings $1,200 to $4,000 in this market. Paying us to scrap it would be the most expensive mistake on this page.
  • The car isn't legally yours and you haven't done the 30-day notice. We will decline. Not as a formality — hauling someone else's vehicle exposes both of us to a civil conversion claim, and the previous owner tracing a VIN through a salvage yard's state-mandated reporting happens more often than you'd think. Do the Sheriff's Office paperwork first. Yes, it's 4 to 8 weeks.
  • You need it gone today because of a code enforcement letter. Pueblo County typically gives 10 to 30 days on nuisance vehicle notices, which is usually enough — but if you're at day 28, our honest answer is that a licensed tow operator with an existing abandoned-vehicle process is faster than us for a titleless car. Call one. We'll happily handle the rest of the yard.
  • There are only one or two items and the car is the whole job. Our economics are built around truckloads. A single rolling sedan at $150 is close to our floor, and you'll get better value bundling it with the trampoline, the old shed, and the deck. Bundled jobs in Pueblo West typically save $100 to $250 versus separate trips.
  • The car still runs and passes emissions. A running 2004 Corolla with 240,000 miles is a $1,000–$2,500 private sale, not a junk removal call. Even non-running-but-complete cars often clear $500 on marketplace listings within 14 days.

Two more honest limitations. We are not a title service — we don't file your DR 2173, we don't run VIN checks, and we can't tell you whether a lien from 2011 is still attached (a $0 lien-check call to the Clerk on Bonforte will tell you that). And we don't pay for cars. If someone tells you they'll haul your titleless vehicle *and* pay you $300 cash with no paperwork, that's not a better deal — that's an unlicensed operation, and the VIN stays tied to you until a title actually transfers, which means the parking tickets, the abandonment fees, and any liability follow your name.
