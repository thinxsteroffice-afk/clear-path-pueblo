HOUSE CLEANOUT COST: $450–$2,800

A house cleanout in Pueblo West runs $450 to $2,800 for most single-family homes, with the average landing around $1,150. That figure assumes a 1,400–2,000 sq ft house with a garage, three to four truckloads of material, and no hazardous waste. Priced by volume, expect $250–$375 per quarter truckload, $550–$750 per half load, and $900–$1,400 for a full 16-foot dump trailer (roughly 18–20 cubic yards). Whole-estate cleanouts with a packed garage, outbuildings, and a shed can hit $3,500–$6,000. Below is how that number actually gets built, and — just as important — when hiring anyone is the wrong call.

What Actually Drives the Price in Pueblo West

Volume is the headline number, but four other factors move a Pueblo West quote more than most homeowners expect.

Landfill fees. Everything hauled out of a Pueblo West home ends up at a transfer station or the regional landfill, and disposal is charged by weight, not by how full the trailer looks. Mixed household waste runs roughly $45–$65 per ton at the tipping scale. A packed 20-yard load of ordinary household goods weighs 2.5 to 4 tons — so $110–$260 of your quote is a receipt from the scale house, not profit. Any honest hauler builds that in.

Weight-dense material. This is where quotes double. Concrete, tile, dirt, roofing shingles, and old plaster weigh 8–12x more per cubic foot than furniture and boxes. A half-trailer of drywall and tile can weigh more than a full trailer of couches. If your cleanout includes a demo component, you are paying by the ton, and it will be quoted separately from the household portion. See [construction debris removal pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) for how that side gets priced.

Access and distance. Pueblo West is spread out. A property off McCulloch or Purcell with a paved drive and a two-car garage is a different job from a five-acre parcel out past Bandera with a soft dirt drive, a 200-foot carry from the back bedroom to the truck, and a gate the trailer barely clears. Every 100 feet of carry distance adds roughly 20–30% to the labor time on a given load. Second-story bedrooms with a tight stairwell add similar.

Sort requirements. A "take everything" cleanout moves fast. A cleanout where the crew must set aside anything that might be valuable, photograph documents, and stage items for family review can run 2–3x the labor hours for the same volume.

> The single most expensive words in a cleanout quote are "wait, let me look at that one." Sorting is labor, and labor is the bill.

Real Pueblo West Price Ranges by Job Type

These are what typical local jobs actually cost, not national averages pulled from a Denver or Colorado Springs market where labor and tipping fees run higher.

  • Single room or bedroom clearout: $175–$350. Usually a quarter load, 45–90 minutes on site.
  • Standard two-car [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo): $400–$850. Two to four hours, one to two truckloads.
  • Full 1,400–1,800 sq ft house, furnished but not hoarded: $850–$1,600. Typically a half day, three loads.
  • 2,500+ sq ft home with garage, shed, and yard debris: $2,200–$4,500. One to two full days, five to eight loads.
  • Estate cleanout with sorting and family walkthroughs: $1,800–$5,500. The sorting, not the volume, sets this price. Details on [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo).
  • Hoarding-condition property: $3,500–$9,000+. PPE, biohazard handling, floor-level debris, and often 10+ loads.

Add-on items priced separately in almost every Pueblo West quote: refrigerators and freezers $45–$95 each (refrigerant recovery is required by law), mattresses $35–$60 each, standard tube TVs $40–$75, propane tanks $15–$30, tires $12–$25 each, and pianos $250–$600 depending on type and stairs.

The Part That Costs Us Money: When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the section most junk removal pages skip. Hiring a hauler is genuinely the wrong financial decision in several common situations, and if you're in one of them, we'd rather tell you now than take the job and have you feel taken.

Your load is under about 1.5 cubic yards. If everything you need gone fits in one pickup bed, you are paying a $150–$200 minimum service charge for something a friend with a truck and a Saturday morning does for the price of lunch and a $28 disposal fee at the transfer station. Below roughly 2 cubic yards, DIY wins on math almost every time.

You have time, help, and a truck — and the material is light. A homeowner with a half-ton pickup, two able bodies, and three free weekends can clear a normal house for $150–$400 in dump fees and fuel. That's a real $700–$1,200 savings. What you're buying from us is compressed time and spared backs, not a magic price. If those aren't scarce for you, don't buy.

You're clearing a lot of clean, sellable material. If the house holds a functioning appliance set, mid-century furniture, or a shop full of tools, an estate sale company or auctioneer taking 25–40% commission may net you money instead of costing you $1,500. Get one valuation call before you get a hauling quote. We'll be the second call, for the 30% that doesn't sell.

You're mid-probate without authority to dispose. If the estate isn't settled and you're not the personal representative, hauling contents can create real legal exposure for you. No reputable company should touch that property until the paperwork exists. Wait. Being told "not yet" is worth more than a fast schedule.

The material is hazardous, and we can't take it. Nobody hauling household junk in Pueblo County will legally take paint thinner, gasoline, pesticides, asbestos-containing floor tile or pipe wrap, or medical sharps. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events — those are typically free or under $25 for residents, and they're the correct route. A pre-1980 Pueblo West home with popcorn ceiling or old vinyl tile may need $400–$1,500 in asbestos testing and abatement before anyone can legally proceed. That is a separate contractor, not us.

A dumpster beats us on multi-week projects. If you're renovating over three weeks and generating debris continuously, a 20-yard roll-off at $375–$550 for a 7–10 day rental is cheaper per cubic yard than repeat hauls — as long as you're willing to load it yourself and you have driveway space and HOA tolerance for a container. Roll-offs lose when the material is heavy (overage fees at $50–$85 per ton past the included tonnage bite hard) or when you can't do the loading.

Where full-service hauling actually fails. It doesn't fail often, but it fails predictably: same-day requests during spring cleanup season (March–May) often can't be met; weather stops outdoor work when the wind is up past 35 mph on the mesa; and any quote given sight-unseen over the phone is a guess that can move 20–35% once a crew sees the actual volume. Insist on a photo or on-site estimate for anything above a single room. A number given without seeing the pile is not a price.

How to Get a Quote That Doesn't Change

Five things cut the odds of a surprise on cleanout day:

  • Send photos of every room, plus the garage and any outbuildings — wide shots, not close-ups. Closed closets and attic contents are the two biggest sources of quote drift.
  • State the access honestly. Stairs, gates, gravel drives, and low-hanging trees on the approach all matter. A crew that arrives with the wrong trailer loses two hours.
  • Separate anything staying before the crew arrives, ideally in one marked room. Blue tape on what stays beats verbal instructions every time.
  • Flag the special items up front — freezers, tires, propane, paint, TVs — so they're in the quote instead of added at the end.
  • Ask what's included in the disposal fee and whether the price is capped. Ask specifically: "If this turns out to be four loads instead of three, what's the fourth cost?"

Cost Comparison: Four Ways to Clear a Pueblo West House

For a typical 1,600 sq ft house with a garage, roughly 15 cubic yards of material:

  • Full-service hauling: $950–$1,500. Done in 4–7 hours. Zero labor from you.
  • 20-yard roll-off dumpster: $425–$600 rental, plus 15–25 hours of your own loading. Real cost depends on what your weekend is worth.
  • Pickup truck DIY: $180–$310 in dump fees and fuel across 8–10 trips, plus 20–30 hours and the risk of a strained back or a scratched tailgate.
  • Estate sale first, then haul the remainder: Possible net positive $500–$3,000, but takes 3–6 weeks and requires a house with genuinely sellable contents.

The honest summary: hauling costs about $60–$95 per cubic yard all-in, and DIY costs about $12–$20 per cubic yard plus your labor. You're paying a 4–6x premium to not do it yourself. Sometimes that's obviously worth it — a house 200 miles away, a deadline from a title company, a bad knee. Sometimes it plainly isn't.

Pueblo West Specifics Most Pages Miss

Two local details that don't show up in generic cost articles:

HOA and covenant timing. Several Pueblo West subdivisions restrict how long a dumpster or trailer can sit on a driveway or street — some as short as 72 hours. If you're planning a roll-off, check your covenant before you book a 10-day rental you can't legally keep.

Wind is a scheduling variable here. Spring gusts on the mesa routinely hit 40–50 mph. Loose material — insulation, cardboard, foam — cannot safely be loaded in open trailers on those days. Build a day of slack into any deadline-driven cleanout between March and May. A title company closing date does not care about the wind, so plan backward from it.

Also worth knowing: many Pueblo West properties have detached shops, sheds, or a decades-old above-ground pool that the homeowner mentally excludes from "the house" but which represents 30–50% of the actual volume. Count them when you're budgeting.
