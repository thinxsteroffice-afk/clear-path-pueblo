ALL ABOUT JUNK REMOVAL: COST & TIME

Junk removal is a service where a crew comes to your property, loads unwanted items into a truck, and takes them away to be recycled, donated or sent to the landfill. You only point at what goes. In Pueblo West, most jobs are priced by how much of the truck your items fill, not by the hour. A single item usually runs about $100–$175, and a full truckload usually runs $450–$700, depending on weight and what the material is. Most jobs take 30 to 90 minutes on site. It's a good deal when you have more than a pickup load, heavy or awkward items, or not much time. It's a bad deal in some cases too, and this article covers those.

How Junk Removal Actually Works, Step by Step

People who have never hired a hauler tend to picture a dumpster. Full-service junk removal works differently:

  • You describe or photograph the items. Many companies give a ballpark price from photos sent by text. The final price is set on site.
  • The crew gives you a price before lifting anything. That price is based on how much truck space your items take up. If you don't like the number, you can turn it down.
  • The crew does all the carrying. They bring items out of basements, garages, back corners of the lot and second floors. You don't have to stage anything at the curb.
  • The load gets sorted. Scrap metal goes to a recycler. Usable furniture can go to donation. Electronics have to go to a certified recycler. Whatever is left goes to the landfill.
  • The crew sweeps up the area before they leave.

A standard junk-removal truck holds about 15 cubic yards. A regular full-size pickup bed holds about 2 to 2.5 cubic yards if you pile it level, so a full junk truck equals about six pickup loads. That ratio helps a lot when you're deciding whether to do the job yourself.

What It Costs in Pueblo West (and Why Prices Vary)

Prices here fall into fairly predictable ranges. The numbers below are typical for the area. They aren't fixed quotes, because a pile of drywall weighs far more than a pile of couch cushions.

  • Single item (one mattress, one recliner): about $100–$175
  • Quarter truck (a room's worth of furniture, a small garage corner): about $175–$275
  • Half truck (a typical one-car-garage cleanout): about $300–$425
  • Full truck (estate or full garage cleanout): about $450–$700
  • Hot tub removal, including cutting it apart: commonly $350–$600

Prices go up for three reasons:

1. Weight. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles and wet yard waste are charged by weight, because the landfill charges the hauler by the ton. One cubic yard of broken concrete can weigh over 3,000 pounds. That's why [concrete rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is priced differently from a load of furniture. 2. Appliances with refrigerant. Federal rules say Freon has to be recovered from refrigerators, freezers and AC units before they're scrapped. That usually adds $25–$75 per unit. See [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) for details. 3. Hard access. Long carries across a large Pueblo West lot, steep driveways and crawlspaces take more labor.

Pueblo West lots are bigger than most, often an acre or more. Junk spreads out on them: an old trailer in one spot, a pile of fence posts in another, a dead camper shell behind the shed. If your items are scattered around the property, say so when you ask for a quote. A crew can price a scattered job correctly when it knows the layout ahead of time.

What Junk Removal Companies Can and Can't Take

Most of what's in a garage or yard can go: furniture, mattresses, appliances, lumber, fencing, sheds, playsets, carpet, tires (usually for a small fee per tire), brush and household clutter.

Here's what any honest hauler in Colorado will turn down or send somewhere else:

  • Paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals and motor oil. These are household hazardous waste. Take them to a county HHW collection event or drop-off.
  • Propane tanks. Many propane retailers take them back. Haulers generally won't.
  • Anything you suspect contains asbestos. This includes old popcorn ceilings, some vinyl floor tiles from before 1980, and old pipe insulation. Colorado requires testing and licensed abatement before this material can be disturbed.
  • Electronics in the trash stream. Colorado has banned TVs, computers and monitors from landfills since July 1, 2013. A legitimate hauler will charge a recycling fee for these, usually $15–$50 per TV. If a hauler says it's free with no explanation, ask where it's going.

When Junk Removal Is NOT Worth It

This section might talk you out of hiring us, and that's fine. You should know when your money is better spent somewhere else.

You have one or two items and a truck. If you own a pickup and the item fits in the bed, hauling it yourself to the landfill or a transfer station usually costs a modest gate fee plus gas. Call ahead for current rates. That will almost always be cheaper than a $100+ single-item pickup. Pay for the service only if the item is too heavy to lift safely or you can't spare the trip.

Your project will produce debris over several weeks. For a kitchen remodel or a re-roof, a roll-off dumpster is usually the better choice. A 10–20 yard dumpster commonly rents for $350–$550 for about 7 to 14 days, and you fill it as you go. Junk removal makes sense at the end of a project or for a one-day cleanout. It doesn't make sense if you'd be calling us back every few days.

Your junk is worth money. If you have a stack of steel, copper wire, aluminum or a pile of old car batteries, a scrap yard will pay you for it. Paying someone to haul it away means losing that money. Sort out the valuable metal first and hire out the rest.

Your stuff is good enough to sell or donate, and you have time. A working dresser or an intact patio set can often go for $20–$100 on a local marketplace, or someone will pick it up for free within 48 hours. If you're not in a hurry, try that first. Hire a crew for whatever doesn't move.

You want hazardous waste gone. Nobody legitimate can take it in a junk truck. A company that says yes to paint cans and chemicals is either breaking the rules or dumping illegally.

> The cheapest junk removal quote can end up the most expensive one if your old couch turns up in an arroyo off a dirt road with your name on a piece of mail in the cushions.

The failure mode to watch for: cash-only haulers who dump illegally. Illegal dumping on vacant land is a real and visible problem around Pueblo West and the open county land nearby, because there is so much empty acreage. An unlicensed hauler who charges $50 for a load that legally costs much more to dispose of is probably making up the difference by not paying landfill fees. If dumped items get traced back to you, you can face cleanup costs and fines. Before you hire anyone, ask:

  • Are you insured, and can you show me proof?
  • Which facility does my load go to?
  • Is your price written down and agreed before you start loading?

If you can't get straight answers to all three, don't hire them.

Volume pricing has its own limitation. The final price is the crew's estimate of how much truck space your items take. Two estimators can look at the same pile and see it differently. Ask the crew to show you the load in the truck before you pay, so you can see what you're paying for. Also, a quote based only on photos can change on site. Photos rarely show how heavy or how bulky something really is.

How to Estimate Your Own Job Before You Call

You can work out roughly what size job you have, so the quote you get won't surprise you:

  • A queen mattress plus box spring takes up about 1/12 of a truck.
  • A sectional sofa takes up about 1/6 to 1/4 of a truck.
  • A packed one-car garage is usually 1/2 to 3/4 of a truck. A packed two-car garage is often a full truck or more.
  • An above-ground pool or a large wooden playset, once taken apart, is usually 1/4 to 1/2 of a truck.

To measure a pile yourself, take length × width × height in feet and divide by 27 to get cubic yards. Compare that to the truck's 15 yards. A pile 9 feet long, 6 feet wide and 4 feet tall is 8 cubic yards, which is just over half a truck.

Pueblo West Specifics Worth Knowing

A few things about working here make a difference:

  • Wind. Afternoon gusts over 40 mph are common in spring. If you're hauling your own load, tarp and strap it. Debris falling from an uncovered load is a citable offense in Colorado, and loose plywood on Highway 50 is a real danger to other drivers.
  • Morning appointments. Summer afternoons regularly go above 90°F. Crews move faster and more carefully in a cool morning slot, especially when clearing out a hot shed or garage.
  • Big outdoor jobs. Old fencing, decks, sheds and hot tubs are common on large lots. These jobs need demolition before hauling, so they're priced as their own projects. Our [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) and [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) pages explain how those work.
  • Estates and move-outs. These often mean a full house in one day. An [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) can include sorting out keepsakes and documents before anything goes in the truck.
