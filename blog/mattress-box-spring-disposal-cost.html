MATTRESS & BOX SPRING DISPOSAL COST

In Pueblo West, hauling away a single mattress runs $75 to $95, and a mattress-and-box-spring set runs $95 to $140, usually same day or next day. That covers carrying it out of the bedroom, loading, the roughly 16-mile drive to the Pueblo landfill, and the disposal fee. Each additional set on the same stop drops to $50 to $75. If you'd rather do it yourself, budget a $25 to $35 minimum dump fee, a $10 to $25 per-mattress special-item surcharge, and about an hour round trip from Pueblo West Drive. Both routes work. Which one is smarter depends on your truck, your back, and whether the mattress is going down a staircase. Here is the honest math.

Why mattress disposal is its own problem in Pueblo West

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no municipal trash service. Every household contracts with a private hauler, and almost none of those contracts include a mattress at the curb. A queen set does not fit in a 96-gallon cart, and haulers will not take it loose. Some offer a bulk pickup once or twice a year, or a special pickup for $25 to $60, but you are typically scheduling that two to three weeks out and dragging the set to the road yourself.

That gap is the whole reason "mattress and box spring disposal near me" gets typed from a 81007 zip code at 9 p.m. on a Sunday. Your old bed is in the garage, the new one arrives Tuesday, and nobody in your normal waste chain wants it.

The DIY route, priced honestly

Do this yourself if the numbers work. Here is what nobody tells you before you tie a box spring to a roof rack.

  • A queen mattress is 60 by 80 inches. A standard 6.5-foot pickup bed is 78 inches long with the tailgate up. It does not lie flat. A short 5.5-foot bed is 66 inches, so a queen hangs out more than a foot past an open tailgate.
  • A king is 76 by 80 inches and will not sit flat in any consumer pickup bed. King box springs usually come as two split units, each 38 by 80 inches, which is the one piece of good news in this paragraph.
  • A memory foam queen weighs 80 to 110 pounds and has no rigid frame to grab. Innerspring is lighter but flops. Neither is a one-person job down a flight of stairs.
  • Pueblo West gets spring gusts of 40 to 50 mph. A box spring is a sail with a wood frame. Colorado law puts unsecured-load liability on the driver, and if it leaves your truck on Highway 50 you own whatever it hits.
  • Call the landfill scale house before you drive. Rates, hours, and the mattress surcharge change, and arriving at 4:45 p.m. on a Saturday to a closed gate costs you the whole trip.

If you have a trailer, a helper, ratchet straps, and a free Saturday morning, DIY is genuinely cheaper. Roughly $40 in fees and a tank of gas beats $95 every time.

The Colorado recycling reality check

Search results will tell you mattresses are 75 to 80 percent recyclable by weight — the steel, the foam, the cotton batting, the wood in the box spring frame. An innerspring queen holds around 25 pounds of recoverable steel. All true.

What those pages leave out: only four states have mattress producer-responsibility programs — California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon. California funds it with a recycling fee of about $10.50 collected at the register on every mattress sold. Colorado has none of that. There is no state-funded free mattress drop-off in Pueblo County, and no manufacturer is paying to recycle the bed you bought in 2017.

> Any Pueblo West company that promises your mattress will be recycled should be able to tell you the name of the facility and what it costs them. If they cannot, "recycled" means "landfilled" and they are counting on you not asking.

We will not tell you your mattress gets recycled. In this market, mattresses go to the landfill, and the honest thing is to say so. The volume we divert on a typical job is metal, appliances, and clean furniture — not bedding.

When you should NOT hire us

This is where we talk ourselves out of the job. Four situations where paying $95 is the wrong move.

  • You are buying a new bed this month. Nearly every mattress retailer will haul the old set on the delivery truck for $0 to $50, and many include it free with the purchase. You must arrange it when you order, not when the truck shows up, and most refuse anything stained, wet, or infested. Cost you $0. Cost us the job. Take the deal.
  • You already pay for a hauler with bulk service. Read your contract or call them. If your plan includes one or two bulk pickups a year, a mattress set is exactly what that benefit is for. Using it costs nothing beyond dragging the set 40 feet to the curb.
  • You own a truck and a Saturday. One mattress is not worth a service call if you can move it yourself. We are worth the money on stairs, on multi-item loads, on tight turns, and when nobody in the house should be lifting 100 pounds. We are not worth it for one twin mattress in a one-story garage with a truck in the driveway.
  • It is one small item and you want it gone this hour. Single-item jobs carry a minimum charge because the cost is the truck and two people driving out, not the mattress. If you have a mattress plus a couch plus a broken dresser, our per-item price falls hard. If it is one twin, you are paying mostly for the drive.

Two more limits worth naming. We will not remove a mattress from a room we cannot legally or safely enter, which matters on rentals where the tenant has not vacated. And we do not offer guaranteed arrival windows narrower than two hours. Pueblo West is spread across 50-plus square miles of half-acre and five-acre lots, and a job on the west end can run 40 minutes past estimate. If your day requires a 10:00 a.m. hard stop, say so when you book and we will schedule you first.

Bed bugs, stains, and the disclosure that saves everyone money

Tell us before we arrive. Nobody is judging you, and it changes how we handle the job.

  • Bed bugs survive roughly five months to a year without feeding, depending on temperature. A set carried loose through your hallway sheds them along the route.
  • Encase the mattress and box spring in plastic before we arrive if bugs are suspected. Mattress bags run $10 to $20 at any hardware store. That single step protects the rest of your house, our truck, and the next customer's home.
  • Undisclosed infestation adds a decontamination charge and may mean we decline on site. Disclosed infestation is just a normal job with plastic on it.
  • Stains, urine, and water damage do not raise the price. They only kill your donation and retailer haul-away options, which is why they matter to your decision, not ours.

Donation is usually off the table regardless. Goodwill, Salvation Army, and most Colorado thrift operations refuse used mattresses outright for liability reasons. Shelters will occasionally take a set less than a year old with a receipt. Assume no.
