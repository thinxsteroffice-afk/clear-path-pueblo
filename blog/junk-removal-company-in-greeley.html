When hiring us is NOT worth it

This is the section most junk removal pages leave out. Here's when you shouldn't hire us, and sometimes when you shouldn't hire anyone:

  • You're in Greeley, Evans, Windsor, Loveland or anywhere in Weld or Larimer County. We won't take the job. A 340-mile round trip makes us the wrong choice on cost and on scheduling. A local company can usually come out in 24 to 48 hours. We'd need you to wait for a day we could give up to the drive.
  • You have less than about 2 cubic yards of junk. That's about one pickup bed. Most haulers, us included, have a minimum charge that covers the truck, two people and the dump fee. If you own a truck or have a friend with one, a trip to the transfer station costs a fraction of that minimum.
  • Your stuff is still worth money. A working washer and dryer, a solid wood dresser or a newer sectional will usually sell on Facebook Marketplace or go to a charity pickup for free. Paying someone to haul away things people would happily take is a bad deal. Sell or donate first, then call a hauler for whatever's left.
  • You have hazardous waste. Paint cans with liquid in them, motor oil, pool chemicals, pesticides, propane tanks and asbestos materials aren't junk removal items. Most haulers can't legally take them, and a company that says it'll take "everything" without asking questions is a warning sign. Use your county's household hazardous waste program.
  • You need a dumpster for a multi-week remodel. If you're producing debris a little at a time over 2 to 4 weeks, a roll-off dumpster you fill yourself is often cheaper than several hauling visits. Junk removal wins when you want it gone in one visit and someone else does the lifting.
  • You expect a firm price from one blurry photo. We can give a good estimate from clear photos. But volume-based pricing means the final number is set on site, once we can see what fills the truck. If a fixed number before anyone looks matters more to you than speed, book an on-site estimate first.

What a junk removal job actually costs on the Front Range

We'd rather you know the numbers before you call anyone. These are typical Colorado market ranges, not quotes. Always get your price in writing.

  • Single item (a couch, mattress or appliance): usually $100 to $200, depending on weight and stairs.
  • Quarter truck (about 3 to 4 cubic yards): often around $175 to $275.
  • Half truck (about 7 to 8 cubic yards): often around $300 to $450.
  • Full truck (about 15 cubic yards): commonly $500 to $750.
  • Hot tubs: often $300 to $600. A spa can weigh 500 to 1,000 pounds empty and usually has to be cut apart. See our [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) page for how that works.

For scale, 15 cubic yards is about what fits in a single-car garage packed waist-high, or the contents of a 10x15 storage unit. A whole-house estate cleanout often takes 2 to 4 full truckloads, which is why those jobs land in the low thousands.

Things that raise the price with any company: stairs, long carries from the back of a lot, heavy materials like concrete, dirt or roofing (these get priced by weight, not volume), and items with disposal fees. Refrigerators, freezers and AC units need refrigerant recovered under EPA Section 608 before they can be scrapped. Colorado has banned most electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013, so TVs and monitors have to go to a recycler.

How to choose a junk removal company in Greeley (or anywhere)

If you're in Greeley, here's how to screen local companies. We'd want you using these same checks on us:

  • Ask how they price. Volume-based pricing (by how much of the truck you fill) is the industry standard and the easiest to check. Be wary of hourly pricing with no cap. A slow crew costs you more.
  • Ask whether the quote includes disposal. Some low quotes leave out dump fees, then add them to the invoice. Ask for "all-in" in writing.
  • Check that reviews are recent. A 4.9 rating from reviews that are 3 years old says less than 50 reviews from the last 6 months. Look at how the owner answers the negative ones.
  • Confirm insurance. A crew carrying a 300-pound gun safe down your stairs needs general liability coverage. Ask for the certificate. A real company can email it in under 10 minutes.
  • Ask where your stuff goes. A good hauler can tell you which items get donated, which get recycled and which go to the landfill. If they can't answer, assume everything goes to the landfill. Also make sure they aren't the kind of outfit that dumps loads on county roads, which Colorado counties fine.
  • Get the arrival window in writing. "Sometime Tuesday" isn't a schedule. A 2-hour window is reasonable.
