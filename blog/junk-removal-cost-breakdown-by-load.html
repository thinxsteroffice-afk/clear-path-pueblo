JUNK REMOVAL COST BREAKDOWN BY LOAD

Junk removal in Pueblo West typically runs $95–$175 for a single-item or minimum load, $250–$375 for a quarter-truck, $400–$525 for a half-truck, and $600–$800 for a full 15-cubic-yard load. About 40–55% of that price is labor, 20–35% is landfill tipping fees, 10–15% is truck, fuel, and insurance, and whatever's left is margin. A standard three-car garage cleanout in Pueblo West lands between $450 and $700. A hot tub runs $350–$600. Below is the actual line-item math behind those numbers, what pushes a quote up, and — importantly — the three situations where hiring anyone at all is the wrong call and you should rent a trailer instead.

The Four Costs Inside Every Quote

Every legitimate junk removal price in Pueblo County is built from the same four inputs. Any company that won't explain them is hiding one.

  • Labor. Two people, loaded by hand. A half-truck load takes 45–90 minutes on site. At a loaded labor cost of roughly $28–$38 per person-hour (wage, payroll tax, workers' comp), that's $85–$115 in labor on a typical half-load, more if there are stairs or a long carry.
  • Disposal. This is the input customers never see and the one that varies most. Landfill tipping in Pueblo County runs in the neighborhood of $45–$60 per ton with a minimum charge around $25–$30 per visit. A half-truck of household junk weighs 800–1,400 lbs, so $30–$50 in tip fees. A half-truck of concrete, dirt, or shingles weighs 4,000–6,000 lbs, and that same load costs $110–$180 to dump.
  • Transport. Pueblo West to the county landfill and back is roughly 30–40 miles round trip and 60–75 minutes including the scale and unload. A one-ton dump truck at 9–11 MPG burns $12–$18 in fuel per trip, plus commercial insurance, DOT-required maintenance, and truck payment that amortize to $25–$45 per job.
  • Overhead and margin. Licensing, liability coverage, phone, scheduling, and the 30–40% of estimates that never book. A healthy local operator nets 15–25% of the ticket. Anyone claiming to work on 5% is either subsidizing with scrap resale or planning to add fees on arrival.

Add those on a half-truck: roughly $100 labor + $40 disposal + $35 transport = $175 in hard cost against a $400–$525 price. That gap isn't gouging — it's the unbilled hours, the no-shows, and the jobs that turn out to be twice the estimate.

> The single most useful question to ask any hauler: "Is your price by volume or by weight?" Volume pricing on heavy debris means you're getting a bargain. Weight-based pricing on light bulky junk means you are.

The Pueblo West Volume Ladder, With Real Examples

Our truck holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly the same cube as a 6x12 enclosed trailer packed to the ceiling. Here's what each price tier actually looks like in a Pueblo West home:

  • Minimum load — $95–$175. One couch, one refrigerator, three mattresses, or a small pile at the curb. Loading time under 20 minutes.
  • Quarter truck — $250–$375. A bedroom set plus boxes, or the contents of a 5x10 storage unit.
  • Half truck — $400–$525. A single-bay garage cleaned to the walls, or the debris from a 10x12 shed teardown.
  • Three-quarter truck — $525–$650. A typical [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) on a half-acre Pueblo West lot with a workbench, tires, and 20 years of paint cans.
  • Full truck — $600–$800. A full three-car garage, a modest [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo), or a 12x24 deck tear-out.

Multi-truck jobs — hoarding conditions, full estate clear-outs, foreclosure turnovers — price at $1,400–$4,200 depending on truck count and whether biohazard handling is involved.

Items That Break the Volume Formula

Some things cost far more or far less than their cube suggests, and this is where quotes diverge wildly between companies.

Cost more than they look: - Hot tubs — $350–$600. Not the volume; the demolition. A 7x7 spa takes 60–120 minutes of sawzall work and fills a third of a truck with fiberglass and wet foam that Pueblo landfills charge full weight on. See [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo). - Concrete, dirt, rock, and brick — $85–$140 per ton, priced separately. A 10x10 patio slab 4 inches thick weighs about 6,000 lbs. That single slab costs $300–$500 to dispose of before anyone swings a hammer. [Concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is always a weight job, never a volume job. - Mattresses — $35–$75 each surcharge. Many transfer stations charge a per-unit mattress fee on top of tonnage. - Tires — $8–$25 each. Colorado bans tires from landfills; they route to a processor. - Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units — $25–$50 refrigerant recovery fee per unit, required by EPA Section 608. Any hauler quoting $0 for fridge freon handling is dumping it improperly. - Paint, solvents, pesticides, and pool chemicals — $0 through the county household hazardous waste program, but $40–$120 per box if a hauler handles it. Take these yourself; it's free and we can't beat free.

Cost less than they look: appliances with steel value, aluminum, copper wire, and clean scrap metal. A washer, dryer, and water heater together carry $18–$40 in scrap value at a Pueblo scrapyard, which is why [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) prices below its volume.

When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out. There are three clear scenarios where paying a hauler is a bad financial decision, and one where it's simply the wrong service.

1. You have a heavy, single-material load and a free weekend. If you're clearing 4 tons of concrete, dirt, or roofing tear-off, our price is dominated by tipping fees we're passing through with markup. Rent a dump trailer for $80–$130 per day in Pueblo, pay the landfill directly at $45–$60 per ton, and you'll spend $300–$400 on a job we'd quote at $700–$900. You'll lose a full Saturday and put 200+ miles on your truck. If your Saturday is worth less than $50/hour, do it yourself. If a whole weekend of hauling is on the table, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster at $375–$525 for a 7-day rental beats us outright — you load at your pace and there's no per-truck ceiling.

2. Your load is under $150 worth of junk and you own a pickup. One mattress, one recliner, four bags of yard debris. Our minimum charge exists because the truck, the two-person crew, and the round trip to the landfill cost the same whether we haul one couch or ten. You're paying $95–$175 for what costs you $30 at the scale house. No amount of convenience math fixes that ratio. Same story with a single curbside item: many Pueblo West trash haulers include 1–2 bulk pickups per year at no charge, or charge $25–$50 per item. Call your hauler before you call us.

3. The stuff is genuinely sellable or usable. A dining set in good condition, working appliances under 8 years old, functional tools, or usable lumber. Pueblo West Facebook buy/sell groups clear decent furniture in 24–72 hours, and Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Pueblo will often take working appliances and cabinetry free, with pickup. Paying us $400 to haul away $600 of resale value is a $1,000 swing in the wrong direction. We'll tell you this on the walkthrough if we see it.

Where we're the wrong service entirely: we are not a demolition contractor for structures on permanent foundations, we do not do asbestos or lead abatement, and we won't touch a job where the debris is inside a structure that's been red-tagged. Homes in Pueblo West built before 1980 with popcorn ceilings, old floor tile, or duct wrap need testing first — an asbestos survey runs $400–$900 and abatement is a licensed trade. Hiring a junk crew to rip out suspect material is how a $600 job becomes a $15,000 remediation.

Other honest limitations: hard-to-access properties on the west side of Pueblo West with long unpaved drives, soft ground after monsoon rain, or steep grade add $50–$150 because a loaded one-ton truck will get stuck. Fire ash, animal waste, and medical sharps are declined or surcharged. And a quote given over the phone from a description is an estimate — final price is set at the truck. Anyone who guarantees a firm price sight-unseen is either padding it 30% or planning to renegotiate in your driveway.

How to Compare Three Quotes Without Getting Played

  • Ask for the price as a fraction of the truck, plus the truck's cubic yardage. "Half truck" means nothing until you know if the truck is 12 yards or 20. A $450 half-load in a 20-yard truck beats a $400 half-load in a 12-yard one by about 25% on value.
  • Ask whether tipping fees are included or added. Included is standard for household junk. Excluded is standard for concrete and dirt. Excluded on household junk is a red flag.
  • Ask what happens if the load runs over. Good answer: a stated per-yard overage, typically $35–$55 per additional cubic yard. Bad answer: "we'll figure it out."
  • Ask for proof of general liability coverage. A crew carrying a 400-lb safe down your stairs without $1M in coverage puts your homeowner's policy on the hook.
  • Get the arrival window in hours, not "morning." A 2-hour window is professional. "Sometime Tuesday" means you're the filler job.

Pueblo West-Specific Cost Factors

Pueblo West isn't Pueblo proper, and pricing reflects it. Lots here average a half-acre to 2.5 acres, which means more accumulated outdoor junk — old swing sets, above-ground pools, wire fencing, and windrows of tumbleweed and Russian thistle — and longer carries from back lot to driveway. Expect $40–$100 in access surcharge on properties where the truck can't get within 75 feet of the pile.

Because Pueblo West Metro District doesn't run municipal trash collection, residents contract private haulers individually, and bulk service varies by contract. Check your hauler's included bulk allowance before paying anyone. Watch the county's periodic cleanup events too — they can wipe out a small load's disposal cost entirely.

Wind is a real scheduling cost here. Spring gusts of 40+ mph mean tarped loads or no loads; a windy-day reschedule is free with us but not with everyone. And monsoon season, roughly July through mid-September, turns unpaved drives into a no-go for a loaded truck for 24–48 hours after a hard rain.
