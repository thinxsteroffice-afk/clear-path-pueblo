CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS DISPOSAL SITES

Construction debris from a Pueblo West job site has three legitimate destinations, and the right one depends almost entirely on what's in the pile. Clean concrete, brick, block, and asphalt go to a crushing/recycling yard along the US 50 corridor — usually $0–$25 per ton, sometimes free if the load is genuinely clean. Mixed construction and demolition debris (wood, drywall, shingles, insulation, siding, carpet) goes to the permitted landfill serving Pueblo County, southeast of the city, typically 20–35 minutes from most Pueblo West addresses at gate rates in the $45–$75 per ton range with a $30–$45 minimum charge. Everything else — anything with refrigerant, paint, solvent, or suspected asbestos — gets turned away at the scale house and has to be handled separately. Call any facility the morning you plan to go; hours and tipping rates change without notice.

That's the short answer. The rest of this page is the part that actually saves money: how to figure out which of those three routes your specific pile belongs in, what it costs to do it yourself versus hire it out, and the several situations where hiring [construction debris removal in Pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) is the wrong call.

Weigh Your Pile Before You Decide Anything

Almost every bad disposal decision in Pueblo West starts with underestimating weight. Volume is what you see; weight is what you pay for.

  • Asphalt shingles: roughly 250 lb per square (100 sq ft) for standard 3-tab, 350–400 lb for architectural. A 30-square reroof is 3.75–6 tons. That is four to six pickup loads, and it will exceed the payload rating on a half-ton truck in about 20 minutes of loading.
  • Broken concrete: about 4,000 lb per cubic yard. A 10x10 patio at 4 inches thick is roughly 1.2 cubic yards — 2.4 tons. A single pickup bed holds about 2 cubic yards of loose material, but you'll hit axle limits at less than half that with concrete.
  • Drywall: 1/2-inch board runs about 1.6 lb per square foot. Gutting a 12x14 bedroom to the studs produces around 700–900 lb.
  • Dimensional lumber and framing scrap: roughly 300–400 lb per cubic yard.
  • Old deck boards, fencing, and shed framing: a 10x12 shed teardown typically yields 3–4 cubic yards and 1,200–1,800 lb.

Run those numbers before you rent anything. A homeowner who books a 10-yard roll-off with a 2-ton included weight allowance for a 30-square reroof is going to eat 2–4 tons of overage at $65–$95 per ton — an extra $130 to $380 on top of the rental.

What Self-Hauling Actually Costs From Pueblo West

The gate fee is the smallest line item. Price the whole trip.

A round trip from a Pueblo West address to the landfill runs 40–70 minutes of driving, plus 10–25 minutes at the scale house and tipping floor during weekday morning peak. Call it 90 minutes per load, conservatively. Three loads is a four-and-a-half-hour Saturday, gone.

Add: truck or trailer rental at $19.95–$29.95 per day plus $0.99–$1.29 per mile and fuel, or the wear on your own vehicle. Add a tarp and ratchet straps if you don't own them — Colorado requires loads to be covered and secured, and a spilled-load ticket runs into the hundreds of dollars before you factor in the person behind you who caught a 2x4 with their windshield. Pueblo West gets sustained spring winds with gusts over 40 mph; an uncovered load of shingles or insulation on Purcell or McCulloch will redistribute itself across three neighborhoods.

Realistic all-in for a DIY three-load mixed-debris haul: $180–$320 and half a day. For a single small load — a few sheets of drywall and some scrap lumber — DIY is often $35–$50 total, and hiring anyone is money poorly spent.

> The break-even isn't a weight or a dollar figure. It's whether your Saturday is worth more than about $60 an hour, and whether your truck's suspension is worth more than the tip fee you're saving.

The Asbestos Rule Nobody Mentions Until It's Too Late

This is the item that turns a $400 disposal job into a $6,000 one, and most disposal pages skip it entirely.

Colorado's air quality regulations require an asbestos inspection by a state-certified building inspector before you disturb material in a structure that could contain it. For single-family residential work, the trigger thresholds are 32 square feet, 50 linear feet, or the volume equivalent of a 55-gallon drum of suspect material. Below that, you're generally outside the regulated scope. Above it, an inspection is required first — and no reputable hauler or landfill will knowingly take the debris without it.

Suspect materials in Pueblo West housing stock: popcorn ceiling texture and joint compound in anything built or remodeled before the mid-1980s, 9x9 floor tile and its black mastic, old linoleum backing, HVAC duct wrap, and cement-asbestos siding and pipe.

Numbers to plan around:

  • A residential asbestos survey: $350–$650, results typically in 3–7 business days.
  • Abatement of a single popcorn-ceiling room by a licensed contractor: $1,500–$4,000.
  • Disposal of confirmed asbestos-containing material: a permitted facility only, with manifesting — not the ordinary tipping floor, and not our trucks.

If your house predates 1985 and you're pulling down ceilings or removing more than a small patch of old flooring, get the survey before you swing the first hammer. A rejected load at the scale house means the debris comes home with you, and now it's contaminated material sitting in your driveway under a Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenant that doesn't allow it to sit there.

Separate the Concrete — It's the Single Biggest Lever

Clean concrete, block, brick, and asphalt are worth separating out even on a small job, because the disposal economics are completely different from mixed debris. Recyclers along the US 50 corridor accept clean rubble at a fraction of landfill rates, sometimes at no charge, because they crush and resell it.

"Clean" is strict: no rebar sticking out more than a few inches, no wire mesh mats, no dirt clods, no wood forms, no plastic sheeting. One 2x4 in a 3-ton load of concrete can reclassify the whole thing as mixed debris and triple the cost.

On acre-plus Pueblo West lots, there's a better option than hauling it anywhere. Crushed concrete makes excellent base under a shop pad, a gravel drive extension, or a horse-trailer turnaround. If you're already planning grading work, keeping the material on site eliminates both the haul and the cost of imported road base, which runs $18–$32 per ton delivered. That's the overlap between [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) and [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) — the cheapest ton of debris is the one that never leaves the property.

When You Should Not Hire Us

Plainly, because it matters more than the pitch:

  • You have one small load and a truck. A half-yard of scrap lumber and a couple of doors is a $35 tip fee and 90 minutes. Any hauler's minimum charge — ours included, and the market minimum in this area sits around $150–$200 — is worse math than your own Saturday.
  • You're running a multi-week remodel. Debris that accumulates over 3–6 weeks belongs in a roll-off container sitting on site, not in repeat service calls. A 20-yard roll-off at $450–$650 for a two-week rental with 3–4 tons included beats three or four separate haul-offs. Ask us and we'll tell you the same thing; we'd rather lose the job than sell you a worse structure.
  • Your load is clean concrete only and you own a dump trailer. Take it to the recycler yourself. You'll pay near-nothing in tip fees and our labor adds no value to material that loads with a skid steer in 20 minutes.
  • You're a GC with a dump trailer and a laborer on payroll. Your marginal cost is fuel and gate fees. Ours includes truck, insurance, two people, and margin. We're not going to beat you on price per ton at volume — we compete on the weeks your crew is billable elsewhere.
  • The material is hazardous or regulated. Confirmed asbestos, liquid paint and solvents, motor oil, propane cylinders, pressurized tanks, ammunition, and medical sharps are all outside what we can legally load. Appliances with refrigerant require certified recovery first, usually a $25–$45 line item. We'd rather say no up front than discover it in the pile.
  • Access is the actual problem. If the debris is behind a locked gate, down a slope our truck can't reach, or on a driveway that won't carry 26,000 lb loaded, the honest answer may be a container placed curbside rather than a haul-off crew.

Two more limitations worth naming. First, we are not the cheapest option on any single load — self-hauling always wins on pure dollars if your time is free. What you're buying is the four hours, the tarping, the scale-house line, and not putting 3 tons of shingles through your own leaf springs. Second, same-day service isn't guaranteed in peak season. April through October, Pueblo West reroofs and yard projects stack up; realistic lead time is 1–3 business days, sometimes longer after a hail event when every roofer in the county is tearing off at once.

How to Prep So the Job Costs Less

Whether you're self-hauling or hiring, these move the number:

  • Sort concrete, metal, and clean wood into separate piles. Scrap metal often has positive value; concrete tips cheap; mixed debris is the expensive bucket. Sorting for 45 minutes can cut a disposal bill 20–30%.
  • Pull nails and stack lumber flat rather than tossing it in a tangle. Loose crisscrossed framing takes roughly twice the truck volume of stacked, and volume is what you're charged for on a truckload basis.
  • Keep the pile off the street and out of the drainage swale. Pueblo West Metro District covenants restrict accumulated debris and outdoor storage; a neighbor complaint mid-project is an avoidable headache.
  • Photograph the pile and text it over for a quote. A photo with a person or a truck in frame for scale produces a far more accurate estimate than a verbal description, and it prevents on-site price surprises.
  • Ask what the quote includes. Fuel surcharge, dump fees, and labor should be one number. If they're separate, ask for the total before anyone starts loading.

Straight Answers on the Common Edge Cases

Treated lumber and old fence posts — accepted as mixed debris at the landfill, not accepted at wood recyclers, and never burn it.

Roofing tear-off with felt and nails — fine as mixed C&D; use a magnet sweeper on the driveway afterward, because roofing nails and tires are a $200 lesson.

Popcorn ceiling scrapings from one small closet — likely under the regulatory threshold, but the landfill can still refuse a visibly suspect load. Bag it, keep it small, and expect questions.

Dirt and sod from a foundation dig — not landfill material in any economical sense at 2,700 lb per cubic yard. Find a neighbor doing fill, or plan grading around it.

A shed or deck you haven't torn down yet — demolition and disposal priced together almost always beats hiring the teardown and the haul separately. See [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) or the full [services](/services) list.
