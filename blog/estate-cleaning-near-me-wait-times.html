What "availability" actually means in Pueblo West

Availability isn't one number. It's three, and they move independently.

  • Estimate availability: We can walk a property in Pueblo West within 24 hours, usually same-day. Estimates take 20–35 minutes for a standard home, 60–90 minutes if there's a detached shop, a shed, or 20+ years of accumulation in the garage.
  • Crew availability: Two-person crew, 1–3 days out most of the year. Four-person crew for a full-house job, 3–7 days out. Peak weeks — the last two weeks of May and the first week of September — push that to 9 days.
  • Disposal availability: This is the one nobody thinks about. The Pueblo County Landfill on Blue Ribbon Road is closed Sundays and closes at 4:00 p.m. weekdays, 2:00 p.m. Saturdays. A crew that finishes loading at 3:15 p.m. in Pueblo West is a 22-minute drive from the scale house. Miss it and that load sits on the truck overnight, which costs you a day.

The practical consequence: a job that "starts Tuesday" in Pueblo West realistically means loading Tuesday, second load Wednesday morning, and broom-swept by Wednesday afternoon. Anyone promising a full four-bedroom cleanout in a single day is either bringing three trucks or leaving the garage untouched.

When an estate cleanout service is NOT worth it

This is the part most companies leave out, so read it carefully. There are five situations where hiring us is the wrong financial decision, and we'd rather tell you now than after you've paid.

1. You have more than 30 days and fewer than 8 rooms. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster in Pueblo West rents for $385–$525 for a 7-day placement, including 3 tons of disposal. If you and two family members can spend three weekends filling it, you'll spend roughly $500 instead of $1,600. You're trading about 26–34 hours of your own labor for $1,100. If your time is worth less than $35/hour and you're physically able, do the dumpster. We'll tell you this on the phone.

2. The estate has genuine resale value and you haven't had it appraised. We are haulers, not appraisers. We are reasonably good at spotting obvious value — we set aside firearms, coins, and anything that looks pre-1950 — but we will absolutely throw away a $900 piece of studio pottery because it looks like a brown bowl. If there's any chance the house holds collectibles, tools, or Western art, spend $200–$450 on a local appraiser first, or bring in an estate sale company. A Pueblo-area estate sale typically nets the family $2,000–$9,000 on a fully furnished home and charges 30–40% commission. That math beats us badly on the right house. Call us for what's left afterward — which is usually 40–60% of the volume and about half the price.

3. Probate isn't settled and you're not the personal representative. If the estate is in probate in Pueblo County District Court and letters testamentary haven't issued, hauling contents can create real liability for you personally. We will ask who has authority. If the answer is "my brother and I are figuring it out," we'll ask you to wait. Colorado informal probate typically takes 4 to 9 months to close; the appointment of a PR usually happens within 10 to 21 days of filing. Wait for the paperwork.

4. It's a single room, a single item, or a garage. A full-service estate crew is overkill and overpriced for that. A [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) job runs $280–$600 and takes 3–5 hours. Don't buy a whole-house package for a garage.

5. You expect us to sort, itemize, or find things. We don't. Our crews move at roughly 3 to 5 cubic yards per person per hour on a clean load-out. If you want every drawer checked for cash and documents, that's a different service, it runs about $65–$85/hour per person, and it can triple the cost of a $1,400 job. Families routinely underestimate this. Do your own document sweep first — bank statements, deeds, the safe, the freezer, the backs of picture frames, taped under drawers. Give yourself 6 to 10 hours for a house someone lived in for 30 years.

Failure modes we've actually run into

  • The "one more room" problem. Roughly 1 in 5 estate jobs grows 25–40% in scope between the estimate and the work day, almost always because a sibling arrives with different opinions or a locked room gets opened. Our estimate holds if the scope holds; if it grows, we re-quote on site before touching anything, and you can say no.
  • Weather. Pueblo West gets around 12 inches of precipitation a year, which sounds like nothing until you hit a June hailstorm or 40 mph gusts off the mesa. We won't load an open trailer in sustained wind above about 35 mph. That's a rescheduled day, and in peak season the next slot may be 4–6 days out.
  • Septic and well access. A lot of Pueblo West is on septic. Trucks and skid steers don't go over leach fields. If your driveway is the only approach and the load-out is from the back, we're carrying by hand, which adds 2 to 4 hours and typically $150–$300.
  • Hoarding-level accumulation. If the floor isn't visible, this isn't an estate cleanout — it's a [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo), it costs 1.5–2.5x more, and it may require biohazard handling we'd need to subcontract. Say so on the first call; discovering it on site costs everyone a day.
  • Donation timing. Habitat ReStore and local thrift operations take furniture but not everything, and their pickup schedules run 7 to 14 days out. If your closing is in 6 days, donation is off the table and it all becomes disposal tonnage.

How to get the fastest slot

Practical, in order of how much time each actually saves:

  • Call with your hard date first. We schedule backward from closings and probate deadlines. A job with a real deadline gets priority over a flexible one, every time.
  • Text photos before the estimate. Ten photos — each room, the garage, the shed, the yard — often lets us quote firmly without a site visit, which pulls the start date forward by 1–2 days.
  • Book Tuesday through Thursday. Mondays and Fridays fill first. Midweek slots in Pueblo West are typically 2–3 days sooner.
  • Clear the driveway and confirm gate width. A 7-foot gate stops a skid steer. Knowing that in advance changes the crew we send.
  • Ask for the landfill cutoff plan. Any competent hauler will tell you what time they need to stop loading. If they can't answer, they haven't thought about your timeline.
