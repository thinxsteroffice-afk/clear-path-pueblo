What a mattress actually costs to get rid of in Pueblo West

Three routes exist here, and they price very differently.

  • Haul it yourself to the transfer station. Budget $25 to $45 for a minimum load, plus about 35 to 45 minutes of round-trip drive time from most of Pueblo West, plus fuel. Call ahead — mattress surcharges and hours change, and some scales enforce a one-ton minimum charge whether you bring 80 pounds or 2,000.
  • Retailer haul-away with a new bed. Mattress Firm, Furniture Row, and most delivery contracts in Pueblo will take the old set for $0 to $50 when they deliver. This is almost always the cheapest option available to you, and we'll tell you to take it.
  • Full-service pickup. Ours runs $95 to $145 for a set, roughly $75 to $95 for a single mattress with no box spring, and $45 to $60 per additional mattress on the same stop. The whole visit takes 15 to 25 minutes.

The reason our first item costs more than our fourth is simple: the truck, the fuel, the insurance, and the two-person crew cost the same whether we grab one queen or six. A single-mattress job is the least efficient thing we do.

Pueblo West has no curbside bulk pickup — and that's the whole problem

This is the part most articles about mattress disposal get wrong, because they're written for cities.

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, governed by a metropolitan district rather than a municipality. Your trash service is a private contract with a hauler, not a city utility. That means there is no annual "large item amnesty week," no spring bulk cleanup, and no calling public works for a special pickup the way a City of Pueblo resident inside city limits sometimes can. Most residential contracts out here cap you at what fits in the cart with the lid closed.

Practically: a mattress left at the end of your driveway on El Camino or Purcell will still be there next week, and covenant enforcement in some Pueblo West filings will notice it before your hauler does.

Colorado does not have a mattress recycling law — here's what that means for you

Four states — California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon — run mattress stewardship programs funded by a fee added at purchase, which gives residents free drop-off at designated sites. Colorado is not one of them. No recycling fee is collected when you buy a bed here, and no free take-back network exists.

The consequence is concrete. A mattress is roughly 80 to 90 percent recyclable by weight — steel springs, polyurethane foam, cotton and felt padding, and a wood frame in the box spring. Around 25 pounds of a typical innerspring queen is recoverable steel. But recovering it takes a facility with a disassembly line, and the nearest real mattress recycler is in the Denver metro, about 110 miles north — a four-hour round trip charging roughly $20 to $35 per unit to accept it.

We will say this plainly: for a Pueblo West job, your mattress is going to the landfill. Driving one queen to Denver burns about $40 in fuel and half a day to recycle $1.50 worth of scrap steel at current per-pound rates. If a hauler tells you they "recycle everything" on a Pueblo West stop, ask them to name the facility. We route metal, appliances, and clean cardboard to scrap and recycling all the time — mattresses, honestly, we don't, because there's nowhere within economic range to take them.

When you should NOT hire us

We'd rather lose a $95 job than take money from someone who didn't need to spend it.

  • You're buying a new mattress in the next 60 days. Wait. Delivery haul-away is $0 to $50 and requires nothing from you. Paying us now and paying delivery later is spending twice.
  • You own a pickup and know one person who can lift. A queen weighs 70 to 90 pounds; a pillow-top king can hit 130 pounds. Two average adults can manage it. A 6.5-foot short bed will take a folded-over queen with ratchet straps; an 8-foot bed takes it flat. Total cost: a $35 tipping fee and an hour. That's a $60+ savings for one hour of work, which is a fine hourly rate.
  • You're already renting a truck or a dumpster for a move. Add the mattress to the load. If you've got a 20-yard roll-off in the driveway for a remodel, our stop is redundant.
  • You have one mattress and want the cheapest possible outcome, full stop. We are not the cheapest. We're the fastest and the one that doesn't involve your back or your Tacoma's paint.
  • You need it gone in the next two hours. We can sometimes do same-day, but we schedule in windows. If you're closing on a house at 3 p.m., don't bet the closing on our routing.

Failure modes worth knowing about. Our published range assumes ground-floor or a normal staircase. A king box spring will not turn a tight 90-degree stairwell — box springs are rigid, and unlike a mattress they don't bend. If yours went in before a wall was framed or came up a stairwell that's since been carpeted and narrowed, the options are cutting the frame apart on site (we'll do it, adds 10 to 15 minutes) or a second-story window exit, which we will decline on anything above the first floor. Confirmed bed bugs change the job: we'll still take it, but it must be sealed in a disposal bag before we touch it, we charge $25 to $40 more for the containment and truck decontamination, and some transfer stations require the wrap at the gate. Say so when you book. We don't care, but a crew that finds out in your bedroom will have to re-plan the whole route.

And the limitation nobody advertises: we cannot donate your used mattress. Neither can anyone else in Pueblo. Colorado sanitation rules and every legitimate charity's own policy mean Goodwill, Salvation Army, and local shelters refuse used mattresses outright, regardless of condition. If yours is two years old and spotless, that changes nothing. The only realistic reuse path is selling it yourself on Facebook Marketplace, and expect $40 to $120 for a good queen and about 10 to 20 days of messaging strangers.
