FURNITURE REMOVAL & DISPOSAL COST

Furniture removal in Pueblo West runs $95–$175 for a single item, $225–$375 for a small load (roughly a sofa, loveseat, and a couple of chairs), and $450–$650 for a half-truck load — think a full bedroom set plus a sectional. A whole-house furniture clearout typically lands between $700 and $1,100. Those numbers include labor, loading, hauling, and landfill fees. What moves you inside that range is volume first, then access: a sectional coming out of a walkout basement with a 90-degree stair turn costs more than the same sectional sitting in an open garage on Purcell Boulevard.

Below is how that pricing actually gets calculated, what the cheaper alternatives really cost once you add them up, and — importantly — the situations where you should not hire us at all.

How Furniture Removal Pricing Actually Works

Nearly every reputable hauler in Pueblo County prices by volume, not by weight or by the hour. The unit is a fraction of the truck bed.

A standard junk removal truck holds about 15 cubic yards — roughly 12 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 4.5 feet of usable stacked height. Here's the math most companies never show you:

  • A standard 84-inch three-seat sofa occupies about 2.5 cubic yards once it's loaded and can't be crushed — about 1/6 of the truck
  • A queen mattress and box spring together take 1.5–2 cubic yards, but they don't stack or compress, so they eat space out of proportion to their weight
  • A recliner is roughly 1.2 cubic yards; a dresser, about 1 cubic yard
  • A 6-piece dining set (table plus four chairs plus a leaf) runs 2–2.5 cubic yards if the legs come off, closer to 3.5 if they don't
  • A sectional with a chaise is often 4–5 cubic yards all by itself — that's a third of a truck for one piece

So when a quote says "1/4 truck, $275," that's about 3.75 cubic yards. A sofa plus a recliner plus a nightstand gets you there. Understanding this is the difference between feeling gouged and understanding the invoice.

Weight matters only at the extremes. Pueblo's transfer and landfill facilities charge by the ton, and rates get adjusted periodically, so verify current numbers before you plan around them. A pressboard dresser might weigh 90 pounds; a solid oak armoire can hit 350. That's why an all-particleboard load and an all-hardwood load of the same volume can differ $40–$80 in disposal cost — a difference most companies absorb rather than itemize.

What Drives Your Price Up or Down in Pueblo West Specifically

Pueblo West is not Pueblo proper, and it is definitely not Colorado Springs. Three local realities affect what you pay:

Lot size and driveway access work in your favor. Most Pueblo West properties sit on 1-acre or 2.5-acre lots with long driveways and detached or oversized garages. We can usually back a truck within 20–30 feet of the load. In older Pueblo neighborhoods near the Riverwalk with narrow alleys and street parking, that carry distance can be 100+ feet, which adds 15–25 minutes of labor per load and typically $40–$75 to the job.

No curbside bulk pickup. Pueblo West doesn't have a municipal bulky-item program the way some Front Range cities do. Residents contract private trash service individually, and most residential subscriptions cap bulk items sharply — commonly one item per quarter, or none at all without a surcharge. Confirm with your specific hauler, because plans vary, but plan on your regular trash service not being the answer for a sofa.

Travel surcharges from out-of-area companies. National-brand haulers dispatching from Colorado Springs are running a 40-mile leg down I-25 each way. Some absorb it; many add $50–$95 in trip fees or simply won't quote a single-item job west of Pueblo. A local crew doesn't have that problem — check our [service areas](/areas) for coverage.

Other real cost factors:

  • Stairs: typically $25–$50 per flight per large item. A walkout basement in the Regency Ridge or Desert Hawk areas counts.
  • Disassembly: budget $35–$75 if a sectional, bed frame, or Murphy bed has to come apart to clear a doorway.
  • Same-day service: commonly a $50–$100 premium if we're rearranging a booked route.
  • Volume discount: per-item cost drops sharply after the third piece. One sofa might be $145; four pieces averaging out to $78 each at the small-load price is normal.

The Cheaper Alternatives, Priced Honestly

Self-haul with a rented truck. A 10-foot box truck from a Pueblo rental counter runs about $29.95/day plus $0.99/mile, and a Pueblo West round trip to a Pueblo disposal site is 35–45 miles. Add fuel, add a minimum disposal charge in the $30–$60 range for a small pickup-sized load (call ahead for current rates and accepted materials), and you're at roughly $110–$150 out of pocket — plus 3 to 4 hours and a second person. If you own a truck and have a friend, self-haul genuinely wins on cost. That's not a sales pitch against ourselves; it's arithmetic.

Donation. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar organizations will take furniture, but understand the filters: most decline anything stained, torn, pet-damaged, or smoke-exposed, most won't take mattresses at all, and free pickup schedules commonly run 2–4 weeks out with a first-floor, curbside-or-garage-only rule. If your sofa is clean and you're not on a deadline, donate — you get the item gone plus a deduction (a used sofa in good condition typically values at $50–$200 for tax purposes; confirm with your preparer).

Facebook Marketplace "curb alert." Free, works maybe 40–50% of the time for decent pieces, and requires you to tolerate no-shows. It does not work for mattresses, and leaving furniture at the curb indefinitely on a Pueblo West property can draw a code complaint.

> The honest version of this business: if your item is clean, portable, and you're not in a hurry, you probably shouldn't be paying anyone. Our value is volume, weight, stairs, and speed — not the act of picking something up.

When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the part most furniture removal pages skip. Here's where paying a hauler is the wrong call:

  • A single lightweight item you can move yourself. One nightstand, one office chair, one folding table. Paying $95–$125 to remove something two adults can load into an SUV in four minutes is a bad trade. We'll tell you this on the phone.
  • Furniture in genuinely good condition with time on your side. If it's resale-grade, a $145 removal fee plus a forfeited $250 sale price is a $395 swing. List it first. Call us in three weeks if it doesn't move.
  • You already have a dumpster on site. If you're mid-remodel with a 20-yard roll-off in the driveway, the marginal cost of throwing a sofa in it is close to zero. Don't pay twice.
  • Mattresses, if cost is your only concern. Colorado has no statewide mattress recycling program that subsidizes disposal — unlike California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon, where a fee paid at purchase covers the back end. Mattress disposal here is a real cost with no offset, and single-mattress jobs are among the worst value-per-dollar we sell. Details on our [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page.
  • You need it gone in the next two hours on a Saturday. We can sometimes do it, but you'll pay a same-day premium and the schedule isn't guaranteed. If a hard deadline is the whole point, book 48–72 hours out or rent a truck.

Failure modes worth knowing about, plainly stated:

Doorway math beats muscle. A 38-inch-deep sofa will not clear a 32-inch doorway with a tight turn no matter who's carrying it. If disassembly isn't possible without destroying the piece, the options are to cut it apart on site (we'll do it, but it's irreversible and we'll get your explicit go-ahead first) or leave it. Occasionally the honest answer is: this came in before a wall went up, and it isn't coming out intact.

Weather stops work. Pueblo West gets high wind days where gusts exceed 40 mph, and strapping a load in that is unsafe. We reschedule rather than risk it, usually within 24–48 hours.

We can't take everything. Paint, solvents, propane tanks, tires, and anything with refrigerant in it fall under separate rules and separate facilities. Furniture with visible bed bug activity gets declined outright — no exceptions — because contaminating a truck affects every customer after you. Check our [FAQ](/faq) for the full excluded-items list.

Estimates given over the phone are estimates. If the description was "a couch" and the reality is a couch plus a hide-a-bed frame plus two chairs in the shed, the price changes. We re-quote on site before touching anything, and you can walk away at zero cost.

Getting an Accurate Quote in Under Five Minutes

Text photos. Not a description — photos. Include one wide shot showing the room and the path to the door, and one shot of each large piece with something for scale. Add three details: how many stairs, whether the driveway is paved or gravel, and whether anything needs to come apart. That's enough for a firm number within about 10%, and it prevents the on-site surprise that wastes both our time.

For most Pueblo West households clearing one or two rooms, the realistic all-in number is $225–$450, done in 45–90 minutes, with everything donation-eligible routed to a donation partner before it sees a landfill.
