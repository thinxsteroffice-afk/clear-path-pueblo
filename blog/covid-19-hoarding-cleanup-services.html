COVID-19 HOARDING CLEANUP SERVICES

Yes. You can hire hoarding cleanup in Pueblo West during or after a COVID-19 illness, and a well-run crew can do the job safely. COVID is usually not the biggest health risk in a hoarded home, though. The CDC estimates that the chance of catching COVID-19 from touching a contaminated surface is generally less than 1 in 10,000. Lab testing published in 2020 found the virus lasted up to 72 hours on plastic and stainless steel, 24 hours on cardboard, and 4 hours on copper. So if the house sits empty for three days before work starts, most of the surface concern is gone. In Pueblo West, the hazards that more often hurt cleanup crews and families are rodent droppings, ammonia from pet urine, mold, and falling stacks. This page covers how ClearPath handles a hoarding cleanout when COVID is part of the picture, what it costs, and when you should hire someone else or not hire anyone yet.

How We Run a Hoarding Cleanout When COVID-19 Is Involved

Most calls we get with "COVID" in them fall into one of three situations:

  • Someone in the home tested positive recently. We schedule around their recovery. Current CDC guidance says a person can return to normal activity once they've been fever-free for 24 hours without medication and their symptoms are improving, then take extra precautions for the next 5 days. We plan the work for after that point, or we work while the person is somewhere else.
  • The resident died, and COVID was the cause or a factor. The home is often closed up for weeks before family can deal with it. Any virus on surfaces is long gone by then. The real issues are the backlog of belongings, spoiled food, and family members trying to make decisions while grieving. Many of these jobs look like our [estate cleanout service in Pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) with more volume.
  • A family member is worried about germs in general. That's reasonable. Our answer is the same protective setup we'd use on any hoarding job, as described below.

On every hoarding job our crew wears N95 respirators (rated to filter at least 95% of airborne particles), nitrile gloves under work gloves, and disposable coveralls when the house calls for them. Those precautions cover COVID, dust, mold spores, and droppings all at once. You don't pay extra for a separate "COVID protocol," because the hoarding protocol already includes it.

The Risk Pueblo West Homes Actually Have: Deer Mice and Hantavirus

Competing pages focused on COVID tend to skip this part. Pueblo West sits on open prairie, and deer mice move into garages, sheds, and cluttered rooms. Deer mice carry hantavirus. Colorado regularly reports some of the highest hantavirus case counts in the country, and hantavirus pulmonary syndrome kills roughly 38% of the people who develop it.

You catch it by breathing dust from dried droppings and nesting material, and that dust gets stirred up during a cleanout. A home that has been packed for years almost always has some. Our crew follows the CDC method:

  • No dry sweeping and no vacuuming of droppings, because both send particles into the air.
  • Spray droppings and nests with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution and let it soak for 5 minutes before picking anything up.
  • Open the house to air it out for at least 30 minutes before work begins in closed rooms, sheds, or crawl spaces.
  • Bag contaminated soft goods (bedding, upholstered furniture, cardboard) on site instead of carrying them loose through the house.

> If you're trying to decide whether to clean a cluttered Pueblo West house yourself, be more concerned about the mouse droppings behind the boxes than about COVID on the doorknobs.

What Hoarding Cleanup Costs in Pueblo West

We price hoarding jobs mainly on volume and labor time. The exact number comes from a free on-site walkthrough. These are typical ranges for Southern Colorado so you can plan:

  • One full truckload (about 15 cubic yards): usually $450–$650, including labor and disposal fees.
  • A typical moderate hoarding job in a 1,200–1,800 sq ft home: usually 2 to 6 truckloads, which works out to roughly $1,200–$3,500.
  • A severe job (Clutter Image Rating 7–9 out of 9, with rooms you can't walk through): can run $4,000–$8,000+ and take 3 to 5 working days with a crew of 3 to 4.
  • Surcharges apply to items with special disposal fees, such as mattresses, tires, refrigerators with refrigerant, and paint. See our [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) and [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) pages for how we handle those.

Hoarding jobs take longer per truckload than regular junk removal because we sort as we go. Families who have cleaned out a parent's house will tell you that cash, savings bonds, jewelry, deeds, and photos turn up in strange places. Before we start, we agree with you on a "save" list, such as documents, keepsakes, and anything that looks valuable. Those items go into marked bins and don't go on the truck. Sorting adds time. If you tell us you want everything gone with no sorting, the price goes down.

When Hoarding Cleanup Is NOT Worth It (or We're the Wrong Company)

Being clear about this up front saves everyone money and frustration. Don't hire us, or wait before hiring anyone, in these situations:

  • There are bodily fluids, blood, or an unattended death. If someone died at home and wasn't found for some time, the space needs a licensed biohazard or trauma cleanup company. A junk removal company isn't qualified to remediate bodily fluids, and we won't pretend otherwise. The biohazard firm goes first. We can come in afterward for the furniture and bulk items.
  • You need certified COVID disinfection. We remove belongings and debris. We don't offer certified disinfection, fogging, or a documented "decontamination" for a landlord, school, or care facility. If a lease, insurance claim, or regulator requires one, hire a remediation company that uses EPA List N products and provides written documentation.
  • There's mold over about 10 square feet. The EPA says mold patches larger than roughly 10 square feet generally call for a professional mold remediator. We can clear the contents so the remediator can get to the walls, but we don't treat mold.
  • The person living there hasn't agreed. This is the hardest one to hear. Hoarding disorder affects an estimated 2–6% of adults, and a cleanout forced on someone without their participation or any mental health support often ends with the house filling up again, sometimes within a year. That can mean paying $3,000 now and again later. If the resident is alive, still lives there, and is opposed, consider bringing in a therapist who treats hoarding disorder, or a county adult protective services caseworker if safety is at stake, before you book a crew. The exception is an immediate safety threat, such as a fire hazard, blocked exits, or a code enforcement deadline. In that case clearing the house is the right call, and we can work through the clutter in stages so it isn't one traumatic day.
  • The job is small. If the real volume is under half a truckload and you have a trailer, a healthy back, and a free Saturday, hauling it to the landfill yourself will cost much less than our minimum. If you go that route, use the bleach method above for any droppings.
  • You're counting on insurance. Standard homeowners policies usually don't cover hoarding cleanup because the clutter built up gradually rather than resulting from a sudden covered event. Check with your insurer before you build your budget around a claim.
  • There's structural damage. Sagging floors, roof leaks, or rotted joists under the clutter need a contractor's assessment. Removing weight can expose problems that we can't fix.

What a Typical Job Looks Like, Day by Day

Here's a realistic timeline for a moderate job, a 3-bedroom Pueblo West home at about level 5–6 out of 9:

1. Walkthrough (30–60 minutes). We look at every room, the garage, and any outbuildings, check for droppings, mold, and pet waste, and give you a written estimate. 2. Day 1. We clear entryways and hallways first so there are safe exits. We set up save bins and start on the rooms with the most volume. 3. Day 2. We work through bedrooms and the kitchen. Spoiled food and pantry items go straight out in sealed bags. We stop to show you anything questionable. 4. Day 3. We clear the garage and outbuildings and do a final sweep after the droppings have been treated. If the garage is a big job on its own, our [garage cleanout service](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) covers it.

At the end, the house is broom-swept. We don't deep clean. Most families bring in a house cleaner or carpet company afterward. Usable furniture and household goods go to Pueblo-area donation centers when they're in good enough condition to accept, which is often not the case for items from a heavily hoarded home.
