WASTE MANAGEMENT MATTRESS COST

Waste Management charges roughly $50 to $115 to pick up a single mattress in the Pueblo area, and that price assumes you are already a subscribing residential customer. It is billed as a bulky-item or special pickup on top of your regular monthly rate, it has to be scheduled in advance, and it is not covered by standard cart service at any price tier. Your two alternatives in Pueblo West: haul it to the landfill yourself for $30 to $55 all in, or hire a local junk removal crew for $85 to $150 for one mattress. Below is the actual math on all three, including the cases where hiring us is the wrong call.

What Waste Management Actually Charges Here

Bulky-item fees are set regionally and they move. The number you find on a national blog written for Houston or Phoenix will not match your invoice in Pueblo County. As of 2026, the pattern across southern Colorado haulers looks like this:

  • $50 to $115 per bulky item, with mattresses and box springs usually counted as two separate items. A queen set is often billed at $100 to $190 total, not $50.
  • Existing customers only. Most haulers will not do a one-off bulky pickup for a non-subscriber. If you cancelled service or never had it, the quoted price is irrelevant.
  • 7 to 14 days of lead time. Special pickups get slotted onto your normal service day, and the next open slot is frequently a week and a half out.
  • Curbside placement required by 6:00 a.m., which means the mattress sits at the end of your driveway through however many days of Pueblo West wind and afternoon thunderstorms fall in between.
  • Bagged, or it may be skipped. A soaked mattress gains 40 to 60 pounds of water weight and drivers are within their rights to leave it.

Call your hauler and ask for the bulky rate in writing before you assume. Fifteen minutes on hold beats a $190 surprise line item.

Why Your Cart Won't Take It, No Matter What You Pay For

Automated side-load trucks in Pueblo West grab a 96-gallon cart with a mechanical arm and dump it. A mattress does not fit in a 96-gallon cart, and cutting one up to make it fit is a bad afternoon: you are looking at 80 to 120 pounds of foam bonded to a steel innerspring grid, and standard tin snips will get you about a third of the way through before your hands quit.

There is a local wrinkle a lot of national pages miss. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. The Metro District handles water, sewer, parks and fire, but it does not run municipal trash collection. Every household here contracts privately, which means your neighbor's bulky-item rate may be genuinely different from yours because you are with different companies. There is no single "town rate" to look up.

The Landfill Route: Real Numbers for Pueblo West

Hauling it yourself is the cheapest option and it is not close. Honest accounting from a Pueblo West address:

  • Tipping minimum: $28 to $45 for a small load at the Pueblo-area landfill, plus a per-mattress surcharge that commonly runs $12 to $30 per unit at facilities that assess one.
  • Distance: 18 to 26 miles round trip depending on which side of Pueblo West you are on, call it 50 to 70 minutes with the gate line.
  • Fuel: $6 to $9 in a half-ton truck at 15 mpg.
  • Truck rental if you don't own one: $19 for the first 75 minutes at the big-box load-and-go counters, or roughly $19.95 plus $0.99 per mile on a standard pickup rental, which lands near $45 for this trip.
  • Total: $34 to $55 if you own a truck. $75 to $100 if you rent one, at which point the rental has eaten the entire advantage.

Bring a helper and two ratchet straps. A queen mattress in an unsecured truck bed at 65 mph on Highway 50 becomes someone else's windshield, and Colorado's unsecured-load statute carries fines that start around $65 and climb sharply if the load causes an accident.

Colorado Has No Mattress Recycling Program, and That Changes the Math

Here is the piece almost every national article gets wrong for Colorado readers. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Oregon run extended producer responsibility programs where you pay a recycling fee at purchase and drop the old mattress at a network collection site for free later.

Colorado has no such program. No purchase-time fee, no free drop-off network, no state-funded recycler in Pueblo County. Any Colorado page telling you to "find your free state mattress recycling site" is copying text written for Sacramento.

The practical consequences:

  • Roughly 75% of a mattress is recyclable by weight in steel, foam, wood and fiber, but with no subsidized processor within reasonable driving distance of Pueblo, the recovered material does not pay for the labor to separate it.
  • The scrap steel in a queen innerspring is about 25 pounds. At recent scrap rates near $0.06 per pound, that is under $2, against 20 to 30 minutes of teardown.
  • Any Pueblo-area company charging you a $30 to $40 "recycling fee" should be able to name the facility. Ask. If they cannot, you paid extra for a landfill trip.
  • Colorado's overall landfill diversion rate sits near 16%, roughly half the national average. That is the honest context, not a marketing angle.

An estimated 15 to 20 million mattresses hit US landfills annually, each occupying about 23 cubic feet. Real, and also not something a single Pueblo West household solves by paying a $35 upcharge.

The Illegal Dumping Math Nobody Talks About

Mattresses dumped on county roads southwest of Pueblo West are a recurring problem, and it is worth stating what the downside actually is. Colorado littering statutes and Pueblo County code carry fines that can reach $1,000, with jail exposure up to 90 days for repeat or large-volume dumping. Loads are traced through mailing labels, prescription bottles and delivery slips more often than people expect. A $45 landfill fee is a bargain against a four-figure citation.
