What "Junk Removal and Cleaning" Actually Means Around Here

The phrase covers three genuinely different jobs, and conflating them is how people end up paying twice.

  • Junk removal — labor plus a truck plus dump fees. Two people carry it out, it leaves, you get the floor back.
  • Broom-clean / trash-out — junk removal plus sweeping, vacuuming loose debris, and bagging the small stuff. This is the standard for [foreclosure and eviction cleanouts](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) and most real estate turnovers.
  • Detail cleaning — appliances degreased, bathrooms scrubbed, windows, floors mopped. Priced per hour or per square foot by a cleaning company, typically $0.12–$0.20/sq ft locally, or $45–$65/hour per cleaner.

We do the first two. A 1,600 sq ft house in Pueblo West that needs both usually runs $600–$900 for the haul-out and another $220–$320 for a move-out clean from a separate crew. Bundling them under one invoice doesn't lower that total — it just hides which half you're paying for.

Pueblo West Pricing, With The Numbers That Move It

Volume sets the base price. Four things move it after that, and they move it more here than in Colorado Springs or Denver.

Dump fees. The Pueblo County landfill charges by weight, and a full truck of mixed household junk typically weighs 2,800–4,200 lbs, running $90–$160 in tipping fees alone. That's baked into our quote, not added later. Where it stops being baked in: concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, and rock go to different facilities at different rates — a half-load of [concrete or rock](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) can carry $120–$200 in disposal on its own because weight climbs past 6,000 lbs fast.

Distance. Pueblo West is spread across roughly 47 square miles. A job off Purcell near the Joe Martinez corridor and a job out on the west end past Avenida del Oro are a 20–25 minute drive apart. We don't charge a trip fee inside Pueblo West, but for acreage properties with a 400-foot driveway to the barn, add 30–45 minutes of labor to the estimate.

Stairs and access. A second-floor sectional adds $40–$75. A basement with a 90-degree turn at the landing sometimes means the couch gets cut in half — we'll ask first.

Item-specific surcharges. These are the ones people don't budget for:

  • Mattresses: $30–$45 each in disposal surcharge. Pueblo facilities charge separately for them. See [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo).
  • Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units: $35–$50 for refrigerant handling — federally required, not a markup.
  • Tires: $8–$14 each. Farm tires more.
  • Paint, solvents, pesticides: we won't take them. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events; check dates before you plan the job around it.
  • CRT televisions: $25–$40 e-waste fee for the old glass-tube sets.

> The honest math: about 70% of a junk removal invoice is labor and truck time, and the rest is disposal. Anyone quoting you dramatically under market is either lightweighting the load or planning to dump it somewhere they shouldn't.

When This Isn't Worth It — And Who Should Not Hire Us

This is the section most companies leave out. Here's where hiring us is the wrong call.

You have fewer than 8–10 items and a pickup truck. A single load to the Pueblo County transfer station costs roughly $35–$60 depending on weight. If you can lift the stuff and you have a free Saturday, you'll save $120–$200. Do that instead. Call us for the piano, not the box of paperbacks.

You're doing a slow renovation over several months. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster rented in the Pueblo area runs about $375–$500 for 7–10 days, and you fill it at your own pace. For a kitchen gut or a re-roof, that beats three separate truck visits at $500 apiece. We'll tell you to call a dumpster company. Where we win instead: [construction debris](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) that's already piled and needs to vanish in one visit, or debris you physically can't load.

Your stuff has real resale value. A solid-wood dining set, a working riding mower, decent appliances under 8 years old — those sell on Pueblo-area marketplace listings inside a week. Paying us $450 to haul away $700 of sellable goods is a $1,150 swing against you. We donate what's donatable, but you don't get the cash.

You need a licensed abatement contractor. Asbestos-containing floor tile, popcorn ceilings in pre-1980 homes, vermiculite insulation, black mold beyond a small surface patch — we walk. Colorado requires certified abatement above defined thresholds, and a junk hauler disturbing that material makes the problem worse and more expensive. Expect $1,500–$5,000+ for proper abatement before anything gets hauled.

You expected a cleaning service. Repeating this because it's the single most common mismatch on this search term. We sweep, vacuum loose debris, and haul the bags. We do not scrub toilets, wipe cabinet interiors, clean ovens, or shampoo carpet. If the listing agent said "make it show-ready," half of that isn't us.

Other real limitations:

  • Weather stops work. High wind on the mesa makes tarping a loaded truck unsafe. February frozen ground makes [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) impractical for days at a stretch.
  • Estimates over the phone drift. Sight-unseen quotes land within about 15% maybe two-thirds of the time. Garages and storage units are the worst offenders — people forget what's behind the front row. Send photos.
  • Full trucks can't finish big jobs in one trip. A genuine [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) is commonly 3–7 truckloads across 2–4 days, in the $2,400–$6,500 range. One visit will not do it, and anyone promising otherwise hasn't seen the house.
  • We can't recycle everything. Realistic diversion on a mixed residential load is 35–55% — metal, appliances, cardboard, usable furniture. The rest is landfill. Claims of "90% diverted" on household junk don't survive contact with a real load.

What Same-Day Actually Looks Like

"Same-day service" is marketing language on nearly every junk removal site in Colorado. Concretely, in Pueblo West:

  • Calls before 9:00 AM on a weekday have a decent chance of a same-day slot — call it 30–40% in normal weeks.
  • Late-spring and early-fall are peak. In May and September, 48–72 hours out is the realistic window.
  • The first Saturday after a big wind event, [yard debris](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) calls spike and we book out 4–6 days.
  • Two-person crews handle roughly 2–4 jobs per day, so a single sectional pickup slots in easily; a full [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) needs a half-day block.

If your deadline is a closing date or a lease end, book 7–10 days out. Emergency scheduling is real but it isn't free capacity.

How To Get A Quote That Doesn't Change

The gap between the phone number and the invoice is where trust dies. Close it:

  • Photograph every wall of the space, including behind the pile. Four photos beats a paragraph of description.
  • Say the words "and the shed" — or the crawlspace, or the side yard. Forgotten zones are the #1 cause of price revision.
  • Flag the heavy and the regulated items by name: hot tub, safe, piano, propane tank, paint cans, tires, fridge. A [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) is a $350–$650 job on its own, not a line item on a general cleanout.
  • Measure doorways if furniture came in before a wall went up.
  • Ask what's excluded in writing. Ours: hazardous waste, ammunition, medical sharps, asbestos, and anything requiring a licensed trade.

Deciding Between One Vendor Or Two

Simple test. Answer these:

  • Is the floor covered in stuff? → Junk removal first, always. Nobody can clean around a pile.
  • After it's empty, would you eat off it? If the answer is "no, but a broom fixes it," you're done with one vendor.
  • Is there grease, mold, pet damage, smoke residue, or a leasing agent with a checklist? → Two vendors. Budget $220–$400 for the cleaning half on a typical Pueblo West three-bedroom.
  • Is this a rental turnover you'll repeat? → Build a relationship with both. Landlords running 4+ doors in Pueblo West typically spend $1,800–$3,500/year across haul-outs and turnover cleans.

We'll refer you to a cleaner when that's what you need. Losing half the job is better than showing up with the wrong truck and the wrong expectation.
