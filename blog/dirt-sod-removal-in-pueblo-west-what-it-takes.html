DIRT & SOD REMOVAL IN PUEBLO WEST: WHAT IT TAKES

Fall is when a lot of Pueblo West folks finally get to the yard. They rip out the dead lawn, dig a garden bed or level a spot for a shed pad. The digging goes fine. Then you're stuck with a pile of dirt and rolled-up sod and nowhere to put it. Here's what it takes to get rid of it.

Why Dirt Isn't Like Other Junk

The problem is weight. A cubic yard of dry dirt weighs about a ton, and wet dirt weighs more. Sod with the roots and soil still on it is about the same. A pickup bed holds roughly two yards, which is well past what most half-ton trucks should carry. Landfills charge by weight, so a lot of haulers who price by volume either turn dirt jobs down or charge more once they see the pile.

How We Handle It

We bring a skid steer with a bucket and load straight into a 7-ton dump trailer. Shoveling by hand takes a crew all afternoon. With the skid steer it usually takes under an hour, and most home-sized piles go in one visit. When we're done we rake the area so you're not left with a dirt stain where the pile used to be.

What Drives the Cost

  • Size of the pile. Measure length × width × height in feet and divide by 27. That gives you a rough number of cubic yards.
  • What's mixed in. Clean dirt is the easiest. Rock, roots, chunks of concrete or trash mixed in change where it can be dumped.
  • Access. Can the skid steer get to it? Measure your gate opening and tell us when you call.
  • Wet or dry. Wet dirt is heavier, and heavier costs more to dump.

We'll give you a firm number before we start, so there are no surprises when the trailer's full.

Before You Call

  • Text us a photo or two of the pile with something in the shot for scale.
  • Mark your sprinkler heads and any buried lines you know about.
  • Tell us where your septic is. A lot of Pueblo West lots are on septic, and we keep heavy equipment off the tank and leach field.
  • If you can, keep trash out of the dirt pile. Clean loads are cheaper.

Could You Just Keep It?

Sometimes, yes. If it's clean topsoil, a neighbor may take it for free. Post it on Nextdoor or Marketplace before you pay anyone to haul it. If nobody bites, or it's mixed with rock and roots, that's when you call us.

Other Yard Debris Too

If you're cleaning up the yard anyway, we can take the rest in the same trip: branches, old fencing, broken pavers and whatever else is sitting out back. Doing it all at once costs less than two separate visits.
