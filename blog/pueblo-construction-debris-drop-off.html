PUEBLO CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS DROP-OFF

Homeowners and contractors in Pueblo West have two real options for construction debris: haul it yourself to the Pueblo City Landfill at 1002 S. Pueblo Blvd (roughly 12–18 miles from most of Pueblo West, open Mon–Sat, minimum charge around $25–$35 for a pickup load, more like $60–$90 for a trailer), or have someone load and haul it for you. ClearPath Junk Removal covers the second option across Pueblo West, Rye, Colorado City, Beulah, and Avondale — typical jobs run $180 for a quarter-truck of drywall scrap up to $650–$800 for a full 15-cubic-yard load of mixed demo debris, loading included, usually same-day or next-day.

That's the direct answer. Below is the part most pages skip: the actual numbers, the material-by-material rules that get loads rejected, and the several situations where hiring us is the wrong call and you should just drive it yourself.

What "Drop Off" Actually Means Around Pueblo West

There is no construction-debris transfer station inside Pueblo West proper. Nothing on Purcell, nothing off McCulloch. Every "drop off near me" search from a 81007 ZIP resolves to one of these:

  • Pueblo City Landfill (Pueblo Regional Landfill), 1002 S. Pueblo Blvd — the default. Accepts C&D debris, wood, drywall, shingles, concrete in limited amounts. Cash and card. Scale-based fees above the minimum, generally in the $45–$65 per ton range for construction and demolition material.
  • Private concrete/aggregate recyclers along the Highway 50 corridor — these take *clean* concrete, brick, and block only. If your load has rebar-heavy chunks, dirt, or a single bag of drywall mixed in, expect a rejection or a contaminated-load surcharge.
  • Roll-off dumpster rental — a 20-yard container in the Pueblo West area typically runs $425–$575 for a 7-day rental with a 2–4 ton weight allowance, plus $55–$75 per ton overage.
  • Full-service haul-off (what we do) — we bring the truck and the labor, you point at the pile.

Nobody in Pueblo West is dropping off construction debris at a nearby facility, because there isn't one. You're driving to Pueblo either way. The only question is whether you drive, or we do.

Real Numbers: DIY vs. Dumpster vs. Full-Service

Here's a bathroom gut — vanity, tub surround, tile, about 2,400 lbs of debris — priced three ways for a Pueblo West address:

  • DIY, two trips in a half-ton pickup: roughly $70–$110 in landfill fees, ~50 miles round trip (about $18 in fuel at 14 mpg), plus 5–6 hours of loading, driving, unloading, and re-loading. Add $45 if you rent a trailer for a half-day.
  • 20-yard roll-off: ~$495 base for the week, and you still load every pound yourself. Overweight on a tile-and-mortar load is common — tile runs ~14 lbs per square foot with mortar, so 300 sq ft is over two tons before you add the tub.
  • ClearPath full-service: typically $340–$480 for that job, loaded and gone in about 90 minutes.

> The dumpster looks cheapest on paper and almost never is on tile, plaster, or shingle jobs — those loads go overweight, and the overage bill arrives two weeks later when you've forgotten the container even exists.

Full-service wins on speed and on your back. It loses on price for large-volume, low-weight material — see the section below where we tell you not to hire us.

Material Rules That Get Loads Rejected in Pueblo County

This is where DIY trips go sideways at the scale house. Sort before you load:

  • Asbestos-suspect material — pre-1980 popcorn ceiling, 9x9 floor tile, black mastic, pipe wrap, some old drywall joint compound. Colorado requires abatement by a state-certified contractor above trigger levels (260 linear feet of pipe or 160 square feet of surfacing material). We will not touch it, and neither will the landfill. Testing runs $75–$200 through a Pueblo-area lab and is worth every dollar before you swing a hammer on a 1974 build.
  • Treated lumber and railroad ties — accepted as landfill waste, never accepted at wood recycling. Common in Pueblo West retaining walls.
  • Paint, stain, thinner, roofing tar — no liquids. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events; dried-out latex paint (kitty litter, 48 hours, lid off) can go as solid waste.
  • Concrete over about 18 inches thick or with heavy rebar — needs breaking down first. Our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) crew handles this with equipment; a half-ton pickup does not.
  • Dirt and rock — free-dumping myths persist here. Clean fill has value and there are Pueblo West landowners who will take it, but landfills charge for it by weight like anything else, and dirt is ~2,700 lbs per cubic yard. One yard of soil in a pickup bed is already a half-ton over most half-ton ratings.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Move

We would rather lose a $200 job than take money from someone who didn't need us. Skip full-service hauling if:

  • Your debris fits in one pickup load and you own a pickup. One truckload of scrap lumber is a $25–$35 landfill ticket and 70 minutes of your Saturday. We'd charge $180–$220 for the same thing. That's a bad trade unless you physically can't load it or your time is worth more than $130/hour.
  • You're a contractor with an active, multi-week job. If material is coming out steadily for three weeks, a roll-off on site beats calling us six times. At six visits, you'd pay $1,200–$1,800 to our $500–$700 dumpster equivalent. We'll tell you this on the phone and point you to a container instead.
  • The load is huge, light, and easy. Vinyl siding, insulation batts, cardboard, empty cabinet boxes — high volume, near-zero weight. Volume-based pricing punishes you here. A 20-yard roll-off is the better math on a full re-side.
  • The material is pure clean concrete or clean asphalt. Take it straight to a Highway 50 recycler. Some accept clean loads at $0–$20 per ton — far below landfill rates, and far below what any hauler charges.
  • You need the debris gone in the next two hours on a Sunday. We're not a 24/7 operation. Our realistic window in Pueblo West is same-day if you call before about 10 a.m., otherwise next business day. If you have a hard Sunday-evening deadline, we will probably miss it.

Additional limits, stated plainly: we don't do asbestos, lead abatement, mold remediation, underground storage tanks, or anything requiring a hazmat manifest. We don't cut structural elements or take down anything load-bearing. On loads that are more than about 40% concrete, tile, brick, or dirt by volume, we price by weight, not by truck fraction — a "quarter load" of mortar-set tile can cost the same as a half load of framing lumber, and if you're expecting the volume price, that conversation is unpleasant. We'd rather have it before we show up.

Weather is a real constraint here. Pueblo West wind above about 35 mph makes tarping a loose drywall and insulation load genuinely unsafe, and we will reschedule. In winter, frozen ground and iced driveways on unpaved lots off Bandera or Joe Martinez can stop a loaded truck from getting out. We're not sandbagging — we've had to leave and come back.

What Full-Service Actually Buys You

Three things, and it's worth being honest that they're the only three:

  • Labor. Roughly 70% of a demo cleanup's real cost is loading, not disposal. Two people carrying 3,000 lbs of drywall out of a basement is the actual work. That's what you're paying for.
  • Liability transfer. If a nail goes through a tire or a sheet of OSB comes out of a bed on Highway 50, that's our problem, not yours. Improperly secured loads carry real fines in Colorado.
  • Speed. A job that eats your entire weekend gets done in 60–120 minutes.

If none of those three matter for your specific pile, don't hire anyone. Load your truck.

What We Do With It After Pickup

Not everything goes to the landfill, and this changes the price on some jobs:

  • Clean metal — studs, ductwork, appliances, copper, rebar — goes to Pueblo scrap yards. On teardowns with meaningful metal content, we've credited $40–$150 back against the invoice.
  • Reusable fixtures — solid-core doors, working appliances, cabinet runs in decent shape, unbroken windows — go to reuse channels or donation rather than the pit. This drops disposal tonnage and occasionally your bill.
  • Clean concrete and rock — routed to aggregate recycling, which is cheaper per ton than landfilling and keeps it out of the hole.
  • Mixed C&D and drywall — landfill. No way around it. Drywall in particular is heavy, non-recyclable in practice, and a major driver of tonnage cost.

Realistically, 35–55% of a typical residential demo load gets diverted. On a stripped-to-studs remodel with a lot of drywall, it's closer to 20%. We don't claim a 90% diversion rate, because in this market that number isn't achievable.
