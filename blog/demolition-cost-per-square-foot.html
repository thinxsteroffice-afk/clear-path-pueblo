DEMOLITION COST PER SQUARE FOOT

Pull a Pueblo West demolition quote apart and it comes down to a dollar figure per square foot, so here it is: interior demolition (gut a kitchen, strip a basement, take out non-structural walls) runs about $2 to $7 per square foot in Pueblo West. Full structural teardown of a house or garage runs $4 to $12 per square foot, and a detached garage or shed is usually $3 to $6. A 1,200 sq ft ranch teardown lands around $9,000–$14,000 all-in. A 200 sq ft kitchen gut is $600–$1,400 in labor and haul-off. Concrete flatwork is priced separately at $2 to $6 per square foot for a 4-inch slab. Those are Pueblo County numbers, not national averages — and the per-square-foot math breaks down in ways worth understanding before you sign anything.

Why per-square-foot pricing is the wrong unit for small jobs

The square-foot number exists because it's easy to compare. It is also the least accurate way to price anything under about 400 square feet.

A demolition crew's cost is driven by three things: hours on site, tons to the landfill, and equipment mobilization. None of those scale cleanly with floor area. Mobilizing a skid steer to a Pueblo West lot costs the same $150–$300 whether you're taking out a 120 sq ft shed or a 900 sq ft garage. Spread that over 120 square feet and your "per square foot" rate looks like $9. Spread it over 900 and it looks like $2.50. Same crew, same day, same truck.

This is why you'll see quotes that seem wildly inconsistent. They usually aren't. Ask for the quote broken into labor hours, disposal tonnage, and equipment, and the apparent inconsistency disappears.

> The per-square-foot figure is a sanity check, not a price. If your bid is more than about 40% off the ranges above, something specific is driving it — and you should know what.

Pueblo West cost ranges by job type

Real numbers from work in the 81007 area and surrounding Pueblo County:

  • Interior gut, non-structural: $2–$7/sq ft. A 1,000 sq ft basement finish removal (drywall, framing, carpet, drop ceiling) typically runs $2,800–$5,500.
  • Detached garage teardown, slab stays: $3–$6/sq ft. A 24x24 (576 sq ft) garage is usually $2,200–$3,400.
  • Full house teardown, foundation removed: $6–$12/sq ft. Add $2,500–$6,000 if there's a basement to backfill.
  • Shed demolition: rarely worth per-sq-ft math. Most 8x10 to 12x16 sheds are flat-rate $350–$900. See [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Deck removal: $4–$9/sq ft, higher if it's elevated or attached to the house band joist.
  • Concrete slab, 4-inch, unreinforced: $2–$4/sq ft. With rebar or wire mesh, $4–$6/sq ft. Driveways in Pueblo West are frequently 5–6 inches thick because of the expansive clay, which pushes this to the top of the range.
  • Above-ground pool pad and structure: $500–$1,600 depending on liner disposal and whether the sand base gets hauled.

The line items people forget to budget

Demolition quotes get compared on the headline number. The overruns almost always come from somewhere else:

  • Landfill tipping fees. Pueblo County transfer station rates run roughly $45–$70 per ton for construction and demolition debris. A 1,200 sq ft house generates 55–75 tons. That's $2,500–$5,200 in disposal alone before anyone gets paid for labor.
  • Utility disconnects. Black Hills Energy gas disconnect and Pueblo West Metro District water shutoff have to be documented before a permit issues. Budget 2–3 weeks lead time and $0–$400 depending on the meter situation.
  • Asbestos survey. Colorado requires a certified inspection before demolition of most structures. If your building predates 1990, this is not optional. A survey runs $400–$900. If popcorn ceiling, vinyl floor tile, or duct wrap tests positive, abatement adds $3,000–$15,000 and 1–2 weeks.
  • Permits. Pueblo County demolition permits typically run $100–$350 for residential. Structures on well and septic have additional requirements.
  • Backfill and grade. Removing a foundation leaves a hole. Import fill for a full basement is often $1,800–$4,500 in Pueblo West given haul distances. Our [dirt work excavation pueblo](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) crews price this as a separate line for exactly that reason.

When demolition is NOT worth paying for

This is the part that costs us jobs, and it should be said plainly.

Don't pay a crew for a job you can do in a weekend for the price of a dumpster. A 10x12 shed with no concrete, no electrical, and clear access is four hours with a reciprocating saw, a crowbar, and a trailer. You'll pay $280–$450 for a 15-yard roll-off in Pueblo West. If you're physically able and have a helper, hiring that out at $600–$900 is buying convenience, not expertise. Be honest about which one you want.

Skip a full gut when you're only replacing surfaces. Homeowners routinely demo a whole kitchen down to studs to replace cabinets and counters. If the plumbing and electrical stay where they are and the drywall is sound, you've just added $900–$2,000 in demo and drywall replacement to a job that didn't need it. Cabinet pull-and-replace is a fraction of that.

Demolition rarely pays for itself on a resale. If you're tearing down a detached garage to "clean up" a listing, run the math first. Pueblo West buyers frequently value an outbuilding — even a rough one — at more than the $2,500–$3,400 it costs to remove. Removing it can lower your number. Ask your agent before you ask us.

A structure with real salvage value may be worth deconstructing instead. Hand-deconstruction costs 2–3x machine demolition, but if you have old-growth framing, quality brick, or a metal building, salvage credits and a Colorado charitable-donation deduction can offset a meaningful share. That said, this only works out on maybe 1 in 10 buildings we look at. Don't assume yours qualifies.

Where per-square-foot quotes go badly wrong

The failure modes are predictable:

  • Multi-story doesn't cost 2x. A 1,200 sq ft two-story with an 800 sq ft footprint is often 15–25% *cheaper* than 1,200 sq ft on one level, because roof and foundation area — the expensive parts — are smaller.
  • Access beats area every time. A garage 12 feet from the street and a garage behind a fenced backyard with a 36-inch gate are the same square footage and not remotely the same price. The second one can double, because material moves by wheelbarrow instead of bucket.
  • Attached structures are structural work. Demolishing a garage attached to a house means shoring, cutting, and weatherproofing the exposed wall. Add $1,500–$4,000 and an engineer's letter for the permit.
  • Hidden material changes everything. Plaster-and-lath weighs roughly 3x drywall. Two layers of shingles instead of one adds 4–6 tons. Neither is visible from the driveway during a walkthrough.
  • Slab thickness is guesswork until someone cores it. We've quoted 4-inch driveways in Pueblo West that turned out to be 7 inches of rebar-laced concrete over a pad — a 60–80% cost increase discovered on day one.

Any contractor who gives you a firm per-square-foot number over the phone without seeing the structure is either padding heavily or is going to change the price later. Both are bad for you.

How to compare three bids honestly

Get every bidder to answer the same five questions in writing:

  • Is disposal included, or billed at actual tonnage? A "$4,200 plus dump fees" bid and a "$6,800 all-in" bid may be identical.
  • Who pulls the permit and who pays for it?
  • Is the concrete slab included or excluded?
  • What's the plan if asbestos comes back positive — stop-work and re-quote, or a pre-agreed unit rate?
  • What does the site look like at completion? Cleared and rough-graded, or cleared and left with a pile?

That last one is where most disputes come from. "Demolition" and "demolition plus haul-off plus grade" are different scopes with a $1,500–$4,000 gap between them. Debris left on site after a contractor's demo is a common reason people call our [construction debris removal pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) crew as a second trip — a trip that should have been in the original bid.

Realistic timelines in Pueblo West

  • Interior gut, 800–1,200 sq ft: 2–4 working days
  • Detached garage: 1 day for demo, 1 day for haul-off if the load exceeds one truck
  • Full house teardown: 3–6 days on site, but 3–5 weeks total once utility disconnects, asbestos survey, and county permitting are counted
  • Concrete flatwork under 800 sq ft: typically a single day
  • Wind delays: budget 1–2 lost days in spring. Pueblo West gets sustained 30–40 mph gusts, and no responsible crew drops a wall or runs a dumpster tarp in that.

The permitting lead time surprises people more than the cost does. If you're working against a closing date or a rental turnover, start the paperwork before you start collecting bids.
