CITY OF BOSTON APPLIANCE REMOVAL

If you searched "city of boston appliance removal," here's the quick answer. Boston residents book a curbside appliance pickup through BOS:311, by phone (311) or the 311 app. Refrigerators, freezers, and AC units have to be scheduled ahead because their refrigerant must be recovered before the metal is scrapped. This page is written for Pueblo West, Colorado, which works very differently: there's no city to call. Pueblo West is a metropolitan district, not a city with its own trash department. Every household contracts with a private trash hauler, and most of those haulers won't take a refrigerator at the curb. Below is how appliance removal actually works here, what it costs, and when you shouldn't pay anyone to do it.

Why Pueblo West Has No "City" Appliance Pickup

Boston, Denver, and Colorado Springs run municipal or city-contracted bulk-item programs. Pueblo West doesn't. The Pueblo West Metropolitan District handles water, roads, parks, and fire, but not household trash. Residential pickup comes from whichever private company you subscribe to, and their standard service covers what fits in your 96-gallon cart.

So the first step is to call your hauler and ask two questions:

  • Do you take bulk items, and how often? Some offer bulk pickup once or twice a year. Others charge per item. Some don't offer it at all.
  • Do you take appliances with refrigerant? Many haulers that accept a couch will refuse a fridge, freezer, dehumidifier, or window AC unit because of the refrigerant handling rules below.

If both answers are no, you're choosing between hauling it yourself, retailer haul-away, scrap, donation, or a junk removal service.

The Refrigerant Rule Most People Don't Know About

Under the federal Clean Air Act, Section 608 (40 CFR Part 82, Subpart F), the refrigerant in a fridge, freezer, or AC unit has to be recovered by a certified technician or a facility that verifies recovery before the appliance is disposed of or scrapped. A typical household refrigerator holds roughly 4 to 8 ounces of refrigerant. Units built before about 1995 often contain R-12, an ozone-depleting CFC. Newer ones usually hold R-134a or R-600a.

In practice, this means:

  • Many scrap yards won't take a fridge unless the refrigerant has already been removed and the unit is tagged.
  • Some landfills charge a separate appliance fee for refrigerant units, on top of the weight charge.
  • If you cut the lines yourself to "empty" it, you've vented refrigerant illegally. The EPA can fine individual violations, and civil penalties run into the tens of thousands of dollars per day on paper. Homeowners rarely get fined, but a scrap yard that sees cut lines may turn you away.

Washers, dryers, dishwashers, ranges, and water heaters have no refrigerant, so they're much easier to get rid of.

When Paying for Appliance Removal ISN'T Worth It

This section might cost us some jobs, and that's fine. Here are the situations where you shouldn't hire us or anyone else:

1. You're buying a replacement that's being delivered. Lowe's, Home Depot, Best Buy, and most local appliance stores will take the old unit when they drop off the new one, often for free or under $40. That beats any junk removal price. The catch is that they usually require the old unit to be disconnected, emptied, and sitting where the new one goes. Ask about this *before* delivery day, not when the truck is in your driveway.

2. The appliance still works and is less than about 10 years old. A working 6-year-old washer can sell for $100–$250 on Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo area, and buyers often pick it up themselves. Habitat for Humanity's ReStore also accepts working appliances in many cases and may arrange pickup. Paying to haul away something someone would pay you for doesn't make sense.

3. You have a truck, a dolly, and one strong friend. A dryer or dishwasher is a 20-minute job for two people with a pickup. Take non-refrigerant appliances to a scrap yard and you'll get a few dollars back.

4. It's a single small item. A microwave, a mini-fridge, or a window AC unit doesn't need a truck crew. The minimum trip charge will feel steep next to the size of the item.

5. It's still connected. This is the biggest failure mode we see. Junk removal crews, including ours, do not disconnect gas lines or hardwired electrical. A gas range or gas dryer needs a licensed plumber or gas fitter to cap the line properly. That typically runs $75–$150 for a service call, and it has to happen before anyone hauls the unit. A hardwired water heater needs an electrician or plumber too. If you book removal while the stove is still on gas, the crew will leave without it and you'll have paid for a wasted trip or a rebooking.

> If a gas appliance is still connected, call a plumber first and a hauler second. Doing it in the other order is how a $150 job turns into a $300 one.

6. You're a renter. Appliances in a rental almost always belong to the landlord. Getting rid of one, even a broken one, can come out of your deposit. Get written permission first.

What Makes an Appliance Pickup Go Smoothly

If you've decided removal makes sense, these steps cut the time on-site and keep the price at the low end of the quote:

  • Empty and defrost the fridge or freezer 24–48 hours ahead. A chest freezer full of ice weighs 50–100 lb more than an empty one and leaks all over your floors and the truck.
  • Shut off and disconnect water lines. The fridge ice maker line and washer supply hoses should be turned off at the valve. Leave the hoses attached or remove them, but make sure they're not pressurized.
  • Drain the washer. Front-loaders hold a surprising amount of standing water in the drum and pump.
  • Clear a path. Measure doorways. Most full-size refrigerators are 30–36 inches wide and won't fit through a 32-inch interior door without taking the doors off.
  • Mention basements and stairs when you call. A 300-lb side-by-side coming up a basement stairway takes two people, an appliance dolly, and more time. That's a legitimate cost, and quotes that leave it out tend to change on the day.

Where Your Old Appliance Actually Ends Up

Boston's program can promise recycling because the city controls the contract. In Pueblo West, it depends on who hauls your appliance, so ask. A responsible hauler should be able to tell you:

  • Whether refrigerant units go to a facility that does certified recovery
  • Whether metal appliances go to scrap recycling instead of the landfill (roughly 75% of an appliance by weight is steel, which is fully recyclable)
  • Whether working units get donated instead of scrapped

If a hauler's answer is "it all goes to the dump," that's cheaper for them and worse for you if anyone ever traces a vented fridge back to your address. It also tells you something about how they handle everything else.

Pueblo West Specifics Worth Knowing

A few local details that national articles leave out:

  • Distance matters. Some Pueblo West lots sit on long gravel driveways or out past the paved grid. Crews and haulers sometimes add a charge for remote addresses, so ask up front.
  • Wind. Spring gusts here regularly top 40 mph. Don't leave an empty fridge or freezer at the curb with the door on. An abandoned fridge with a latching door is a real suffocation risk for kids, and a lightweight dishwasher can blow over. Take the doors off or tape them firmly open if it'll sit outside even overnight.
  • HOA rules. Some neighborhoods limit how long bulk items can sit visible from the street, sometimes to 24 or 48 hours. Schedule pickup for the day you drag it out.

The Bottom Line for Pueblo West Homeowners

If you're actually in Boston, call 311 and book the free city pickup. If you're in Pueblo West, the honest order of operations is:

1. Buying a new one? Use the retailer's haul-away. 2. Still works? Sell it or donate it. 3. Have a truck and it's not a fridge? Take it to scrap. 4. Gas or hardwired? Get it disconnected by a pro first. 5. Heavy, in a basement, a refrigerant unit, or one of several items?
