FURNITURE REMOVAL RATES & QUOTES

For furniture removal in Pueblo West, expect to pay about $75–$150 for one small piece like a chair or end table, $125–$225 for a couch or sectional piece, and $350–$650 for a room full of furniture, depending on how much space it takes up in the truck. You can get a written online quote in 15–30 minutes by texting or uploading 3–4 photos of each item. Two things raise the price above those numbers: stairs, and items that have to be taken apart. A good hauler confirms the final price on site before loading, and you pay nothing if you say no. The rest of this page covers how the rates are set, what makes an online quote accurate, and when you shouldn't hire us.

How Furniture Removal Rates Are Actually Calculated

Most junk haulers in the Pueblo area, us included, price by volume: the share of a 15–16 cubic-yard truck your items fill. Weight matters less for furniture than for things like concrete. Furniture is bulky, and a sofa takes up far more room than its weight suggests. Hourly pricing is rare, and a fixed "per item" charge usually only applies when there's just one or two pieces.

Typical ranges in our market:

  • Single small item (nightstand, dining chair, ottoman): $75–$110
  • Single large item (sofa, recliner, dresser, armoire): $125–$225
  • Sectional sofa (3–5 pieces) or sleeper sofa: $175–$300. Sleeper sofas weigh 150–250 lbs because of the steel frame.
  • Quarter truckload (about a bedroom set): $200–$300
  • Half truckload (living room plus dining set): $350–$475
  • Full truckload (whole-house furniture, estate situations): $550–$750

The price covers loading, hauling, and disposal or drop-off at a donation center. It does not cover the add-ons below.

Common add-ons:

  • Stairs: $25–$50 per flight for heavy items
  • Disassembly (bunk beds, wall units, bolted sectionals, pool tables): $25–$75, depending on how long it takes
  • Mattresses and box springs, which usually have their own disposal cost. See our [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page.

Why Pueblo West Jobs Price Differently Than Downtown Pueblo

This is something the national price guides don't cover. Pueblo West has large lots, often ½ acre to 5+ acres. Many homes sit on unpaved roads or have long gravel driveways, and a lot of houses have detached garages or sheds set far back. That changes the job in ways that affect price:

  • Carry distance. Hauling a 200-lb sleeper sofa 30 feet to a curb is quick. Hauling it 150 feet across gravel because the truck can't back up a soft driveway takes two people roughly twice as long. Tell us about this when you ask for a quote.
  • Truck access after rain or snow. Dirt roads west of McCulloch Blvd can get rutted. If we can't bring a 16-foot box truck close, the job may take longer or need a smaller vehicle, which means more trips.
  • Wind. Afternoon gusts over 30 mph are common here in spring. We load lighter pieces like cushions and particleboard first. If you've set furniture outside ahead of time, strap or weigh it down. We've seen loveseat cushions end up two lots away.

None of this adds a fixed surcharge. It's why a quote for "one couch" can differ by $40–$60 between two Pueblo West addresses.

How to Get an Online Quote That Doesn't Change on Pickup Day

Most complaints about online quotes come from the photos, not the pricing. If the photos leave something out, the price goes up when the crew arrives. For an accurate quote the first time:

1. Shoot each item from a corner, not straight on. A corner shot shows depth, and depth is how we estimate volume. 2. Put something for scale in the frame, like a door, a person, or a standard kitchen chair. 3. Photograph the path out, including stairs, narrow hallways, tight turns, and the driveway surface. 4. Mention the hidden stuff. Sleeper mechanisms, recliners with power motors, hide-a-beds, glass tabletops, and anything bolted to the wall. 5. Include everything, even the "maybe" pile. Adding a dresser on the day of pickup is fine, but it moves you into a different price tier.

Quotes built from photos like these are usually within 10–15% of the final price. Quotes built from a text like "a couch and some other stuff" can be off by 50% in either direction, and neither you nor we want that surprise.

> An online quote is only as accurate as the least-flattering photo you send. Photograph the stairs, not just the sofa.

Where Your Furniture Actually Goes

A fair share of what we pick up in Pueblo West doesn't go to the landfill. On a typical load:

  • Donation-ready pieces, meaning solid wood, no stains or tears, and working mechanisms, go to local charities and thrift stores. Plan on 20–30% of a household load qualifying. Upholstered pieces qualify much less often because of stains, pet hair, and smoke odor.
  • Metal frames, bed rails, and hardware go to scrap recycling.
  • Particleboard furniture (flat-pack dressers, bookshelves, TV stands) almost always goes to the landfill. It swells when it gets wet, falls apart when it's moved, and has almost no resale value.

Donation doesn't lower your price, because the crew still has to load it and make the drop-off. It does keep usable furniture in use, and you can ask for a donation receipt when one is available.

When Furniture Removal Isn't Worth It (Who Shouldn't Hire Us)

This section loses us some jobs. If one of these describes you, you'll save money doing something else:

  • You have one piece and a pickup truck. If a friend can help you lift a recliner into a truck bed, you can take it to the landfill yourself. The gate fee for a small load is usually far below our $75–$110 single-item minimum. A 45-minute trip is cheaper than hiring us.
  • Your furniture is in good condition and you're not in a rush. A 5-year-old solid-wood dining set can sell for $150–$400 on Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo area, often within 1–2 weeks. Then the buyer shows up and carries it out, and you've made money instead of spending it. Several charities offer free pickup for furniture in good condition, but you'll often wait 1–3 weeks for a slot, and they turn down anything stained or torn.
  • You're already hiring movers. Many moving companies will haul away 1–2 unwanted pieces for a small fee while their truck is already at your house. Ask them before you book a separate pickup.
  • Your trash service offers bulky-item pickup. Some Pueblo West residential haulers pick up a single bulky item for an added fee, often less than a dedicated junk removal trip. Call and ask first. The downside is usually a curbside-only rule and a wait of up to a week.
  • The furniture has bed bugs. We can remove infested furniture only if it's fully wrapped in plastic before we arrive, and some local donation centers won't let those items onto a truck that's carrying donations. Treat the room first. If you haul the furniture out untreated, the bugs spread through the house on the way out.
  • You need it gone in the next 2 hours. We often do same-day or next-day pickup, but we can't promise a specific hour on short notice, especially on Fridays and at the end of the month when leases turn over.

Two ways online quotes go wrong, stated plainly: (1) your photos underrepresent the job and the on-site price goes up, and (2) the item turns out to be something we can't haul on a regular furniture run, like a piano (upright pianos run 300–800 lbs) or a gun safe, which takes different equipment and a different price. If either happens, you'll get the revised number before we load anything, and you can say no.

What Can Push Your Rate Up or Down

Things that lower your cost:

  • Putting items in the garage or on the driveway before we arrive, which removes carrying time and any stair fees
  • Combining furniture with other junk in one trip. One half-truckload costs less than two quarter-truckloads booked separately.
  • Weekday morning pickups, which are easier to schedule

Things that raise your cost:

  • Upper-floor bedrooms in homes without a straight staircase
  • Solid hardwood armoires and entertainment centers over 6 feet tall
  • Wet furniture that has been sitting outside. It weighs more and falls apart while we load it.
  • Adding items after the quote

If you're emptying a whole house after a death or a move into assisted living, a single furniture quote usually isn't the right fit. Ask about an [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) job instead, which covers furniture, closets, and the garage in one price.
