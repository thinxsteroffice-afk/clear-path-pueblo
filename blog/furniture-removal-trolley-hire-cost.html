FURNITURE REMOVAL TROLLEY HIRE COST

Furniture removal trolley hire is what Americans call dolly rental. In Pueblo West you can rent one for about $7–$15 a day from a truck-rental counter or a big-box tool rental desk. Most people get a 4-wheel furniture dolly (flat platform, rated around 800–1,000 lbs) or an upright appliance hand truck with straps (usually rated 600–800 lbs). Renting a dolly makes sense when you're moving furniture you're keeping, you have a second strong adult, and you have somewhere to take it. If the furniture is leaving for good, the dolly is the cheap part. The hard part is the truck, the dump trip and your back. That's where a junk removal crew usually costs less overall.

What You Can Actually Rent Around Pueblo West

Pueblo West doesn't have much rental inventory of its own. Most people drive 15–20 minutes east on US-50 into Pueblo, where the truck-rental lots and home-improvement tool counters are. Here's what you'll find:

  • 4-wheel furniture dolly (moving dolly): A carpeted platform about 18" x 30" on four casters. Good for dressers, cabinets and boxed items on flat, hard floors. It's close to useless on gravel, which matters out here because so many Pueblo West lots have gravel drives and dirt yards.
  • Upright hand truck: Two wheels with an L-shaped nose plate. Fine for boxes and small cabinets, but it tips easily under a tall load.
  • Appliance hand truck: A taller frame with a ratchet strap and often stair-climbing belts. This is the right tool for fridges, washers and tall wardrobes. Expect to pay at the top of the $7–$15 range or a little more.
  • Moving blankets: Often around $10–$20 per dozen to rent. Without them, dollies scratch both the furniture and your door frames.

Most rentals run on a 24-hour clock. A late return often means paying for a second day, which is easy to miss if your Saturday move drags on.

The Real Cost of the DIY Route

The dolly is never the whole bill. Here's what a DIY furniture haul-away from a Pueblo West home usually involves:

  • Dolly and straps: $7–$15
  • Truck, if you don't own one: In-town truck rentals usually advertise a low base rate (often $20–$40) plus a per-mile charge. A round trip from Pueblo West to the rental lot, home, the landfill and back adds up fast.
  • Fuel: Pueblo West homes are spread out. Many errands here are a 25–40 mile round trip once you add the run into Pueblo.
  • Disposal fees: Landfills and transfer stations charge by weight or by load, and mattresses and upholstered pieces sometimes cost extra. Call ahead for current rates, because they change and we won't guess them here.
  • Your time: A realistic DIY haul of a sofa, a recliner and a dresser takes 3–5 hours from picking up the rental to returning it.

Add it up and a "cheap" dolly rental often becomes a $75–$150 afternoon before you count the risk of getting hurt.

> The dolly costs ten bucks. The pickup bed, the landfill line and the pulled muscle are what make it expensive.

When Renting a Dolly Is the Right Call (and Hiring Us Isn't)

This is the part most junk removal sites leave out. In plenty of situations you should rent the dolly and skip us:

  • You're moving furniture, not getting rid of it. If the couch is going from the living room to a new place across Pueblo West, you need a mover or a dolly and a friend. A junk removal crew is the wrong service. We haul things away to be donated, recycled or dumped. We don't deliver your furniture to a new address.
  • It's one or two light pieces. A nightstand, a small bookshelf or a flat-packed desk that's already broken down can go in your own pickup bed. A standard short bed is about 5.5–6.5 feet long. Paying a crew for one end table is a poor use of your money.
  • You already have a truck, a helper and a free weekend. Then your real cost is the dolly plus disposal, and that's hard to beat.
  • The item is still in good shape. Local thrift stores and charities sometimes pick up free, but schedules can run 1–3 weeks out, and they often turn down stained, torn or pet-damaged upholstery. If you can wait, try them first.
  • You enjoy doing it yourself. That's a fine reason.

### Where DIY dolly jobs go wrong

These are the problems we see most often:

  • Gravel and dirt. Dolly casters stop dead in gravel. A lot of Pueblo West properties have 50–150 feet of gravel drive or yard between the door and where a truck can park. On gravel you end up carrying the load anyway.
  • Stairs. A standard 4-wheel dolly does nothing on stairs. An upright hand truck going down stairs with a 200+ lb load needs one person above and one below. Most dolly injuries happen here.
  • Sleeper sofas and recliners. A sleeper sofa can weigh 200–350 lbs, and power recliners hide motors and wiring that throw off the balance. Both are awkward on any dolly.
  • Doorways. Most interior doors are 30–32 inches wide and exterior doors are usually 36 inches. Many sectionals and box springs don't fit through without being taken apart or tilted, so check before you rent.
  • Summer heat. Pueblo West regularly hits the 90s°F from June through August, and at about 4,700 feet of elevation you'll dehydrate faster than you expect. Plan heavy lifting for before 10 a.m.

When a Junk Removal Crew Beats the Dolly

Hiring out makes sense when the furniture is leaving for good and one or more of these is true:

  • You'd need to rent a truck as well as the dolly.
  • There are stairs, a narrow hallway or a long gravel carry.
  • It's three or more large pieces, or furniture mixed with other junk like boxes, a mattress or an old appliance.
  • You can't count on a second able-bodied adult.
  • You're on a deadline, such as a rental move-out, a home sale or an estate.

Who Should NOT Hire Us for This

Here's a straight list of when we're the wrong choice:

  • You only need furniture moved inside the house. We can do it, but a neighbor and a $10 dolly will cost much less.
  • You're relocating your belongings. Hire a licensed mover. We don't deliver to a new address.
  • Your only goal is the lowest possible price, and you have a truck, a helper and time. DIY will almost always cost less in dollars.
  • The item is antique or valuable and you want to sell it. Sell it, consign it or list it online first. Once we haul something, it goes to donation, recycling or the landfill, and you don't get paid for it.
  • The job is mostly heavy debris, not furniture. Concrete, dirt and demolition waste need different equipment. See our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) and [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) pages.

If you fit one of these, rent the dolly. We'd rather say so now than have you wish you'd saved the money.

A Quick Decision Checklist

Use this before you reserve a dolly or call anyone:

1. Is the furniture staying or going? If it's staying, rent a dolly or hire a mover. If it's going, keep reading. 2. Do you own a truck or trailer? If not, add $50–$100+ in rental and mileage to your DIY estimate. 3. How many heavy pieces are there? For 1–2, DIY is reasonable. For 3 or more, compare quotes. 4. Are there stairs or a gravel carry? If so, budget extra time and a second helper, or hire out. 5. Do you know where it's going? Call the landfill or a charity before you load anything. Showing up after the gate closes is a common mistake that wastes an afternoon.
