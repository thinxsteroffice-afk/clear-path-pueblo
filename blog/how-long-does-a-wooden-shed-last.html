HOW LONG DOES A WOODEN SHED LAST?

A wooden shed in Pueblo West typically lasts 15 to 25 years with no maintenance, and 30 to 40 years if you stain it every three or four years and keep its floor frame off wet dirt. That's on the long end of the national 15-to-20-year figure, for one reason: we get about 12 inches of precipitation a year, so sheds here rarely rot out the way they do in Missouri or Oregon. What kills them instead is sun, hail, wind, and soil movement. The shell outlives the roof by two decades. The paint fails three times over. And a shed sitting directly on Pueblo West's clay can rack itself out of square long before a single board decays.

What Actually Kills Wooden Sheds Here (And It Isn't Rot)

At roughly 4,900 feet, UV intensity runs about 4% higher per 1,000 feet of elevation — call it 18-20% more ultraviolet load than a sea-level shed gets, across 250-plus sunny days a year. Relative humidity sits in the 25-40% range most of the year. That combination does something specific: it bakes the lignin out of exposed wood surfaces, drives seasonal moisture content down into the 6-8% range, and shrinks boards until fasteners back out and T1-11 siding checks and cups.

The four real failure modes, in the order we see them:

  • UV and thermal cycling. South and west walls fail first. A stain job that lasts 5-7 years in the Midwest lasts 3-4 years here. Skip two cycles and you're not repainting anymore, you're replacing siding.
  • Hail. Pueblo County sits in a corridor that averages several damaging hail days per season. A 25-to-30-year architectural shingle on a shed roof realistically gives you 12 to 18 years here, and a 3-tab closer to 10.
  • Wind. Spring gusts of 50-60 mph are routine on the mesa. Sheds that were never anchored walk, rack, and eventually tear the floor frame away from the walls.
  • Expansive clay and freeze-thaw. Southern Colorado runs well over 100 freeze-thaw cycles a year. Swelling clay lifts one corner, drops another, and the door stops latching. That's the single most common reason a Pueblo West shed gets condemned by its owner while the wood is still sound.

Lifespan by Part, Not by Shed

"How long does a shed last" is really four questions, because the parts age at wildly different rates.

  • Floor frame / skids. Pressure-treated, ground-contact-rated (UC4A) lumber on a gravel pad: 20-40 years. Untreated pine or fir sitting on bare dirt or caliche: 4-8 years. This is the part that decides everything.
  • Wall framing. 2x4 studs inside a dry, shaded cavity in this climate: 40+ years. We routinely demo 1990s sheds with framing you could reuse.
  • Siding. T1-11 plywood, maintained: 20-25 years. Unmaintained, west-facing: 8-12 years before delamination. OSB siding: cut that roughly in half.
  • Roof. Shingles 12-18 years. The decking under them, if the shingles failed and nobody noticed, 2-4 years after that.
  • Doors and hardware. Hinges and hasps are the first thing to go — often by year 6-8 — and they're a $40 fix people put off for a decade.

> In Pueblo West, the wood usually isn't what's worn out. The foundation moved, the roof gave up, and the whole structure got written off because of two problems that cost less than $2,000 to fix.

The Five-Minute Inspection

Walk out there with a screwdriver and a level.

  • Probe the bottom plate and skids at all four corners. If the screwdriver sinks more than 1/4 inch with light pressure, that's active decay. If more than a third of the bottom plate is soft, you're into a rebuild.
  • Check square. Measure both diagonals inside. More than 1 inch of difference on a 10x12 means it's racked.
  • Look up at the decking from inside on a bright day. Any daylight, dark staining, or sag between rafters means water has been getting in for at least two seasons.
  • Push on the walls. Movement at the top plate with nothing holding it means the sheathing nails have pulled.
  • Check the perimeter. Dirt or mulch piled against the siding is the #1 avoidable killer. You want 6 inches of clearance from grade to wood, minimum.

Repair or Replace: Run the Numbers

A new wood shed in the Pueblo market runs $4,000-$9,000 for a 10x12 built on site, or $2,800-$5,000 for a kit you assemble yourself. A comparable metal or resin unit is $1,200-$3,000 and will outlast wood on UV but dent badly in hail.

Against that, typical repairs:

  • Re-roof a 10x12: $700-$1,400 with new decking, or $250-$400 in materials if you do it yourself over a weekend.
  • Jack it up, replace skids, set it on a 4-inch compacted gravel pad: $900-$2,200.
  • Re-side one wall: $300-$650.
  • Clean and stain the whole thing: $120-$280 in materials and 14-20 hours of your time.

The working rule: if repairs exceed 50% of replacement cost, replace. A 22-year-old shed needing a roof, two walls, and a foundation reset is at $3,000-$4,000 of work on a structure worth maybe $1,500. Let it go. A 14-year-old shed needing a roof and stain is a $1,100 project that buys you another 15 years. Keep it.

When Hiring Us to Haul It Is the Wrong Call

This is the part most companies leave out, so here it is plainly.

Don't pay us if the shed just needs maintenance. We get calls where someone has decided a shed is "shot" because the door sags and the paint is gray. We show up, probe the frame, and it's solid. We'll tell you that, and you'll have spent a service call to learn that $200 of stain and a $35 hinge kit fixes it. Do the five-minute inspection above first.

Don't pay us if you can DIY it and you actually will. A small 8x10 wood shed is genuinely a one-weekend demolition for two reasonably fit people with a sawzall, a pry bar, and a trailer. You're looking at 1,200-2,200 pounds of debris and 3-5 cubic yards of volume. Pueblo-area tip fees run roughly $40-$60 per ton with per-load minimums (call the scale house — rates change), so the dump run is $25-$60. You'd save $400-$700. The honest catch: this is 10-14 hours of hot, splintery, nail-filled work, and a lot of people get the roof off, run out of ambition, and leave a half-dead shed in the yard for eight months. If that's you, hire it out — but know that convenience, not necessity, is what you're buying.

Don't demo it if it still has resale value. Sheds under about 10 years old with a sound floor sell on local marketplaces for $250-$900, and buyers with trailers will sometimes disassemble and haul it themselves for free. Give it two weeks of listing before you pay anyone.

Don't call us yet if you're mid-insurance claim. After a hail event, removing the damaged structure before the adjuster documents it can cost you the payout. Wait for the inspection and written scope.

Don't call us if a contractor is already on site with a dumpster. If you're re-roofing the house or doing a remodel, that crew can usually absorb a shed for a few hundred dollars in added dumpster capacity. Cheaper than a separate mobilization.

What We Can't Do, and Where Shed Jobs Go Sideways

  • Asbestos. Some pre-1985 sheds have cement-asbestos roof panels or transite siding. We don't abate asbestos, and nobody should break it up in a backyard. That requires a licensed abatement contractor and adds $1,500-$4,000 to the job. Pre-1980 structure with gray corrugated panels? Get it tested first.
  • Live electrical or plumbing. If power was run to the shed, it needs a licensed electrician to disconnect at the panel before we touch it. We will not cut a live feed.
  • Hazardous contents. Old paint, pesticides, herbicides, pool chemicals, propane cylinders, and automotive fluids can't go in our truck. Those go to Pueblo County household hazardous waste events. We'll remove everything else, but the chemical shelf is yours.
  • Concrete floors. A shed on a slab is two jobs. Breaking out and hauling a 10x12 slab is separate work — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) — and often adds $600-$1,500. Many people keep the slab for the next shed. That's usually smart.
  • Access. Pueblo West lots are generous, but if the shed sits behind a 36-inch gate or inside a fenced run with no equipment path, everything comes out by hand and the labor roughly doubles. Where we can get machinery in, [skid steer work](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) cuts a full day down to a few hours.
  • Rodent contamination. Heavy mouse or packrat infestation means droppings, nesting, and a real hantavirus consideration in this region. That changes how the job gets done — wetted down, respirators on — and it costs more. Say so up front when you call.

For reference, full shed teardown and haul-off in this market generally lands between $350 and $1,900 depending on size, flooring, contents, and access. An empty 8x10 on skids is the low end. A 12x20 with a slab, a loft full of junk, and a narrow gate is the high end. Permits and the Pueblo West Wrinkle

Accessory structures under 200 square feet generally fall below the building-permit threshold in unincorporated Pueblo County, and demolition of a small shed usually doesn't require a separate permit — but verify for your parcel with Pueblo County Planning & Development before anything comes down, especially if the shed is larger, has electrical, or sits near a property line.

Separately, the Pueblo West Metropolitan District has its own covenants governing accessory structures, setbacks, and materials. People get surprised here: your old shed may be grandfathered in a spot where a new one isn't allowed. Check the setback rules *before* you demolish the existing structure, not after.

How to Get 35 Years Out of the Next One

  • Set it on 4-6 inches of compacted road base, crowned and extending 12 inches past the footprint, with pressure-treated ground-contact skids. Never on bare clay.
  • Keep 6+ inches between soil and any wood, and don't let sprinklers hit the siding.
  • Anchor it. Ground augers or concrete piers. 60-mph gusts are not hypothetical here.
  • Stain or paint on a 3-year cycle with a quality solid or semi-solid product rated for high UV. Set a calendar reminder; this is the whole ballgame.
  • Spend the extra $300-$600 on a metal roof instead of shingles. In a hail belt, a 26-gauge panel roof will outlive two or three shingle roofs and only dent.
  • Add gable or soffit vents. Trapped summer heat above 140°F cooks shingles from underneath and shortens their life by years.

Do those six things and the shed outlasts your mortgage. Skip the stain and the gravel pad, and you'll be pricing removal in year 14.
