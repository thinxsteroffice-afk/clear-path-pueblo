GARAGE ALTERNATIVES IN PUEBLO WEST

The best alternative to a garage in Pueblo West depends on what you need the garage for. To cover a vehicle, a wind- and snow-rated metal carport costs about $3,000–$8,000 installed, compared with $45,000–$75,000 for a detached two-car garage. To store things, a 10x12 shed runs about $3,500–$8,000, and a storage unit runs about $90–$180 a month. If you already have a garage that's too full to park in, the cheapest option is usually to clear it out. A one-day cleanout typically costs $250–$650 and gives you back 400+ square feet you already own. The rest of this guide covers which option fits which situation, what Pueblo West's wind and hail do to each one, and when clearing out the garage won't solve the problem.

Why Pueblo West homeowners are asking this question

Pueblo West has big lots. Plenty of properties sit on half an acre to five acres, so there's room for a second structure. Spring winds, summer hail and the cost of building anything new all limit what's practical, though. Pueblo West homeowners searching "alternative to a garage" usually have one of these problems:

  • There's no garage at all, often on an older ranch home or manufactured home.
  • The garage exists but holds no vehicles. It's full of boxes, old furniture and tools from three hobbies ago.
  • The vehicle doesn't fit. A 2024 crew-cab pickup is about 19–20 feet long, and many older garages are only 20–22 feet deep.
  • They need room for a trailer, side-by-side, boat or RV that no standard garage door will take.

Each problem has a different answer.

The real options, with real Pueblo West numbers

The ranges below are typical installed prices in Southern Colorado as of 2026. Actual quotes depend on size, site prep and materials.

1. Detached garage (the baseline) A 24x24 detached garage built on a slab usually lands at $75–$130 per square foot once you include the foundation, framing, a door, and electrical. That works out to about $45,000–$75,000. You'll need a permit through the Pueblo Regional Building Department, and the build usually takes 2–4 months from permit to finished. It's the best protection and adds the most resale value. It also costs the most by a wide margin.

2. Metal carport A 20x20 steel carport costs about $3,000–$8,000 installed, depending on gauge, height, and whether you enclose the sides. Most companies install in a single day. In Pueblo West, buy the certified version, rated for at least 105 mph winds and a meaningful snow load. It costs roughly 20–30% more, and it's worth it. The uncertified units sold on price get lifted and folded by spring gusts that regularly top 50–60 mph out here.

3. Storage shed A 10x12 shed (120 square feet) runs about $3,500–$8,000 for a quality prefab or site-built unit. Detached one-story accessory buildings of 200 square feet or less are often exempt from a building permit under the residential code. Confirm that with the Pueblo Regional Building Department and check your subdivision's covenants before you buy. A shed handles lawn equipment and bins well. It won't hold a car.

4. Storage unit Units in the Pueblo area typically run $90–$130/month for a 10x10 and $150–$220/month for a 10x20. These numbers look small until you add them up. Three years in a $120/month unit costs $4,320, which is more than many carports and about the same as a good shed. And you still have to drive across town every time you want your Christmas decorations.

5. Portable fabric garage or car cover These cost $300–$1,500. Out here, treat them as temporary. Fabric shelters take a beating from UV at 4,700 feet of elevation and from the wind, and in our experience many don't last past 2–3 seasons.

6. Clearing out the garage you already have A typical two-car garage cleanout takes 2–4 hours and usually costs $250–$650, depending on how much of a truck load it fills. Afterward you have 400–576 square feet of enclosed, secure, insulated space with a door. Getting that same square footage by building new costs $45,000+.

Compare by cost per square foot, not total price

Compare the options by what you pay per usable square foot over five years:

| Option | Approx. 5-year cost | Usable sq ft | Cost per sq ft | |---|---|---|---| | New 24x24 garage | $60,000 | 576 | ~$104 | | Certified 20x20 carport | $6,000 | 400 (open) | ~$15 | | 10x12 shed | $5,500 | 120 | ~$46 | | 10x10 storage unit ($110/mo) | $6,600 | 100 | ~$66 | | Garage cleanout (one-time) | $450 | 400–576 | ~$1 |

> If your garage holds more stuff than cars, you're probably paying to store things you'd never pay to replace.

A storage unit costs more per square foot over five years than a shed, and it isn't even on your property. The cleanout is only that cheap because you already own the building. That's why we always ask first whether a garage is already sitting under all that stuff.

When clearing out the garage is NOT the right answer

We do [garage cleanouts in Pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo), and we'd still rather tell you upfront when a cleanout won't fix your problem. Here are the cases where hiring us is the wrong call:

  • Your vehicle physically doesn't fit. If your garage is 20 feet deep and your truck is 19.5 feet long, clearing every box won't help. You'll have about 6 inches of clearance and a dented bumper. You need a carport, an extension, or a different vehicle.
  • You need RV, boat, or trailer storage. Most residential garage doors are 7 feet tall, and most travel trailers need 12–14 feet of door height. A cleanout won't change that. Look at a tall carport or an RV storage lot.
  • Most of what's in there is worth money. If the garage is full of tools, a working table saw, and furniture in good condition, sell it first. A weekend garage sale or Facebook Marketplace listing can bring in more than our fee. Call us afterward for whatever's left, or don't call us at all.
  • You have a pickup, a free Saturday, and a strong back. Hauling it yourself to the landfill is cheaper than hiring anyone, often well under $50 a load (check current county rates). What you pay us for is labor, time and the truck. If you have all three, you don't need us.
  • You'll fill it back up within six months. This is the most common way a cleanout fails. If the habit that filled the garage hasn't changed, you're paying to reset the clock. Before you book, decide where incoming stuff will go: a shelving wall, a bin rule, or a one-in-one-out habit.
  • The garage itself is failing. A cracked or heaving slab, a rotted sill plate, or a roof that leaks every monsoon season are structural problems. An empty garage with a bad roof is still a bad garage. Get it inspected before you spend money organizing it.
  • You need a workshop, not parking. If what you want is a dedicated shop with 240V power and dust control, adding a shed or shop building may serve you better than sharing space with a car.

Some of our least happy customers called us for a cleanout when their real problem was one of the above. We'd rather lose the job than leave you with an empty garage that still doesn't do what you need.

Pueblo West factors that change the math

Some local conditions affect every one of these options:

  • Wind. Pueblo West's spring winds destroy cheap structures. Anchor carports with concrete or helical anchors, not just rebar stakes in sandy soil.
  • Hail. Southern Colorado's hail season runs roughly May through August. An open carport protects a car from hail but not from blowing debris. A fully enclosed garage protects against both. Factor in a hail claim on your deductible, often $1,000–$2,500, when you're deciding how much protection you want.
  • Site prep. A lot of Pueblo West lots need leveling, a gravel pad, or rock removed before a carport or shed can go in. A gravel pad for a 20x20 carport might add $800–$2,000. We handle [skid steer services in Pueblo](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) and [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) when the ground needs work first.
  • Covenants. Some Pueblo West subdivisions limit metal outbuildings, colors, or setbacks. Read yours before you order anything.

A practical decision path

1. Do you have a garage? If you do, measure its depth and your vehicle's length. If the vehicle fits with 2+ feet to spare, clear out the garage first. 2. Is the overflow worth money? Sell it before you pay anyone to haul it. 3. Still need more covered space? A certified carport costs the least per square foot for vehicles. A shed is the better choice for gear. 4. Paying for a storage unit? Open it and look at what's inside. If you wouldn't buy those items again today, stop paying rent on them.
