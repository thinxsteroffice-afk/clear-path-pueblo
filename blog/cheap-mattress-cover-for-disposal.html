CHEAP MATTRESS COVER FOR DISPOSAL

The cheapest mattress cover that works for disposal is a plastic mattress bag, usually $5–$15 at a hardware store, moving-supply shop or truck-rental counter in Pueblo. Pick one that's at least 3 mil thick and made for your mattress size. A 1.5-mil bag will tear on the first corner. Don't buy a zippered encasement for this. Those cost $20–$60 and are made to protect a bed you're keeping. Also, if you're hiring a hauler, you probably don't need a cover at all. Most junk removal crews, including ours, load mattresses bare unless there are bed bugs. Below you'll find which cover to buy, a few cheaper options, and when to skip the cover and the DIY trip.

Do you actually need a cover to throw out a mattress?

In Pueblo West, a cover solves three problems:

  • Bed bugs. A bag keeps them from spreading through your hallway, your vehicle and the next place the mattress goes. This is the one case where a cover really matters.
  • Dust and stains in your vehicle. An old mattress holds years of skin flakes and dust. If it's going in an SUV with the seats folded down, a bag keeps that off your upholstery.
  • Moisture. A mattress that sits outside during an afternoon thunderstorm can soak up water and get much heavier. Recyclers often turn away wet or dirty mattresses, so a bag keeps the recycling option open.

If none of those apply, you're hauling it in an open truck bed, and it's going straight to the landfill, a cover isn't doing much for you.

The cheapest covers that work, ranked by cost

Here's what's available, from cheapest to most expensive, with honest notes on each.

1. A plastic drop cloth and packing tape (about $8–$12 total). A 9×12-foot plastic sheet runs about $3–$6. A roll of packing tape is about $4. Wrap the mattress like a gift and tape the seams. It's cheap and fits any size, but thin drop cloths (often 0.7–1 mil) tear easily. Use this only if the mattress doesn't have bed bugs.

2. A disposal or moving bag (about $5–$15). This is the best choice for most people. Twin and full bags are at the low end, and queen and king bags cost a few dollars more. Look for:

  • Thickness of 3–4 mil, printed on the package
  • A gusset (the side panel that sets the bag's depth) of at least 12–15 inches. Standard mattresses are 9–12 inches thick, but pillow-tops can reach 14–18 inches.
  • The right size. A queen mattress is 60×80 inches and a king is 76×80. A queen bag won't stretch over a king.

3. Stretch wrap (about $15–$25 for an 18-inch roll). This is the plastic film used on pallets. It's great for memory foam mattresses: roll the foam up, wrap it tight, and it stays compact enough for a sedan trunk. The downside is that one mattress uses a lot of the roll, so it only saves money if you'll use the rest.

4. Heavy contractor bags cut open and taped (about $2–$4 in bags). Cut two or three 42-gallon, 3-mil contractor bags along the seams and tape them together. They're thicker than most drop cloths and you may already have them in the garage. It takes about 10–15 minutes to do neatly.

5. A bed bug disposal bag (about $10–$20). Some bags are sold specifically for infested mattresses. They're thicker, often 4–6 mil, and some have a printed warning on them. If you have bed bugs, spend the extra money.

> If you're paying for a zipper, you're buying a cover for a mattress you plan to keep. For a mattress you're throwing out, a $10 bag is enough.

A Pueblo West problem: wind and open truck beds

Most disposal guides skip this, but it matters here. Pueblo West is open and exposed, and spring wind gusts of 40–50 mph are common between March and May. A bagged mattress in a pickup bed behaves like a sail. The slick plastic also removes the friction that would help a bare mattress stay put.

Before you drive to Highway 50 or I-25:

  • Use at least two ratchet straps (about $10–$20 for a pair) crossing over the top. Don't rely on bungee cords, which stretch and let the mattress lift.
  • Lay the mattress flat, not leaning on the tailgate. A mattress standing on edge catches far more wind.
  • Tape down loose bag ends. Flapping plastic can shred at 55 mph and up, and then you're dropping plastic along the road.

Colorado's spilling-loads statute (C.R.S. 42-4-1407) makes it a traffic offense to drive with a load that isn't secured to keep it from falling or blowing off. A mattress in the road is dangerous for the drivers behind you. Strap it down whether it's bagged or not.

What DIY disposal actually costs in Pueblo West

The cover is usually the smallest cost. Here's a realistic DIY budget for one queen mattress. Fees change, so confirm current rates with the facility before you go:

  • Cover: $5–$15
  • Straps, if you don't own them: $10–$20
  • Truck rental, if you don't own one: hardware-store and moving-company pickups are often advertised around $19.95–$29.95 for the base rental, plus mileage and fuel. From Pueblo West to a disposal site and back, many households end up at $40–$70 all in.
  • Landfill or transfer-station fee: usually charged by weight or with a minimum per load. Call the facility first, because some charge extra for mattresses.
  • Time: usually 2–3 hours including pickup, loading, the drive and unloading

A queen innerspring mattress weighs about 50–90 pounds, and a queen memory foam mattress can weigh more. It's floppy and hard to grip, and a plastic bag makes it slipperier. Plan on two people.

Pueblo West doesn't have city trash service. Households contract with private haulers, and most don't take mattresses in weekly pickup. Some offer bulk pickup for an extra fee, often in the $25–$50 range per item. Call your hauler before you buy anything, because this can be the cheapest option.

When our service isn't worth it

We're a junk removal company, and a mattress pickup is something we sell. Here's when you should not hire us or anyone else.

You have one mattress, a truck and a free afternoon. A $10 bag, straps you already own and a disposal fee will almost always cost less than a hauler's trip charge. A one-item pickup has to cover a truck, fuel and a two-person crew, so it can't compete with doing it yourself.

You're buying a new mattress. Many retailers will haul the old one away when they deliver, sometimes free and often for $0–$50. That usually beats any other option. Check the terms before delivery day. Some retailers require the old mattress to be bagged or free of stains and pests, and they'll refuse it at the door otherwise.

Your trash hauler offers bulk pickup. If your hauler will take it for a fee, that's often cheaper than a special trip.

The mattress is in good shape. A clean, stain-free mattress with no pests may be worth listing free online, where someone may pick it up within 24–48 hours. Many thrift stores won't take used mattresses because of health rules, so call before you drive it there.

You have bed bugs and are counting on a cheap bag alone. A bag keeps bugs from spreading while you move the mattress, but it doesn't treat your home. If the mattress has bugs, the bed frame, box spring and nearby furniture probably do too. Treat the room first, bag the mattress on the day it leaves, and label the bag so nobody picks it up off the curb. This is also where cheap covers fail: a 1.5-mil bag dragged down a stairwell can tear and spread bugs the whole way. Double-bag it or use 4 mil or thicker.

The cover causes its own problems. Plastic makes a mattress slippery on stairs and in truck beds, it traps moisture if the mattress was already damp, and it's one more piece of plastic in the landfill. If none of the reasons for a cover apply to you, skip it.

  • You don't own a truck and truck rental plus the disposal fee comes close to the price of a pickup.

Quick checklist before you buy a cover

  • Measure the thickness of your mattress. Pillow-tops often need a bag with a 15-inch gusset or deeper.
  • Check the mil rating. Buy 3 mil for a normal mattress and 4 mil or more for bed bugs.
  • Match the size. Queen and king bags aren't interchangeable.
  • Call your trash hauler or the retailer first. Either one might take the mattress for less than a DIY trip.
  • Check the wind forecast. Pueblo West gusts can turn a loose bag into litter within a mile.
  • Get a second person. A bagged 70-pound mattress is harder to carry than a bare one.
