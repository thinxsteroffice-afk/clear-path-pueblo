DO JUNK REMOVAL COMPANIES RECYCLE?

Yes, but the honest answer is "some of it, and less than the marketing implies." A typical mixed household load out of Pueblo West gets roughly 25% to 40% diverted from the landfill by weight, mostly metal, appliances, cardboard, and a handful of donatable items. Metal-heavy loads run 85% to 95% diverted. A load of broken particleboard furniture, carpet, and worn mattresses may be under 10%. Colorado recycles about 16% of its total waste against a national rate near 32%, and southern Colorado sits below the state average because landfill space here is cheap. Any local company promising "100% recycled" is either counting donations as recycling or not counting at all. Ask for scale tickets before you believe a number.

What actually gets recycled out of a Pueblo West load

Recycling in the junk removal context is not curbside recycling. It is sorting on the trailer and dropping material at four or five different destinations before the landfill gets whatever is left. The categories that reliably move:

  • Ferrous and non-ferrous metal. Water heaters, swing sets, bed frames, fencing, appliances, cast iron tubs. This is the single biggest diversion category by weight in almost every load.
  • Appliances with refrigerant. Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers require certified refrigerant recovery under EPA Section 608 before the shell can be scrapped. Expect a $25 to $50 handling fee per unit anywhere in Pueblo County.
  • Electronics. Colorado banned electronics from landfill disposal on July 1, 2013. TVs, monitors, printers, and computers legally cannot go in the hole, so they get pulled whether or not the company advertises recycling.
  • Clean concrete, block, and rock. Crushable material stays out of the landfill entirely when the load is clean. Contaminate it with rebar-free assumptions, drywall, or dirt clods and it becomes trash.
  • Cardboard, clean untreated lumber, and usable household goods. Small volumes, real diversion, low weight.

The steel side has a genuinely local advantage that national junk removal blogs never mention. The electric arc furnace steel mill on Pueblo's south side runs on recycled scrap, and it sits about 12 to 15 miles from most of Pueblo West. Scrap metal pulled off a driveway on Purcell or McCulloch travels a very short distance to become rail and rod. In most of the country, "we recycle the metal" means a 60 to 200 mile haul chain. Here it means one short trip down I-25. That is why metal diversion in Pueblo is real and why almost nothing else in a mixed load matches it.

Why the recycling rate is worse here than in Denver

Landfill tipping fees in southern Colorado run somewhere in the $25 to $45 per ton range, against a national average closer to $60 and well north of that in states with landfill scarcity. Cheap disposal is the entire explanation for Colorado's low diversion numbers. When burying a ton costs $35 and driving that same ton to a specialty processor costs $90 in fuel, labor, and gate fees, the math punishes recycling.

Distance compounds it. Pueblo West to the disposal site is roughly a 12 to 18 mile run, about 20 to 25 minutes. The nearest dedicated mattress recycler is in the Denver metro, about 120 miles north. Hauling eight mattresses to Denver burns a full workday, roughly 240 miles of round trip diesel, and costs several hundred dollars more than the tipping fee it avoids. No local company absorbs that on a standard job, and any that claims to is either aggregating loads over months or telling you a story.

> The question is not whether a company recycles. It is which materials, to which facility, at whose expense.

What "we recycle" means when a company says it

There are four different things behind that phrase, and they are not equal:

  • Scale-ticket diversion. The company weighs loads, drops metal at a scrap yard, e-waste at a certified processor, and can produce receipts. This is the real version.
  • Donation counted as recycling. Usable furniture to a thrift store or the local Habitat ReStore is genuinely good, but it is reuse, not recycling, and it is a small share of weight.
  • Cherry-picking. Pulling the copper and the aluminum because it pays, then landfilling everything else. Common, not dishonest, but it is not a recycling program.
  • Pure marketing. A recycling icon on the truck and a single destination on the route sheet.

If diversion matters to you, ask three questions before booking: which specific facilities do you use, will you send me the weight tickets, and what percentage of my load type typically gets diverted. A company that answers with a number and a facility name is telling the truth. A company that answers "we recycle everything we can" is telling you nothing. When hiring us is the wrong call

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out. Several situations exist where paying a hauler is a bad decision, and some of them cost us work.

  • You have one mattress and nothing else. Paying a minimum charge in the $95 to $150 range to move a single mattress 15 miles is poor value. Check whether your regular trash service offers a bulky item pickup first. If you have a truck, the trip is under an hour. Our [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page exists for volume jobs, apartment turns, and people without a vehicle, not for a single box spring you could drop yourself.
  • You have leftover paint, stain, or solvent. Colorado's PaintCare program takes leftover architectural paint free at participating retail drop-off sites, generally up to 5 gallons per visit. We would have to charge you for handling it. Take it yourself and keep the money.
  • You are doing a multi-week remodel. For a project generating debris over 10 or 15 days, a roll-off dumpster at a flat rate usually beats three or four separate hauls. We are priced for one-and-done removal, not for standing by. The exception is when your driveway or HOA setback makes a 20-yard container impractical, which happens on plenty of Pueblo West lots with long gravel approaches.
  • Everything you own is genuinely donatable and in good condition. Call the thrift stores first. Some will pick up furniture at no charge. You will get a receipt for a deduction, and we will get nothing, which is the correct outcome.
  • You want a guaranteed zero-landfill outcome. We cannot promise that and neither can anyone else within 100 miles. If a company promises it in writing, ask them to attach the diversion percentage to the contract and watch what happens.

The material that simply does not recycle here

Some things go in the hole regardless of who hauls them, and pretending otherwise wastes your money.

Hot tubs are the clearest example. A dry spa shell runs 700 to 900 pounds. The frame, pump, heater, and copper wiring are recyclable, but the acrylic shell and closed-cell foam insulation are not, and they are most of the mass. Realistic diversion on a [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) is roughly 12% to 18% by weight. Anyone quoting higher is guessing.

Also functionally unrecyclable in this market: laminate and particleboard furniture, carpet and pad, most upholstered goods, pressure-treated and painted lumber, drywall, insulation, and yard debris that has been mixed with trash. Treated deck boards from a [deck or fence teardown](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo) cannot be chipped or burned and cannot go into clean wood streams. That entire load is landfill-bound, and the price reflects tipping, not processing.

Clean loads are the exception worth planning around. Separated concrete and rock diverts nearly completely, so keeping a [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) job free of dirt, trash, and wood changes both the destination and the cost. The same logic applies to a metal-only pull.

How to get the highest diversion on your job

  • Separate before we arrive. Metal in one pile, clean concrete in another, donatables inside. Ten minutes of sorting on your end moves more material than any promise on our website.
  • Book appliance work as its own job. An [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) run diverts far more than the same appliances buried inside a general cleanout, because the whole load routes to scrap.
  • Do not bag small items. Bagged material gets landfilled by default because nobody sorts through sealed trash bags on the tailgate.
  • Expect sorting to add time. Genuine on-site separation adds 20 to 45 minutes per load, and honest companies build that into the quote rather than hiding it.
  • Ask about the ticket. On jobs over about two tons, we can usually document where material went. Below that, weights get combined across stops and the number becomes an estimate.

Colorado's statewide producer responsibility program is expanding residential recycling access through 2026, which will help packaging streams. It does not touch bulky waste, construction debris, or furniture, which is what junk removal actually moves. Do not expect the diversion math on a garage cleanout to change much in the next few years.
