HOW MUCH FURNITURE REMOVAL COSTS

Furniture removal in Pueblo West typically runs $85 to $150 for a single item, $175 to $325 for two or three pieces, and $350 to $600 for a full truckload of roughly 15–16 cubic yards. A standard three-seat sofa lands around $110–$140. A sectional, because it eats four to five cubic yards, usually falls between $180 and $280. Mattress and box spring sets run $95–$160 as a pair. Those numbers assume ground-floor access, no more than 15 miles from central Pueblo West, and no hazardous contamination. Stairs, long gravel driveways, and disassembly push the total up. Below is the actual math behind those figures, plus a plain list of situations where hiring anyone — us included — is the wrong call.

The Pricing Model Almost Every Hauler Uses (and Why It Matters)

Furniture removal is priced by volume, not weight or piece count. The truck is the unit of measure. A typical junk removal truck bed holds 15–16 cubic yards, which is about four full-size pickup loads. Haulers slice that into fractions and attach a price to each fraction.

Here is the conversion most companies won't spell out for you:

  • Three-seat sofa (84" × 36" × 34") — about 2.2 cubic yards
  • Sectional, 5–6 pieces — 4 to 5.5 cubic yards
  • Queen mattress + box spring — about 2.4 cubic yards combined
  • Recliner — 1.3 to 1.7 cubic yards
  • 6-drawer dresser — roughly 1 cubic yard
  • Dining set, table plus 6 chairs — 3 to 4 cubic yards

Run the division and the incentive becomes obvious. A $95 single-item pickup of a 2-yard sofa costs you about $47 per cubic yard. A $599 full 16-yard load costs about $37 per cubic yard. The minimum charge — usually $85 to $125 in this market — exists because the truck, fuel, two people, and the landfill trip cost the same whether it's hauling one recliner or twelve.

Practical takeaway: if you have a sofa going out this month and a dresser going out next month, one trip beats two by $80 to $120. Walk the garage, the shed, and the back bedroom before you book.

> The single most expensive thing you can do with furniture removal is book it twice.

What Actually Changes the Price in Pueblo West

Pueblo West is not Pueblo proper, and the differences show up on the invoice.

Distance and the landfill run. Most Pueblo West addresses sit 12–18 miles from the disposal facilities on the south side of Pueblo. Tipping fees for bulky household waste generally land in the $45–$70 per ton range, and a loaded furniture run frequently weighs 900 to 1,600 lbs. That's $25–$55 of pure disposal cost buried inside your quote, plus 35–45 minutes of round-trip drive time. Addresses out toward the far west end of Pueblo West, or up past the mesa, often carry a $25–$50 travel add-on.

Lot layout. The one-acre-plus lots common here mean long gravel or dirt driveways. If the truck can't get within 40 feet of the item, crews carry — and carrying adds 15–30 minutes, which is typically a $25–$40 labor bump. After a wet spring, some drives won't hold a loaded 12,000-lb truck at all, and the crew will park at the road.

Stairs and tight turns. A second-floor removal usually adds $25–$50 per large item. Split-level entries with a 90-degree landing are the worst offender; a sectional that needs to be unbolted, carried in three trips, and reassembled outside can add 40 minutes.

Disassembly. Bed frames, sleeper sofas, and modular sectionals often need to come apart. Budget $20–$45 for that on top.

Contamination. Furniture that's been rained on, mouse-infested, or stored in a leaking shed weighs 30–50% more and can't be donated. Expect the higher end of every range. Water-damaged upholstery from a swamp cooler leak is common here and is the number one reason a phone estimate goes up on arrival.

Real Quote Ranges for Common Pueblo West Jobs

  • One recliner, garage, ground floor — $85–$110
  • Sofa and loveseat, front room, no stairs — $165–$230
  • Full bedroom set: mattress, box spring, frame, dresser, nightstand — $245–$340
  • Sectional plus coffee table plus two chairs — $290–$390
  • Whole-house furniture clear-out, 3-bed rancher — $550–$900, typically 1.5 truckloads
  • Basement family room, exterior stair access — $320–$475

Those are honest working numbers, not a bait price. If a company quotes you $59 for a couch over the phone, ask what the on-site number becomes after stairs, mileage, and mattress surcharges. That gap is where the complaints come from.

When Hiring a Furniture Removal Company Is a Bad Deal

This is the part most pricing pages skip. Several situations exist where paying $85–$300 is a genuine waste of money, and you should know them before you call.

Don't hire us if you own or can borrow a pickup and have one item. A single dresser or dining chair going to the transfer station is a $25–$40 tip fee and 90 minutes of your Saturday. If your time is worth less than $50/hour and you have a helper, you're ahead doing it yourself. A U-Haul pickup at $19.95 plus $0.99/mile runs about $45–$55 for a Pueblo West round trip. That beats any hauler's minimum.

Don't hire us if the furniture is genuinely good. Clean, structurally sound, non-stained upholstery and solid-wood pieces have real value. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar charities will often collect qualifying furniture at no charge, and a documented donation of a decent sofa can carry a $100–$300 fair-market deduction if you itemize. A "curb alert" post in a Pueblo West community group frequently moves a usable couch in under 4 hours. Free beats $110. The tradeoff is scheduling: charity pickups commonly book 2–4 weeks out and reserve the right to refuse on sight.

Don't hire us for a slow-motion, multi-week project. If you're clearing a house over six weekends, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster at roughly $400–$575 for a 7–10 day rental gives you 20 yards of capacity and unlimited loading time. Three separate hauler visits at $300 each is $900 for the same volume. Dumpsters lose on convenience and driveway damage risk, and win on cost per yard for extended projects.

Don't hire us if a dumpster is already on site. If a contractor's roll-off is sitting in your driveway with room in it, ask permission and use it. Paying twice for disposal is just paying twice.

Don't hire us if the volume is under the minimum and you're not in a hurry. Pueblo West Metropolitan District has historically run community cleanup events with free or low-cost bulky-item disposal for district residents. Dates and rules change year to year, so verify with the district before planning around one — but if your sofa can wait for a scheduled cleanup day, that's the cheapest option on this page.

Failure Modes and Hard Limits — What We Cannot Do

Being direct about this saves everyone a wasted trip fee.

  • Confirmed bed bug or active infestation. Most haulers, ours included, will decline or require the item bagged and sealed before it comes out the door. Cross-contaminating a truck affects every customer after you.
  • Phone estimates are estimates. A quote given from a text photo can move 20–40% once the crew sees stairs, weight, or water damage. Anyone who promises a locked price sight-unseen is either padding it or planning to renegotiate in your driveway.
  • No donation guarantee. We route what we can to reuse, but a hauler cannot promise your grandmother's armoire finds a home. Charities reject soiled, torn, particleboard, and pre-1980 upholstered pieces routinely.
  • Built-ins and attached furniture need carpentry, not hauling. Removing a wall-anchored bookcase or a bolted bunk system leaves drywall and anchor damage we don't repair.
  • Weight caps are real. A commercial safe, a slate pool table, or a cast-iron piece can exceed what two people and a standard truck safely handle. Those need specialty equipment and separate pricing.
  • Same-day is not always same-day. During the March–June and September–October peaks, next-available can be 2–5 business days out.

And one more that costs people real money: illegal dumping. Leaving a couch on a vacant lot or a county road on the mesa is a citation-level offense, and cleanup cost recovery lands on the responsible party. A $110 pickup is dramatically cheaper than a dumping fine.

How to Get an Accurate Number in One Phone Call

Have these five things ready and any competent hauler can quote you within 10%:

  • Photos of every item, taken from far enough back to show scale
  • The exit path — front door, patio slider, or basement stairs — including one photo of the stairs
  • Whether the truck can reach within 40 feet of the door
  • Whether anything is wet, moldy, or has been stored outdoors
  • Everything else you'd add if it were free — old grill, box spring, patio chairs

Bundling is where the savings live. Furniture combined with a garage clear-out or an appliance almost always beats separate visits.
