MATTRESS DISPOSAL COST NEAR ME

Getting rid of a single mattress in Pueblo West costs $0 to about $165, depending on how much of the work you do. Self-hauling to the Pueblo County landfill runs roughly $25–$50 once you add the minimum dump fee and the mattress surcharge, plus your fuel and about 90 minutes. Adding a bulk-item pickup to your existing hauler subscription typically runs $25–$75 per item but you wait for the scheduled date. Full-service pickup from ClearPath — we come to the bedroom, carry it out, load it, and dispose of it — runs $85–$125 for a single mattress and $110–$165 for a mattress and box spring together. Additional sets on the same trip drop to $40–$60 each.

Those are real Pueblo West numbers, not national averages pulled from a franchise price sheet in Dallas.

The four real options, priced out honestly

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There's no municipal trash pickup here — the Metro District handles water, parks, and roads, not your garbage. Every household contracts privately, which means your mattress options depend partly on which hauler you already pay.

  • Self-haul to the county landfill. Budget a minimum-load charge in the $25–$45 range plus a per-mattress surcharge that commonly lands between $10 and $25. Call the scale house before you drive out — these rates get revised and nobody updates the website promptly. Total time from Pueblo West including load, drive, weigh in, unload, weigh out: 60–100 minutes.
  • Bulk pickup through your subscription hauler. Usually $25–$75 per item, sometimes with a two-item annual allowance included. The catch is scheduling: most bulk routes run monthly, so you may sit on the mattress for 2–4 weeks, and it has to be at the curb by 6 a.m.
  • Give it away or sell it. Free, if it's genuinely clean and under about 8 years old. Realistically, a used mattress on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo gets flakes, no-shows, and lowballs. Plan on 3–10 days and at least two cancelled meetups.
  • Full-service removal. $85–$165 for one or two pieces, same-day or next-day, no truck, no straps, no stairs, no landfill line. That's what [mattress removal in Pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) covers.

What actually moves the price

Junk removal pricing is volume-based, and a mattress is a volume-hostile object: roughly 23 cubic feet for a queen, awkwardly shaped, and it doesn't stack or compress. A queen innerspring weighs 60–120 lbs; a memory foam queen with a dense base layer can hit 140 lbs. A box spring adds another 40–70 lbs and is rigid, so it won't bend around a stair landing.

The specific things that push a Pueblo West quote toward the top of the range:

  • Second-floor bedroom with a switchback stairwell. Adds 10–20 minutes and a second person. A king box spring physically will not turn some corners — more on that below.
  • Distance out. Properties off the west end of McCulloch, out past Joe Martinez, or on unpaved county roads add drive time. Anything 20+ minutes past the main corridor may carry a small trip charge.
  • Quantity. One mattress is priced near a minimum. Four mattresses from a rental turnover cost far less per unit — often $50–$65 each — because the drive is amortized.
  • Contamination. Bed bugs, mold, or heavy urine saturation means the mattress gets bagged and landfilled rather than recycled, and it can add $15–$30 in handling.
  • Curbside vs. inside. If it's already at the driveway when we arrive, we can often price at the low end.

The Denver recycling math nobody runs for you

Here's something you won't find on the national junk-removal sites: Colorado has no statewide mattress recycling program. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon fund recycling through a fee added at the point of sale. Colorado doesn't. That means mattress recycling here is voluntary, privately funded, and concentrated in the Denver metro.

A mattress is genuinely recyclable — roughly 80–90% by weight breaks down into recoverable material. A standard innerspring queen yields about 25 lbs of steel, plus foam that becomes carpet padding, cotton batting, and a wood frame.

The problem is geography. The nearest dedicated mattress recycler is in the Denver metro, about 115 miles from Pueblo West. That's a 230-mile round trip. In a half-ton pickup at 18–20 mpg, that's roughly 12 gallons — call it $36–$42 in fuel at current Pueblo prices — plus 4 to 4.5 hours of your day, plus the recycler's intake fee, which typically runs $15–$40 per unit. You're at $55–$85 and half a day to recycle one mattress.

> Driving a single mattress to Denver to recycle it burns more fuel than the mattress diverts in landfill impact. Consolidate or don't bother.

If you're clearing four or six mattresses — a rental property, a motel refresh, a [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) — the trip starts to make sense. For one mattress out of a guest room, it doesn't, and we'll tell you that rather than sell you a green story that doesn't hold up.

The failure modes, plainly

Truck-bed hauling goes wrong more often than people expect. A queen mattress is 80 inches long. A standard short-bed pickup is 67 inches. It hangs out, and on I-25 or the Highway 50 stretch at 60–65 mph with a typical Pueblo crosswind, an unsecured mattress becomes a 100-lb sail. Two ratchet straps minimum, front and rear, run through the mattress handles — not bungee cords. Losing one on the highway is a citation and a liability exposure far past what you saved.

King box springs frequently don't come out. A one-piece king box spring is 76 x 80 inches and doesn't flex. If your stairwell has a landing under about 42 inches of clearance, it isn't coming down intact. It came in before the drywall went up, or through a window. The solution is cutting it apart in place — 20–30 minutes with a utility knife and a reciprocating saw — and yes, we do that, but it's a real add and we'd rather you know before you're surprised.

Illegal dumping is not a shortcut. Mattresses show up on county roads and in arroyos around Pueblo West constantly. Colorado's littering statutes carry fines that reach into the hundreds of dollars, plus cleanup restitution, and dumped mattresses usually still have a receipt, a mailing label, or a delivery tag inside. A $95 pickup is dramatically cheaper than the citation.

Bed bugs change everything. If you suspect them, seal the mattress in a bag *before* it leaves the bedroom, not at the front door. Dragging an infested mattress through a hallway is how a one-room problem becomes a whole-house problem and a $1,200–$2,500 treatment bill. Tell us up front — we don't refuse the job, we just handle it differently.
