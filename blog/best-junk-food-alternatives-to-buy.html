BEST JUNK FOOD ALTERNATIVES TO BUY

If you want to buy junk food alternatives in Pueblo West, buy foods that feel like snacks but aren't mostly sugar, refined starch and frying oil. Good options are roasted chickpeas, plain nuts, air-popped popcorn, plain Greek yogurt, cheese sticks, fresh fruit, and Pueblo green chile with corn tortillas. All of them are sold at the big grocery stores along Highway 50. Most cost about the same per serving as chips or candy, and some cost less. Plain popcorn kernels run about $0.10 per 3-cup serving, while a single-serve bag of chips costs about $1.50 to $2.00. Fruit, eggs and plain dairy are the cheapest swaps. "Healthy" bars and puffs labeled keto or protein are the most expensive.

The rest of this guide covers what to buy and what it costs. It also covers when switching isn't worth it, which most snack guides skip.

The swaps that actually work, with numbers

A good swap keeps what you like about the snack, whether that's the crunch, the salt or the sweetness, and cuts the parts you don't need. Here's how common swaps compare. Calorie and protein figures are standard nutrition-label values per serving:

  • Potato chips → air-popped popcorn. A 1-oz bag of chips is about 150 calories and 10 g of fat. Three cups of air-popped popcorn is about 93 calories and 1 g of fat, plus roughly 3.5 g of fiber. You get the same crunch and salt for about 40% fewer calories.
  • Candy bar → dates or dried mango with a few almonds. Two Medjool dates have about 130 calories. That's still sugar, but it comes with fiber and potassium. An ounce of almonds (about 23 nuts) adds 6 g of protein, which keeps you full until dinner.
  • Flavored yogurt cup → plain Greek yogurt with fruit. A 5.3-oz cup of plain nonfat Greek yogurt has about 15 g of protein and around 6 g of naturally occurring sugar. Many fruit-on-the-bottom cups have 15 to 20 g of sugar, and a lot of that is added.
  • Crackers and dip → roasted chickpeas or snap peas with hummus. A ½-cup serving of roasted chickpeas has around 6 g of protein and 5 g of fiber.
  • Soda → sparkling water with a splash of juice. A 12-oz can of regular soda has about 39 g of sugar. Unflavored sparkling water has none.

The swaps worth making are the ones that add protein or fiber. Those two keep you full. A swap that only lowers calories usually leaves you hungry and back in the pantry within an hour.

Where to buy them in Pueblo West

You don't need a specialty health food store. Pueblo West is served by full-size supermarkets and a Walmart Supercenter along the Highway 50 corridor, and Pueblo's larger stores are a 15 to 20 minute drive east. Every swap on the list above is stocked year-round.

Some local factors are worth knowing:

  • Chile season is your best snack season. Pueblo chiles are harvested from about late August through early October, and roasters set up at farm stands and in parking lots. A bushel of fresh-roasted Pueblo chile usually sells for about $30 to $45, depending on the year and the farm. That's enough to freeze in bags for months. Green chile on eggs, in a quesadilla, or as a salsa for raw vegetables gives you salt and flavor with almost no added fat.
  • Mid-summer farm stands along the St. Charles Mesa sell melons, sweet corn and peaches in quantity. Pueblo's Mirasol cantaloupes are usually at their best in July and August.
  • Buy nuts in bulk bags, not snack packs. Single-serve 1-oz nut packs often cost 2 to 3 times more per ounce than a 1-lb or 3-lb bag. Portion them into reusable containers yourself.
  • Store-brand plain Greek yogurt in a 32-oz tub usually costs well under half of what you'd pay per ounce for single cups.

What a week of swaps costs

Here's a rough budget for one adult replacing one junk snack a day. Prices are typical Colorado grocery prices in 2026 and will vary by store and sale cycle:

| Swap | Weekly cost (approx.) | |---|---| | Popcorn kernels, 2 lb bag (lasts a month+) | ~$1.00 per week | | Plain Greek yogurt, 32 oz | $4–$6 | | Almonds or peanuts, 1 lb | $4–$8 | | Bananas and apples, 7 pieces | $3–$5 | | Canned chickpeas to roast, 2 cans | $2–$3 |

That works out to about $14 to $23 per week for all five, and you won't eat all five every week. For comparison, one vending machine or gas station snack a day at $2.00 to $3.00 adds up to $14 to $21 per week. Switching to these foods doesn't cost more. What it does cost is planning time.

When buying junk food alternatives isn't worth it

This part of the guide is less flattering, but it may save you money.

> A "healthy" snack you don't enjoy is just a more expensive way to end up eating chips anyway.

1. Products that are junk food with better marketing. Veggie straws, many "protein" chips, yogurt-covered pretzels, and most granola bars are close to regular junk food in nutrition. A typical veggie straw serving has about the same calories and fat as potato chips, and you get very little vegetable from it. Many granola bars have 10 to 15 g of sugar. If the first two ingredients are a starch and a sugar, it's not an alternative. Don't pay extra for the label.

2. Specialty keto, paleo and protein snacks. A $6 bag of keto cookies or a $3.50 protein bar costs much more than a hard-boiled egg (about $0.25 to $0.40 each) or a cheese stick (about $0.40 to $0.60). If cost is your reason for switching, skip this whole aisle.

3. Buying fresh food you won't eat in time. The USDA estimates that 30 to 40% of the U.S. food supply is wasted, and a lot of that is produce that spoils at home. If your schedule is unpredictable, a big haul of berries and spinach will likely be in the trash by day six. Buy shelf-stable or frozen versions instead: frozen fruit, canned beans, popcorn kernels, nuts.

4. Changing everything at once. If you replace every snack in the house in one shopping trip, you'll probably be back to old habits within two weeks. Swap one snack, keep it up for a few weeks, then add another.

5. People who should get personal advice first. If you have diabetes, kidney disease, a nut allergy, a history of disordered eating, or you're feeding young children, a general list like this one isn't enough. Nuts are a choking hazard for kids under 4, and dried fruit has a lot of concentrated sugar. A registered dietitian or your doctor should set your plan.

6. If the real problem isn't the food. If you snack because you're stressed, bored or short on sleep, changing what's in your cart won't fix that. You'll eat the almonds the same way you ate the chips.

Clearing out the old stuff

Swapping snacks often turns into a bigger kitchen project. People throw out stale pantry items, finally get rid of the chest freezer full of freezer-burned food in the garage, or clear shelves to make room for bulk containers.

Throw away the food yourself, because it's normal household trash. The larger items are where we can help. ClearPath hauls old refrigerators and freezers through our [appliance removal service in Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo). Old units are a real cost to keep: a 15 to 20 year old fridge can use roughly twice the electricity of a current Energy Star model. If the garage has filled up with boxes, broken shelving and an extra freezer, a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) can clear it in one visit.

A practical order for the whole project:

  • Check the pantry and throw out anything expired or stale.
  • Empty the freezer, and decide whether the unit is worth keeping.
  • Buy shelf-stable alternatives first, then add fresh food in amounts you'll actually eat within 4 to 5 days.
  • Keep the alternatives at eye level and put any remaining junk food somewhere harder to reach.

A simple starting list

If you just want a shopping list for your next trip:

  • 2 lb bag of popcorn kernels
  • 32 oz plain Greek yogurt
  • 1 lb bag of unsalted or lightly salted almonds or peanuts
  • 2 cans of chickpeas
  • A bag of frozen berries
  • A dozen eggs
  • In season: a bushel or half-bushel of roasted Pueblo green chile to freeze

That's about $25 to $35 total, depending on chile season and the store. It covers most of your snack replacements for a week or two, and nothing on the list spoils in three days.
