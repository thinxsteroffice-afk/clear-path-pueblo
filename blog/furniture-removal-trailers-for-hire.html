FURNITURE REMOVAL TRAILERS FOR HIRE

If you searched "furniture removal trailers for hire" in Pueblo West, you probably need one of two things. You may want a trailer you rent and load yourself, or a crew that shows up with a trailer and does the lifting. ClearPath Junk Removal is the second option. We bring a dump trailer and two people, carry the furniture out of your house, and haul it away, usually the same day or the next. A single couch typically runs $75–$150, and a full trailer load runs $350–$600. If you have a truck with a hitch, a free afternoon, and a strong back, renting a trailer can be cheaper. This page shows the math for both so you can decide.

What "Trailer for Hire" Means in Pueblo West

People searching this phrase usually want one of three services. They are priced very differently.

  • Rented utility trailer (DIY): You pick up an enclosed or open trailer from a rental counter in Pueblo, tow it home, load it yourself, drive it to the landfill or donation center, and return it. In-town rentals usually start around $20–$35 per day for a 4x8 or 5x8 cargo trailer, before damage coverage.
  • Dumpster or roll-off rental: A 10- to 20-yard container is dropped in your driveway for 3–7 days. Expect roughly $350–$550, depending on size and tonnage. This works for remodels. It works poorly for one sectional and a dresser.
  • Hauling crew with trailer (what we do): Our crew loads, hauls, sorts for donation, and disposes. You don't lift anything, tow anything, or make a dump run.

Our main [furniture removal pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) page covers the service itself. This page covers the trailer decision.

The Real Cost of Renting a Trailer Yourself

The rental counter quote is the smallest number in a DIY furniture haul. Here is a typical Pueblo West job: one sleeper sofa, one recliner, a queen box spring, and a dresser.

  • Trailer rental: about $25–$35 for the day
  • Damage coverage add-on: commonly $5–$15
  • Fuel: Pueblo West to the rental counter, then to disposal, then back can easily cover 40–60 round-trip miles. Towing cuts fuel economy by 15–25%.
  • Disposal fees: Landfill gate fees in the Pueblo area are generally charged by weight or by load. A small pickup or trailer load commonly costs $30–$70, and there are separate surcharges for mattresses and items with refrigerant.
  • Time: Plan on 4–5 hours from pickup to return.
  • Second person: A sleeper sofa weighs 200–350 lbs. Most people can't move one alone. If you're paying a friend with pizza and beer, count that too.

A realistic DIY total is $90–$160 in cash plus half a Saturday. For that same load, our quote would typically fall in the $200–$300 range. The cash gap is usually $100–$150. You'll have to judge whether your afternoon and your back are worth that.

> The trailer rental is the cheap part. The fuel, the dump fee, and the four hours of your weekend are the expensive part.

When Renting a Trailer Yourself Is the Smarter Move

We mean this. If you match these conditions, rent a trailer and keep your money:

  • You already own a truck with a hitch and working trailer lights. Without one, many rental trailers can't be towed by a sedan or small crossover. Some larger units require a 2-inch ball and a vehicle rated for at least 2,000 lbs of towing.
  • The furniture is light and close to the door. A futon, two nightstands, and a bookshelf in the garage take 20 minutes to load. You don't need us for that.
  • Everything is going to a donation center, not a dump. If your furniture is clean and sellable, haul it to a thrift store drop-off yourself and skip disposal fees entirely. Some charities in the Pueblo area also offer free pickup of resale-quality furniture.
  • You have a flexible schedule. We can usually come within 24–48 hours. If you aren't in a hurry, though, waiting for a free charity pickup can cost you nothing.
  • It's one item. A single recliner may cost you only a landfill gate fee if you have a pickup. Paying for a crew to move one light chair rarely makes sense, and we'll tell you that on the phone.

When You Should NOT Hire Us (And When Neither Option Works)

This section costs us sales, but it saves you from a bad call.

Don't hire us if your furniture is worth selling. A solid-wood dining set or a newer sectional in good shape can bring $100–$400 on a local marketplace listing. We donate what we can, but we don't pay you for items. If something has resale value and you can wait one to two weeks for a buyer, list it first.

Don't hire us for a whole-house move. We remove things you're getting rid of. We are not a moving company. If you want your furniture delivered to a new address, call a licensed mover. We can't legally insure your belongings in transit like a mover does.

Don't expect us to take everything. These are the plain limitations:

  • Furniture with active pest infestations (bed bugs especially) needs to be wrapped and sealed before we touch it. We may decline it or charge extra for handling, because we drive our trailer to other customers' homes the same day.
  • Hazardous materials hidden in furniture, like paint cans in a cabinet or propane cylinders in a patio set, have to be removed first. We can't haul them in a mixed load.
  • Electronics have been banned from Colorado landfills since 2013. TVs on an entertainment center go to e-waste recycling, often for a separate fee.
  • Built-in furniture like attached shelving, bolted cabinets, or a fixed bar needs demolition before removal. That is a bigger, pricier job.

Our quote can come in higher than you expect. Trailer pricing is volume-based. A sectional that looks like "one item" can take up a sixth to a quarter of a trailer once its pieces are separated. If you were picturing $75 and we quote $175, that is why, and you're free to say no. We don't charge for on-site estimates.

Weather and access can delay us. Spring wind in Pueblo West regularly gusts above 40 mph. On those days we tarp and strap every load. Colorado law penalizes unsecured loads, and we won't risk a recliner cushion sailing across Highway 50. Long gravel driveways on rural lots, or homes with no turnaround space, sometimes mean we park at the road and carry farther. That adds labor time.

What Our Trailer Actually Holds

Most people underestimate volume. That's how a simple dump run turns into two trips. Here's what fits in a standard junk removal dump trailer:

  • 1/8 load: A loveseat and an end table, or a single mattress set
  • 1/4 load: A full sofa, a recliner, and a coffee table
  • 1/2 load: A full living room set plus a queen bedroom set
  • Full load: Roughly the contents of a two-bedroom home's worth of furniture, or one heavily packed single-car garage

A rented 5x8 cargo trailer holds about one-third of what our dump trailer holds. It also doesn't tip. Every piece you load, you unload again by hand at the landfill. People forget that half of the job until they're standing at the tipping floor in July heat.

Where Your Furniture Goes After We Leave

Pueblo West has no city-run bulk trash pickup, because it's a metropolitan district and not an incorporated city. Residents use private trash haulers, and most of those won't take a couch at the curb without an extra fee. When we haul, we sort roughly like this:

1. Donation first. Clean, intact pieces go to local charities and resale shops. 2. Recycling second. We pull metal frames, recliner mechanisms, and box spring steel for scrap. 3. Landfill last. Only what can't be reused or recycled goes to disposal.

Mattresses get separate handling. See our [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page for those specifics.
