CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS DUMP NEAR ME

Pueblo West doesn't have its own construction debris dump. Your nearest legal disposal site for drywall, lumber, shingles, and mixed C&D is the Pueblo County Landfill (Southside Landfill), 1100 36th Lane, Pueblo, CO 81006 — roughly 18–22 miles and 25–30 minutes from the center of Pueblo West via US-50 East to CO-47. It's open Monday through Saturday, and construction debris runs roughly $48–$62 per ton with a 1-ton minimum, cash or card, weighed in and out on the scale. Bring a tarp: unsecured loads get cited under Colorado's load-securement statute, and the landfill can turn you away at the gate. Clean concrete, dirt, and asphalt go to a separate aggregate recycler, not the C&D pile, and cost less.

That's the short answer. The longer answer is whether hauling it yourself is actually cheaper than having someone else do it — and for about half the jobs we see, it isn't.

The Actual Numbers on a Self-Haul from Pueblo West

Here's what a real self-haul run costs, using a bathroom gut as the example — about 1.5 tons of tile, drywall, a cast-iron tub, and a vanity.

  • Truck rental: $19.95/day base plus $0.99/mile at Home Depot or U-Haul on Highway 50. A 45-mile round trip from Pueblo West Metro District puts you at roughly $65 before fuel and the mandatory damage waiver.
  • Fuel: A loaded 3/4-ton pickup or box truck gets 11–14 MPG. At $3.15/gallon, that's $11–$13 per round trip.
  • Tipping fees: 1.5 tons at $55/ton averages $82.50. Add the $3–$8 environmental surcharge some scales apply.
  • Your time: Load (90 min) + drive (60 min round trip) + scale queue (15–40 min on a Saturday) + unload (25 min) = roughly 3.5 hours.
  • Total out of pocket: $160–$185, plus a Saturday.

A single full trailer load handled by a crew — pickup, load, haul, disposal, and sweep-up — typically runs $375–$625 in the Pueblo West area depending on weight and material mix. So self-haul genuinely saves you $200–$450 on a job that fits in one load and doesn't hurt your back.

That's a real saving. Take it if the job fits. Details on the crewed version are on our [construction debris removal Pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) page.

Where the Self-Haul Math Falls Apart

The one-load assumption is where most people get burned. Debris volume is deceptive — a 10x12 room stripped to studs generates 1.8–2.4 tons and about 6 cubic yards, which does not fit in a 6.5-foot pickup bed. It fits in three trips.

Three trips means three rentals or three fuel runs, three scale queues, and roughly 9 hours of your weekend. At that point you've spent $280–$340 and a full Saturday plus half of Sunday to save maybe $80 against a crew. Run the trip count honestly *before* you rent anything.

The other trap: the 1-ton minimum. Show up with 600 pounds of scrap lumber and you still pay for a full ton. Two undersized trips cost more in tipping fees than one properly loaded trip.

> If your debris pile is under 1,500 pounds and fits in one bed, self-haul wins clearly. Between 1,500 pounds and 2 tons it's a coin flip. Past 2 tons, you are paying money and losing weekends to break even.

What We Will Not Take — The Hard Limits

Some materials cannot go in a standard C&D load, and no hauler in Pueblo County can legally take them for a normal rate:

  • Asbestos-containing material. Pre-1980 popcorn ceilings, floor tile mastic, HVAC duct wrap, and some siding. This requires CDPHE-certified abatement, testing at $45–$95 per sample, and disposal at a permitted cell. Abatement on a small residential job starts around $1,800 and climbs fast. If your Pueblo West home was built before 1985 and you're tearing out flooring or ceilings, test first. Handing an asbestos load to a general hauler exposes them, you, and the landfill.
  • Wet or unhardened paint, solvents, adhesives, and stains. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events; a few gallons of cured latex with the lids off can go in the load, liquid cannot.
  • Treated lumber in some volumes, railroad ties, and creosote-soaked material — these get flagged at the scale.
  • Freon-bearing appliances pulled during a remodel need certified recovery before disposal, typically a $25–$45 per unit fee. See [appliance removal Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Anything with a live rodent problem or standing sewage. That's a different job with different equipment.

Failure Modes Nobody Warns You About

The overweight ticket. A half-ton pickup rated at a 1,600-pound payload holds about 12 cubic feet of wet drywall before you're over GVWR. Overloaded suspension on the CO-47 grade is how people end up with $2,400 in rear-axle repairs to save $300 in hauling.

The tarp violation. Colorado requires secured loads. Shingles blowing out on Highway 50 is a citation plus liability for whatever they hit. A $22 tarp and four ratchet straps solves it.

The Saturday scale queue. Between 9 AM and noon on Saturdays, the weigh-in line at the county landfill can hit 25–40 minutes each direction. Tuesday at 10 AM you're through in under 6 minutes. If you must self-haul, go midweek.

The HOA notice. Several Pueblo West Metro District neighborhoods and covenanted subdivisions restrict visible debris piles beyond 7–14 days. A remodel that runs long turns a debris pile into a violation letter. Know your covenant before you plan to stage material for a month.

The "I'll do it next weekend" pile. The single most common call we get is from someone whose one-weekend self-haul has been sitting in the side yard since March. Nothing wrong with that — it's just a more expensive job by then, because the bottom layer is soaked, heavier, and grown into the weeds.

When a Service Actually Beats Self-Haul

Clear cases where paying makes sense:

  • Over 2 tons or more than 2 truckloads. The multi-trip penalty erases your savings.
  • You're paying a crew by the hour. A framer at $55–$75/hour spending two hours on dump runs costs more than the haul does. Keep them on the tools.
  • Second-story or stair-carry debris. Loading is the actual labor; the drive is the easy part.
  • You don't own a truck. Rental plus mileage plus waiver eats most of the margin immediately.
  • You need it gone on a deadline — a final inspection, a closing date, a rental turnover. Same-day and next-day slots exist; a Saturday you have to schedule around does not.
  • Mixed loads with heavy items. Cast iron tubs, old boilers, and steel doors are where people get hurt.

Getting a Number You Can Trust

Ask any hauler these four questions before booking:

1. Is the quote per load, per ton, or per hour? Per-ton pricing on a light-but-bulky load (insulation, trim, siding) is usually cheaper for you. Per-load is cheaper on dense material. 2. Are tipping fees included or billed separately? A $295 "haul fee" plus pass-through disposal can land higher than a $425 all-in number. 3. Is loading included? Some outfits quote curbside-only and charge extra to enter the structure. 4. What's the overage rate if the load runs bigger? Get it in writing before the truck arrives.

For jobs that mix demolition with hauling — a shed, a deck, a fence line — the demo labor usually dominates the price, not the disposal.
