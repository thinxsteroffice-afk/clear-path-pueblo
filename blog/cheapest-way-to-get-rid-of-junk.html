CHEAPEST WAY TO GET RID OF JUNK

The cheapest way to get rid of junk in Pueblo West depends on what it is. In order of cost:

  • Free: give it away. Post usable items on Facebook Marketplace or Nextdoor marked "curb alert," or donate them.
  • About $10–$25: haul it yourself to a community cleanup event.
  • One landfill gate fee: if you already own a pickup, drive the load to Southside Landfill yourself.

Paying a junk removal crew, ClearPath included, is almost never the cheapest choice for a single couch or a few bags. It starts to pay off when the pile is heavy, the job takes more than one trip, or the items need special handling, like appliances with refrigerant, hot tubs or concrete. The rest of this guide shows where each option stops saving you money.

Option 1: Give it away (cost: $0)

Anything that still works should leave your property for free. In Pueblo West, people with trucks will drive out to pick up free items within a day or two, especially:

  • Working appliances, including washers, dryers and chest freezers
  • Solid-wood furniture, dressers and bookshelves
  • Scrap metal, old fencing and T-posts
  • Usable lumber, pavers and landscape rock
  • Trampolines and playsets, if you let the person take them apart

How to do it well: Post with clear photos and say "must be gone by Saturday." Leave the item at the end of the driveway so you don't have to be home. If nobody bites in 72 hours, the item is junk, and you move to the next option.

Where it fails: Stained mattresses, particleboard furniture, broken recliners and anything with a smell won't move, even for free. Don't waste two weeks reposting them.

Option 2: Community cleanup events (cost: about $5–$25 per load)

Cleanup events have historically been the cheapest way to get rid of a full truckload in this area. Here are the details, and one catch most people miss.

The Pueblo West Community Clean Up has been held at the Metro District Public Works yard, 280 E. McCulloch Blvd., from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. In 2022 the prices were:

  • $25 per level pickup load
  • $5 per tire
  • $10 per mattress

Tickets had to be bought ahead of time through the Pueblo West Chamber of Commerce. Seniors and disabled residents could call 719-647-9086 to ask about pickup. These events aren't held every year on a fixed schedule, so check the Chamber's events calendar before you plan around one.

Here's the catch: the City of Pueblo's free "Team Up to Clean Up" day and its 50%-off landfill discount days are for residents inside city limits. You need a photo ID or a current utility bill with a city address. Pueblo West is unincorporated and has its own metro district, so most Pueblo West addresses won't qualify. Don't load your truck for a free dump day and get turned away at the gate.

The city events have also had strict limits: one pickup load per household, up to nine tires (off the rim), and no concrete, soil, rock, appliances, TVs, computers, other electronics or hazardous materials.

Option 3: Haul it to the landfill yourself (cost: one gate fee plus your time)

If you own a pickup and the junk fits in one trip, the cheapest any-day option is the Waste Connections Southside Landfill. It has been open Monday–Friday 7 a.m.–5 p.m. and Saturday 8 a.m.–1 p.m. Gate rates change, so call ahead for the current per-load or per-ton price.

What actually raises the cost of a self-haul:

  • An uncovered load costs double. Southside has charged twice the normal gate rate for loads that aren't tarped or covered. A $20 tarp and a few ratchet straps pay for themselves on the first trip.
  • The drive. From most of Pueblo West it's a round trip across town. Plan on 1.5–2 hours for loading, driving, waiting in line and unloading.
  • Items the landfill won't take. Appliances, electronics, liquids, paint, tires and construction or roofing debris are all restricted. Colorado has banned TVs, computers and monitors from landfills since 2013, so electronics have to go to an e-waste recycler, which often charges per item.
  • Renting a truck. If you don't own one, a rented pickup usually costs $20–$30 for the first 75–90 minutes, plus mileage and fuel. A cross-town landfill run often goes past the base window, and one load can easily cost $60–$100 before you pay the gate fee.

> If the junk fits in one pickup bed and you own the pickup, nobody will beat what you pay at the landfill gate. Every extra trip, borrowed truck and sore back reduces those savings.

Option 4: Rent a dumpster (cost: usually $350–$600)

A roll-off dumpster makes sense when the junk comes out slowly over 5–10 days, like during a remodel, a roof tear-off or a long garage sort. Southern Colorado quotes for a 10- to 20-yard container commonly run $350–$600. That price usually includes a weight allowance, with overage charged by the ton.

Where dumpsters fail in Pueblo West:

  • You still do all the lifting, and you have to lift everything over a wall about 4 feet high.
  • On a gravel or dirt driveway, the truck can leave ruts. Many companies want plywood under the rails.
  • Neighbors toss their own junk in overnight, and you pay for their weight.
  • Concrete, dirt and rock are so heavy that they often need a separate container type or cost extra.

Option 5: Hire a junk removal crew (cost: usually $100–$700+)

Junk removal companies in Southern Colorado usually price by how much of the truck your stuff fills:

  • Single item (a couch, a mattress, a fridge): often $75–$175
  • About a quarter to half a truck: often $200–$400
  • Full truck (a garage cleanout or estate): often $450–$700+

The price includes the labor, loading, disposal fees, recycling and sorting donations. For a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or anything that would take you 3+ landfill trips, the gap between doing it yourself and hiring a crew gets small, sometimes under $100, once you count truck rental, gate fees, fuel and a lost Saturday. Prices and what's included are on our [junk removal Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) page.

When paying us is NOT worth it

Hiring a crew is the wrong choice for a lot of people. Here's who shouldn't call us:

  • You have one or two items and a truck. A single recliner or a few contractor bags are cheaper to haul yourself, even at full gate price. Our minimum charge will cost more than the landfill.
  • The item still works and someone will take it. A running washer, a solid dresser or a usable trampoline should go out for free on Marketplace. Paying anyone to haul off something people want is wasted money.
  • Your timeline matches a cleanup event. If a community cleanup is 2–3 weeks out and you can wait, a $25 truckload beats any quote you'll get from us.
  • The junk is mostly clean scrap metal. Metal recyclers pay you by the pound. Haul it yourself and get paid instead of paying someone.
  • It's hazardous waste. We can't legally take paint, solvents, pesticides, fuel, propane tanks, asbestos or car batteries mixed in with general junk, and neither can most haulers. Those go to household hazardous waste programs. If someone offers to take them with your load, ask where they're dumping them.
  • You're doing an active remodel. If debris will keep coming for a week, a dumpster sitting in the driveway usually beats paying for 3–4 separate truck visits.

Where crews tend to fail customers is unclear pricing. If a company won't give you a price range before the truck arrives, or won't explain how it handles "heavy material" surcharges, get another quote. That applies to us too.

Items that cost more no matter who hauls them

Some items cost extra whether you take them yourself or hire someone, and it helps to know before you plan:

  • Refrigerators, freezers and AC units have refrigerant that has to be recovered by a certified technician before the metal can be recycled. That's why [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) often carries a per-unit fee.
  • Mattresses are bulky and often carry a separate fee. The 2022 Pueblo West cleanup charged $10 each.
  • Tires are banned from regular landfill loads and are usually charged per tire.
  • Hot tubs, concrete and dirt are heavy enough that it can take 2–3 people or equipment to move them. Hauling them yourself here leads to injuries and damaged trailers.

How to pick the cheapest option

1. Sort first. Separate items into give-away, recycle, landfill and restricted. 2. Give away what you can. Post usable items. Allow 72 hours. 3. Check for a cleanup event. If one is scheduled within a few weeks and you have a truck, use it. 4. Count your trips. One trip with your own truck means self-haul. Three or more, or no truck at all, means you should get a junk removal quote and compare honestly. 5. Ask what's included. Get labor, disposal fees and item surcharges in writing before work starts.
