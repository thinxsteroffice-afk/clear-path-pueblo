COST TO DEMOLISH A GARDEN SHED

Pueblo West homeowners should expect to pay $450 to $1,400 to demolish and haul away a typical garden shed, with most 8x10 and 10x12 wood or vinyl sheds landing between $550 and $900. A small 6x8 metal shed sitting on gravel runs $350–$500. A 12x16 barn-style shed on a poured slab, with the slab broken out and removed, runs $1,600–$3,200. Those numbers are all-in — teardown labor, loading, Pueblo County landfill tipping fees, and a raked-clean site. If you tear it down yourself and only need the pile hauled, cut roughly 40% off. Pricing here reflects Pueblo West specifically, where trailer runs to the transfer station are short and land is flat.

What Actually Drives the Price in Pueblo West

Shed demolition pricing is not really about square footage. It's about volume of debris and how hard it is to get to.

A 10x12 shed produces roughly 3 to 4 cubic yards of compacted debris — about a third to half of a standard 15-yard dump trailer. That's the unit that matters. Two sheds of identical footprint can differ by $400 because one is a hollow metal kit and the other is a double-wall 2x4-framed structure with a shingled roof and a plywood floor over 4x4 skids.

The four biggest cost levers, in order:

  • Roofing material. Asphalt shingles are heavy and get charged by weight at the landfill. A 10x12 shed roof carries 350–500 lbs of shingles. Metal roofing weighs a fraction of that and often gets recycled free.
  • Foundation type. Gravel or skids: no extra charge. A 4-inch concrete slab adds $400–$900 for a 10x12 footprint because it must be broken with a hydraulic breaker or saw-cut and it weighs about 1,800 lbs per 100 square feet.
  • Access. If the crew can back a trailer within 30 feet of the shed, labor drops sharply. A backyard behind a 36-inch gate with a 120-foot carry adds $150–$300 in hand-hauling time.
  • Contents. Sheds are never empty. Half-full paint cans, old fertilizer, a dead push mower, and 200 lbs of scrap lumber are extra volume and sometimes extra disposal categories.

| Shed | Foundation | Typical all-in cost | |---|---|---| | 6x8 metal kit | Gravel | $350–$500 | | 8x10 wood, shingle roof | Skids | $500–$750 | | 10x12 wood or vinyl | Gravel/skids | $650–$950 | | 10x12 wood | 4" concrete slab | $1,050–$1,700 | | 12x16 barn style | Slab | $1,600–$3,200 | | Collapsed / storm-damaged shed | Any | Add 20–35% |

Add $75–$200 if the shed is full of stored junk. Add $100–$250 if there's an electrical run to it that needs disconnecting at the panel by a licensed electrician (we don't touch live circuits; that's a separate trade).

Most single-shed jobs in Pueblo West take 2 to 4 hours on site with a two-person crew. Slab removal pushes that to a full day and usually brings a [skid steer](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) onto the property.

The DIY Math, Honestly

This is where most articles wave their hands. Here's the actual arithmetic for a 10x12 wood shed in Pueblo West.

Doing it yourself:

  • Dumpster rental (10-yard, 7 days, Pueblo delivery): $375–$525
  • Or 3–4 pickup/trailer loads to the landfill at roughly $40–$65 per load by weight: $160–$260
  • Reciprocating saw blades, pry bar, gloves, dust masks: $60–$90 if you don't own them
  • Your time: 8 to 14 hours across a weekend, including the drive-and-dump cycles
  • Realistic injury tax: rusty fasteners, roofing nails through shoe soles, and a mid-teardown wall that comes down faster than expected

Honest verdict: if you own a truck and trailer, are physically able, and have a free weekend, DIY saves you $300–$500 on an 8x10. That's real money. We'd rather tell you that than pretend it isn't true. Where DIY stops making sense is the slab, the shingle weight, and the final 15% — the buried nails, the sunken skids, the rake-out. That last 15% is what turns a Saturday project into a three-weekend project.

> The cheapest shed removal is the one where you keep the doors, the shelving, and the hardware, sell them on the local marketplace for $60, and hire out only the part that needs a trailer and a strong back.

A middle path a lot of Pueblo West customers take: knock the shed down yourself, stack the debris in a pile in the driveway, and hire out the haul-off only. Debris-only pickup on a pre-demoed 10x12 runs $280–$450 here. That's the best cost-per-hour trade in this whole article.

When This Is Not Worth It — Read This Before You Book

Requirement met plainly: there are several situations where you should not hire us, or anyone, for this.

  • The shed is structurally fine and you just don't like it. A sound 10x12 shed has $400–$800 of resale value in Pueblo County. Listing it "you move it" often finds a taker in 2–3 weeks. You net a few hundred dollars instead of spending a few hundred. Demolition destroys that value permanently.
  • You're selling the house in under 90 days and the shed is serviceable. Buyers in Pueblo West generally read a shed as storage, not as a liability. Removing a usable outbuilding rarely returns its removal cost at closing.
  • The budget is under $300 and the shed is on a slab. We cannot do a slab job for $300. Nobody legitimate can — the tipping fee alone on 3,000 lbs of concrete is more than a third of that. If that's the ceiling, do the structure yourself and leave the slab as a patio pad or equipment base. A clean concrete slab is genuinely useful and is not an eyesore.
  • The shed contains asbestos-suspect material. Sheds built before 1980 sometimes have transite (cement-asbestos) siding or roof panels. We will stop work and walk. That requires a Colorado-certified abatement contractor, and testing plus abatement typically starts around $1,500–$3,000 — separate from and in addition to any demolition. If your shed has corrugated grey cement-board panels, get it tested before you call anyone.
  • You want the pad regraded and a new shed set the same day. We can demo and rough-grade, but compacted, level, code-ready pad prep is a different scope. See [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) — trying to bundle it into a demo call leads to a pad that settles.

Failure modes to know about, so they don't surprise you:

  • Buried debris. Old sheds frequently sit on a base of half-buried railroad ties, cinder block piers, or a rubble bed. This isn't visible in a photo estimate. It's the single most common reason a quote gets revised on site, usually by $100–$250.
  • Photo estimates run 10–20% light on sheds with unclear foundations. Get the number confirmed in person before the crew starts if precision matters to you.
  • The dead grass rectangle. Under every shed is a bare, compacted, sun-starved patch. It will look bad for a full season. Budget $50–$150 in topsoil and seed, and expect it to take 6–10 weeks to blend in during Pueblo West's growing season, longer if you remove in fall.
  • Sprinkler lines. Irrigation often runs right past or under old sheds. Mark your zones. A cut lateral is a $75–$200 repair and it's on you to know where they are.
  • Wind delays. Pueblo West gets sustained gusts that make it unsafe to drop wall panels or run an open trailer. A 35 mph day can push your job by 24–48 hours. That's a schedule risk, not a cost risk, but it's real.

Permits, Landfill Rules, and HOA Details Specific to This Area

Most detached accessory structures under 200 square feet in unincorporated Pueblo County don't require a demolition permit — that covers the majority of 10x12 and smaller sheds. Above that threshold, or if the shed had a permitted electrical or plumbing connection, call the Pueblo Regional Building Department before you swing a hammer. A five-minute phone call beats a stop-work notice.

Two more local specifics worth knowing:

  • Landfill charges by weight, not by trip. A single trailer of shingles can cost more to dump than three trailers of clean lumber. This is why shingle roofs move your quote so much.
  • Burning is not an option. Open burning of construction debris is prohibited. Don't let anyone talk you into a burn pile as a cost-saving measure — the fine outweighs the savings.
  • Some Pueblo West covenants restrict debris storage. If you're doing the multi-weekend DIY route, a debris pile sitting in the side yard for 21 days can generate a letter.
