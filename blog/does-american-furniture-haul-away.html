DOES AMERICAN FURNITURE HAUL AWAY?

Yes — American Furniture Warehouse (AFW) will take your old furniture, but only as an add-on to a delivery of new furniture you bought from them. It's a one-for-one swap: buy a sofa, they'll take a sofa. It is not a standalone pickup service. You have to request it when you place the order (not when the truck shows up), the old piece has to be accessible and reasonably clean, and in most years there's been a modest per-piece fee in the $20–$40 range rather than a flat free haul-off. If you're not buying replacement furniture from AFW, the answer is no. Policies shift year to year, so confirm with the Pueblo store before you count on it.

That's the short version. Here's what actually happens to Pueblo West customers, because the gap between "they offer haul-away" and "they hauled my stuff away" is where people get stuck with a recliner in the driveway.

The five conditions that decide whether the truck takes it

Every big-box furniture retailer with a removal program — AFW, Ashley, Furniture Row, the national mattress chains — runs it on roughly the same logic. The crew is on a routed delivery schedule with a 2–4 hour arrival window and six to twelve stops behind you. Removal only works when it's been built into that route. The conditions we see enforced most often:

  • It must be a replacement in the same category. New sofa in, old sofa out. They are not taking your old dresser because you bought a sofa, and they're not taking the coffee table, the lamp, or the box of books on top of it.
  • It's piece-for-piece, not room-for-room. This is the one that burns people. A 6-piece sectional coming out and a 3-piece sectional going in means they take three pieces. The other three stay.
  • It has to be requested in advance — add it at the register or at least 48–72 hours before the delivery date so it lands on the manifest. A driver with a full truck physically cannot take a 84-inch sofa that isn't on the paperwork.
  • It has to be clean and pest-free. Visible bed bug evidence, heavy pet soiling, mold, or biohazard means an immediate refusal at the door, and that refusal is non-negotiable regardless of what you paid.
  • It has to be accessible and empty. Emptied drawers, no stairs that weren't disclosed, not bolted to a wall, not in a detached shed 60 feet down the driveway.

Mattresses carry an extra rule at nearly every retailer: the old one often needs to be bagged before the crew will touch it. A disposal bag runs about $8–$12 at a hardware store. Buy it the day before, not the morning of.

> The haul-away is a courtesy bolted onto a delivery, not a junk removal service. It exists to move one box off a truck and one box on. Ask it to do anything else and it fails.

What AFW's program does not cover, specifically

Because this is the part nobody tells you until the truck is pulling away:

  • Anything you're not replacing. The old patio set, the broken bed frame in the garage, the TV armoire nobody wants.
  • Non-furniture items. Appliances, hot tubs, exercise equipment, electronics, construction debris, yard waste.
  • Items in outbuildings, basements, or upstairs when that wasn't noted on the order.
  • Disassembly work. They will not take apart your bunk bed or unbolt a wall unit. A two-person crew has roughly 15–25 minutes budgeted per stop.
  • Same-day additions. "Can you grab this too?" gets a no about 95% of the time, and the honest reason is that the truck is already loaded to the door with the next four deliveries.

When AFW's haul-away is the better deal and you should not call us

We'd rather tell you this than have you pay us for something you can get cheaper.

If you're buying one replacement piece from AFW and they'll take the old one for $20–$40, take that deal. Our minimum for a single-item furniture pickup in Pueblo West starts around $95–$125, because a dedicated truck, two people, 25–30 miles round trip from our yard, and a disposal fee cost real money whether we pick up one sofa or four. On a straight one-for-one swap, the retailer wins on price every time. That isn't close.

If you own a pickup and the item is light and whole, do it yourself. A loveseat, a dinette set, two nightstands — that's one trip. Expect a $30–$50 minimum load charge at the landfill and about 90 minutes of your Saturday. You'll save $70 or more.

If the piece is still genuinely good, donate it instead. Habitat for Humanity's Pueblo ReStore, Salvation Army, and local nonprofits will often pick up for free. The trade-offs are real: pickup lead times run 1–3 weeks, they screen hard against rips, stains, smoke odor, and particleboard that's started to swell, and roughly 4 in 10 of the pieces people assume are donatable get declined at the door. If your sofa has a torn cushion or a sagging frame, skip the donation step and don't waste three weeks.

If your trash service includes bulk pickup, use it. Some Pueblo-area residential plans include 1–2 large items per quarter at no extra charge, with a size cap. Check your hauler's bulk-item rules before paying anyone. Free beats cheap.

If you can wait, wait and bundle. Our per-item economics improve sharply with volume. One sofa at $125 versus a sofa, mattress, dresser, and a dozen boxes for $250–$350 as a half-trailer load is the difference between roughly $125 per item and roughly $20 per item. If you've got a spring cleanout coming, hold the sofa in the garage and do it once.

Our honest limitations

We won't pretend we're the answer to everything.

  • We have a price floor. Under about $95, we lose money and will tell you to handle it yourself or wait.
  • We can't always do same-day. Weekday same-day happens maybe 40% of the time; Saturdays in April through June book out 5–10 days.
  • Confirmed bed bug infestations change the job. We can still take it, but it becomes a contained, wrapped removal with a disposal surcharge, often $50–$100 above the base. Hiding it doesn't help you — the crew will see it.
  • We are not a resale outlet. If your piece has real market value, you'll do better selling it than paying us to remove it. A clean mid-grade sofa still moves locally for $100–$300.
  • Weight limits are physics. A solid-wood armoire or a stone-top table can run 300–450 lbs. Narrow stairwells and tight basement turns sometimes mean partial disassembly, which adds time to the quote.

The practical sequence for Pueblo West

1. Before you buy: ask the salesperson, in these words, "How many pieces will your crew remove, from where in the house, and what's the charge per piece?" Get it written on the order. 2. 48–72 hours out: confirm the removal is on the delivery manifest. Call the store, not the 800 number. 3. Night before: empty every drawer, bag the mattress, clear a path, move the vehicle off the driveway apron. 4. Day of: if the crew refuses, don't argue for 20 minutes. Note the reason and move the item to the garage, not the curb — a sofa sitting outside for a week in Pueblo West weather becomes a soaked, unusable, heavier sofa. 5. After: bundle whatever's left over into one pickup. Volume is the only real lever on cost.

AFW's haul-away is a decent, narrow tool. Used inside its limits — one piece out, one piece in, arranged ahead — it's the cheapest option on the board. Outside those limits it does nothing, and that's where a truck and two people with no delivery schedule to keep become worth paying for.
