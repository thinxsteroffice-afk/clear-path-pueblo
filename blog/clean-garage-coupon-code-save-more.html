CLEAN GARAGE COUPON CODE? SAVE MORE

Short answer: there isn't one universal "clean garage coupon code" that works in Pueblo West. Most local junk removal companies, ClearPath included, don't sell through checkout pages where a promo code applies. Prices are quoted on-site based on how much of the truck your stuff fills. A coupon usually saves $20–$50, or 10–15%. You'll save more by changing how you book than by finding a code: shrinking the load, choosing a weekday slot, and pulling out items with surcharges before the crew arrives. This guide covers what those savings look like in real numbers, which national coupon claims to ignore, and when paying for a garage cleanout isn't worth it at all.

Why coupon codes rarely matter for garage cleanouts

Coupon sites list "garage cleanout promo codes" because people search for them. Most of those codes belong to national franchises, have expired, or only apply to a minimum load size. A typical franchise coupon reads "$25 off a half-truck or more." The half-truck already costs $300 or more, so you save about 8%.

Volume-based pricing explains why. A standard junk truck holds about 12–15 cubic yards. Every company prices by how much of that space your stuff takes up. If you cut what goes on the truck by a quarter, you save more than any printed coupon will. On a typical two-car garage, that can mean $100–$200.

> The cheapest cubic yard is the one that never goes on the truck.

If you see a code online, call and ask whether it applies before you book. Don't count on it to make your budget work.

What a Pueblo West garage cleanout typically costs

Here are realistic market ranges for the Pueblo area. These are typical industry ranges, not a ClearPath price list. Your actual quote depends on what's in the garage and how easy it is to reach.

  • Minimum pickup (a few items): roughly $75–$150
  • Quarter truck (about 3–4 cubic yards): roughly $150–$275
  • Half truck (about 6–8 cubic yards): roughly $300–$450
  • Full truck (about 12–15 cubic yards): roughly $550–$800
  • Refrigerators and freezers: often an extra $25–$75 each, because refrigerant has to be handled properly
  • Tires: commonly $5–$15 each, since landfills charge extra for them

An average cluttered two-car garage in Pueblo West fills about half to a full truck. The crew is usually on-site for 2–4 hours. A packed three-car garage, or one with a workshop, can run past one load. Many Pueblo West homes sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots, and those garages often hold years of overflow: old fencing, leftover lumber, broken mowers, dead freezers. Plan for that.

Seven ways to save more than a coupon would

None of these need a code. Each one lowers what goes on the truck or how long the crew spends.

1. Sort before we arrive. Make three piles: keep, donate, haul. If the crew has to sort for you, labor time can go up by 30–60 minutes. 2. Drop off donations yourself. Pueblo thrift stores and donation centers take working tools, bikes, and furniture. Pulling those out often cuts a load by 10–20%. 3. Sell scrap metal. Old water heaters, steel shelving, and copper wire are worth money at local scrap yards. The amount is small, but it beats paying to haul them. 4. Book a weekday. Saturday slots fill first from April through September. Weekday mornings are easier to schedule, and some companies give better rates for flexible timing. Ask. 5. Bundle jobs. If you're also clearing yard debris or an old shed, one visit is cheaper than two separate minimums. Our [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) can be done on the same trip. 6. Get a photo quote first. Send 3–4 photos of the garage from different corners. You'll get a price range before anyone drives out, so the on-site number won't catch you off guard. 7. Pull out the surcharge items. If you have a truck, taking a couple of old tires or a can of paint to the right drop-off yourself saves the per-item fees.

When hiring a garage cleanout isn't worth it

This is the section that can talk you out of hiring us. In some situations, paying for a crew is the wrong call.

  • You have one or two items and a pickup truck. A single couch or a few bags of trash isn't worth a $75–$150 minimum if you can get to the landfill yourself. A self-haul load at a regional landfill often costs less than half that.
  • Most of it is hazardous waste. Junk removal crews generally can't take leftover paint, motor oil, pesticides, pool chemicals, or propane tanks with gas in them. If that's most of your garage, you need Pueblo County's household hazardous waste drop-off instead. We'd only be able to take part of the job.
  • You're doing a slow, multi-week clean-out. If you plan to clear a little each weekend for 6–8 weeks, renting a dumpster may cost less. A 10-yard dumpster for 7 days usually runs $300–$500 in southern Colorado, and you set the pace. The trade-off is that you do all the lifting. The dumpster also sits in your driveway, which some Pueblo West HOAs restrict.
  • The garage is mostly valuables you haven't gone through. If you're clearing a parent's garage and haven't sorted the tools, collectibles, or paperwork, wait. A crew will haul what you point at. Nobody can undo a load once it's at the landfill.
  • You're expecting a coupon to make it cheap. If the job only works at 50% off, it'll probably still feel too expensive at 15% off. Be honest about that before you book.

Garage cleanouts also go wrong in a few predictable ways:

  • Underestimating the load. Garages hold more than people think. Overhead rafters, shelving, and the space behind parked cars often push a "half load" closer to a full one. Look up and behind everything before you guess.
  • Changing your mind on-site. Adding items after the quote raises the price, and that's fair. If you add half a truck after agreeing to a quarter, expect the price to roughly double.
  • Forgetting access. A garage door that won't open, a steep gravel drive, or a big dog in the yard all slow the crew down. Fix those the day before.

Pueblo West–specific things to know

Pueblo West isn't the same as hauling in downtown Pueblo, and a few local details affect your price.

  • Distance to disposal. Pueblo West is spread out. Homes off far streets like Purcell or out past McCulloch sit farther from disposal sites. Some companies add a trip charge for outlying addresses, so ask before you book.
  • Wind and loose debris. Spring gusts in the area often hit 40–50 mph. If you're staging junk outside before pickup, tie down or bag the light stuff. Otherwise, insulation and cardboard end up in your neighbor's yard.
  • Summer heat. Garage temperatures can go above 100°F in July. Morning appointments are faster and safer for everyone, and faster jobs cost less.
  • Mixed loads are common. Many Pueblo West garages hold leftovers from landscaping, fencing, or construction. Heavy materials like concrete or rock are priced differently from household junk. If you have them, mention them up front. See [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) for how those are handled.
