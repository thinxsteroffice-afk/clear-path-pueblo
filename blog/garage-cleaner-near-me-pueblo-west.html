The Three Services Hiding Behind One Search

"Garage cleaner" gets typed by people who want completely different outcomes. Sorting yourself into the right bucket before you call saves a wasted estimate:

  • Junk removal / cleanout crew (that's us) — physical removal of everything you no longer want: the broken treadmill, 14 boxes from a move three years ago, the dead chest freezer, warped particleboard shelving, half a pallet of leftover pavers. Priced by truck volume and labor.
  • Epoxy or polyaspartic floor coating installer — grinds and coats the concrete. In Pueblo County that runs roughly $4–$8 per square foot, so $1,800–$4,200 for a typical 500-square-foot two-car slab. They will not touch your junk; most require the garage be completely empty before they arrive.
  • Professional organizer or residential cleaner — sorts, categorizes, installs bins and wall systems. Bills hourly, often $55–$95/hour, and is genuinely worth it if your problem is *organization*, not *volume*.

The order matters. Haul first, coat second, organize third. Every installer who's had to reschedule because the garage was still full will tell you the same.

Pricing here is volume-based, not hourly, so you know the number before we start loading. Our truck holds 15 cubic yards — about six full-size pickup loads.

  • Minimum load (about 1/8 truck) — $145–$185. A couple appliances, some boxes, one piece of furniture.
  • Quarter truck — $195–$275. The single-bay corner that got out of hand.
  • Half truck — $340–$460. Most two-car garages where the cars still fit but the perimeter is stacked.
  • Three-quarter to full truck — $520–$775. Three-car garages, detached shops, or 20-plus years in the same house.
  • Multi-truck jobs — quoted on site. Deep Pueblo West lots with a garage *and* a shop *and* a lean-to will sometimes hit two or three loads.

Those numbers include labor, loading, disposal fees, and the sweep-out. What they don't include: appliances with refrigerant carry a $35–$45 recovery fee each, mattresses run $30–$50, and passenger tires are $8–$14 apiece because the landfill charges separately for all three. We'd rather itemize those on the quote than bury them.

Pueblo West's specific quirk is lot size. A large share of properties north of Highway 50 sit on an acre or more, which means the garage often isn't the only accumulation point — there's a side yard with a collapsed shed and a fence line stacked with scrap. Bundling saves real money, since a second stop on the same property typically adds 20–35% rather than doubling the price. Same logic applies if you're already booking [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) or a [shed demolition](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out. There are several situations where calling us is the wrong financial decision, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

Your garage is a half-day of your own work and you own a truck. If you can see the floor, the pile is under roughly 2 cubic yards, and none of it is heavy or hazardous, drive it yourself. A trip to the county landfill from a Pueblo West address is about 45–60 minutes round trip including dump time, and disposal for a light pickup load typically lands in the $25–$50 range. You'll save $150. We charge for labor and logistics, and if you don't need either, we're overpriced for you.

You have more than about 25% resale value sitting in there. Working tools, a serviceable riding mower, decent furniture, a functioning appliance — those go on Facebook Marketplace, not in our truck. A running push mower clears $75–$150 locally; a decent tool chest, $200–$400. We will haul them, but we're not going to pretend that's smart. Sell the good stuff first, then call us for the remainder. The remainder is usually a smaller, cheaper load anyway.

You're mid-renovation and generating debris weekly. If drywall, framing scrap, and demo waste will keep coming for three weeks, a 10-yard dumpster at roughly $325–$450 for a 7-day rental beats calling us four separate times. Dumpsters win on repeat volume; we win on one-and-done, tight access, and anything requiring someone to carry it out. (If it's a single clean-up at the end of a project, that's a different math problem — see [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo).)

You want the concrete cleaned, not emptied. Oil stains, tire marks, and dusty slabs are a pressure-washing and degreasing job. We sweep; we do not degrease, seal, or coat. Anyone telling you a junk crew will leave your floor looking new is selling.

The contents are contested. Divorce, an estate with multiple heirs, a tenant's belongings still under a legal notice period — do not have us haul until the ownership question is settled in writing. Colorado has specific procedures around abandoned tenant property, and we've walked away from jobs where the paperwork wasn't there. That's what [foreclosure and eviction cleanouts](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) exist for, and they get scheduled differently on purpose.

> The honest failure mode of this service isn't the price. It's the customer who empties the garage, feels great for six weeks, and refills it — because the volume was a symptom and the intake was never fixed.

What We Legally Cannot Take

No hauler in Pueblo County can put these in a standard truck, and any company that says yes is either lying or dumping illegally:

  • Liquid paint, stains, solvents, and pesticides — must go to a household hazardous waste collection, and Pueblo County holds these on limited dates. Latex paint can be dried out with kitty litter or a $8 hardener packet and then disposed of as solid waste; oil-based cannot.
  • Motor oil, antifreeze, gasoline — auto parts stores in Pueblo accept used oil free, typically up to 5 gallons per visit.
  • Propane tanks with fuel in them, including the 20-pounders behind every Pueblo West grill. Exchange them.
  • Asbestos-containing material — common in pre-1980 flooring and pipe wrap. Requires a licensed abatement contractor and CDPHE notification.
  • Ammunition, medical sharps, and pressurized cylinders.

Batteries, electronics, and scrap metal we handle — those just route to recycling instead of the landfill, which is where roughly 30–40% of an average garage load ends up going.

That's not a comfort footnote — heat is why a 3-hour job becomes a 5-hour job, and why we book cleanouts for 7:00–10:00 a.m. slots between June and mid-September.

  • Wind off the mesa will redistribute a pile of loose cardboard across your neighbor's acre in about four minutes. We tarp and load progressively rather than pre-staging everything at once.
