COLORADO SPRINGS JUNK REMOVAL COST

Junk removal in Colorado Springs typically runs $150–$250 for a single item, $250–$400 for a quarter-truck load, $400–$600 for a half truck, and $650–$900 for a full 15-cubic-yard truck as of 2026. Pueblo West sits about 50 miles south of those companies, and most Springs-based crews either decline the job, add a $75–$150 travel surcharge, or quote from the top of their range to cover a 100-mile round trip. Local Pueblo West pricing for the same load usually lands 20–30% below a Springs quote — roughly $180 for a single item, $300–$450 for a half truck, and $500–$700 full. If you got a Springs number and it felt high, distance is usually why.

Why the Colorado Springs Number Isn't Your Number

Pricing in this industry is driven by three things: how much room your stuff takes in the truck, how heavy it is at the scale, and how far the truck has to drive to get to you and then to a disposal site.

That third factor is what makes "junk removal Colorado Springs cost" a misleading benchmark for anyone in Pueblo West. A Springs crew running down I-25 to Pueblo West burns roughly 100 miles round trip. A loaded box truck gets 8–10 mpg, so that's $35–$60 in diesel before anyone touches a couch. It also burns about two hours of crew time that could have serviced two more jobs inside Colorado Springs. Nobody eats that cost — it gets priced in, or the job gets declined.

The disposal side works the same way. A Springs company hauling from Pueblo West has to either drive your load back north to an El Paso County facility or figure out Pueblo County's landfill on a route they don't run. Local crews already have the account, the gate hours, and the scale ticket routine. Tipping fees in this region generally run $40–$70 per ton for mixed municipal waste, and rates change — confirm current fees with the facility before you assume a number.

What Actually Sets the Price: Volume, Weight, and Access

The advertised "full truck" is roughly 15 cubic yards, or about the bed of a standard dump trailer filled level plus a bit. Here's what that means in real terms:

  • 1/8 truck (~2 cubic yards): a mattress and box spring plus two nightstands. $120–$180.
  • 1/4 truck (~4 cubic yards): a sectional sofa, or a two-car garage's worth of boxes. $220–$320.
  • 1/2 truck (~7.5 cubic yards): a typical [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) with shelving, old paint cans, and a lawnmower. $300–$450.
  • 3/4 truck (~11 cubic yards): a full apartment's furniture. $420–$580.
  • Full truck (~15 cubic yards): a small [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) or a shed's contents. $500–$700.

Weight overrides volume on certain materials. A single cubic yard of concrete weighs about 4,000 pounds — two tons. Fill a truck with broken patio slab and you've got 30 tons of theoretical capacity against a truck rated for maybe 5. That's why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is quoted by weight, not by how much room it takes, and why a half-truck of concrete costs more than a full truck of cardboard.

Then there are item-specific disposal fees, which exist at essentially every company:

  • Mattresses: $30–$75 each, because most transfer stations charge a separate mattress fee.
  • Refrigerators, freezers, AC units: $25–$50 for certified refrigerant recovery, which is federally required.
  • Tires: $8–$25 each depending on size; ag and truck tires run higher.
  • CRT televisions and monitors: $25–$60 each for e-waste handling.
  • Paint, solvents, propane tanks, chemicals: frequently refused outright — these need a household hazardous waste event, not a junk truck.

The Pueblo West Factors That Change a Quote

Pueblo West isn't Colorado Springs geographically, and a few local realities move pricing in ways a Springs-focused article won't mention.

Acreage lots and long driveways. A lot of Pueblo West sits on one-acre-plus parcels with 150-foot gravel drives. If a truck can back to within 20 feet of the pile, you're at the low end of the range. If the crew is carrying couches 200 feet uphill from a back lot, expect $50–$125 more for the labor.

Wind and burn restrictions. High-wind days in this corridor regularly shut down open burning, and Pueblo County fire restrictions can run for weeks in summer. That pushes people toward hauling brush instead of burning it, and [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) demand spikes right along with it — book a week out in June and July.

Well and septic infrastructure. Buried tanks, old pump houses, and abandoned leach field debris turn a "just haul this pile" job into equipment work. That's a different service line and a different rate — usually $125–$200 per hour for [skid steer work](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo), not a per-load price.

Concrete and rock everywhere. Decomposed granite landscaping, old rock beds, and slab from 1970s-era pads are common here. See the weight math above.

> A quote that doesn't ask about your driveway, your item list, or whether anything is concrete isn't a quote. It's a placeholder that will change when the truck arrives.

When You Should Not Hire Us — or Anyone

This is the part most pricing pages skip. There are several situations where paying a junk removal company is the wrong call, and you should know them before you call.

You have one or two items and a truck-owning friend. A single mattress at $120–$180 is real money for a 15-minute job. If you own or can borrow a pickup, a self-haul to the Pueblo County landfill will cost you a gate fee and an hour of your Saturday. Our price buys convenience and disposal compliance, not magic. For a lone [mattress](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo), self-haul is often the honest answer.

Your load is genuinely large and you have time. A 10-yard dumpster rental runs roughly $300–$450 for a 7-day period in this area. If you're doing a slow multi-weekend cleanout and you have a spot to park a container, that beats three separate truck loads at $400+ each. We do full-service work; if your project is "fill it gradually," a container wins on cost.

Your stuff is worth money. Solid-wood furniture, working appliances under 8 years old, and decent tools sell. A working washer/dryer pair can fetch $150–$300 locally. Paying us $180 to remove something you could sell for $200 is a $380 swing in the wrong direction. Try Facebook Marketplace first; call us for what doesn't sell.

You need a free estimate to satisfy an insurance or probate requirement, but not the work. We'll do it, but be upfront. Sending a crew on a 30-minute drive for a paperwork exercise is a cost, and honest scheduling beats a wasted trip for both of us.

You're expecting a firm price over the phone with no photos. Any company that gives you a hard number sight-unseen on a mixed load is guessing. We give ranges by phone and lock the price on site before we load. If a fixed phone quote matters more to you than accuracy, we're the wrong fit — and the company that gave you one will likely revise it in your driveway anyway.

Hazardous material, asbestos, or biohazard. We refuse it, and so should anyone else without the license. Asbestos abatement in Colorado requires a certified contractor and costs $5–$15 per square foot — an order of magnitude above junk removal rates. Anyone who agrees to haul suspect popcorn ceiling or old pipe wrap in a junk truck is doing something illegal.

Failure modes worth naming: trucks fill faster than customers expect, so a "one load" job becomes two about 1 in 5 times on hoarding and estate work; weather closes gravel access after heavy rain; and same-day service in peak season (May through September) is often unavailable regardless of what you're willing to pay.

How to Compare Quotes Without Getting Burned

  • Ask for the price by truck fraction, not "per job," and ask what fraction they're estimating from your photos.
  • Ask whether disposal fees, fuel, and labor are included or added after.
  • Ask specifically about travel charges to Pueblo West — this is where Springs-based quotes quietly inflate.
  • Get the item-specific fees in writing: mattresses, appliances, tires, TVs.
  • Confirm they're licensed and insured and will show you the certificate. A crew damaging your drywall on the way out is your problem otherwise.
