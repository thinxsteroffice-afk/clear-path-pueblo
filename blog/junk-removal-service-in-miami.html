JUNK REMOVAL SERVICE IN MIAMI?

If you searched "junk removal service Miami," we're the wrong company. ClearPath Junk Removal is based in Pueblo West, Colorado, and Miami is about 1,950 driving miles away. We don't haul in Florida, and we don't refer jobs there. This page is for two kinds of readers. If you're in Miami, the next section covers what to check before you hire anyone, including the free county option many people skip. If you're in Pueblo West or Pueblo County and the search engine mixed up your location, the rest of the page explains how full-service junk removal works here: what it costs, how long it takes, and when you shouldn't pay for it.

If You're Actually in Miami: Check This Before Calling Anyone

We can't take your job, but here's what we'd tell a friend in Miami-Dade:

  • Check the county's bulky waste pickup first. Miami-Dade's Department of Solid Waste Management has offered eligible households two free bulky waste pickups per year, up to 25 cubic yards each. That's more than one full junk truck. Call 311 or check the county site to see whether your address qualifies, because many incorporated cities run their own programs.
  • Ask whether the quote is by volume or by the hour. Volume pricing (what fraction of the truck you fill) is easier to check against what you see. Hourly quotes reward slow crews.
  • Ask where the load goes. A licensed hauler should name the transfer station or landfill without hesitating.
  • Get the price before the crew starts lifting. A crew that won't quote until everything is on the truck has already given itself the leverage.

That's all we have for Miami. Everything below is about Colorado.

How Full-Service Junk Removal Works in Pueblo West

Full-service junk removal means a crew comes to your property, carries the items out, loads them, and takes them away. You don't lift anything, rent anything, or drive to the landfill. On a typical Pueblo West job:

1. You call or send photos, and we give a price range based on volume. 2. We schedule a window, often within 24–48 hours on weekdays. 3. The crew arrives, looks at the pile, and confirms the price before loading. 4. Most residential loads take 30–90 minutes from arrival to drive-off. 5. Items that can be donated or recycled get separated, and the rest goes to disposal.

Pueblo West has no single city trash department. Most homes subscribe to a private hauler, and those weekly carts aren't built for a sleeper sofa, a dead chest freezer, or 300 square feet of torn-out carpet. That's the gap we fill. What Junk Removal Typically Costs Here

We'd rather give you real ranges than "call for a quote." These are typical prices for full-service junk removal in southern Colorado. Your quote depends on volume, weight, and access.

  • Single bulky item (couch, recliner, mattress): typically $75–$150
  • Quarter truckload (a room's worth of furniture): typically $150–$275
  • Half truckload (a garage corner or a small move-out): typically $275–$425
  • Full truckload (about 15 cubic yards, roughly 6 pickup beds): typically $450–$700
  • Heavy material (concrete, dirt, roofing shingles) is priced by weight, because a load of broken concrete can top 2 tons in a space that would hold a few hundred pounds of furniture.

Things that push a price up:

  • Items on a second floor, or anything that needs disassembly
  • Appliances that held refrigerant. Federal EPA Section 608 rules require refrigerant recovery from fridges, freezers, and AC units before disposal. See our [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) page.
  • TVs and computer monitors. Colorado has banned electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013, so they go to certified e-waste recyclers, and that costs more than dumping.
  • Hot tubs, which usually need cutting apart and a 2–3 person crew. See [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo).

> The cheapest junk removal job is the one where you know the price before anyone touches the pile. If a hauler won't commit to a number on-site, keep looking.

When Junk Removal Is NOT Worth It (and Who Shouldn't Hire Us)

This section loses us some business, but you're better off knowing it before you call.

You have one item and a free option. If your trash hauler offers a bulk pickup, or a neighbor has a truck and an afternoon, paying $75–$150 for one recliner isn't a great deal. Use the free option.

You own a pickup and the items are light. If it's a bed's worth of bagged clothes and a broken bookshelf, and you're fine doing a 30–45 minute round trip to the landfill, self-hauling will cost you the gate fee plus gas. That's almost always less than our minimum. You're paying us for labor, speed, and your back. If none of those matter to you, keep your money.

Your project lasts weeks, not hours. For a kitchen gut or a multi-week remodel where debris piles up slowly, a roll-off dumpster often wins. A 10–20 yard dumpster for 7–14 days usually runs $350–$600 in this region and sits in your driveway while you fill it at your own pace. Calling us three separate times over a month will usually cost more. (If your remodel produces one big pile at the end, we're competitive. See [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo).)

You want to sell the stuff. If that dining set is worth $400 on Facebook Marketplace, list it first. We donate usable items when we can, but we're not a consignment service and we won't pay you for your belongings.

You have hazardous materials. We don't take:

  • Liquid paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, or fuel
  • Asbestos-containing materials (common in some pre-1980 flooring, siding, and popcorn ceilings)
  • Medical waste or sharps
  • Propane tanks that aren't fully purged

Those need a household hazardous waste drop-off or a licensed abatement contractor. Any junk hauler who says they'll take asbestos tile in a regular load is putting you and themselves at legal risk.

Failure modes we've seen, including from other haulers:

  • Underestimated volume. Photos make piles look smaller. A "quarter load" that turns out to be half a load means a higher bill. Send photos from several angles and include something for scale.
  • Access problems. A gate too narrow for the truck, a steep gravel drive after rain, or an HOA rule against trucks before 8 a.m. can delay or reprice a job. Tell us in advance.
  • Mixed loads with hidden weight. A box of "old papers" that's really full of tile scraps changes the price. We'd rather know up front than surprise you.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in 5 Minutes

You'll get a more accurate number on the phone if you have these ready:

1. Photos. 3–5 wide shots of the pile, plus close-ups of anything heavy or unusual. 2. Location of the items. Garage floor, basement, backyard, or second story. 3. Access notes. Gate width, driveway surface, stairs, and any HOA rules. 4. Special items. Anything with refrigerant, any screens or electronics, any tires, and anything that might be hazardous. 5. Your deadline. A closing date, a lease end, or an estate sale gives us something to plan around.

With that, a phone quote is usually within 10–15% of the final price. The on-site price is the one you pay, and we confirm it before loading.
