What the price actually depends on (and why nobody can quote this over the phone)

Hoarding pricing is not junk pricing. Regular [junk removal in Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) is volume math: you point, we load, we charge by how much of the trailer you fill. Hoarding is labor math, and the labor is dominated by one variable — how much sorting has to happen before anything gets carried out.

Here's the spread we see on real Pueblo West jobs:

  • No sorting, everything goes. A crew of 3 moves roughly 10 to 14 cubic yards per hour out of an accessible ground-floor room. This is the cheap end.
  • Light sorting (pull the obvious keepsakes, check for documents). Throughput drops to about 5 to 7 cubic yards per hour.
  • Item-by-item sorting with a family member present, opening boxes, checking envelopes for cash and titles. Now you're at 1.5 to 3 cubic yards per hour — a 4x to 6x difference in cost for the exact same pile.
  • Compacted floor layers. Material that's been sitting 8+ years compresses and adheres. Removing an 18-inch layer off a 1,200 sq ft floor can add 6 to 10 labor hours by itself.
  • Access. A Pueblo West half-acre lot with a straight driveway to the door is fast. A back bedroom down a blocked hallway with one 34-inch doorway adds 20 to 40% to labor.

Disposal is the second line item. County transfer station tipping runs on weight, and a packed household averages 180 to 260 pounds per cubic yard — a mid-size hoarding house is commonly 4 to 8 tons. That's real money before a single hour of labor is billed, which is why a "$99 truckload" ad and a hoarding cleanout have nothing to do with each other.

> If a company gives you a firm hoarding price sight-unseen over the phone, they are either lowballing to get in the door or they're a lead broker who will never touch your house.

When this is NOT worth hiring out — read this part

We would rather lose the job than take money for the wrong work. Here's where hiring a hoarding cleanout company is a bad decision:

  • The person living there hasn't agreed to it. This is the number one failure mode, and it is not rare — we'd estimate 1 in 4 of the calls we get are from an adult child or sibling hoping we'll show up and force the issue while Mom is at an appointment. Do not do this. Forced cleanouts against a resident's will have a documented pattern of rapid re-accumulation, and they can destroy the family relationship permanently. If the resident is competent and says no, the answer is no. Save your money and start with a therapist who treats hoarding disorder, not a trailer.
  • You're under $600 of actual material. If it's one bedroom, a closet, and a stack of boxes, you're better off renting a truck for $40 plus mileage and paying the transfer station gate fee yourself. We'll say so on the estimate. Same logic for a single load of furniture — [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) is a much cheaper line item than a hoarding crew.
  • There's active biohazard. Human waste beyond an isolated incident, decomposition, sharps volume, black mold across framing, or a structure with more than 20 or 30 animals living in it — that is remediation work requiring IICRC-certified technicians, negative air, and a different insurance policy. We are not a biohazard remediation firm. We'll walk it, tell you what we see, and hand you names. Paying a junk crew to do remediation work gets you a house that looks clean and still fails an inspection.
  • You're expecting us to find and preserve everything valuable. We can sort carefully at $85 to $110 per crew-hour, and we do find cash, deeds, and jewelry — but on a house with 300 boxes, careful sorting can add 25 to 40 hours. If the estate has real value in it, hire an estate liquidator *first*, then call us for the remainder. That's how [estate cleanouts](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) should sequence.
  • You need it done tomorrow for a court date. A 1,500 sq ft hoarding home is realistically 2 to 4 days with a 3-person crew. If you have a Pueblo West Metro District nuisance notice with a 30-day compliance window, call at day 3, not day 27. Rushed jobs cost 15 to 30% more and sort worse.
  • You think insurance will cover it. Standard Colorado homeowners policies do not cover hoarding cleanup as a loss. Coverage generally only appears when there's a covered peril underneath — a burst pipe, a fire. Budget as if you're paying out of pocket, because you probably are.

Pueblo West specifics most companies won't mention

Local conditions change the job here in ways a Front Range franchise doesn't account for.

Outbuildings are the hidden cost. Half-acre and one-acre lots out here mean detached garages, sheds, carports, and often two or three vehicles' worth of accumulation outside the house. On more than half our Pueblo West hoarding jobs, the exterior volume exceeds the interior volume. That's a separate estimate line, and it's why a photo of the living room tells us almost nothing. If the shed itself is done, that becomes [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).

Rodents. Packrat and deer mouse nesting in stored material is common in Pueblo County outbuildings, and dried rodent droppings are a genuine hantavirus concern in this part of Colorado. Our crews wet-wipe rather than sweep, run N95s minimum, and we will not dry-sweep a nested shed floor. If a crew shows up to your property with a push broom and no respirators, that's a bad sign.

Wind and dust. Loading an open trailer on a 35 mph gust day scatters your neighbor's yard with insulation and paper. We tarp between loads. It costs us 10 to 15 minutes per load and it's not optional.

Covenants. Pueblo West Metro District enforces property maintenance standards, and the practical trigger is usually visible outdoor storage, not interior conditions. If a notice is what's driving your timeline, the exterior is what gets you compliant — often for $1,400 to $3,000 — and the interior can be phased later. We'll tell you that even though phasing means we bill less this month.
