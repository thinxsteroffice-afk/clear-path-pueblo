YARD DEBRIS REMOVAL COST NEAR ME

Most Pueblo West yard debris removal jobs run $150 to $450, with the typical single-load cleanup — branches, tumbleweeds, hedge trimmings, a few bags of leaves — landing around $175 to $265. Priced by volume, expect roughly $95 for a minimum load (about a pickup bed's worth), $175–$225 for a quarter truck, $250–$300 for a half truck, and $375–$450 for a full 15-cubic-yard load. Big acreage clears — a full tumbleweed fence line, a dead elm taken down in sections, five years of dead juniper — commonly run $600 to $1,400 because they take multiple loads. Same-day and next-day service in Pueblo West usually adds nothing; a weekend or after-storm rush call can add 10–20%.

Now the details that actually change your number.

What Pueblo West Yard Debris Removal Actually Costs in 2026

Nearly every hauler in Pueblo County — us included — prices yard waste by volume in the truck, not by weight or by the hour. A standard dump trailer or dump truck holds 12 to 16 cubic yards. That's the unit everything gets measured against.

Realistic Pueblo West examples:

  • Storm branch pickup after a March wind event (one downed Siberian elm limb, 8–10 ft, plus scattered debris): $95–$150, usually 20–30 minutes on site.
  • Tumbleweed clearing along 200 ft of fence line: $225–$400. Tumbleweeds are almost pure air — 200 feet of packed fence fills a truck fast.
  • Full-yard cleanup on a half-acre lot (leaves, dead shrubs, trimmings, old landscape timbers): $350–$550.
  • Dead tree removed in sections and hauled (you cut, we haul): $300–$700 depending on trunk diameter; rounds over 18 inches get heavy and slow.
  • Landscape demo — rock, weed barrier, dead junipers over ~500 sq ft: $700–$1,500. Rock is where the price jumps, and it's usually quoted separately as [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).

Most crews charge a $95–$120 minimum no matter how small the pile, because the drive out, the load, and the trip to the transfer station cost the same whether the trailer is a tenth full or half full.

Why Pueblo West Prices Run Different From Pueblo City

Two things separate Pueblo West from the city side, and both show up in the quote.

Lot size. Pueblo West lots commonly run 1 to 2.5 acres versus a 6,000–8,000 sq ft city lot. A "normal yard cleanup" out here is frequently 3 to 4 times the volume of the same job on Northern Avenue. When a national booking service quotes you "$199 for a yard cleanup," they're pricing a city lot and they've never seen your back acre.

Drive time. The Pueblo County landfill and transfer facilities sit on the far side of town. From most Pueblo West addresses that's 20–30 minutes each way, so every dump run burns roughly an hour of round-trip time plus fuel and tipping fees. A three-load job isn't three times the labor — it's three times the labor *plus three hours of driving*. That's why per-load pricing gets slightly cheaper as loads stack up, and why one big scheduled cleanup beats four small calls.

> The single biggest cost driver in Pueblo West isn't your debris. It's how many separate trips it takes to move it.

The Volume-vs-Weight Problem Nobody Explains

Here's the piece most cost articles skip, and it determines whether hiring out is smart or wasteful.

Landfills charge by the ton (Pueblo-area self-haul disposal generally runs in the $30–$50 per ton range with a $25–$30 minimum — confirm current rates before you go, they change). Haulers charge by the cubic yard. Those two units disagree wildly depending on what you have:

  • Tumbleweeds, dry brush, leaves: enormous volume, almost no weight. A full 15-yard truck of tumbleweeds might weigh 300–500 lbs and cost about $15 in tipping fees. You are paying almost entirely for labor, space, and drive time.
  • Green elm and cottonwood cut in spring: heavy with water — a single truckload can hit 1.5 to 2 tons.
  • Sod, dirt, and rock: the killer. One cubic yard of wet soil weighs roughly 2,700 lbs. A trailer legally maxes out on weight long before it looks full, which is why dirt and rock get quoted separately or handled with [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) rather than hand-loading.

Practical takeaway: if your pile is light and bulky, hiring out is good value. If it's heavy and compact, the labor savings shrink and self-hauling gets more competitive.

When Yard Debris Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

This is the part that costs us jobs, and it should. Do not hire a hauler in these situations:

  • You have a truck, a trailer, and one free Saturday, and the pile is small. Two pickup loads of brush, self-hauled, costs maybe $25–$35 in tipping fees and $15 in gas. Paying $175 for that is paying $130 for four hours you may not mind spending. If your time is genuinely free that weekend, keep the money.
  • Your debris is compostable and you have room. Leaves, grass, and small trimmings break down. A back-corner pile costs $0. It's ugly for a season and worth it if your budget is tight.
  • You're doing a full landscape renovation in the next 60 days. Don't pay twice. Stage everything and pay for one large removal at the end — usually $200–$400 cheaper than two separate calls.
  • You're a landlord with a one-time small tenant mess. If it's two bags and a broken chair, a $95 minimum charge is a poor use of money. Bundle it with an actual [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or wait until you have a real pile.
  • You're expecting demolition or tree work included. Junk haulers haul. We do not climb trees, we do not run chainsaws through standing timber, and we're not licensed arborists. If a tree is still standing, you need a tree service first — and you'll pay both. Budget $400–$1,800 for removal of a mature tree in Pueblo West *before* any hauling.

Failure modes and limits, plainly stated:

  • Weather kills schedules. During March–May windstorms — sustained gusts over 40 mph are routine here — cleanup demand spikes and lead times stretch from same-day to 3–7 days. If you need it gone this afternoon in April, you may not get it.
  • Volume estimates over the phone are guesses. A homeowner's "small pile" is wrong more often than not — usually by 30–50% low. Any hauler who gives you a firm price sight-unseen without photos is either padding it or planning to renegotiate in your driveway.
  • Not everything can be taken. Chemically treated railroad ties, tires, and anything soaked in fuel or pesticide have separate disposal paths and separate costs. Dirt and rock are usually excluded from "yard debris" pricing outright.
  • Frozen ground and snow-buried piles are a no. Between December and February, debris frozen into the ground either can't be extracted or takes twice as long and costs accordingly.
  • Noxious weed loads have rules. Russian thistle and goathead go to the landfill, not the compost stream, and loads must be tarped in transit under Colorado's covered-load requirements. An untarped load reseeding your neighbor's field is a real outcome. Open burning is not a shortcut — Pueblo County requires a permit and issues fire restrictions most summers.

What Raises Your Quote — and How to Cut It

Controllable factors, roughly in order of impact:

  • Distance from the pile to the truck. Debris more than 75 feet from a driveway, or behind a locked gate, adds 15–25%. Dragging brush across an acre is the job.
  • Whether it's pre-cut. Branches cut to 4 feet or shorter stack far denser than whole limbs. Doing this yourself can shave 20–30% off a brush load, because it's the difference between one load and two.
  • Piled vs. scattered. A consolidated pile in one spot cuts on-site time by 30–45 minutes on a typical half-acre cleanup.
  • Mixed loads. Yard waste mixed with an old mattress, a broken swing set, or fencing changes the disposal path and the price. Those get quoted as separate line items — see [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) versus [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo).

Compare Before You Book: DIY, Dumpster, or Hauler

For a medium half-acre cleanup in Pueblo West:

  • Self-haul, two pickup loads: roughly $50–$80 out of pocket, 5–7 hours of your time, plus wear on your truck.
  • 10-yard dumpster rental, one week: commonly $350–$500 delivered in the Pueblo area, and you still load it yourself. Worth it if you're working over multiple days.
  • Full-service hauling: $350–$550, 60–90 minutes of crew time, zero labor from you.

The dumpster only wins when the work spans several days. For a single-day cleanout, it's usually the worst of both worlds — you pay near hauler prices and still do the lifting.

Getting an Accurate Number in Under Ten Minutes

Text or email 3–4 photos: one wide shot showing the whole pile, one close-up for scale (stand next to it), one of the path from the pile to the driveway, and one of any gate or obstacle. With those, a real quote takes about five minutes and holds — no driveway surprises.

Ask any hauler these four things before booking: Is the price all-in including disposal and labor? What's the minimum charge? What won't you take? And will the price change when you arrive?
