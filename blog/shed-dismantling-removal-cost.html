SHED DISMANTLING & REMOVAL COST

Shed dismantling and removal in Pueblo West typically runs $350 to $2,200, and most jobs land between $550 and $1,100. A basic 8x10 wood or vinyl shed on a gravel or skid base is usually $350–$650. A 10x12 to 12x16 shed with a wood floor and shingled roof runs $650–$1,200. Big barn-style or two-story sheds, anything with electrical run to it, or structures over 200 sq ft push $1,300–$2,200. Metal utility sheds are the cheapest at $250–$450 because they come apart fast and the steel has scrap value. Breaking out and hauling a concrete pad underneath adds $400–$1,500 on top. Price is driven by three things: square footage, what the shed sits on, and whether a truck and trailer can get within 40 feet of it.

What Actually Drives the Price on a Pueblo West Lot

Most quotes people get over the phone are wrong because the caller describes the shed and not the site. On a typical one-acre Pueblo West lot off Purcell, McCulloch, or Joe Martinez, access is usually good — that works in your favor. But here's what moves the number:

  • Debris volume, not footprint. A 10x12 wood shed with plywood floor, framing, and asphalt shingles generates roughly 1.5 to 2.5 tons of material. Disposal at the transfer station is billed by weight, generally in the $45–$70 per ton range plus a minimum charge, and shingles often get assessed at a higher rate than clean wood.
  • The base. A shed on 4x4 skids or gravel: no extra cost. A shed on a 4-inch concrete pad: budget $400–$800 to break, load, and haul. A 6-inch pad with rebar or wire mesh: $800–$1,500, and it needs a jackhammer or a breaker attachment rather than a sledge.
  • Gate width and slope. If we can back a dump trailer to within 40 feet, a 10x12 comes down in about 2 to 3 hours. If everything has to be hand-carried 150 feet around a house through a 36-inch gate, the same shed takes 5 to 6 hours and the price goes up roughly 25–35%.
  • Anchoring. This is the Pueblo West detail nobody accounts for. Because of the wind we get coming off the mesa, a lot of sheds out here are strapped down with auger anchors or 18–24 inch ground stakes, and older ones are bolted to the pad with expansion anchors. Pulling those takes time, and if the shed is bolted down and you try to tip it, you tear the floor off and leave the frame.
  • What's inside. Quoted prices assume an empty shed. Half a garage worth of paint cans, tires, and old lawn equipment inside can add $150–$400, and some of it — paint, fuel, propane, tires — can't ride in the same load as construction debris.

> The shed is rarely the expensive part. The slab under it, the fence it's leaning against, and the 30 years of stuff inside it are where the money goes.

Realistic Timeframes

A standard single-story shed under 160 sq ft is a half-day job — two people, 2 to 4 hours, including load-out and site rake. Add the concrete pad and you're at a full day, 6 to 8 hours. Scheduling in Pueblo West is usually 24 to 72 hours out in spring and fall, longer in the two weeks after a windstorm when half of these calls are damaged structures.

Winter matters here. Between late November and early March, frozen ground makes anchor removal harder, and if there's snow on the roof, that's another 200–400 lbs you're paying to move. If your shed isn't a hazard, waiting for a dry week in March or October is the cheaper play — those are the shoulder months when crews aren't booked solid.

DIY vs. Hiring Out: The Honest Math

A 10x12 wood shed is genuinely a DIY-able weekend project if you're able-bodied and have help. Here's what it actually costs you:

  • 15-yard roll-off dumpster: $375–$600 for a 7-day rental in the Pueblo area, and a 10x12 shed will fill most of it.
  • Reciprocating saw blades: $25–$40 in demolition blades, because nail-embedded lumber destroys them.
  • Or, pickup trips: roughly 3 to 5 round trips to the transfer station at about 1,000–1,400 lbs per load, plus fuel and per-load minimum fees.
  • Your time: 8 to 14 hours for two people, plus a full day of cleanup for nails, staples, and shingle grit in the grass.

Net out, DIY on a small wood shed saves you maybe $150–$300 over a professional crew. On a metal shed, DIY saves more. On anything with a concrete pad, DIY usually costs more once you rent a breaker ($85–$130/day) and discover a 4-inch pad weighs about 1,800 lbs per 100 sq ft — a 10x12 slab is roughly 2,100 lbs of concrete you now have to lift into a truck by hand. Most half-ton pickups are over their payload with a third of that slab in the bed.

When Shed Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

This is the part that costs us jobs, and we'd rather lose one than have you spend $900 badly.

Don't pay us if the shed is structurally fine and you just don't like it. A sound 10x12 shed has real resale value. Listed locally for $400–$900, sheds move — and buyers with trailers and jacks will frequently move it themselves for free just to get it. You go from spending $700 to collecting $500. That's a $1,200 swing for the cost of taking six photos. Same goes for donation: a shed in decent shape may be worth more to a neighbor with livestock than to a landfill.

Don't pay for full removal if you only need it gone from where it is. Sliding an intact shed 60 feet with jacks, pipe rollers, and a skid steer often runs $250–$450 — considerably less than demo plus disposal. If your issue is a setback, a fence line, or a driveway expansion, relocation is the cheaper answer. Ask about it before you ask for a quote to demolish.

Don't hire this out if it's a small metal shed and you own a socket set. An 8x8 sheet-metal shed is 30 to 60 bolts. Two people, 90 minutes, and the steel panels are scrap — local yards pay in the neighborhood of $70–$140 per ton for light steel, which won't make you rich but will cover lunch. Paying $300 for that is paying for convenience, and you should know that's what you're buying.

Where we're limited, plainly:

  • Asbestos. Sheds built or re-roofed before the mid-1980s can have asbestos-cement siding or roofing. If suspect material is present, we stop. Colorado requires certified abatement for regulated asbestos work, testing runs about $75–$250 for a few samples, and abatement can add $1,500–$4,000+. No junk removal company can legally shortcut that, and any that offers to is a liability you're inheriting.
  • Permits and utilities. Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, so demolition of a permitted accessory structure — and disconnection of any electrical service run to it — goes through the Pueblo Regional Building Department. That's your responsibility as the property owner, not ours. Sheds with power are also a common source of surprise: a buried, un-permitted extension circuit from the house is a stop-work item until an electrician kills it.
  • Rodents. Long-vacant sheds out here frequently have deer mouse nests. Dry-sweeping droppings aerosolizes them, which is a genuine hantavirus concern in southern Colorado. We wet-down and use respirators; if you DIY, do the same, and don't shop-vac it.
  • Fences, retaining walls, and shared structures. If your shed wall is doing structural work for a fence or a slope, removing it creates a new problem. We'll flag it, but repairing it is a different trade.
  • We don't do foundations beyond breakout. Removing a slab leaves a bare, compacted rectangle. Backfill, grading, and reseeding are separate — see [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) if you need the ground finished, not just cleared.

How to Get a Quote That Doesn't Change on Site

Photos beat descriptions. Send: one wide shot showing the path from the street to the shed, one of the base where the wall meets the ground, one of the roof, and one of the interior. Then tell us the outside dimensions and whether there's power. That's enough for a firm number about 90% of the time.

Ask any company you call three specific questions: Is disposal included or billed separately after the fact? Is the concrete pad in the quote or excluded? Do you rake and magnet-sweep for nails when you're done? Vague answers on any of those are where a $600 quote becomes a $950 invoice.
