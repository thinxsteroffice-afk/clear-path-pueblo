APPLIANCE REMOVAL ST ANDREWS: COSTS

If you searched "appliance removal St Andrews" and you're in Pueblo West, here's the short answer. Getting one old refrigerator, washer, dryer or range hauled away usually costs $75–$150. Two or three appliances on the same trip usually run $150–$300. Most pickups take 15–30 minutes on site and can be booked within 24–72 hours. Anything with refrigerant in it, like a fridge, freezer, window AC unit or dehumidifier, has to have that refrigerant recovered before the metal can be scrapped. That's a federal requirement under Section 608 of the Clean Air Act. If you're searching from St Andrews, Scotland, St. Andrews, Manitoba, or some other St Andrews outside southern Colorado, we're not your company, and the rest of this page won't help you much.

The rest of this article is for Pueblo West homeowners. It covers what appliance removal really costs, what you can do yourself for free, and when paying a hauler is a waste of money.

What Appliance Removal Costs in Pueblo West

Prices depend on three things: how heavy the item is, how hard it is to reach, and whether it needs special handling. These are typical ranges for our area:

  • Refrigerator or chest freezer: $85–$150. These weigh 250–350 lbs, and refrigerant recovery adds handling.
  • Washer or dryer: $75–$110 each, or $130–$190 for a pair
  • Electric or gas range: $75–$120. A gas range has to be shut off and capped first.
  • Dishwasher (already disconnected): $60–$90
  • Water heater (40–50 gallon): $90–$150. Expect the high end if it's still full of sediment.
  • Window AC unit or dehumidifier: $40–$75

Stairs add $20–$50 per item. That matters in Pueblo West because so many homes have walkout basements, and a fridge going up 12 steps is a two-person job with a stair dolly. A long carry from a back shed or a detached garage across a gravel lot can add a similar amount.

If you're also clearing out a garage or a whole room, it's usually cheaper to price everything by truckload than appliance by appliance. When Appliance Removal Is NOT Worth Paying For

This section will cost us some jobs, and that's fine. Here are the situations where you shouldn't hire us or anyone else.

You're buying a new appliance anyway. Big-box retailers and most local appliance dealers will haul off your old unit when they deliver the new one. It's often free or under $30. That's almost always cheaper than a separate pickup. The catch is that most delivery crews won't disconnect anything. If the old washer is still plumbed in or the gas range is still connected, they can refuse to take it, so disconnect it the night before.

You have a truck, a dolly and one healthy friend. A washer, dryer or range can go to a scrap metal recycler, and some yards in Pueblo will pay you a few dollars for clean steel instead of charging you. You might net $5–$20 for a washer. That's not much, but it beats paying $90. Refrigerators are harder because many scrap yards won't take them unless the refrigerant has been recovered and the unit is tagged. Call ahead before you load one.

The appliance still works and is less than 8–10 years old. A working fridge, washer or dryer under 10 years old will often sell for $50–$200 on local marketplace listings, and buyers will usually haul it themselves. Habitat for Humanity ReStores and some local charities also take working appliances. Policies change, so call before you drive.

Your utility runs a recycling rebate. Some Colorado utilities have offered $35–$75 rebates for picking up old, working second fridges and freezers. Black Hills Energy serves most of Pueblo West. Check whether a program is running before you pay anyone to take a working fridge. Programs come and go, and we can't promise one exists right now.

It's one small item. Paying $40–$75 to have a hauler take away a microwave or a countertop ice maker rarely makes sense. Those can often go to an e-waste drop-off or out with regular trash, depending on your hauler's rules.

> A hauler is worth it when the problem is weight, stairs, time or refrigerant. If none of those apply, you probably don't need us.

### Where hauling services fall short

Even when hiring someone does make sense, there are real limits:

  • We don't disconnect gas lines. No reputable junk hauler should. Have a licensed plumber or your gas provider shut off and cap a gas range or dryer line. That typically costs $75–$150, and it can be more than the removal itself.
  • We don't drain water heaters that won't drain. If the valve is seized or broken, that's a plumbing job.
  • We can't take appliances full of food or liquid. A freezer packed with spoiled meat after a power outage has to be emptied first. That's partly about smell and partly about weight. A full chest freezer can top 500 lbs.
  • Same-day isn't guaranteed. In late spring and around the end-of-month move-out rush, wait times can stretch to 3–5 days.

The Refrigerant Rule Most People Don't Know About

Here's something most appliance removal pages skip. Under EPA rules, anyone disposing of a refrigerator, freezer, window AC, dehumidifier or water cooler has to make sure the refrigerant is recovered by a certified technician before the appliance is scrapped or landfilled. Older units, roughly anything made before the mid-1990s, may hold R-12 (Freon), which is an ozone-depleting refrigerant. Newer units usually hold R-134a or R-600a, a hydrocarbon. R-600a is flammable.

In practice this means three things:

1. You can't just leave a fridge at the curb. Most residential trash haulers won't take one, and many charge a separate bulky-item fee if they do. 2. Cutting the lines yourself is a bad idea. Venting refrigerant on purpose is illegal, and the newer hydrocarbon refrigerants are a fire risk in a closed garage. 3. Ask any hauler what happens to the fridge after it leaves your house. A legitimate answer names a recycler or scrap processor that handles refrigerant recovery. "We take care of it" is not an answer.

When we haul a refrigerant-bearing appliance, it goes to a facility that recovers the refrigerant before the metal is processed. That's part of why fridges cost $10–$40 more than a washer of similar size.

How to Prep Before Pickup (and Save $20–$50)

A little prep shortens the job and keeps the price at the low end of the range:

  • Defrost and empty fridges and freezers 24 hours ahead. Leave the doors propped open so they dry out and don't grow mold.
  • Disconnect the water. Shut off the supply valves behind washers and ice-maker fridges, and keep a towel handy. Washer hoses usually hold 1–2 quarts.
  • Unplug everything and tape the cords to the back. Then nobody trips over a cord on the stairs.
  • Clear a path. Move rugs, shoe racks and anything else in the hallway. A refrigerator needs about 30–36 inches of clearance through doorways. If it came in through the door, it can go out the same way, but sometimes the doors have to come off first.
  • Have a plumber cap any gas line before we arrive. We'll leave a gas appliance in place if it's still connected.

Pueblo West Specifics That Change the Job

Pueblo West isn't a dense suburb, and that affects appliance pickups in a few ways:

  • Lot sizes. Many properties sit on half an acre to several acres. An old freezer stored in a detached shop 200 feet from the driveway costs more to remove than one in an attached garage.
  • Gravel and dirt drives. After snow or a rare heavy rain, dirt drives can get soft. We'll sometimes carry farther rather than risk getting a loaded truck stuck. Tell us about your driveway when you book.
  • Wind. Spring gusts here regularly top 40 mph. Don't drag an appliance to the end of the drive the night before and leave the door on. That's a hazard to kids and animals, and an old latching fridge door is a real entrapment risk.
  • Older well and septic homes. Water heaters and water softeners in older Pueblo West houses are often in tight utility closets. Removing one sometimes means taking off a closet door frame. We'll tell you before we touch it.

Should You Bundle Appliances With Other Junk?

Usually, yes. Most of the cost of any pickup is the truck showing up and two people's time, not the item itself. Here's how the numbers tend to work out:

  • One fridge alone: about $85–$150
  • A fridge, an old couch and 10 bags of garage junk: often $250–$350 as a partial truckload
  • Booking those as three separate pickups: easily $400+

Quick Decision Checklist

Before you book anyone, run through these questions:

1. Am I buying a replacement? If so, use the retailer's haul-away first. 2. Does it work and is it under 10 years old? Then sell it or donate it. 3. Is it a washer, dryer or range, and do I have a truck? Then take it to a scrap yard yourself. 4. Does it contain refrigerant, is it heavy, or is it downstairs? This is where paying a hauler makes sense. 5. Is there other junk in the garage too? Bundle it all into one trip.
