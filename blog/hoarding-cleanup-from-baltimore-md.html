How a Long-Distance Hoarding Cleanout in Pueblo West Works

Most of our out-of-state clients follow the same pattern. Someone gets a call from a neighbor, a landlord, Adult Protective Services or a hospital social worker. They realize the house is in worse shape than they knew. Then they have to deal with it from 1,600 miles away.

Here's the process we use with remote families:

  • Video walkthrough first. We walk every room, the garage and any outbuildings on a live video call, so you see what we see before you agree to a price.
  • Written keep-list. You tell us what to search for and set aside: documents, photos, jewelry, firearms, military records, specific heirlooms.
  • Daily photo updates. At the end of each workday you get pictures of finished rooms and of anything we pulled aside for you to decide on.
  • Found-items box. Cash, keys, checks, prescription bottles, titles and anything with a Social Security number go into a sealed box. We don't throw those away.
  • Final walkthrough on video before we call the job done.

In hoarded homes, people regularly find cash tucked in books, coat pockets and freezer bags. Uncashed checks and savings bonds turn up too. If you skip the sorting step to save a few hours of labor, you can lose more than the job costs.

What It Costs in Pueblo West

Hoarding jobs are priced by volume and labor, not by square footage. A tidy 2,400-square-foot house can cost less to clear than a packed 900-square-foot one. These are typical Southern Colorado ranges. Your quote will be written and specific to the property.

| Clutter level (Clutter Image Rating 1–9) | Typical volume | Typical time on site | Typical cost range | |---|---|---|---| | Level 3–4: crowded rooms, paths still clear | 1–2 truckloads | 1 day | $600–$1,400 | | Level 5–6: rooms unusable, narrow goat-trails | 3–5 truckloads | 2–3 days | $1,800–$4,000 | | Level 7–9: floor-to-ceiling, blocked exits | 6–10+ truckloads | 4–7 days | $4,500–$10,000+ |

A standard junk-removal truck holds about 12–15 cubic yards, roughly the volume of a full-size pickup bed piled high four to five times over. Several things push the price toward the top of a range:

  • Pueblo West lot sizes. Many homes here sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots. The hoard often doesn't stop at the house. Sheds, shipping containers, horse shelters, dead vehicles and backyard piles can double the volume. If there's a falling-apart outbuilding too, see [shed demolition removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Heavy items mixed into soft goods. Appliances, tires, paint cans and automotive fluids have separate disposal rules, and some of them cost extra to dispose of.
  • Sorting time. A haul-everything job goes 2–3 times faster than a careful search for documents and valuables.
  • Access. Blocked doorways, sagging floors or a garage door that won't open add labor hours.

> The cheapest quote is often the one that skips sorting. If your parent hid $3,000 in a cereal box, "fast" just got expensive.

Colorado-Specific Health Risk: Hantavirus

National hoarding-cleanup pages rarely mention this, but it matters here. Deer mice in Colorado carry hantavirus, and the state has reported cases every year since the virus was identified in 1993. Hoarded homes, and especially closed-up sheds and garages on rural-edge lots like Pueblo West's, are prime deer mouse habitat. The illness, hantavirus pulmonary syndrome, kills about 35–38% of people who develop it.

The risk comes from breathing in dust from dried droppings and nesting material. That means the worst thing a well-meaning relative can do is fly in and start sweeping out the garage. Follow these rules whether or not you hire us:

  • Air the space out for at least 30 minutes before going in, with doors and windows open.
  • Never sweep or vacuum droppings. Soak them with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution (or a commercial disinfectant), let it sit 5 minutes, then wipe up with paper towels.
  • Wear gloves and at least an N95 respirator. A paper dust mask won't protect you.
  • Bag the contaminated material twice before it goes out.

Our crews follow these procedures. Rodent-heavy areas are one of the main reasons hoarding work costs more per hour than a normal [junk removal](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) job.

When This ISN'T Worth It (and Who Shouldn't Hire Us)

This section will cost us some jobs. We'd still rather you hear it now than be disappointed after you pay.

1. The house is in Baltimore. We can't drive there and won't refer you to someone we haven't vetted. Look for a Maryland company with local reviews.

2. The person still lives there and hasn't agreed. This is the biggest failure mode in hoarding cleanup. Hoarding disorder affects an estimated 2–6% of adults, and it's a recognized mental health condition, not laziness. If a family clears the home while the person who lives there objects, the result is often severe distress and a broken relationship, and the house frequently fills back up within months. We'll haul whatever you pay us to haul. But if the occupant is alive, competent and refusing, your money is better spent on a therapist who treats hoarding disorder (cognitive behavioral therapy is the established approach) before you spend it on trucks. The exception is a code enforcement or eviction deadline. Even then, slow and partial beats sudden and total.

3. There's a biohazard. If there is human waste, a decomposed body, large amounts of animal waste, used needles or blood, you need a certified biohazard or trauma remediation company, not a junk hauler. We remove belongings. We don't do biohazard decontamination, and we'll tell you to stop and call a remediation company if we find those conditions during the walkthrough.

4. There's an animal hoarding situation. If live animals are involved, especially more than a few, call Pueblo Animal Services first. Their welfare comes before the cleanup, and there may be legal steps involved.

5. The volume is small and the family is local and willing. At Clutter Image Rating 1–3, you probably don't need us. A 20-yard roll-off dumpster usually rents for $450–$650 a week in this area. Add four able-bodied relatives and a weekend and you'll pay less than our labor rate. Be honest with yourself about the "willing" and "able-bodied" parts, though.

6. The house has bigger problems than the stuff. If the roof has failed, there's black mold on the drywall or the floors are rotted through, clearing the contents may only reveal a teardown. Get a home inspector or contractor to look before you spend $5,000 emptying a house that will be bulldozed anyway. In that case the demolition contractor may haul the contents as part of their job.

7. You expect us to clean it. We remove items and leave the rooms broom-swept. We are not a deep cleaning or odor-removal service. If you want the house ready to list, plan on a separate cleaner, and possibly flooring replacement, after we leave.

Questions to Ask Any Hoarding Cleanup Company

These apply whether you hire us, someone else in Pueblo or a crew in Baltimore:

  • Do you sort, or do you just haul? Get the answer in writing.
  • What happens to cash, documents and guns you find? You want a specific procedure, not "we're careful."
  • Is the quote a flat rate or per truckload, and what triggers extra charges?
  • Are your workers insured? A crew member falling through a weak floor in a hoarded home is a real risk.
  • What will you NOT handle? Any honest company has a list, and if they say "nothing," be wary.
  • How much is donated or recycled versus landfilled? Metal, appliances and usable furniture shouldn't all go to the dump.
