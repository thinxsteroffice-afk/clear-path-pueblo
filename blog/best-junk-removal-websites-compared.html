BEST JUNK REMOVAL WEBSITES COMPARED

The best junk removal website for a Pueblo West homeowner is usually a local hauler's own site. Look for one that shows real price ranges, takes a photo quote, and names Pueblo West instead of just "Southern Colorado." After that, in order: national franchise sites like 1-800-GOT-JUNK and College Hunks (easy booking, but crews may drive in from Colorado Springs, about 45 miles away), lead marketplaces like Angi, Thumbtack, and Yelp (easy to compare, but your phone number may go to 3 to 5 companies), and free listing sites like Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, and Nextdoor (these cost nothing but only work for items someone wants). The right choice depends on what you're getting rid of. The rest of this page covers how to tell the options apart.

The four kinds of junk removal websites, ranked for Pueblo West

Most search results mix four very different kinds of sites together. Each one works differently, and so does the price you end up paying.

1. Local operator websites. This is the company that owns the truck and sends the crew. When you call, you usually reach the owner or a dispatcher who knows Pueblo West roads. There's no middleman fee added to your price. The downside is quality: some local sites are one page with a phone number and no prices, so you can't compare until you call.

2. National franchise websites. 1-800-GOT-JUNK, College Hunks Hauling Junk, and Junk King have polished online booking, 2-hour arrival windows, and set pricing by truck volume. Across the country, franchise prices often run from about $100–$150 for a minimum load to $600–$800 or more for a full truck. The catch in Pueblo West is where the franchise is based. If the nearest one is in Colorado Springs, you may see fewer open time slots, or a quote padded to cover a 90-mile round trip.

3. Lead marketplaces. Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, and Yelp's "Request a Quote" don't haul anything. They sell your request to haulers, and each hauler usually pays somewhere between $15 and $100 per lead, depending on the platform and job size. That cost gets built into what you pay. The bigger issue: if you submit one form at 9 a.m., you may get 4 or 5 calls and texts before noon. Some people like that. Most don't.

4. Free giveaway and resale sites. Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist's free section, Nextdoor, and Buy Nothing groups. A working fridge, a solid-wood dresser, or a pile of clean fill dirt can disappear in 24 to 48 hours for $0. A stained mattress, a rotted deck, or a broken sectional won't.

What a good junk removal website actually shows you

Whichever site you're on, look for these before you book:

  • A price structure you can see. "Call for a quote" with no numbers at all is a yellow flag. Good sites price by truck volume (for example, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, or a full load) and give at least a range.
  • Photo or video quoting. Texting 3 or 4 photos should get you a price range within a few hours. You shouldn't have to take time off work for an estimate visit on a basic pickup.
  • Pueblo West named specifically. A site that lists 40 cities in the footer may just be a call center. A site that talks about acreage lots, long gravel driveways, and outbuildings has probably been out here.
  • What they won't take. Honest haulers say it up front: wet paint, solvents, pesticides, propane tanks, asbestos, and medical waste. If a site says "we take everything," it isn't being straight with you.
  • Where the junk goes. Colorado has banned computers, TVs, and other covered electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013. Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units need their refrigerant recovered under EPA Section 608 rules before scrapping. A site that explains how it handles those items is more likely to handle them legally.
  • Proof of insurance. Ask for a certificate of general liability insurance. $1 million per occurrence is a common standard. It matters when a crew is carrying a 300-pound gun safe past your new tile.
  • Recent reviews. Filter Google reviews to the last 90 days. A company with 200 reviews from 2021 and 3 from this year isn't the same company anymore.

> The fastest test of any junk removal website: call the number on it at 4 p.m. on a weekday. If you reach someone who can tell you whether they're in Pueblo West tomorrow, you've found a real operator. If you reach a script and a "someone will call you back," you've found a lead broker.

When hiring a junk removal company isn't worth it

This is the section most hauler websites leave out. Sometimes paying us, or any company, is the wrong call. Here's when.

You have a pickup and it's one load. If the junk fits in a full-size truck bed and you can lift it, haul it yourself to the landfill or transfer station. You'll pay a gate fee, often well under $50 for a small residential load, plus about an hour of your Saturday. A hauler's minimum charge will almost always cost more than that. We'd rather tell you that now than have you feel overcharged later.

Your stuff has resale value. A matching washer and dryer under 8 years old, a newer riding mower, or a clean leather couch can go on Facebook Marketplace for $50–$300 instead of costing you $100 or more to remove. Give it a week. If it doesn't sell, then call someone.

It's a multi-week remodel. If you're gutting a kitchen or re-roofing over 2 to 3 weeks, a roll-off dumpster usually costs less. A 10- to 20-yard dumpster often runs roughly $350–$600 for a 7-day rental in Colorado markets, and you can fill it as you go. Junk removal makes more sense for one-time debris after the work is done. See our [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) page for where that line usually falls.

It's a single appliance and you're buying a new one. Many appliance retailers haul away the old unit when they deliver the new one, often free or for $25–$50. That beats a separate trip every time.

It's hazardous waste. Paint cans with liquid still in them, motor oil, pool chemicals, and fertilizer don't belong in a junk truck. No legitimate hauler should take them. Use Pueblo County's household hazardous waste drop-off options instead.

It's still usable and you'd rather donate it. Charity pickups and donation drop-offs, such as the Habitat for Humanity ReStore in Pueblo, will take good furniture, cabinets, and building materials. Pickup schedules can be 1 to 3 weeks out, and they turn down anything stained, broken, or missing parts. If you have time and the items qualify, it's cheaper and better.

Failure modes to watch for on any junk removal site

These are the problems that actually cost people money:

  • The "estimate" that isn't binding. An online quote of "$250–$450" can become $600 at the curb if the crew says the load was bigger than the photos showed. Before the truck arrives, ask: "Is this a firm price for what's in the photos?"
  • Surcharges for specific items. Mattresses, tires, refrigerant appliances, and hot tubs often carry extra fees of $20–$75 per item on top of the volume price. Good websites list these. Bad ones spring them on you at the curb.
  • Trip or fuel fees for "rural" addresses. Some out-of-town companies treat Pueblo West as outlying and add $25–$75. Ask before you book.
  • Heavy material priced by volume. A truck-bed of concrete or rock can weigh 2 to 3 tons. Many haulers charge weight or disposal surcharges for dense material. Our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) page explains why that pricing works differently.
  • Marketplace "top pro" badges. On lead sites, rankings can be influenced by how much a company spends on the platform. A badge doesn't prove good work.
