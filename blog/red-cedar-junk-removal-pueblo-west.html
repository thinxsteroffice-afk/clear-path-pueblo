RED CEDAR JUNK REMOVAL PUEBLO WEST

If you're looking for a junk removal service to clear red cedar in Pueblo West, the short answer is yes: we haul it. "Red cedar" here almost always means the Rocky Mountain and one-seed junipers that cover the lots west of Highway 50. We load cut trunks, limbs, brush piles, dead skeletons and old juniper mulch, and we take all of it off your property in one visit. Most residential cedar jobs take 2 to 4 hours on site and fill a quarter to a full trailer load. We give you a fixed price before we start loading. We don't fell large standing trees near power lines and we don't grind stumps, and this page explains why that matters before you call anyone.

What we haul, and what we don't

Most Pueblo West owners have the same problem. Somebody cut the junipers down, or the drought killed them, or a windstorm knocked them over, and now a pile of prickly, resin-heavy wood is sitting in the yard. That's where we come in.

We take:

  • Cut trunks and limbs of any length. We cut them down on site so they load tight.
  • Brush piles left over from a defensible-space thinning project.
  • Dead, gray, standing junipers under about 12 feet that can be pulled or cut at the base safely.
  • Old shredded juniper or cedar mulch piled against foundations and fences.
  • Mixed loads, like cedar brush plus an old fence, a broken shed or a pile of yard junk, all in one trip.

We hand off or decline:

  • Live trees over roughly 15 feet, or any tree whose branches reach within 10 feet of an overhead line. That's a job for a certified arborist, and near lines Black Hills Energy has to be involved. If we try it and it goes wrong, the outcome is a downed line, not a bad haircut on a tree.
  • Stump grinding. We can pull small root balls with our [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo), but grinding a mature juniper stump flush to the ground takes different equipment.
  • Chipping on site so you can keep the mulch. We haul the wood away. We don't run a chipper.

Why red cedar is a removal problem out here

Junipers aren't junk in the hillside sense. They hold soil, and on a big Pueblo West parcel a healthy stand is part of why the land looks the way it does. Once they're cut or dead, though, they're one of the worst things to leave sitting near a house.

Pueblo West gets roughly 12 inches of precipitation a year, sits at about 4,700 feet, and has long, windy dry stretches. Juniper foliage is full of volatile oils. A dead juniper, or a cut pile left to cure for a summer, works more like kindling than firewood. The Colorado State Forest Service defensible-space guidance splits your property into three zones:

  • Zone 1, 0 to 5 feet from the house: nothing combustible. That includes juniper shrubs and the cedar mulch that so many builders spread around foundations here.
  • Zone 2, 5 to 30 feet: thinned, spaced vegetation with no ladder fuels that let a ground fire climb.
  • Zone 3, 30 to 100 feet: managed, with dead material removed.

The usual mistake we see is someone doing the hard part, thinning 30 feet of juniper with a chainsaw over a weekend, and then stacking the brush pile 20 feet from the house "to deal with later." Later is usually August. In fire terms that pile is worse than the trees it came from, because now it's dry, dense and sitting on the ground.

> Cutting the junipers is only half the job. The fire risk doesn't leave your property until the brush does.

What it costs

We price by how much of the trailer your load takes up, not by the hour. That means a slow, awkward pile of tangled brush doesn't cost you more because it took longer to load. These are typical ranges for cedar and brush jobs in Pueblo West. Your actual number is set when we see the pile, and it doesn't change once we start.

  • Minimum load, a few cut limbs or 1 to 2 small dead trees: about $100 to $175
  • Quarter load, a typical single-tree takedown pile: about $175 to $275
  • Half load, 4 to 6 mid-size junipers cut and stacked: about $300 to $425
  • Full load, a full defensible-space thinning on a 1-acre lot: about $475 to $650

Bigger parcels sometimes need 2 or more loads, especially the 2- to 5-acre lots off Purcell, McCulloch and the far west side. Brush is bulky but light. It takes up more trailer space per dollar than dense junk like concrete, so we cut and compact it as we load to keep you from paying for air.

Things that raise the price: piles you can't get a trailer near (we carry everything by hand across a long distance), wood mixed with rock and dirt, and buried root balls that have to be dug out. Things that lower it: stacking the wood within about 50 feet of where we can park, and cutting trunks to 4 to 6 foot lengths if you're already holding the saw.

When paying us isn't worth it

This is the section most junk removal pages skip. It will cost us some jobs, and we're fine with that.

You own a truck or a trailer and the pile is small. If the brush fits in a full-size pickup bed, about 2 cubic yards, you'll probably spend less hauling it yourself, even after counting your Saturday and the drop-off fee. Paying us $100 or more to move what your own truck can carry in one trip isn't a good deal.

You want the wood. Juniper burns hot and smells good. If you heat with a wood stove or have a firepit, split the trunks and keep them. Stack firewood at least 30 feet from the house, not against the garage. We'll gladly take only the brush and leave the rounds.

The trees are still alive and you want them gone. As we said above, mature live trees near structures or lines belong with an arborist. Some people book a junk hauler to save money on a takedown. Then the tree lands on a fence, and the repair bill costs more than the arborist would have. Call the arborist for the felling and call us for the haul-off, which is usually cheaper than paying the tree company to haul too.

You're hoping removal alone fixes fire risk. It doesn't. Hauling the dead material is one step. Spacing, limbing up the remaining trees and keeping Zone 1 clear are ongoing chores. If you're preparing for an insurance inspection, ask your insurer exactly what they want to see before you pay anyone for anything.

You're in a hurry during peak season. From about late April to June, everyone in Pueblo West cleans up at the same time. We usually schedule within 2 to 5 days, but sometimes it's longer in spring. If you need it gone tomorrow for a closing or an inspection, say so right away. If we can't make the date, we'll tell you rather than overpromise.

You're thinking about burning it instead. We can see why: it's free and it's satisfying. But open burning around Pueblo West needs a permit and stops completely whenever fire restrictions are in effect, which in dry years can last for months. Check with the Pueblo West Fire Department before you light anything. If you can't burn legally, hauling is the realistic option.

What a cedar cleanup looks like with us

1. Send photos or ask for an on-site look. Photos of the pile from two angles, plus where a truck can park, get you a close estimate in most cases. 2. Fixed price before we lift anything. If the load turns out smaller than we quoted, you pay less. It never goes up after we've started. 3. Cut, load, compact. We bring saws to break down long trunks and pack brush tight. A typical job takes 2 to 4 hours. 4. Rake-out. Juniper sheds needles and bark everywhere. We rake the pile footprint so you aren't left with a flammable mat where the pile was. 5. Disposal. Clean green waste goes where it can be processed, not tossed into a dumpster with household trash.

If the cedar is part of a bigger yard project, we handle all of it in one visit through our [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) service: tumbleweeds, old landscape timbers, rock edging and the broken picket fence the junipers grew through. If you're also clearing ground for a shed pad, RV parking or a new driveway apron, our [dirt work and excavation](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) crew can grade it in the same trip.

A few things to do before we show up

  • Mark anything you want to keep: a juniper you like, sprinkler heads, buried drip line. Junipers hide a surprising amount of old irrigation.
  • Wear gloves if you're stacking the wood yourself. Juniper bark and dead twigs cause more cuts and splinters than any other brush we handle.
  • Pull vehicles out of the way so we can get within reach of the pile. Every extra 25 feet of hand-carrying adds time.
  • Tell us about slopes and soft ground. Lots of Pueblo West lots have sandy wash areas where a loaded trailer will sink after rain.
