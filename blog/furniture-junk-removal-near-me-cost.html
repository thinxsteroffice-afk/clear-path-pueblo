What Furniture Removal Actually Costs in Pueblo West

Pricing here is by volume, not by weight, because furniture is bulky and light. A 16-foot dump trailer holds roughly 15 cubic yards. Here's how real Pueblo West jobs land:

  • One recliner, dresser, or loveseat: $95–$150. Curbside or garage-level is the low end; second floor with a tight turn is the high end.
  • Sectional sofa (3+ pieces): $175–$260. Sectionals eat more space than people expect — a large L-shape is about 20% of a trailer by itself.
  • Bedroom set (bed frame, mattress, box spring, dresser, nightstand): $180–$290.
  • Full living room + dining set: $300–$450.
  • Whole-house furniture clearout, 3-bed home: $425–$700 depending on whether the garage and shed come too.
  • Minimum charge: $95. Below that we're losing money on fuel and dump fees before anyone lifts anything.

Add-ons that are real, not upsells: $25–$45 per mattress or box spring, because Pueblo's transfer station charges separately for them and they can't be compacted. Stairs past the first flight add $20–$40. A piano is $250–$450 and a gun safe over 600 lbs is quoted on sight — those are rigging jobs, not furniture jobs.

Why the Pueblo West Address Changes the Math

Pueblo West is not Pueblo, and any hauler quoting you a flat metro rate hasn't done the drive. The disposal endpoint for most furniture out here is the Pueblo County landfill on Goodnight Ave, which is about 12 to 18 miles from most Pueblo West addresses depending on whether you're off McCulloch or out past Purcell. That's a 35–45 minute round trip plus scale time. Gate fees run in the $40–$70 range for a light-but-bulky trailer load, and mattresses get surcharged on top.

That drive is baked into a local quote. It is *not* baked into the $69 "starting at" number a national franchise advertises for a metro market 40 miles north. When their truck actually rolls up your driveway on Joe Martinez Blvd, the number moves.

Also specific to here: acre lots and long gravel drives. A lot of Pueblo West properties have 200+ feet between the house and where a truck can safely turn around. If we can't back a trailer to within 40 feet of the door, the crew is carrying a sleeper sofa across gravel, and that's real labor. Tell us about the driveway when you call — it's the single most common reason an over-the-phone quote drifts.

The Failure Modes — What Actually Goes Wrong

  • Bed bugs or heavy infestation. We will not load infested furniture into a shared trailer, because it contaminates the next four jobs. If there's evidence of bed bugs, say so when you book. It's still doable, but it's a specialized job with different handling and a different price, and hiding it just means the crew turns around at the door.
  • It won't fit back out the way it came in. Sleeper sofas and large sectionals are sometimes assembled in the room or came in before a wall went up. Our crew can disassemble most frames, but occasionally the honest answer is "this comes apart with a saw or it doesn't come out." We'll ask permission before cutting anything, and cutting is irreversible.
  • Water-damaged and mold-affected pieces. A soaked sofa can weigh three to four times its dry weight and may need to be bagged. That changes the price on-site.
  • Quotes drift when photos hide things. The number we give over the phone is based on what you tell us. If the "one dresser" turns out to be a dresser plus everything stacked behind it, expect the price to change. Every reputable hauler works this way; be suspicious of one that won't confirm on arrival.
  • We're not a moving company. We don't relocate furniture to your new house or into storage. If it's leaving the property and not going in our trailer, that's a mover's job.
  • Recycling and reuse isn't 100%. Anyone claiming a 90%+ diversion rate on furniture in Pueblo County is stretching. Realistically, metal frames, box spring steel, and appliances get recycled; usable pieces go to reuse channels; upholstered furniture with fabric, foam, and mixed wood mostly lands in the landfill because there's no economical processor within driving distance. Real diversion on a mixed furniture load here is closer to 25–45%. We'd rather quote the honest number.
