What a Pueblo West Mattress Pickup Actually Costs

The price of a mattress pickup is mostly labor and disposal, and disposal is the part people underestimate. A landfill does not want a mattress. Springs wrap around compactor drums and shred tires, so most transfer stations charge a per-item surcharge on top of the gate minimum. In our experience hauling in Pueblo County, you should expect a gate minimum in the neighborhood of $25 to $35 for a small load, plus a $10 to $20 mattress surcharge per unit. Call the scale house for current rates before you plan around them, because they change.

Here is what that looks like against our pricing:

  • One mattress, you haul it yourself: roughly $40 to $55 in gate fees, plus 45 to 60 minutes of driving and a truck you already own.
  • One mattress, we haul it: $85 flat, about 15 minutes of your time, no straps, no ratchet, no dumping fine risk.
  • A three-bedroom house clearing out six units: $115 for the first set, then $60 each for the rest, which lands around $355.
  • A mattress plus a bedroom's worth of other furniture: ask us to price it as a load instead of per item. Bundled loads on a [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) job usually beat item-by-item pricing once you cross three or four pieces.

The spread between doing it yourself and hiring it out is roughly $30 to $45 of real cost and about an hour of your Saturday. That is a small enough gap that plenty of Pueblo West homeowners should just do it themselves, and we will say exactly who below.

Why Pueblo West Makes This Harder Than It Should Be

Pueblo West is unincorporated. The Metro District handles water, parks, and roads, and it does not run curbside trash. Every household here contracts a private hauler individually, which means there is no single municipal bulky-item day to wait for the way there is inside Pueblo city limits.

Private haulers in this area handle bulky items inconsistently. Some will take a mattress with advance notice for a $25 to $40 bulk fee. Some refuse mattresses outright because their trucks are automated side-loaders with no room for an 80-pound spring unit. Call your hauler first. If they will take it for $30 and you can wait until your next service day, that is the cheapest option available to you and you should take it.

The second Pueblo West complication is lot size. Half-acre and one-acre lots with long driveways mean a mattress left out for a week gets wet, gets sun-bleached, and gets heavy. A dry queen innerspring runs 60 to 80 pounds. A king memory foam runs 110 to 140 pounds. The same king after two nights of rain in a monsoon-season thunderstorm can pass 200 pounds and will not fit in a standard 6.5-foot truck bed, since a king is 6 feet 8 inches long before you compress anything.

The Recycling Question, Answered Honestly

Roughly 80 to 90 percent of a mattress by weight is technically recyclable. The steel springs, the polyurethane foam, the cotton felt, and the wood foundation frame all have downstream uses. Four states, California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon, run funded mattress stewardship programs where you pay a small fee at purchase and drop the old one off free.

Colorado is not one of them. There is no state program, no free drop-off, and no recycling fee built into your mattress purchase.

The nearest dedicated mattress recycler to Pueblo West is in the Denver metro, about 110 to 120 miles north. They typically charge a per-unit processing fee in the $25 to $35 range. That is a four-hour round trip and a fee on top, for one mattress.

> If a junk removal company tells you your Pueblo West mattress is getting recycled, ask them which facility and how far it is. The honest answer for a single-mattress local job is that it goes to the landfill.

The economics are the reason. A queen innerspring holds around 25 pounds of recoverable steel. Scrap steel has recently run somewhere in the range of five to nine cents per pound, so the metal in your mattress is worth about $1.50 to $2.25. Nobody drives 220 miles for $2. We will consolidate mattresses and run them north when we have accumulated enough volume from [estate cleanouts](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) and multi-unit jobs to justify the trip. On a single-unit residential pickup, it goes to the landfill, and we would rather tell you that than sell you a green sticker.

Things That Go Wrong on Mattress Jobs

Three failure modes account for nearly everything that turns a 15-minute stop into a problem.

The stairwell it never came up. Foundations and box springs are rigid. If a queen box spring was carried up a straight run of stairs during construction and the homeowner later added a landing, a railing, or a tight turn at the bottom, it may not come back down intact. We will cut it apart if you authorize it, which takes about 20 minutes and produces a pile of lumber and fabric. A split box spring, two 30-inch halves, avoids this entirely and is worth asking for on your next purchase.

Water damage and weight. A mattress that lived on a Pueblo West patio through a summer is a biohazard sponge. We will still take it, but if it has more than roughly doubled in weight we may price it as a heavy item rather than a standard one, and we will tell you on arrival before we touch it.

Storage sheds and outbuildings. Mattresses stored in a detached shed on an acre lot often come with mice, and mice come with droppings. We handle it. Just tell us in advance so the crew arrives with respirators instead of discovering it. Similar issues show up on [shed demolition](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) work all over the district.

Bed Bugs Change Everything

If you suspect bed bugs, say so when you book. This is not a moral judgment and it does not get you refused. It changes the procedure.

An unbagged infested mattress moved through your hallway can seed the rest of your house. Buy a sealed mattress disposal bag at any hardware store for roughly $8 to $15 and seal the mattress in the bedroom before anyone carries it anywhere. Tape the seam. Every mattress recycler in the country rejects infested units, so a bagged mattress goes to the landfill without exception.

We charge an additional $25 per unit on confirmed bed bug jobs to cover disposable coveralls, truck sanitation, and the fact that the load cannot be consolidated with anything else. Hiding it from us does not save you the $25. It just means we find out in the truck and the next customer's furniture gets exposed.

How to Get the Cheapest Legal Outcome

Illegal dumping is the expensive mistake here. Mattresses turn up on county roads and in arroyos around Pueblo West every month, and Colorado's littering statute carries fines that start in the hundreds and escalate well past $1,000 for volume dumping, with cleanup costs assessed on top. Vehicle descriptions get reported. It is not a good gamble against an $85 pickup.
