MATTRESS DISPOSAL & RECYCLING COSTS

Pueblo West has no mattress recycling drop-off. The nearest facility that actually breaks mattresses down into steel, foam, and wood sits up I-25 in the Denver metro, roughly 115 miles and about two hours each way, and it still charges somewhere around $25 to $35 per unit. Locally you have three real paths: haul it to the Pueblo landfill yourself for roughly $25 to $40 in tipping fees, take free haul-away from a mattress retailer when you buy the replacement, or book a pickup with ClearPath in the $85 to $125 range for a single mattress. Below is what each one actually costs, and which one we think you should pick. It is not always us.

Why "recycling" barely exists in southern Colorado

Colorado is not a mattress recycling state. Four states run extended producer responsibility programs where you pay a small fee at purchase and drop the old mattress at a funded site for free: California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon. Those fees run about $10.50 to $23 per mattress depending on the state, and that money pays for the collection network. Colorado has no such law and no such network.

That single fact explains most of what you'll find searching. The pages promising "free mattress recycling near me" are written for the four EPR states or for metro areas with a nonprofit recycler absorbing the cost. Neither describes Pueblo West.

Pueblo West compounds it. The Pueblo West Metropolitan District covers roughly 47 square miles of unincorporated county. A metro district is not a city. There is no municipal sanitation department, no city-run bulky item pickup, no annual "put it at the curb" week. Trash here is a private contract between you and a hauler, and almost none of those residential contracts include a mattress.

Verify every fee below before you drive. Landfill rates and hours change, and a wasted round trip costs more than the phone call.

What's actually inside a mattress, and why nobody recycles it free

Break down a queen innerspring and you get roughly 25 pounds of steel from the coils, 10 to 20 pounds of polyurethane foam, a few pounds of cotton and felt, and a wood-and-cardboard frame if there's a box spring.

Now price it. Ferrous scrap has been trading in the neighborhood of $100 to $150 a ton at regional yards. That 25 pounds of steel is worth about $1.25 to $1.90. Shredded foam sold into carpet cushion might bring another $1 to $2. The wood is worth nothing and often costs money to dispose of.

So the recoverable material in a mattress is worth somewhere between $3 and $6. Tearing one apart takes a trained person 15 to 25 minutes with a hook knife and a reciprocating saw. At $20 an hour that's $5 to $8 in labor before you count the building, the baler, or the freight to move a bale of foam.

> Recycling a mattress recovers about $4 in materials and costs about $8 in labor. That gap is the whole story, and it's why every honest recycler charges a gate fee.

Any page telling you mattress recycling is free in a market without a state program is either describing somewhere else or leaving out who's paying.

Your four real options in Pueblo West, priced honestly

  • Retailer haul-away when you buy new. Denver Mattress on Dillon Drive, Mattress Firm, and most brick-and-mortar sellers include old-mattress removal with paid delivery. Cost is typically $0 to $100, often bundled. Bed-in-a-box brands shipped by parcel almost never offer it. If you're buying a replacement anyway, this is the cheapest option that exists and you should take it.
  • Haul it yourself. Round trip from Pueblo West to the landfill east of town runs roughly 25 to 35 miles. Figure $5 to $7 in fuel in a half-ton truck, a minimum load charge in the $25 to $40 range, and about 90 minutes of your day. Many landfills add a per-mattress surcharge of $10 to $20 because mattresses spring back and foul compactor equipment. Total realistic cost: $30 to $60.
  • Donation. Rare and getting rarer. Colorado's used bedding rules require sanitizing and relabeling before resale, which is why Goodwill, ARC, and most Habitat ReStores refuse mattresses outright. A local shelter or transitional housing program may take a like-new mattress with no stains and no odor. Call first. Showing up with a 12-year-old pillowtop wastes everyone's afternoon.
  • Hire a hauler. Expect $85 to $125 for a single mattress or box spring in Pueblo West, $35 to $50 for each additional piece, and a truckload minimum around $150 if you're combining it with other junk. That's our [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) service, usually scheduled within 24 to 48 hours and finished in 15 minutes on site.

Do not use us if the mattress fits in your cart in pieces. A box spring is a wood frame, a few staples, and a fabric cover. Strip the fabric, cut the frame with a recip saw, and it goes out in a 96-gallon cart over two or three weeks at no additional cost. That trick works on box springs and foundations. It does not work on a foam or innerspring mattress, and cutting into an innerspring with a saw is a genuinely bad idea.

The bed bug rule nobody explains

If the mattress has bed bugs, say so when you book. Not to protect us. To protect the outcome.

An infested mattress cannot be donated, cannot be recycled, and has to be sealed in plastic before it leaves the bedroom. Moving one uncovered through a hallway is how a one-room problem becomes a whole-house problem. Encasement bags run $15 to $30 at any hardware store, and Pueblo County requires nothing special beyond that, but a hauler who finds out on arrival may reschedule you or charge a surcharge.

Same disclosure applies to mattresses soaked in urine, mold, or biohazard. If a mattress sat in a flooded basement for a week, it's a different job. That work usually falls under [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) pricing rather than standard mattress pickup.

What a dumped mattress on the mesa actually costs

Every mattress made in the United States carries a law tag sewn into the seam. It lists the manufacturer, the plant, and the date of manufacture. Retailers match those tags to delivery records. Investigators know this.

Colorado's littering statute escalates fast with volume, and large-quantity dumping moves out of ticket territory into misdemeanor territory with fines that can pass $500 before cleanup costs are billed back. Pueblo County deals with roadside dumping on the mesa and along the arroyos constantly, and a mattress is the single most traceable thing you can leave out there.

Run the arithmetic on why it happens. Americans throw out roughly 20 million mattresses a year. Pueblo County holds about 170,000 people, near 0.05 percent of the country, which works out to somewhere around 10,000 mattresses a year locally, or about 27 a day. Every one of them needs a plan. When the legal plan costs $35 and 90 minutes, a few people pick the ditch instead. The ditch is the expensive option.

Before you book anyone, ask these three things

Ask the hauler what the total is including fuel, disposal, and stairs. Ask whether the price changes if you have a box spring as well as a mattress. Ask specifically where the mattress goes. A hauler who names the facility is telling you the truth. A hauler who says "we recycle everything we can" without naming a place is telling you a slogan.
