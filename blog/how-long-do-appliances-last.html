HOW LONG DO APPLIANCES LAST?

Most household appliances last 8 to 15 years. Here are the realistic ranges for a Pueblo West home: refrigerator 10–15 years, gas range 15–20, electric range 13–15, dishwasher 9–10, clothes washer 10–13, dryer 10–13, over-the-range microwave 9–10, countertop microwave 7–9, garbage disposal 10–12, tank water heater 8–12, tankless water heater 18–20, gas furnace 15–20, central AC 12–15, evaporative cooler 10–15, chest freezer 15–20, water softener 10–15. Those are medians, not guarantees. A dishwasher installed in 2016 is already past its average service life. A 1997 gas range may well outlive the person reading this. Dry air, dust, hard water, and 100°F garages in July all push Pueblo West numbers toward the low end of each range.

Why Your Numbers Will Land Below the National Average

Manufacturer lifespan claims assume a climate-controlled house at sea level with soft water. Pueblo West is none of those things.

  • Altitude. Pueblo West sits around 4,700–4,900 feet. Gas appliances lose roughly 4% of rated input per 1,000 feet of elevation, so an 80,000 BTU furnace here effectively delivers closer to 65,000 BTU unless it was derated and set up correctly at install. Undersized-in-practice equipment runs longer cycles, and longer cycles age igniters, inducer motors, and heat exchangers faster.
  • Dust. Unpaved driveways, dry lots, and spring wind coat condenser coils and dryer lint screens in a way that coastal homes never see. A refrigerator with a plugged condenser coil runs 20–30% longer duty cycles and can lose 3–5 years off its life. Vacuuming that coil twice a year takes 10 minutes.
  • Water hardness. Treated water in the Pueblo area generally runs in the moderately hard range, roughly 7–10 grains per gallon. Private wells west and north of town frequently test 20 grains or higher with measurable iron. At 20 grains, a tank water heater that would last 12 years often fails at 6–8.
  • Garage appliances. A second fridge or chest freezer in an uninsulated Pueblo West garage sees 105°F in July and 25°F in January. Standard refrigerators are rated for roughly 55–110°F ambient; below about 38°F the freezer thermostat stops calling for cooling and your frozen food thaws. Garage-rated units exist and cost $150–$300 more. Non-rated units in that environment commonly die at year 6 or 7.
  • Hard starts. Rural feeder lines and summer brownouts hit compressors and control boards. A $60 surge protector on a $1,800 refrigerator is cheap insurance.

Find Out How Old Your Appliance Actually Is

Most people guess, and most people guess low by three to five years. There is a better way, and almost nobody writes about it.

Find the data plate. On a refrigerator it is usually inside the fresh-food compartment on the left wall or the door jamb. On a dishwasher it is on the side of the door edge or behind the lower kickplate. Top-load washers hide it under the lid rim; dryers put it behind the door or on the back panel. Water heaters print it on the front label; furnaces on the inside of the access door.

Then use one of these three methods:

  • Brand serial decoding. Whirlpool-family units (Whirlpool, Maytag, KitchenAid, Amana, Roper) typically lead with a letter for the year and two digits for the week. GE uses a two-letter prefix — first letter month, second letter year. Frigidaire and Electrolux often encode the year in the third and fourth characters. Free brand-specific decoders exist; pull up yours rather than trusting a general rule.
  • The motor or compressor date stamp. This is the reliable fallback. Sealed compressors, blower motors, and pump motors almost always carry a four-digit stamp like `2317`, meaning week 23 of 2017. Components are built within weeks of final assembly, so that number is accurate to about a month.
  • The UL or CSA certification date printed on the label. Not exact, but it brackets the model year.

> If the compressor stamp says 2011, stop pricing repairs and start pricing replacements. You are not fixing an appliance; you are funding its hospice care.

Repair or Replace: Run the Actual Numbers

The 50% rule is the standard and it holds up: if the repair quote exceeds half the cost of a comparable new unit, replace. Add a second test that matters more — the remaining-life test. Divide the repair cost by the years you can reasonably expect afterward.

Concrete examples from the Pueblo market:

  • Dishwasher that won't drain: $160–$350 for a pump or drain valve. On a 4-year-old machine, worth it. On a 10-year-old machine, you are paying $250 for maybe 18 months, roughly $14 a month to keep a dishwasher that already owes you nothing.
  • Refrigerator sealed-system or compressor work: $500–$1,100. Comparable new mid-range unit: $1,100–$1,900. On a 12-year-old fridge this fails both tests outright.
  • Control boards: $250–$600 installed across most appliance categories. Boards are the single most common "not worth it" repair on units over 8 years old.
  • Dryer heating element or thermal fuse: $150–$280, and dryers are mechanically simple. Repairing a 12-year-old dryer is often the right call — one of the few places where age matters less.
  • Water heater anode rod: the part costs $25–$45. Replacing it at year 4 and year 8 can push an 8-year tank to 14 or 15. Almost nobody does this, which is why almost nobody gets 15 years out of a water heater.
  • Diagnostic fee alone runs $85–$135 in the Pueblo West area, usually credited toward the repair. Budget for the chance that you pay $110 to be told the unit is finished.

Energy math cuts the other way sometimes. A 1998 refrigerator pulls roughly 1,200–1,600 kWh a year. A current ENERGY STAR equivalent uses 350–500 kWh. At about $0.14–$0.16 per kWh locally, that's $120–$170 a year saved — real money, but a 10-year payback on a $1,500 fridge. Replace old appliances because they're failing, not because a salesperson promised the power bill would cover it.

Signs You're in the Final 12 Months

  • Refrigerator running near-continuously, or a freezer that frosts over within two weeks of defrosting — failing door seal or a sealed-system leak.
  • Washer that walks across the floor on spin after the legs have been leveled — bearing or suspension wear, usually $400+ on a front-loader.
  • Any burning or hot-plastic smell, especially from a dishwasher or dryer. Stop using it. Clothes dryers are tied to thousands of US house fires annually, with failure to clean lint the leading cause.
  • Rusty or metallic-tasting hot water, or popping and rumbling from the water heater tank — sediment and a spent anode rod. Tanks fail wet, usually at 2 a.m.
  • Repair number two within 18 months on the same appliance. The third one is coming.

Planning the Swap

Measure the doorway, not just the appliance. Standard interior doors are 30–32 inches; a 36-inch French-door refrigerator doesn't fit, and removing a door slab buys about 1.5 inches. Confirm your electrical: older Pueblo West homes sometimes have a 3-prong 30-amp dryer outlet where the new unit ships with a 4-prong cord — a $25 cord swap, not a rewire.

Easiest sequence is to have the old unit gone before the new one arrives.
