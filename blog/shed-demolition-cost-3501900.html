SHED DEMOLITION COST: $350–$1,900

Demolishing and hauling away a backyard shed in Pueblo West typically runs $350 to $1,900, all-in. A small 8x10 wood shed sitting on skids is usually $350–$650. A standard 10x12 or 10x16 on a wood or gravel floor lands at $600–$1,100. A 12x20 barn-style shed with a loft runs $1,100–$1,900. A cheap metal kit shed is the bargain of the bunch at $275–$500. Add $420–$840 if there's a concrete slab underneath you want gone, and $150–$400 if the shed is packed with contents. Most jobs are finished in 2–4 hours, same day. Below is exactly where those numbers come from, and the specific situations where you should not hire this out at all.

What Actually Drives the Price

Shed demolition pricing isn't really about square footage. It's about volume of debris and how hard it is to get that debris to the truck. Those two things move the number more than anything else.

  • Debris weight. A 10x12 wood shed with asphalt shingles yields roughly 1.5 to 2.5 tons of mixed C&D debris. Shingles alone can be 400–700 lbs of that. At Pueblo-area transfer station rates of roughly $45–$60 per ton for construction debris, you're looking at $70–$150 in tipping fees before anybody swings a hammer.
  • Access. A skid steer turns a four-hour hand-demo into a 45-minute job. But it needs a gate opening of 8 feet minimum, ideally 10. A huge share of Pueblo West backyards are fenced with standard 6-foot double gates, which means hand demolition and an extra $150–$300 in labor. If we have to carry debris more than about 75 feet to the truck, expect the same bump.
  • Construction type. A shed built from a kit with 2x3 framing comes apart fast. A shed someone framed on-site with 2x6s, plywood sheathing, and 3-inch structural screws does not. Owner-built sheds in Pueblo West are frequently overbuilt for wind, and that adds 1–2 labor hours.
  • Roofing layers. Two or three layers of asphalt shingles doubles or triples the heaviest part of the load.
  • Contents. Roughly a third of the sheds we take down are still full — old paint, chemicals, a broken mower, bags of hardened Quikrete. Household hazardous waste can't go in the trailer and has to be routed to a county HHW event, which is a separate trip.

The Slab Is the Plot Twist

This is the single biggest quote-buster we run into, and it's the thing most homeowners don't think about until the shed is already gone and they're staring at a bare rectangle of concrete.

Slab removal in Pueblo West prices out around $3.50–$7.00 per square foot, depending on thickness and reinforcement. A 10x12 slab (120 sq ft) is therefore $420–$840. A 12x20 slab is $840–$1,680 — potentially more than the shed demo itself. Why the range is so wide:

  • A 4-inch unreinforced slab breaks with a jackhammer or a hydraulic breaker in under an hour.
  • A 5–6 inch slab with rebar or wire mesh takes two to three times as long and produces heavier, uglier chunks.
  • Concrete is dense. That 10x12 slab weighs about 1.5 tons on its own, and clean concrete usually has to go to a separate recycler rather than the general C&D pile.
  • If the slab has a thickened perimeter footer — common on anything that was permitted — add 25–40%.

If you plan to reuse the pad for a new shed, keep it. Level it, patch the spalling, and you've saved yourself four figures. If you want lawn there, budget for [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) as a separate line item and expect another $200–$500 in fill dirt and topsoil to bring the hole back to grade.

Permits, Utilities, and the Pueblo West Paperwork

Two things to sort out before demolition day:

Was the shed permitted? Accessory structures under 200 square feet generally didn't require a building permit, and nobody's coming after you for taking one down. But if your shed was permitted — and anything over 200 sq ft, or anything with electrical, likely was — the Pueblo Regional Building Department will want a demolition permit, typically in the $50–$150 range. Call them before demo day, not after. A 10-minute phone call is cheaper than a stop-work notice.

Is there power or water running to it? A surprising number of Pueblo West sheds got a buried circuit run out to them at some point, often not by a licensed electrician. That circuit needs to be killed at the panel and terminated properly before anybody starts pulling walls. Same for a hose bib on a buried line. We won't demo a live-power shed, and no reputable company should.

One more Pueblo West-specific item: if your lot is on septic, know where your tank and leach field are before a skid steer drives across the yard. A loaded machine on a shallow lateral line is a $4,000–$12,000 repair. We route around it if you can point at it.

The Asbestos Question Nobody Asks

If your shed was built before roughly 1985, look at the siding and roofing before you get a quote. Two materials matter:

  • Transite / cement-asbestos board — gray, brittle, corrugated or flat panels, often used as siding or roofing on 1960s–70s outbuildings.
  • Old rolled asphalt roofing and mastic, which sometimes contained asbestos fibers.

Colorado's rules under CDPHE Regulation 8 kick in at 32 square feet of asbestos-containing material for single-family residential work. That's a small area — a single wall of transite panels clears it easily. A sample test runs $150–$400 and takes a few business days. If it comes back positive, licensed abatement on a small outbuilding runs $2,000–$6,000, and it has to happen *before* demolition.

That's an inconvenient thing for a demolition company to tell you. It's also the difference between a legal job and pulverizing regulated material into your own backyard.

> If your shed predates 1985 and has gray corrugated panels, spend $200 on a test before you spend $800 on demolition. Breaking transite is the one mistake on this list you cannot undo.

DIY vs. Hiring Out: The Honest Math

You can absolutely do this yourself. Here's the real accounting for a 10x12 wood shed:

  • Time: 12–20 hours for two moderately fit people, including loading and dump runs.
  • Dump trips: 3–5 trips in a half-ton pickup, or 2–3 with a 6x12 trailer. Round trip from Pueblo West to the transfer station is roughly 30–45 minutes plus scale time.
  • Tipping fees: $70–$150 total.
  • Tool rental: reciprocating saw, pry bars, sledge — $60–$110/day if you don't own them.
  • Roll-off alternative: a 15-yard dumpster in Pueblo West runs about $375–$525 for a 7-day rental with a 2-ton included limit and $55–$75 per ton over. A 10x12 shed will flirt with that limit.

Net DIY cost: $150–$350 plus a weekend. Net hired cost: $600–$1,100 and you're not there for any of it. If your weekend is worth more than about $30/hour to you, hiring out wins. If it isn't, do it yourself — we'd rather tell you that than sell you something you don't need.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Move

Plainly, these are the cases where you should not pay us:

  • The shed is structurally sound and you just want it gone. A 10x12 shed on skids in decent shape is worth $300–$900 on Facebook Marketplace in this market, and shed movers around Pueblo will relocate an intact skid-mounted shed for $250–$600. Selling it flips a $700 expense into a $400 gain. That's a $1,100 swing against hiring demolition. Try listing it for two weeks first.
  • It's a bare metal kit shed. These come apart with a socket wrench in about 90 minutes and fold flat. Scrap steel is running roughly $80–$140 per ton, so a 10x10 metal shed is maybe $25–$50 at the scrap yard — not real money, but the demo is genuinely easy. Paying $400 for this is paying for convenience only. Know that going in.
  • You're demolishing between March and May without a same-day finish. Pueblo West sees regular 50–65 mph gusts in spring. A half-demolished shed with loose sheathing is a projectile aimed at your neighbor's truck. Either finish in one day or wait for a calmer stretch.
  • You need it gone for under $250 and the shed is over 100 sq ft. Our tipping fees plus a two-person crew plus drive time don't fit under that number. Any company quoting it either isn't disposing legally or is going to renegotiate on-site. Rent a dumpster instead.
  • The shed is attached to the house or shares a wall. That's a general contractor job with a structural review, not a junk removal job. We'll decline it.
  • You have asbestos-containing material and no abatement budget. We can't touch it, and neither can anyone else without a license. The honest answer is sometimes "leave it standing until you can afford to do it right."
