FURNITURE REMOVAL TRUCK HIRE COSTS

Two different things get called "furniture removal truck hire" in Pueblo West, and they cost very different amounts. If you want to rent the truck and do the lifting yourself, a cargo van or 15-foot truck from the rental yards on Highway 50 runs about $19.95–$29.95 for the day plus roughly $0.99–$1.29 per mile, an environmental fee near $5, and optional damage coverage of $15–$28 — call it $60–$120 all-in for a Pueblo West round trip to the landfill, before dump fees. If you want a truck plus a crew that carries it out and disposes of it, ClearPath charges roughly $85–$135 for a single piece and $600–$650 for a packed 15–18 cubic yard truck. Same-day service in Pueblo West is usually available; most jobs take 30–60 minutes on site.

Here's the honest version of when each one makes sense.

The 20-minute math that decides it

Before you call anybody, price your own job. Three numbers matter:

  • Distance to disposal. Most of Pueblo West sits 12–20 miles from the transfer station and landfill east of town. Round trip that's 25–40 miles. At $1.09/mile on a rental, that's $27–$44 in mileage alone, on top of the day rate.
  • Tipping fees. Landfills bill by weight, generally in the $40–$60 per ton range, with a minimum charge that often lands near $25–$35 even for a light load. A sofa, a recliner, and a dresser might weigh 400 pounds total — you'll pay the minimum, not the per-ton rate. Call ahead for current rates and hours; they change, and holiday closures catch people with a rented truck and nowhere to dump.
  • Bodies. A three-seat sectional runs 150–250 pounds and doesn't bend. A sleeper sofa with a steel frame hits 200–300. If you don't have a second person who can carry half of that down a hallway without dropping it, the rental option is already off the table.

Add it up. A DIY run with one large item usually lands at $85–$140 once you count gas, mileage, the day rate, and the dump minimum — which is roughly what full-service costs for the same single item. DIY wins on volume, not on single pieces. Renting a truck to move one couch is almost always a wash or a loss.

What Pueblo West does to this job specifically

Pueblo West is not Pueblo, and the differences change the price and the risk.

Lots out here commonly run an acre or more, which means 60–100 feet from the front door to a truck that can't always pull in. A lot of driveways off Purcell, Joe Martinez, and the numbered lanes are gravel or dirt, and after a wet week a loaded 15-foot rental will rut them or sink a rear axle. Our crews back down carefully or stage a shorter carry on purpose. A rental customer usually finds out the hard way.

The wind is the part nobody mentions. Spring gusts through the Highway 50 corridor routinely hit 35–45 mph. A mattress or a sectional cushion strapped in an open trailer bed becomes a sail, and an unsecured load that leaves the highway is your liability, not the rental company's. If you're renting, get the enclosed box truck, not the open trailer — the $10 difference is not where you should economize.

One genuine advantage here: most Pueblo West homes are single-story ranches. No stairs, no elevator reservation, no apartment complex loading dock. That makes DIY meaningfully more feasible than it would be for a third-floor unit in town, and it's part of why our Pueblo West jobs average toward the fast end of the 30–60 minute window.

And because Pueblo West is unincorporated, there's no municipal bulky-item pickup attached to your trash bill the way city residents sometimes have. Your private hauler almost certainly won't take a dresser. The Metro District has run community cleanup events in the past — worth a call to confirm dates before you plan around one, because they're not guaranteed annually and they fill up.

When hiring us is the wrong call

This is the section most junk removal pages leave out. Skip us if any of these fit.

  • You have one item and a pickup truck. A dresser, a headboard, a single chair — if you own a half-ton and know one person with a functioning back, you'll spend maybe $30–$45 and 45 minutes. Paying $85–$135 for that is paying for convenience, and you should only do it if the convenience is worth $50 to you that day.
  • The furniture is actually good. Clean, unstained, no pet damage, no broken frame — that's donation material, and donation pickup is free. Habitat's ReStore, Goodwill, and the local thrift operations will take real furniture. The catch: they schedule out one to three weeks, they refuse anything torn or stained or smelling of smoke, and most will decline mattresses, particleboard flat-pack, and anything with a cracked frame. If you have three weeks and clean furniture, don't call us.
  • You're moving, not disposing. We haul to disposal and donation. We are not a moving company, we don't carry moving insurance on your goods, and we won't take a piece across town to your new place. Hire movers.
  • Your budget is fixed under $80. Our minimum covers two crew members, a truck, fuel, insurance, and the tipping fee. There's no version of this below that number. A rental truck genuinely is the cheaper path if the ceiling is hard.
  • You need it in the next 90 minutes. Same-day is common, but it's a 2-hour arrival window inside an already-booked route. If your closing walkthrough is at noon and it's 10:30 a.m., rent the truck.

Failure modes worth naming on our side too: we can't take paint, solvents, propane tanks, tires in quantity, or anything with refrigerant still charged without special handling — a fridge or freezer needs the [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) process, not a furniture truck. We also can't legally dispose of a mattress the way people assume; there's no free mattress recycling program serving Pueblo County, so mattresses go to the landfill and get priced separately. See [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for what that actually costs.

Why volume pricing beats hourly here

Furniture is bulky and light. A truck packed with two sofas, a loveseat, a mattress set, and four chairs might only weigh 1,100–1,400 pounds — under a ton, so our tipping fee is the minimum, but it fills 15 cubic yards and takes two people 50 minutes to load. Compare that to a half-load of tile and mortar at 3,000 pounds, which costs triple in dump fees and fills a third of the space.

That's why volume pricing is fair for furniture and terrible for masonry, and why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is quoted differently. If a company quotes you the same per-cubic-yard rate for a sectional and a load of flagstone, one of those numbers is wrong, and it isn't the one that favors you.

Rough volume tiers for furniture work in Pueblo West:

  • Single item: $85–$135
  • Quarter truck (roughly a bedroom set): about $225
  • Half truck (living room, or a garage's worth): about $375
  • Full 15–18 yard truck: $600–$650

Two crew, disposal included, no fuel surcharge for anything inside Pueblo West or Pueblo proper.

The questions to ask whoever you call

Ask these of us and of every competitor. The answers separate real operators from a guy with a trailer:

  • Is disposal included in the quote, or billed after at the scale?
  • Is the price given on site before loading starts, or after?
  • Do you carry general liability, and will you send the certificate? Someone carrying a 250-pound sleeper down your hallway should be insured.
  • What's your fee if I cancel after you're in the driveway?
  • Where does it go — landfill, donation, or scrap? Metal bed frames and appliances have scrap value; furniture mostly doesn't.

A quote given only over the phone, sight unseen, is a guess. Ours firms up when the crew sees the pieces, and you can decline at that point at no charge.
