Why Tampa residents end up searching for Colorado junk removal

Southern Colorado and the Tampa Bay area have strong ties. Retirees split time between them, military families rotate through Fort Carson about 45 minutes north, and adult children in Florida often inherit a parent's home in Pueblo West. The situations we hear about most are:

  • An inherited home that has to be emptied before it can be listed
  • A rental property where a tenant left furniture, mattresses and trash behind
  • A storage unit still billing about $90–$180 a month for things nobody plans to keep
  • A foreclosure or eviction with a hard deadline set by a bank or court
  • A backyard hot tub or shed that a buyer's inspector flagged

In each case the owner is in Florida and the property is in Colorado. Flying in to handle it yourself usually costs $250–$500 for a round-trip ticket to Colorado Springs or Denver, plus a rental car, a hotel, and 2–4 days off work. Even then you're still doing the lifting yourself.

What a remote cleanout actually costs in the Pueblo West market

The prices below are typical ranges for the Pueblo area so you can budget. They are not fixed quotes. Every job is priced on volume and weight, so confirm the number with whichever company you hire, including us.

  • Single bulky item (a couch, recliner or mattress): roughly $75–$150
  • Half truckload (about 7–8 cubic yards): roughly $250–$400
  • Full truckload (a standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards): roughly $450–$750
  • Hot tub removal: roughly $300–$600, depending on whether it has to be cut apart and how far it is from the street
  • Full contents of a 3-bedroom home: usually 2–4 truckloads, or about $1,200–$3,000

Compare that to renting a 20-yard dumpster for 7–10 days, which often runs $400–$600 in southern Colorado. A dumpster is cheaper only if someone is on site to fill it, and from Tampa nobody is.

How to run a Pueblo West cleanout without leaving Florida

Most guides on hiring a junk hauler assume you'll be standing in the driveway. A remote owner needs a different checklist.

1. Get a quote from photos or video first. Walk a neighbor, realtor or property manager through each room on a video call, or send 15–30 clear photos that include closets, the garage and the backyard. Pueblo West lots are often half an acre to several acres, and the outbuildings and back corners are where quotes end up wrong. Ask the company to put in writing how much the price can change once the crew sees the job in person.

2. Decide what stays before anyone shows up. This step is the one remote owners skip most often. Make a written "do not remove" list and mark those items with painter's tape. Personal papers, photos, firearms, jewelry and anything with a title or deed should come out before a full clear-out starts. If you can't sort these yourself, hire an estate-sale company or ask a trusted local to do it first.

3. Arrange access you can control. A lockbox code you can change afterward is better than mailing a key. Tell the HOA if there is one (most of Pueblo West has no HOA, but some subdivisions do). Make sure neighbors won't be surprised by a truck in the driveway.

4. Require photos before and after. Ask for photos of every room once the job is finished, along with the disposal or donation receipts. If you can't see the property yourself, the photos are how you confirm the work was done.

5. Pay by card, not cash or wire. A card payment gives you chargeback protection if the work isn't done. Be cautious of any out-of-state request to wire a deposit to a "local" company you've never seen.

When hiring us isn't worth it

These are the situations where we'd tell you to spend your money somewhere else, or not spend it at all.

  • Your junk is in Tampa. We don't send trucks to Florida and we don't broker jobs to Florida haulers. Call a Tampa company or the city's bulk-pickup line.
  • It's 3 or 4 items and you have a local friend with a pickup. The Pueblo-area landfill and transfer stations accept residential loads for a fraction of what a crew costs. One trip on a Saturday may cost less than our minimum.
  • The items still have real resale value. A decent dining set, working appliances under about 5 years old, or tools can sell on Facebook Marketplace or through an estate sale. If the household contents are worth more than about $2,000, an estate-sale company that takes a commission (often 25–40%) may leave you with more money than paying for removal. Bring a hauler in afterward for whatever is left.
  • You haven't sorted the contents. If you can't confirm what stays, don't book a full clear-out yet. A crew can't tell which shoebox has the life-insurance policy in it. Once something has gone to the landfill, it isn't coming back.
  • The job is mostly hazardous material. Paint, solvents, pool chemicals, propane tanks, asbestos-era flooring and some electronics aren't standard junk. Most haulers, including us, either can't take them or have to send them through special disposal at extra cost. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection, which is often cheaper.
  • A renovation will produce debris for weeks. If a contractor is on site every day, a roll-off dumpster that sits there for 2–3 weeks usually beats several separate truck visits.
  • The deadline is unrealistic. A packed 3-bedroom home, especially one in hoarding conditions, can take 2–3 full days. If the bank's lockout is tomorrow morning, no honest company can promise to finish in time. Ask for an extension first.

What's different about Pueblo West junk compared to Tampa junk

If you've only dealt with Florida properties, a few things about Pueblo West will be unfamiliar:

  • No hurricane debris, but plenty of wind and sun damage. Pueblo gets only about 12 inches of rain a year and a lot of sun at roughly 4,700 feet of elevation. UV light turns outdoor plastic, trampolines and playsets brittle, and they often break apart when moved.
  • Big lots collect big junk. On acreage parcels it's common to find old fencing, scrap metal, broken-down sheds and concrete. Those are jobs for [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) or [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo), not a quick truck pickup.
  • Trash service is private. Pueblo West has no city-run bulk pickup like Tampa's. Residents contract with private trash haulers, so the "just put it at the curb" option most Tampa residents have usually doesn't exist here.
  • Hot tubs are everywhere and often dead. A hot tub that has sat empty through several Colorado winters usually has cracked plumbing and can't be resold. See [hot tub removal pueblo](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo).
