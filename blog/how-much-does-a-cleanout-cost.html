HOW MUCH DOES A CLEANOUT COST?

A typical cleanout in Pueblo West runs $225 to $850 for most single-room, garage, or storage-unit jobs, and $1,800 to $4,500 for a full 3-bedroom house. Pricing is by volume — how much space your stuff takes in the truck — not by the hour. Real 2026 numbers in this market: a quarter truckload $225–$325, half load $395–$525, full 16-foot truck (about 15 cubic yards) $650–$850. A 10x10 storage unit is usually $350–$700. Hoarding-level jobs run $3,000–$12,000. Add-ons that move the price: freon appliances (+$35), mattresses (+$50–$95 each), stairs, and anything heavy enough to blow past the truck's weight allowance.

Below is how those numbers are actually built, what makes a quote jump, and — the part most junk removal sites skip — the jobs where hiring us is the wrong call.

The Pueblo West Price List, Line by Line

Volume pricing sounds vague until you see the increments. A standard dump-trailer or box truck in this market holds roughly 450 cubic feet. Haulers sell it in fractions:

  • Minimum charge / single item: $95–$145. This covers one couch, one fridge, one treadmill.
  • 1/8 load (about 2 pickup-bed loads): $135–$185
  • 1/4 load: $225–$325
  • 1/2 load: $395–$525
  • 3/4 load: $525–$650
  • Full load: $650–$850
  • Two-car garage, packed floor to rafters: $700–$1,400 — usually 1 to 2 full loads plus 3–5 labor hours
  • 3-bedroom estate cleanout: $1,800–$4,500 across 3–6 truckloads over 1–2 days
  • 10x20 storage unit: $700–$1,200

Those bands are wide on purpose. The spread inside each one is driven by labor time, not volume. Two guys carrying boxes 30 feet down a flat Pueblo West driveway is a different job than the same volume hauled up from a walkout basement or dragged 120 feet from a back pasture.

Why Pueblo West Is Cheaper Than Most Metros — and Where That Breaks

Pueblo West has a structural cost advantage that Denver and Colorado Springs customers don't get. Lots here are half an acre to five acres, driveways are wide, and there are no alleys, no parking permits, no elevator reservations, and no HOA loading-dock windows. A crew can back a 16-foot truck within 20 feet of your garage door on almost every property between McCulloch and Purcell. That access alone knocks 20–30% off what the identical pile costs to remove in a Denver townhome.

The offset is drive time. Disposal happens in Pueblo proper, which is a 15–20 mile run each way from most of Pueblo West — call it 40–55 minutes round trip per load, plus scale time. On a five-load estate job that's roughly four hours of the day spent driving Highway 50, and it's baked into your quote whether anyone itemizes it or not. This is exactly why the third and fourth truckloads on a big job cost the same as the first two even though the crew is faster by then.

> Volume sets the sticker price. Weight, access, and drive time decide whether the hauler makes money — which is why "it's just a small pile" quotes get revised on site.

The Weight Trap Nobody Warns You About

Here's the thing most cost articles leave out entirely: volume pricing assumes household density, roughly 150–250 lbs per cubic yard. Furniture, boxes, clothes, and plastic toys fit that. A short list of materials does not, and they break the pricing model outright:

  • Concrete, brick, flagstone, and landscape rock — 2,500–4,000 lbs per cubic yard. Half a truck of concrete outweighs three full trucks of furniture. Expect separate per-ton pricing, often $180–$400 per ton delivered to a clean-fill site. See [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Dirt and sod — 2,000–2,700 lbs per yard, same story. That's [dirt work excavation pueblo](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo) territory, often better handled with a machine than a crew.
  • Asphalt shingles — a single square (100 sq ft) of tear-off runs 250–400 lbs. A 20-square roof is 3–4 tons.
  • Tile, plaster, and wet drywall from a remodel — see [construction debris removal pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo).
  • Paint, solvents, oil, propane tanks, tires, and e-waste — these carry per-item disposal fees ($5–$40 each) because the landfill won't take them at the household gate.

If your cleanout is 80% heavy material, ask for per-ton pricing up front. Anyone quoting you "half a truck, $450" for a pile of patio flagstone is either going to renegotiate on site or eat a $300 loss — and vendors who eat losses don't stay in business long enough to warranty anything.

Do It Yourself: The Actual Math

We are going to talk you out of a sale here, because for a lot of Pueblo West jobs the DIY route genuinely wins.

The honest comparison for a one-pickup-load cleanout:

  • Truck rental: $29.95/day plus $0.99/mile. A round trip to the Pueblo landfill from Pueblo West is about 36 miles — roughly $66 all in.
  • Landfill gate fee: most household loads land in the $30–$70 range at current per-ton rates with a minimum charge. Verify the current rate and hours before you drive — rates change and some materials are surcharged.
  • Your time: 3–4 hours loading, driving, unloading, returning the truck.
  • Total: roughly $100–$140 and half a Saturday.

Our minimum for that same load is $135–$185. If you own a truck already, have a friend, and your back works, you save real money doing it yourself and you should. The break-even is around one and a half pickup loads. Below that, hiring anyone is paying $60–$100 for convenience. That may be worth it to you — just know that's what you're buying.

A dumpster beats us outright on multi-day projects. A 20-yard roll-off in this area runs roughly $375–$550 for a 7-day rental with a 2–4 ton allowance and $65–$95 per ton over. If you're gutting a kitchen over two weekends and filling it gradually, a dumpster is 30–50% cheaper than three separate truck visits. Full-service hauling wins when the work is done in one pass, when you physically can't lift the items, or when nobody wants a container sitting in the driveway for a week.

Who Should Not Hire a Junk Removal Crew

Plainly, so you don't waste a phone call:

  • You have one mattress and a truck. Colorado charges a mattress recycling fee at retail; disposal is $50–$95 per unit through a hauler. Handling it yourself saves the labor markup. [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) is for people who genuinely can't move it.
  • Your items are worth more than the haul. Working appliances under 8 years old, solid-wood furniture, and functional tools frequently sell for $75–$400 locally in a weekend. Paying $400 to remove $900 of resellable goods is a $1,300 swing. On estate jobs, get an estate sale quote first — that's a real recommendation even though it costs us the job. Details on [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo).
  • You're on a hard budget and the pile isn't urgent. Two Saturdays with a rented trailer beats a $700 invoice if your time is flexible.
  • You need the property cleared today for a closing. Same-day is possible but priced at a 15–25% premium, and if we're already booked, no amount of money creates a second crew. Book 3–5 business days out.
  • Your material is regulated. Asbestos-containing floor tile, lead paint debris, tires in volume, and hazardous liquids need licensed disposal, not a junk truck. We turn these away — hire the right contractor.

The Failure Modes of a Cheap Quote

Phone quotes are estimates. Three things reliably blow them up, and you should be told this before the truck arrives:

  • Volume is underestimated by 40–60% on average. People describe a garage as "half full" and it's a full load and a half. Take three photos — wide, mid, and inside the closet you forgot about — and text them. A photo quote lands within 10–15% instead of 50%.
  • The stuff behind the stuff. Sheds, crawlspaces, and the side yard are almost never mentioned on the first call and add $150–$400.
  • Access surcharges. Stairs, carries over 100 feet, soft ground after a storm (a loaded truck on wet caliche is a $500 tow), and locked gates.
  • Disposal fees, which never go away. Roughly 30–40% of what you pay is landfill and recycling cost, not profit. That's why nobody in this market can legitimately quote a full truckload under about $400 — below that, the numbers don't work and something else is going on.

A vendor who won't put a not-to-exceed number in writing after seeing photos is a vendor who plans to renegotiate on your driveway. Ask for a firm cap, ask what happens if the load runs over, and get both in a text.

Cutting Your Own Bill Before We Arrive

  • Sort first. Metal is worth money. Pulling appliances, bed frames, exercise equipment, and scrap steel into a separate pile can reduce your quote by $50–$150 because that material offsets disposal cost.
  • Consolidate to one staging area — a garage bay or the driveway apron. Cutting the crew's carry distance can drop labor time 25–40%.
  • Donate the good third. Local charities take furniture and household goods; you shrink the load and may get a deduction.
  • Break down what you can. A disassembled shed or trampoline packs 3–4x tighter than a whole one, and volume is what you're paying for. Applies to [shed demolition removal pueblo](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) and [trampoline playset removal pueblo](/services/trampoline-playset-removal-pueblo).
  • Combine with a neighbor. Two half-loads on the same street is one full load and one trip. Splitting an $800 full truck two ways beats two separate $475 half loads by $150.

What to Ask Before You Book Anyone

Get these answered in writing: Is the price by volume or by hour? What's the minimum? Is disposal included or billed separately? What items carry surcharges? What happens if the load exceeds the estimate — do you stop and call, or keep loading? Are you insured, and can you send the certificate?

For a firm number on your specific pile, photos beat descriptions every time.
