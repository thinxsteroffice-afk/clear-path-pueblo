MATTRESS DISPOSAL DROP OFF: PUEBLO

There is no municipal mattress drop-off site in Pueblo West. Because Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County, there's no city bulk-item pickup and no local dump you can back a truck into. Your three real options: haul it yourself to a landfill or transfer station in Pueblo, roughly a 12 to 20 mile drive each way, where you'll pay a minimum load fee in the $25 to $45 range plus a per-mattress surcharge at some sites; have the retailer take the old one when the new one is delivered, which is usually free to $40; or pay a hauler like us to pick it up at the curb. Call any drop-off site before you drive. Hours, scales, and rates change without notice.

What the self-haul actually costs once you count everything

The landfill fee is the number people quote each other. It's the smallest part of the bill.

A queen mattress is 60 by 80 inches. A standard half-ton pickup bed is about 78 inches with the tailgate up, so the mattress will not lie flat. It goes in diagonally or on top of the rails, which means ratchet straps, not bungees. Foam mattresses are floppy and heavy: a queen memory foam runs 90 to 130 pounds, a king pillowtop can push 140. That is a two-person lift, and the second person has to be there twice, once at your house and once at the scale.

Then the time. Load, drive 15 to 25 minutes each way, wait behind three commercial trucks at the scale, unload, drive home. Two hours of your Saturday is a realistic estimate, and that assumes nothing goes wrong.

  • Minimum load fee: $25 to $45 at most Pueblo-area sites, and you pay it whether you bring one mattress or a full bed of debris
  • Per-item mattress surcharge: some facilities add $10 to $30 per piece on top, because mattresses jam compactors
  • Fuel: a 25 to 40 mile round trip in a truck is $8 to $14, plus whatever a straps-and-tarp run to the hardware store costs
  • Your labor: two hours, two people
  • Risk: a mattress that leaves the truck bed on Highway 50 is a citation and, potentially, someone else's windshield

A box spring is its own item. If you're clearing a full bed set, that's two pieces, two surcharges, and a wood-frame box spring that will not bend around a stairwell corner.

Why "mattress recycling near me" mostly doesn't exist here

This is the part most pages skip. Colorado does not have a mattress stewardship program. California, Connecticut, and Rhode Island run recycling programs funded by a fee you pay at purchase, and Oregon added one recently. Colorado does not. Nobody collected a recycling fee when you bought that mattress, so nobody is holding money to dismantle it.

That matters because mattress recycling is real but not free. A mattress is roughly 90 to 95 percent recyclable by weight: steel springs, polyurethane foam, cotton and felt fiber, and a wood frame in the box spring. The problem is the economics. An innerspring queen contains maybe 25 pounds of steel. Scrap steel in Colorado has been trading in the neighborhood of $100 to $180 per ton, which puts the recoverable metal value at well under $2. Tearing the mattress apart takes a person 10 to 20 minutes. The math doesn't close, which is why recyclers charge $25 to $35 per piece to accept one.

The nearest dedicated mattress recyclers are on the Front Range, mostly Denver metro. That's about 110 miles from Pueblo West, a 220 mile round trip. Driving one mattress that far burns roughly 10 gallons of fuel to divert about 100 pounds of material. That is not an environmental win, and we won't pretend otherwise.

> If a hauler in Pueblo County promises you every mattress gets recycled, ask them which facility and how far away it is. Most mattresses collected here go to a landfill, ours included, and the honest answer is that the infrastructure isn't here yet.

When you should not hire us

We'd rather lose the job than take money for something you can do cheaper.

  • You're buying a new mattress. Almost every mattress retailer and most online sellers will haul the old one for free or $20 to $40 as part of delivery. That is cheaper than any hauler on earth. Ask for it at the point of sale, before delivery day, because crews won't take it as a surprise add-on.
  • You bought online and you're inside the trial window. The bed-in-a-box companies run 100 to 365 night trials, and most arrange free pickup or a local donation on returns. Don't pay us to remove a mattress the manufacturer will collect for nothing.
  • You own a truck, you have a helper, and it's one mattress. Your all-in cost is the tipping fee plus fuel, call it $40 to $60. We can't beat that on one item. Hire us when you value the two hours more than the difference.
  • You already have a dumpster on site. If you're mid-remodel with a roll-off in the driveway, the mattress goes in the roll-off. You've paid for that volume.
  • It's one mattress and nothing else, and you're price-shopping hard. Single-item pickups carry a minimum charge because the truck, fuel, insurance, and two crew members cost the same whether we take one item or twenty. Our per-item price on a single mattress is the worst value we sell. It gets dramatically better when you add anything else, which is why most of our customers combine it with a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or a couch.

There are also jobs we'll turn down. A mattress soaked in sewage, a hoarding situation with biohazard exposure, or a unit with an active rodent infestation needs remediation before a junk crew touches it. We'll tell you that on the phone rather than send a truck and then walk away.

The other limitation: donation is almost never an option

People ask us to donate mattresses roughly once a week. Goodwill of Colorado does not accept used mattresses. Most Colorado thrift operations don't either. Shelters generally can't take them because of liability and infestation risk, and Colorado follows the federal flammability standard for bedding, which makes reselling used mattresses legally messy.

If your mattress is genuinely near-new, try a local Buy Nothing group or Facebook Marketplace first, with clear photos and honest disclosure. Give it 7 to 10 days. If nobody takes a free mattress in a week, nobody is taking it.

Bed bugs and wet mattresses change the rules

Two situations change what you should do.

Bed bugs. Do not drag an infested mattress through the house unwrapped. Buy a heavy-gauge mattress disposal bag, typically $10 to $20 at a hardware store, seal it at the house, and tell whoever handles it. Some facilities require encasement for known infestations, and any hauler who isn't told is going to contaminate the next three houses on the route. We'll still take it. We just need to know so we can bag the truck and treat it appropriately.

Water damage. A mattress that's been rained on in the driveway or sat in a flooded basement can absorb 40 to 80 pounds of water. It gets heavier by the hour and grows mold within 24 to 48 hours. If a mattress is going out, get it out before the next storm rather than after.

Pueblo West specifics that generic guides get wrong

Pueblo West is served by the Pueblo West Metropolitan District, not a city government. Trash service out here is a private hauler arrangement, and standard residential service does not include bulk items like mattresses. Some haulers offer a bulk pickup for an extra charge on a scheduled day. Check your account terms before assuming your regular hauler will take it.

The covenant angle catches people. A mattress leaning against the garage or sitting in the side yard can draw a covenant violation notice, and those typically come with a short cure period measured in days, not months. "I'll deal with it next month" is how a $100 problem turns into a compliance letter.

And the obvious one: dumping it on a county road, in an arroyo, or behind a vacant lot on the west end is illegal dumping under Colorado law. Fines start in the hundreds and escalate well past $1,000 with volume, plus cleanup costs. Mattresses are also trivially traceable. The law tag sewn to the side has a manufacturer and often a retailer code on it.
