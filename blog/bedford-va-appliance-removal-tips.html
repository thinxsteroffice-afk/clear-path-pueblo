What Appliance Removal Costs in Pueblo West

Most of our calls are for a single item: a dead refrigerator in the garage, a washer that quit mid-cycle, or a chest freezer that's sat in a barn since the 1990s. Here's what pricing usually looks like in Southern Colorado:

  • One standard appliance (washer, dryer, dishwasher, range): usually $75–$150, depending on access.
  • Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units: often $20–$50 more than a washer, because the refrigerant has to be recovered legally before the steel can be scrapped.
  • Two to four appliances on one trip: the cost per item drops a lot. Clearing a full laundry room and a kitchen together often costs less than two separate single-item trips.
  • Stairs, basements, or tight turns: add $25–$75. A side-by-side refrigerator weighs 250–350 pounds empty, and getting it up a basement stairwell takes two people and a dolly rated for at least 600 pounds.

The Refrigerant Rule Most People Don't Know About

This is the part most appliance removal pages skip, and it can matter to you financially.

Refrigerators, freezers, dehumidifiers, window AC units, and water coolers contain refrigerant. That's either older ozone-depleting CFCs and HCFCs or newer HFCs, which trap heat in the atmosphere at hundreds to thousands of times the rate of CO2. Section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act requires that refrigerant to be recovered by EPA-certified equipment before the appliance is scrapped or landfilled. Venting it on purpose is illegal, and EPA civil penalties are adjusted for inflation and now exceed $40,000 per day per violation.

What that means in practice:

  • Most scrap yards won't accept a refrigerator unless it comes with a recovery tag or receipt showing the refrigerant was removed. Some take it and charge a $15–$30 recovery fee.
  • A neighbor who "just cuts the lines" to get a fridge into a truck bed is releasing refrigerant, which is a federal violation. It also leaves compressor oil on your driveway.
  • Older chest freezers (common in Pueblo West garages and outbuildings) may contain R-12, a CFC that hasn't been produced in the U.S. since 1996. These need the same recovery and sometimes extra handling.

> A fridge isn't just 250 pounds of steel. Federal law treats it as a container of regulated gas, and whoever hauls it is responsible for getting that gas out properly.

Ask any hauler, including us, what happens to the refrigerant. If they can't tell you where the appliance goes, assume nobody is handling it.

When Paying for Appliance Removal ISN'T Worth It

Some of you shouldn't hire us, or anyone else. Here are the situations where you'd be overpaying:

1. You're buying a replacement appliance anyway. Big retailers often haul away the old unit when they deliver the new one, either free or for roughly $25–$40. That almost always beats a separate junk removal trip for one appliance. Check the haul-away box at checkout. The catch is that the old unit usually has to be disconnected, emptied, and in the same room where the new one goes. If it's a second fridge in the garage, they generally won't take it.

2. You have a pickup, a strong back, and a non-refrigerant appliance. Washers, dryers, ranges, and water heaters are mostly steel. Light iron scrap has recently paid somewhere around $100–$200 per ton depending on the market, so a 150-pound washer brings in maybe $5–$15 at a Pueblo scrap yard. That isn't much money, but it's better than paying $100. If you can load it yourself, haul it yourself.

3. The appliance still works and is less than about 10 years old. Sell it or give it away first. A working washer-dryer set listed for $100–$200 on local marketplace groups in Pueblo West often goes within 48 hours. Some donation centers and ReStores take working appliances, but most want units that are clean, complete, and fairly new, and policies change, so call ahead.

4. Your utility is running a recycling rebate. Utilities sometimes pay customers $35–$50 to pick up a working second fridge or freezer. Black Hills Energy serves Pueblo West, so check whether it has an active program before you pay anyone. These programs start and stop, and some require the unit to be plugged in and running at pickup.

5. You need a gas or water line disconnected. Most junk haulers, including us, do not disconnect gas lines. If your range or dryer is still hooked to gas, you need a licensed plumber or your gas utility to cap it first. That's a separate cost, often $75–$150. Water lines on a fridge with an icemaker or on a dishwasher are usually simple shutoffs, but if a valve is corroded and won't close, that's a plumber's job too.

6. The appliance is built in. Built-in refrigerators, wall ovens, and some dishwashers are trimmed into the cabinetry. Pulling them out can mean removing trim, cutting caulk, or taking off a countertop panel. We'll move what's freestanding. Remodel-style demolition is a different job with different pricing.

Plainly: if your situation is a single freestanding washer, you own a truck, and you have a free afternoon, you probably don't need us.

How to Prep an Appliance So Removal Goes Fast

These steps cut time on the job, and sometimes cut the bill:

  • Empty it completely. Spoiled food in a fridge that's been unplugged for weeks is the most common problem we run into. Clean it out a day ahead and leave the doors propped open.
  • Unplug it 24 hours early so the freezer section can defrost and drain. Put a towel down.
  • Shut off the water at the valve behind the fridge or under the sink for a dishwasher.
  • Measure doorways if you're unsure. Most refrigerators are 30–36 inches deep, and some standard interior doors are only 30 inches wide. Taking a door off its hinges first can save 15 minutes.
  • Clear a path. Move rugs, shoes, and anything else between the appliance and the exit.

What Happens to It After It Leaves

Once an appliance is on our truck, it goes one of three ways. Working units in good shape are offered for resale or donation when someone will take them. Refrigerant-bearing units go to a facility with certified recovery equipment. Everything else is mostly steel, and steel is one of the most recycled materials in the country, so it goes to scrap metal recycling. Very little of a typical appliance ends up as landfill waste.
