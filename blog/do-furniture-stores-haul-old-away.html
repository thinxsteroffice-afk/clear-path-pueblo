DO FURNITURE STORES HAUL OLD AWAY?

Yes — some furniture stores do remove old furniture, but the offer is narrower than the ads make it sound. In and around Pueblo West, the big retailers on Dillon Drive in Pueblo (American Furniture Warehouse, Ashley, Furniture Row) will generally haul away an old piece only when they're delivering a new one that replaces it, one-for-one, same category, from the same room. Expect a fee in the $30–$150 range on top of a $79–$199 delivery charge, and expect the crew to refuse anything wet, infested, or too heavy for two people. Mattresses are the most common free-removal item; sofas and recliners usually cost extra. Buy-nothing, and no furniture store in Pueblo County will come get your couch.

What "haul-away" actually means at a furniture store

The removal is a rider on the delivery, not a service you can order by itself. That one fact explains almost every complaint we hear from Pueblo West customers standing in a driveway on Joe Martinez Blvd with a sofa nobody took.

The typical rules, as of our last round of calls to the Pueblo stores in 2026:

  • One-for-one. Buy one recliner, they take one recliner. Buy a bedroom set, they'll usually take the bed and maybe the frame — not the dresser you also wanted gone.
  • Same category. A new sectional doesn't buy you removal of an old dining table. Some stores enforce this loosely; most enforce it exactly.
  • Delivery-to-room only. If you save money by choosing "threshold" or garage drop-off (often $40–$60 cheaper), you usually forfeit haul-away entirely. The crew isn't coming inside.
  • Driver discretion. Any visible bed bug evidence, cat urine, mold, or water damage and the answer is no, on the spot, with no refund of the haul-away fee you already paid — you dispute it later.
  • No disassembly. Delivery crews are on a route with a 4-hour window and 8–14 stops. They will not take apart a bed frame, unbolt a sectional, or pull a sleeper sofa's mechanism. If it doesn't fit through the door as-is, it stays.

Mattresses are the exception worth knowing: because Colorado retailers have to handle used bedding under state sanitation rules anyway, most Pueblo stores will take the old mattress and box spring free with a new mattress purchase. That's the single best deal in furniture removal, and you should use it.

> If you're buying replacement furniture anyway, take the store's haul-away. Paying us $125 to remove a couch the store would have taken for $40 is a bad trade, and we'll tell you that on the phone.

The Pueblo West geography problem nobody mentions

Pueblo West sits roughly 8–14 miles from the Dillon Drive retail corridor depending on whether you're off Purcell, McCulloch, or out past Industrial Blvd. That's a 15–25 minute drive — close enough that delivery is available, far enough that it matters in two ways.

First, several retailers treat Pueblo West as an outer delivery zone. We've seen surcharges of $20–$50 over the in-city rate, and delivery days limited to two or three per week rather than daily. If your new sofa can only come Thursday, your old sofa can only leave Thursday.

Second, the lead time. Special-order upholstery from the Pueblo stores commonly runs 4–10 weeks, and we've seen custom sectionals quoted at 12 weeks. In-stock items deliver in 3–10 days. Your old furniture sits in the house the entire time, because the removal is tied to the delivery. For people mid-move with a 30-day closing or a lease turnover date, that timeline simply doesn't work.

When you should skip the store — and skip us too

This is the section most junk removal companies leave out, so here it is plainly. Hiring [furniture removal in Pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) is the wrong call in several common situations.

You own a pickup and have one item. A dresser, a chair, a small table — load it and drive it. Landfill and transfer station minimum loads in the Pueblo area run roughly $25–$45 for a pickup-bed load, with a $15–$25 surcharge per mattress. You'll spend an hour and about $35 total. We'd charge $95–$150 for the same item because our minimum has to cover a two-person crew, a truck, fuel, and the same dump fee. That math favors you, not us.

You don't own a truck but you're handy. A Home Depot load-'n-go rental runs about $19 for the first 75 minutes, roughly $5 per additional 15 minutes. A U-Haul pickup is around $19.95/day plus $0.99/mile — call it $45–$60 round trip from Pueblo West with the dump fee. Still cheaper than hiring anyone, if you can lift 120 pounds with a friend.

The furniture is genuinely still good. A clean, structurally sound sofa or dining set should go to a person, not a landfill. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar Pueblo-area nonprofits will often pick up free, though scheduling commonly runs 2–4 weeks out and they reject anything with rips, stains, pet damage, sagging frames, or missing hardware. Our honest estimate from what we see on trucks: more than half of what people call "donation quality" gets declined at the door. But the half that qualifies shouldn't be paying us anything. List it free on a local Pueblo West buy/sell group and it's usually gone in 48 hours.

You have a whole-house volume and a flexible timeline. If you're clearing 3+ rooms, a roll-off dumpster for a week often beats per-item pricing. Our pricing shines on one visit, no container on your driveway, we do the lifting. It does not shine against a $375–$550 dumpster you fill yourself over five days.

You can wait for a community cleanup. The Pueblo West Metropolitan District has historically run seasonal community cleanup events with drop-off containers for residents. Check pueblowest.org for current dates and accepted items before you pay anybody. If your sofa can sit in the garage until then, that's free.

Our actual limitations, named

  • Volume pricing cuts both ways. We price by how much truck space you fill, not weight. A 300-pound sleeper sofa is a bargain for you. A huge, light, three-piece sectional eats a third of the truck and prices like it — often $225–$350.
  • Minimum charge is real. A single nightstand still triggers the minimum. If your total is one small item, you're overpaying and we'd rather you know before we roll.
  • Stairs and tight turns add cost. Narrow basement stairs or a second-floor walkup typically adds $25–$75 for the labor and the risk.
  • We can't take everything. Paint, solvents, propane tanks, tires, and automotive fluids are out. Anything that looks like an active bed bug infestation gets a different conversation and a different price, because the truck needs treatment afterward.
  • Same-day isn't guaranteed. We hold slots, but a Saturday in April or a post-holiday week can push you out 2–4 days. If your closing is tomorrow, say so when you call and we'll be honest about whether we can hit it.
