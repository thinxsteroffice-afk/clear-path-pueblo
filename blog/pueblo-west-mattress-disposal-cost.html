PUEBLO WEST MATTRESS DISPOSAL COST

Mattress disposal in Pueblo West runs $75 to $95 for a single mattress-and-box-spring set picked up from inside your home, and ClearPath Junk Removal can usually be there within 24 to 48 hours. Additional mattresses on the same trip drop to $35 to $50 each. That price covers the labor, the truck, the stairs, the transfer station fee, and the disposal paperwork. If you'd rather not pay for a service at all, you can haul the mattress yourself to the Pueblo City Landfill on North Purcell for a minimum load charge in the $28 to $35 range, plus about 45 minutes of driving round trip from Pueblo West and the cost of borrowing a truck. Both options are legitimate. This page explains exactly when each one makes sense.

What "Mattress Disposal Plus" Actually Is, and Why You Landed Here

Mattress Disposal Plus is a national brokerage. You book online, they route the job to a local contractor, and the contractor shows up. Their published pricing typically starts around $139 for a single mattress in most metros, and they operate on a ZIP-code coverage map rather than a physical yard.

Here's the part nobody tells you: Pueblo West sits at the edge of a lot of those national coverage maps. ZIP codes 81007 and 81008 frequently return either "no availability" or a schedule window five to nine days out, because the broker has to find a subcontractor willing to drive out past the city limits for one mattress. When they do find one, the subcontractor is often a Colorado Springs or Denver crew treating your job as a filler stop.

> A national booking form gives you a confirmation number. It does not give you a truck that knows how to get down Joe Martinez Boulevard in a snow week.

If you found this page searching for a national brand and you're in Pueblo West, you're comparing a $139-plus brokered booking against a $75 to $95 local pickup. That's the honest frame.

Pueblo West Mattress Disposal Pricing, Line by Line

Real numbers for the 81007 area as of 2026:

  • Single mattress, curbside or garage: $65 to $75
  • Mattress and box spring set, from inside the home: $75 to $95
  • Each additional mattress on the same trip: $35 to $50
  • King set with a bulky wooden foundation: $95 to $120
  • Four to six mattresses from a rental turnover: $220 to $340 total, which lands around $55 each
  • Same-day surcharge, when a slot exists: $25 to $40

The Pueblo City Landfill charges roughly $28 to $35 for a minimum pickup-truck load, and a mattress alone often triggers that minimum whether you bring one or three. Puebla County transfer options and hours shift seasonally, so call before you drive. Adding a second and third mattress to your own truck costs you nothing extra at the scale, which is the single biggest argument for hauling it yourself.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the section that costs us money, and it's the most useful one on the page.

Skip the service entirely if you own a truck and have one mattress. You will spend $28 to $35 at the scale and about 45 to 60 minutes of your Saturday. Paying $75 to have someone else do a task you can finish before lunch is a convenience purchase, not a necessity. Say so out loud and decide.

Skip it if your mattress is genuinely clean and under about eight years old. A queen set in good condition has real donation value. Posadas Pueblo, local church resource ministries, and the Pueblo Rescue Mission periodically accept clean bedding, though acceptance rules change and most will refuse anything with a stain, an odor, or a tear. Call first. A donated mattress costs you zero and helps a household that needs one. We would rather you do that than pay us.

Skip it if you're waiting on a retailer delivery. Big-box furniture and mattress retailers will haul away your old set for $0 to $30 when they deliver the new one, and most Pueblo-area deliveries include it if you ask at the point of sale. If your new bed arrives in nine days, wait the nine days. Do not pay us $85 to solve a problem your delivery driver will solve for the price of asking.

Skip it if you have a full house of junk. One mattress at $75 is fine. One mattress plus a couch plus a broken dryer plus 12 bags of garage debris booked as four separate items is bad math. Bundle it into a single [junk removal Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) load and you'll pay by volume instead of by item, which usually saves 30 to 45 percent versus itemized pricing.

Skip it if you need a bed bug remediation. We will take a bed-bug-exposed mattress, but we bag it, we charge $40 to $60 extra for containment, and hauling the mattress out does not solve an infestation. If the bugs are in the frame, the baseboards, and the carpet, you need a licensed pest control company first. The Recycling Claim, and Where It Breaks Down

A lot of mattress disposal marketing promises recycling. Here's the ground truth for Southern Colorado.

Colorado has no statewide mattress stewardship law. California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon do, which is why national companies can honestly claim high recycling rates in those states and quietly say nothing about Colorado. The nearest genuine mattress deconstruction facilities are in the Denver metro, roughly 110 miles north of Pueblo West. Driving a single mattress 220 miles round trip to recycle about 20 pounds of steel and 12 pounds of foam is worse for the environment than landfilling it, and it costs more than the mattress is worth.

What actually happens locally:

  • Steel innerspring units get pulled and scrapped when volume justifies the labor, roughly 15 to 25 pounds of recoverable steel per queen mattress
  • Memory foam and latex have essentially no local recovery market and go to the landfill
  • Wood box spring frames can be broken down and diverted, but only when we're already running a construction load
  • Everything else, honestly, is landfilled

If a company tells you your Pueblo West mattress is being 90 percent recycled, ask them which facility and how far away it is. Most will not have an answer.

Failure Modes Worth Knowing About

Things go wrong in predictable ways. These are the ones we see.

Stairwell geometry. A king mattress will physically not clear some tight L-shaped stairwells or narrow basement doors in older Pueblo homes. Foam mattresses bend. Innerspring mattresses do not. If it came in before a wall was built, it may need to be cut, which we can do but which means the mattress is destroyed and non-donatable.

Weather. A wet mattress can absorb 40 to 80 pounds of water and gets significantly heavier and harder to carry. Between December and March, wind on Pueblo West's open lots regularly hits 40 mph, which can add 20 to 30 minutes to a two-person carry. Store the mattress inside or under cover until pickup day.

Illegal dumping penalties. Colorado litter enforcement fines for illegal dumping start in the $500 to $1,000 range and can climb substantially for repeat or large-volume violations. Pueblo County actively investigates roadside mattress dumping, and mattresses carry manufacturer tags with retailer and date information. The $75 you saved is not worth that exposure.

Curbside pickup isn't a thing here. Pueblo West Metro District does not run a residential bulk-item curbside program the way some Front Range municipalities do. Waste haulers in the area generally require a scheduled bulk pickup with its own fee, and mattresses are frequently excluded outright. Verify with your specific hauler rather than assuming.
