JUNK REMOVAL & DEMOLITION COMPANY

A junk removal and demolition company takes down a structure on your property and hauls every piece away in the same visit, so you don't have to hire a demo crew and then book a separate hauler or rent a dumpster. In Pueblo West, that usually means sheds, decks, fences, playsets, hot tubs, above-ground pools, small concrete slabs, and interior tear-outs like old cabinets or carpet. Typical jobs here cost $400 to $2,500 and take 2 to 8 hours. Disposal weight usually affects the price more than labor does. This kind of company does not take down whole houses, remove asbestos, or handle commercial structural demolition. If your project includes any of those, you need a different contractor, and this page explains why.

What "Light Demolition Plus Hauling" Covers in Pueblo West

Pueblo West lots are often half an acre to several acres. Over 20 or 30 years they collect outbuildings, decking, and fencing that dry out in the sun and wind. Here's what we take down and haul away most often:

  • Wood and metal sheds up to about 200 square feet, including the floor framing and skids. See our [shed demolition and removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) page.
  • Decks and fencing, including footings when they come up cleanly. See [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo).
  • Hot tubs. They're usually cut into sections, because a 400 to 800 lb spa rarely fits through a side gate. See [hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo).
  • Above-ground pools, including the steel walls, the liner, and the sand base.
  • Concrete patios, walkways, and rock beds. See [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Interior tear-outs: cabinets, carpet, non-structural partition walls, and old fixtures.
  • Grading and cleanup after removal, using our [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo).

The advantage is that one crew does the whole job. A crew that only demolishes leaves you with a pile. A hauler that only hauls won't pick up a saw. A combined crew starts and finishes the job on the same day, and you only pay for one trip.

Concrete is what surprises people. Concrete weighs roughly 145 to 150 lbs per cubic foot. A 10×10 patio poured 4 inches thick holds about 33 cubic feet of concrete, which comes to roughly 2.5 tons. That's heavier than many pickup trucks, and you pay for the weight in disposal fees, trips, and equipment time. A small patio can easily cost more to remove than a large wooden shed.

> The cost of a demo job usually comes down to what the material weighs and where it has to go. The labor to knock it down is the smaller part.

The Pueblo West Detail Most Demo Crews Ignore: Your Septic Field

Most of Pueblo West runs on individual septic systems rather than city sewer. A loaded skid steer can weigh more than 7,000 lbs. Driving it across a leach field can crush distribution lines or compact the soil so it stops absorbing water. A septic repair or replacement can cost several thousand dollars, sometimes more than $10,000, which is far more than the demolition job.

Before any equipment comes onto your lot, we ask where your tank and field are. If you don't know, the county septic permit records for your address usually include a site drawing. If the only route to a backyard shed runs over the leach field, we take the shed apart by hand and carry it out instead. That adds maybe 1 to 2 hours of labor, which is a small cost next to a septic repair.

A few other things to check before demolition starts on a Pueblo West property:

  • Buried utility lines. Shed power is often run through a shallow, homemade trench. Call Colorado 811 before anyone pulls footings or posts.
  • Wind. Afternoon gusts here regularly top 30 mph. Insulation, roofing felt, and loose siding have to be bagged or tied down while we work so they don't end up in a neighbor's yard.
  • Wells. If you're on a private well, tell us where the wellhead is so equipment stays clear of it.

When You Should NOT Hire Us (or Anyone Like Us)

This section may cost us some jobs, but sending you to the wrong kind of contractor would cost you more.

1. Whole-house or structural demolition. If you're removing a home, a garage attached to the house, or any load-bearing wall, hire a licensed general or demolition contractor. That work involves utility disconnects, permits from the Pueblo Regional Building Department, and engineering decisions. A junk removal and demolition company is the wrong fit, and any company in our category that says otherwise is taking on risk that becomes yours.

2. Anything that might contain asbestos. Colorado's asbestos rules (CDPHE Regulation 8) require an inspection by a certified asbestos inspector before you disturb more than 32 square feet, 50 linear feet, or the volume of a 55-gallon drum of suspect material. Popcorn ceilings, old vinyl floor tile, some siding, and pipe wrap from older homes all qualify. We stop work if we find suspect material. We don't remove it, and you shouldn't hire anyone who isn't certified to do so.

3. Pre-1978 painted surfaces in large amounts. For homes built before 1978, federal lead-safe rules apply once a job disturbs more than 6 sq ft of interior or 20 sq ft of exterior painted surface. An old painted shed or fence may be fine to remove. A full interior tear-out of a 1970s home needs lead-safe practices, so ask about this before you book anyone.

4. You have a truck, a free weekend, and a 6×8 shed. Take it down yourself. A small, light wooden shed comes apart with a reciprocating saw and a pry bar in an afternoon. If it fits in two pickup loads, you'll save a few hundred dollars by hauling it to the landfill yourself.

5. You're mid-remodel and already paying for a roll-off. If your contractor has a dumpster on site, filling it costs nothing extra. A 20-yard roll-off usually rents for about $450 to $650 a week in this area. If you have the time and someone to do the loading, that can be cheaper than a crew. Our pricing pays for labor and speed, so it only makes sense if you need those.

6. A metal shed in decent shape. Some steel sheds are worth something as scrap, and someone on a local marketplace may take one for free if they dismantle it. Try listing it for one to two weeks before you pay to have it removed.

7. Frozen-ground concrete work. Pulling footings or breaking slabs in January is slower and harder on the ground around them. Unless you're facing a closing date, a concrete job priced in winter may cost less if you wait until spring.

8. Single small items. Most haulers, including us, charge a minimum. If all you need gone is one old recliner, the minimum will feel expensive. Put a few items together into one trip, or check whether the county has a bulky-item drop-off day.

What Happens to the Debris

The cheapest disposal isn't always the landfill. Before anything goes to the landfill, we sort the load:

  • Metal (shed panels, pool walls, hot tub frames, fence posts) goes to scrap recycling.
  • Clean concrete and rock goes to a recycling or fill site when one will accept it. Sending heavy material there instead of the landfill keeps disposal costs down.
  • Usable lumber and fixtures are set aside for donation when they're in good enough condition.
  • Everything else, such as rotted wood, shingles, and treated lumber, goes to the landfill.

Sorting adds about 15 to 30 minutes to a job. On a heavy load it can save more than it costs, and we pass that saving on in the quote.
