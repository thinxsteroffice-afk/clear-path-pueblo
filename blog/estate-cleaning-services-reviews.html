ESTATE CLEANING SERVICES REVIEWS

Estate cleaning services near you in Pueblo West typically run $400–$1,800 for a partial cleanout (one or two rooms, furniture and boxed goods) and $2,200–$6,500 for a full 1,400–2,000 sq ft house including garage and shed. Most Pueblo West jobs finish in 1–3 days with a 2–4 person crew. On reviews: the honest read is that estate cleanout companies serving Pueblo West carry between 40 and 300 Google reviews each, and a 4.9 average built on 41 reviews tells you far less than a 4.7 built on 280. What actually predicts a good outcome is whether the company gave you a written per-load or per-hour rate before the truck arrived, and whether they let you keep what they pull out of drawers.

What "estate cleaning" actually covers, and what it doesn't

The phrase gets used for three different jobs that cost very different amounts:

  • Estate cleanout — hauling everything out: furniture, appliances, boxes, garage, shed, yard. This is what most people mean and what we do. See [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo).
  • Estate sale — an auctioneer or liquidator prices and sells contents, usually keeping 30–45% of gross. They are not haulers. Most leave the unsold 60% of the house sitting where it was on Sunday night.
  • Deep cleaning / turnover clean — mops, baseboards, carpet shampoo, oven. That is a janitorial service, not a junk removal company. We do not do it, and a company that claims to do both well for one price is usually doing one of them badly.

A common Pueblo West sequence: estate sale on a Saturday, we haul the remainder on Monday, a cleaning crew comes Tuesday, and the house lists Thursday. Three vendors, not one.

  • Quarter truck load (a bedroom set, 10–15 boxes): $180–$290

On a half-acre or one-acre parcel off McCulloch or Purcell, there is almost always a shed, a burn pile, a dead swamp cooler, and 15 years of yard debris that nobody mentions on the phone. Budget an extra 20–30% over a comparable in-city Pueblo estate. When you should NOT hire us

This is the part most estate cleanout pages leave out. Hiring a hauler is wrong for a meaningful share of the people who call us, and we would rather say so now than after we've charged you.

  • You haven't finished probate or the personal representative letters aren't issued. In Colorado, informal probate through Pueblo County District Court usually takes 10–21 days to appoint a PR, and the estate stays open a minimum of six months. If you clear the house before you have authority, you personally own the fight with the sibling who wanted the dining set. We have watched it happen twice. Wait.
  • The contents are actually worth something. If there's a running vehicle, a gun safe, sterling flatware, mid-century furniture, or tools, call a liquidator first. A decent Pueblo-area estate sale on a full house grosses $4,000–$15,000. Paying us $3,000 to landfill that is a $10,000 mistake.
  • You have more than 60 days and a pickup truck. The Pueblo transfer station takes self-hauled residential loads, and a two-person family clearing a 1,600 sq ft house over eight weekends will spend maybe $250–$450 in dump fees plus their own labor. You are buying speed and your own back, not magic.
  • Anyone in the family is not yet ready. The single most common failure mode we see is not price. It's a crew halfway through the garage when a daughter arrives and asks us to stop. We stop, we still bill for the time on site, and nobody is happy. If the family isn't aligned in writing on what leaves, postpone.
  • You need documents and valuables sorted, item by item. We are not a sorting service. Our crews set aside obvious paperwork, photos, cash, and jewelry when we find them, and we've handed back everything from a $2,300 bank envelope taped under a drawer to a shoebox of 1940s letters. But a 6-hour crew moving 5,000 lbs will miss things. If a specific document or heirloom matters, find it before we arrive.

> If the contents are valuable, hire a liquidator. If the family isn't ready, hire nobody yet. We are the right call when the decision is already made and the house needs to be empty by a date.

What actually goes wrong on estate jobs

  • Hidden square footage. Crawl spaces and attics in older Pueblo West manufactured homes are routinely full and rarely mentioned. This is the #1 cause of a quote going up mid-job.
  • Hazardous materials. We cannot take paint, solvents, pesticides, propane tanks, or automotive fluids to the transfer station. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events a handful of times a year; outside those windows you're holding them or paying a specialty disposal fee of roughly $75–$200.
  • Refrigerants. Fridges, freezers, and window ACs need certified evacuation. Expect a $25–$45 per-unit surcharge from any legitimate hauler — see [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Biohazard. Bodily fluids, a death that went undiscovered for days, or heavy rodent infestation is remediation work, not junk removal. That's a licensed restoration contractor at $3,000–$10,000, and we will tell you to call one.
  • Weather. From November through March, a Pueblo West job with an outdoor component can lose a full day to wind or ice. Build a buffer if you have a closing date.

How to read the reviews without getting fooled

The search that brought you here was about reviews, so here's the practical filter:

  • Ignore the average, read the 3-star reviews. Five-stars are written by people whose job went normally. One-stars are often disputes about scope. The 3-stars are where you learn how a company behaves when something goes sideways.
  • Look for reviews that name a price. "They quoted $850 and charged $850" is worth thirty vague reviews about a friendly crew.
  • Check review dates against crew turnover. A cluster of great reviews in 2022 and mediocre ones since usually means the original owner sold or stopped running crews.
  • Be skeptical of national brands' local ratings. Franchise operations pool reviews across a metro; the Pueblo West truck may not be the one that earned the 4.8.
  • Ask for two references from the last 90 days, specifically estate jobs. Any company doing real volume can produce them in an hour. A company that can't is not doing many of these.

One more: a company with 40 reviews and no negatives at all is not necessarily better than one with 200 reviews and six negatives. Anyone who has hauled 200 estates has disappointed someone. What matters is the reply.

What our estate process looks like

  • Walkthrough and written quote — free, usually within 24–48 hours of your call. We quote by volume and access, in writing, before anyone touches anything. Same-day quotes are common in Pueblo West.
  • A "do not touch" pass — you tape or tag what stays. Blue tape, painter's tape, anything. This takes 20 minutes and prevents 90% of the problems in this article.
  • The haul — a 3-bed house is typically 6–10 crew hours across 1–2 days. We work room by room so you can stop us at any point.

  • Broom-clean handoff — floors swept, nothing left. Not a deep clean. If your realtor wants carpets shampooed, that's a separate vendor.

Straight answers to the questions we get most

How fast can you start? Usually 2–5 business days out. In April–June and September, closer to 7–10 days — that's peak season here.

Do you charge for the estimate? No, and you should not pay for one. Any Pueblo West company charging an estimate fee for a residential estate is out of step with the market.

Do you buy the contents? No. Some haulers offer a "we'll credit you for resellable items" discount. Be careful: that credit is almost always 5–10% of what the items would bring at a real sale, and it gives the crew a financial interest in what leaves the house.

Can you work if I'm out of state? Yes. Roughly a third of our estate jobs are managed by a family member in another state via photos and video walkthrough. Get a lockbox on the door, and expect the quote to carry a 10–15% contingency because nobody has physically opened the crawl space.

What if you find money or documents? They go in a box on the kitchen counter, and we call you. Every time.
