ESTATE CLEANOUT COST: 2026 PRICING

A typical estate cleanout in Pueblo West runs $1,800 to $3,500 for a standard 3-bedroom ranch on a half-acre lot with an attached garage — that's the number most families here land on. Smaller jobs (a 1-bedroom apartment or a mobile home with furniture only) come in around $600 to $1,200. Larger properties with a detached shop, two sheds, and 20 years of accumulation run $4,500 to $9,000. Hoarding-level conditions can exceed $12,000. The single biggest cost driver isn't square footage — it's whether you need us to *sort* or just *haul*. Sort-and-haul jobs cost roughly 40–60% more than haul-everything jobs, because sorting is billed by the hour and hauling is billed by volume.

What Actually Determines Your Price

Most people assume estate cleanout pricing works off the house size. It doesn't. It works off truckload volume plus labor hours plus disposal surcharges. Here's how those three stack up on a real Pueblo West job:

  • Volume — We price by how much of the trailer you fill. A 16-foot dump trailer holds roughly 14 cubic yards. A full load runs $550 to $750 depending on weight. Half loads run $300 to $425. Our minimum charge is $175, which covers about a pickup-bed worth.
  • Labor — Straight loading of open, accessible items is baked into the load price. Sorting, boxing, disassembly, or anything where we're carrying items down from an attic runs $85 per person per hour, and estate jobs typically need 2–3 people.
  • Disposal surcharges — The Pueblo landfill charges by the ton, and certain items carry their own fees on top. Mattresses typically add $15–$25 each. Refrigerators, freezers, and AC units with refrigerant add $30–$50 each for evacuation. Tires run $8–$20 each depending on size. Paint, solvents, and old pesticides from the garage shelf are household hazardous waste and are not landfill-legal — those need a HHW drop-off event or a licensed handler, and we'll flag them rather than take them.

A 3-bedroom Pueblo West house usually produces 4 to 7 trailer loads. That's the arithmetic behind the $1,800–$3,500 range.

Pueblo West Costs More Than People Expect — Here's Why

Pueblo West isn't Pueblo proper, and the price difference is real. Three local factors push estate cleanout costs up 15–30% compared to an in-city job of the same size:

  • Lot size. Standard Pueblo West lots run a half-acre to a full acre. That means detached sheds, well houses, RV pads, and a back fence line where two decades of scrap metal, old fencing, and a dead swamp cooler have quietly accumulated. Families almost always budget for the house and forget the yard. Add $400 to $1,200 for a typical Pueblo West back lot.
  • No municipal bulk pickup. Unlike some Front Range municipalities, there's no city large-item pickup program to lean on out here. Every couch and mattress is a private haul. That removes the cheap partial option a lot of online cost guides assume you have.
  • Metro District covenant pressure. Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenants restrict visible outdoor storage, inoperable vehicles, and accumulated debris. If a neighbor has already filed a complaint on a vacant estate property, you're often working against a 10 to 30 day compliance window, which removes your ability to shop three bids over a month.

Driveway access matters too. Gravel and dirt driveways after a wet week won't hold a loaded dump trailer, and a truck that has to shuttle 150 feet by hand-cart instead of backing to the door adds 1 to 2 hours of labor.

When an Estate Cleanout Service Is the Wrong Call

This is the part most junk removal sites leave out, so here it is plainly. You should not hire us in these situations:

  • The estate has real resale value and you haven't had it looked at. We are haulers, not appraisers. If Dad's shop has a Bridgeport mill, a decent welder, and 40 years of Snap-on, an auctioneer or estate sale company may net the family $3,000 to $15,000 and charge a commission of 25–40% instead of you paying us $4,000 to throw it in a trailer. Get one estate sale company to walk it first. It costs you nothing and one afternoon. If they pass on it, that's your answer — call us then.
  • You have a truck, a trailer, and three free weekends. A self-haul of a 3-bedroom house is realistically 6 to 10 landfill trips at maybe $40–$65 per ton plus fuel and item surcharges — call it $400 to $900 in hard costs. You'll save $1,500–$2,500 and spend 40–60 hours of your life doing it, plus whatever a rented trailer runs. If your time is the thing you have and money is the thing you don't, do it yourself. We'll still take the last two loads you can't face.
  • The heirs haven't agreed. If two siblings are still arguing about the china cabinet, do not book a crew. Once it's in the trailer it's gone, and we've watched that turn into a family rupture that outlasts the $2,600 invoice. Sort out who decides *before* the date.
  • You're not the personal representative yet. Colorado probate gives authority to a specific person. If letters haven't been issued and you're clearing a house on the assumption it'll be fine, you're taking on personal liability we can't shield you from. Wait for the paperwork.
  • The estate is insolvent and you're paying out of pocket. If the house is going back to the lender or being sold as-is, a full white-glove cleanout may be money you never see again. Many Pueblo West buyers will take a house with junk in it at a discount smaller than the cleanout cost. Ask the listing agent what "as-is with contents" actually does to the price before spending $3,000.
  • It's one item. A single couch or a single fridge is a $175 minimum call. That's fair for us and bad value for you. Bundle it, or check [furniture removal pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) and [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) for what makes sense as a standalone trip.

The Limitations You Should Know Before You Book

Honest constraints, not fine print:

  • We don't do document review. If you want every envelope opened to find a bond certificate, that's a family job or a fiduciary's job. We'll box papers and set them aside, and that boxing time bills at $85/hour.
  • Donation is not free money. We route usable furniture to local donation when it's accepted, but donation centers in Pueblo County refuse stained upholstery, cribs, non-working electronics, and most particle-board furniture. Realistically 10–25% of an estate's contents are donatable. The rest is landfill regardless of who hauls it, and the donation receipt value is set by the charity, not by us.
  • Biohazard, animal waste, and unattended-death scenes are outside our scope. That's a licensed remediation trade. Heavy hoarding with contamination needs that crew first — [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) explains where our line sits.
  • Quotes over the phone are guesses. Phone estimates on estate jobs miss by 30–50% in both directions. A 20-minute walkthrough gets you a fixed number. Anyone who firm-quotes a whole estate sight-unseen is either padding it or planning to revise it on the day.
  • Weather moves jobs. High wind days shut down open-trailer loading in Pueblo West more often than people expect, and a snowed-in gravel driveway pushes a start date.

How to Cut the Bill by $500 to $1,500

  • Pre-tag rooms. Blue tape on what stays, nothing on what goes. This alone typically saves 3–6 labor hours.
  • Consolidate to one staging area. Anything you can move to the garage or driveway before we arrive loads at truckload rates instead of hourly.
  • Separate scrap metal. Appliances, cast iron, and steel fencing pulled into one pile can offset $100–$300 in disposal costs.
  • Book a single visit. Two half-day trips cost more than one full day because you're paying travel twice.
  • Empty the fridge and freezer first. Full appliances are heavier, messier, and slower — a stocked chest freezer can add an hour.
