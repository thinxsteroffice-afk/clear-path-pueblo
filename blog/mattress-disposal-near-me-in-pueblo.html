MATTRESS DISPOSAL NEAR ME IN PUEBLO

Getting a mattress out of a house in Pueblo West and into a place that will legally take it comes down to three options, and the price gap between them is bigger than most people expect.

The direct answer: If you want to haul it yourself, the closest legal disposal point is the Pueblo City Landfill (Waste Connections) at 1000 Lane 15 in Pueblo, roughly 12–18 miles from most Pueblo West addresses depending on whether you're off McCulloch or out past Purcell. Expect a minimum gate fee in the $28–$45 range for a pickup load, plus a mattress surcharge that commonly runs $10–$25 per piece because mattresses are banned from compaction at most Colorado facilities. Budget 75–110 minutes round trip. If you'd rather not do that, full-service pickup in Pueblo West typically runs $85–$135 for one mattress and box spring, done in about 20 minutes at your curb or bedroom door.

That's the whole decision. The rest of this page is the detail that decides which column you land in — including the cases where hiring us is the wrong call.

Why a Mattress Costs More Than Its Weight Suggests

A queen mattress weighs 60–90 lbs. A pickup bed full of drywall scrap weighs 1,200 lbs. The drywall is cheaper to dispose of. That surprises people.

The reason is mechanical, not moral. Mattresses are steel coils wrapped in foam and fabric. When a landfill compactor rolls over one, the springs wrap around the drum shaft. A wrapped compactor is a several-thousand-dollar repair and a half-day of downtime. So facilities either charge a per-unit surcharge to cover the labor of handling them separately, or refuse them at the working face and route them to a segregated pile.

Practical consequences for a Pueblo West household:

  • Per-item pricing, not per-pound. Two twin mattresses usually cost more to dump than one king, despite weighing less combined.
  • Box springs count separately. A queen set is two items, not one. This is the single most common surprise on a self-haul receipt.
  • Cash or card at the scale. Assume the scale house wants payment before you're allowed past the gate, and assume no on-site help unloading.
  • Hours are shorter than you think. Most municipal-area facilities close mid-afternoon Saturday and are shut Sunday. A Sunday-morning garage purge has nowhere to go until Monday.
  • Memory foam is not easier. No springs, but no compaction value either — same surcharge tier at most gates.

> The cheapest mattress disposal is almost never the fastest, and the fastest is almost never the cheapest. Pick which one you actually care about before you start calling.

The Real Self-Haul Math for Pueblo West

Here's an honest accounting for a household off Joe Martinez Blvd taking one queen set to the landfill themselves:

  • Gate minimum + two mattress surcharges: $48–$95
  • Fuel, ~30 miles round trip at 17 mpg: $5–$7
  • Ratchet straps if you don't own them: $18–$30 one time
  • Your time, 90 minutes: priced however you price your Saturday
  • Risk of an unsecured-load citation in Colorado: fines start around $100 and climb sharply if debris leaves the vehicle

All-in on a first-time self-haul with no straps in the garage: roughly $71–$132, which lands inside the range a full-service pickup costs. If you already own a truck and straps and are heading toward Pueblo anyway, self-haul wins clearly — call it $50–$70 and an hour. If you're renting a truck, you've already lost; a half-day rental plus mileage runs $45–$90 before you've paid a single disposal fee.

The variable nobody budgets for is the stairs. Getting a king pillowtop down a switchback staircase alone is where people tear a rotator cuff or gouge drywall. A 14-inch hybrid king is 130–140 lbs of floppy, handle-less mass. Two people minimum. If you're doing it solo, don't.

Bed Bugs, Bans, and the Rules That Actually Bite

Two legal points specific to Colorado that most disposal pages skip:

Colorado has no statewide mattress recycling law. Connecticut, Rhode Island, California, and Oregon run state-funded mattress stewardship programs where drop-off is free to residents. Colorado does not. Bills have been floated and not passed. Any page telling you to "find your free state recycling drop-off" is describing a program that does not exist here. There is no free municipal mattress amnesty day in Pueblo West on a reliable annual schedule.

Bed bugs change everything. If a mattress is infested, it must be sealed in plastic before it leaves the room — not after it's in the hallway, not at the curb. Dragging an infested mattress down a hallway seeds the entire path. Most junk removal companies, including reputable ones, will either refuse the job or charge a premium of $40–$75 per piece for the disposal bagging and truck decontamination. Disclose it upfront. A crew that discovers bugs mid-lift will stop, and you'll have paid a trip charge for nothing.

Also worth knowing: Pueblo West Metro District does not provide curbside bulk trash pickup as a metro service. Trash here is by private hauler contract, and bulky-item allowances vary by company — some include one or two bulk pickups a year with advance scheduling, some charge $25–$60 per item, some won't take mattresses at all. Check your specific hauler's terms before assuming the curb is an option. Setting a mattress at the road and hoping is how you end up with a code complaint and a soaked, unusable mattress after an afternoon thunderstorm.

What Actually Happens to the Mattress

Honest version: most mattresses in this region get landfilled, not recycled. Nationally, roughly 15–20 million mattresses are discarded per year, and recycling rates outside the stewardship states sit in the low single digits to low teens. Southern Colorado does not have a dedicated mattress deconstruction facility. The nearest meaningful recycling infrastructure is up the Front Range, and the transport cost to get a few units there exceeds the scrap value of the recovered steel — a mattress yields roughly 20–25 lbs of recoverable steel, worth about $2–$4 at current scrap prices.

So if a company tells you they "recycle every mattress," ask where. We'd rather tell you plainly: we route what we reasonably can, and the rest goes to a permitted facility. A genuinely clean, recent mattress in good shape is a different story — those can sometimes be placed, but donation channels for mattresses are far narrower than for [furniture](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo), because most charities in Colorado will not accept used mattresses at all for liability and sanitation reasons.
