What You Actually Pay, By Load Size

Junk removal is priced by volume in the truck, not by weight or hour. A standard dump trailer holds roughly 12 to 16 cubic yards, which is about the same as 4 to 5 pickup truck beds. Here's how Pueblo West garages tend to break down:

  • 1/4 trailer (3-4 cubic yards) — $175-$250. One corner cleared: a couple of bikes, a broken dresser, six or eight boxes of holiday stuff.
  • 1/2 trailer (6-8 cubic yards) — $275-$425. The most common single-bay cleanout. Old workbench, exercise equipment, tote pile, three bags of yard junk.
  • 3/4 trailer (9-12 cubic yards) — $450-$600. Full two-car garage where you've been parking outside for two years.
  • Full trailer (14-16 cubic yards) — $600-$800. Detached shop, or a garage plus the side-yard pile that came with it.
  • Multi-load — $1,100-$2,200. Estate situations, 30+ year accumulations, or a shop with a decade of construction leftovers.

Add-ons that move the number: appliances with refrigerant run $35-$75 each because the Freon has to be reclaimed before the metal is accepted. Mattresses are $40-$60. Tires are $8-$15 apiece and most transfer stations cap how many they'll take per trip. A cast-iron tub or a 300-pound gun safe adds labor, not volume — expect $75-$150 for the awkwardness.

> The single biggest cost driver in a Pueblo West garage isn't how much stuff there is. It's how many decisions haven't been made yet. Sorting is billable time.

The Pueblo West Landfill Math Nobody Puts On Their Website

This is the part that decides whether hiring anyone makes sense. Pueblo West sits about 12 to 15 miles from the Pueblo City Landfill on Goodnight Ave. If you have a truck and a trailer, your actual out-of-pocket for a DIY garage cleanout looks roughly like this:

  • Landfill minimum charge: $25-$35 per load for a small pickup, scaling to $55-$75 for a loaded trailer, based on weight at the scale.
  • Fuel for a 30-mile round trip: $8-$12 per run.
  • Your time: 6-9 hours for a full two-car garage if you're doing it alone, spread over two weekends because you'll be sore.
  • Trips required: 3 to 5 for a genuinely full garage, since a half-ton bed holds about 2.5 cubic yards heaped and you cannot legally overload it.

Total DIY cash cost: roughly $110 to $200. Total DIY time cost: a weekend and a half, plus whatever a chiropractor charges. So the honest framing is that hiring us costs you about $200 to $450 more in cash than doing it yourself, and buys back 6 to 9 hours plus your back. If your Saturday is worth more than $40/hour to you, the math works. If it isn't, rent a trailer for $60 and go.

Also worth knowing: Pueblo County's household hazardous waste drop-off does not accept everything, and the landfill won't take liquid paint, motor oil, pesticides, or car batteries in the regular waste stream. Neither will we. More on that below, because it's the number one reason a garage cleanout doesn't get finished in one visit.

When A Garage Cleanout Isn't Worth Hiring Out

Nobody else will tell you this, so here it is plainly. Do not call us if:

  • You have fewer than 8-10 items. A dresser, a mattress, and two bags is a $150-$175 minimum charge, and that minimum exists because the truck, fuel, insurance, and dump fee cost the same whether it's a quarter full or full. You will feel ripped off, and you'd be partially right. Either wait until you've accumulated more, or check our [furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) and [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) pages for single-item pricing that's honest about the floor.
  • You haven't sorted yet. This is the real one. If you call us and then spend the visit holding up items asking "should I keep this?", you're paying two people $100-$150 an hour combined to watch you make decisions. Sort first. Put keeps on one side, trash on the other. We'll do the labor; we can't do the deciding.
  • It's mostly recyclable metal. If your garage is largely scrap steel, appliances, and old fencing, a scrap hauler may take it free or even pay you $0.06-$0.12 per pound. A 400-pound pile of clean steel is $25-$50 in your pocket instead of $200 out of it. We'd rather you knew.
  • It's mostly sellable. Decent power tools, a working snowblower, and mid-century furniture move fast on Pueblo-area Facebook groups. We are a disposal service, not a consignment service. Anything you put in our trailer, you have decided is worth $0 to you.
  • You need it done tomorrow at 7 a.m. in February. We book 2 to 5 days out in the spring and fall, and a Pueblo West winter storm can push a schedule. If you're closing on a house in 48 hours, tell us that on the first call so we can be straight about whether we can hit it.

What We Will Not Haul, And Why That Matters

Every junk removal company in Colorado has this list. Most bury it. Here's ours up front, because finding out mid-job is how a cleanout turns into two cleanouts:

  • Liquid paint, stains, solvents. Dried-out cans are fine. Wet ones aren't — Colorado's PaintCare program takes them free at participating Pueblo retailers, and there's usually a 5-gallon per visit limit.
  • Motor oil, antifreeze, pesticides, propane tanks. Household hazardous waste, and it needs the county's HHW event or a designated drop site.
  • Car batteries. Auto parts stores in Pueblo take these and usually credit you $8-$15.
  • Ammunition, firearms, medical sharps, asbestos-containing material. Not a policy preference — a regulatory hard stop.
  • Anything requiring a licensed abatement contractor. Older Pueblo West homes built before 1978 can have lead paint on trim and cabinetry stored in garages; if it's flaking, that's a different trade.

The practical impact: budget an extra $0 to $40 and one Saturday morning errand for the hazardous corner of your garage. It's usually four or five items.

The Failure Modes Of A Garage Cleanout

Roughly 1 in 5 garage cleanouts we quote turns out to be something other than a garage cleanout. Common ones:

The garage isn't the problem. If the garage is full because the house is full, clearing the garage buys about 8 to 14 months before it refills. That's a [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) conversation, and it deserves a different plan and a different budget — often $2,500-$6,000 and multiple days.

The floor is worse than the pile. Decades of oil staining, a cracked slab, or rodent damage under the shelving shows up only after the stuff is gone. That's not a scope we cover, and you'll want a concrete or pest quote before you get excited about epoxy.

The volume estimate was optimistic — usually yours. People underestimate garage volume by 30-50% on average, because you can't see behind the front row. We quote on site or from photos for this reason. A phone estimate given sight-unseen is a number designed to get us in your driveway, and we'd rather not start that way.

Second-load surprises. If the job exceeds one load, we tell you before we leave, not after. A second load is typically 60-75% of the first load's price, since the mobilization is already paid for.

How To Get The Cheapest Honest Quote

  • Take 6-8 photos in daylight from the open garage door, both back corners, and any side piles. Text them. That's usually enough for a firm number.
  • Pre-sort into three zones — keep, donate, haul. Even a rough sort cuts labor 20-40%.
  • Bundle it. If you also have a [shed to demolish](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo), yard debris, or an old hot tub, one trip is cheaper than three. Combined jobs typically save $100-$250 versus separate visits.
  • Book midweek. Tuesday through Thursday is our lighter window; Saturdays fill first in April, May, September, and October.
  • Ask what's included. Ours includes labor, hauling, dump fees, and a broom sweep. If another quote is $80 lower but excludes disposal fees, it isn't lower.
