PARKING GARAGE CLEANING COST GUIDE

Cleaning a parking structure or parking area around Pueblo West runs roughly $0.12–$0.30 per square foot for a full pressure wash with water recovery, $0.02–$0.05 per square foot for a dry sweep and blow-down, and $175–$750 for debris and bulk-junk haul-off depending on volume. One 30,000 sq ft garage level: about $3,600–$9,000 washed, $600–$1,500 swept. A typical Pueblo West commercial lot, apartment carport row, or storage-facility drive aisle: $400–$1,200 all in. ClearPath handles the debris and bulk-junk half of that work. Below, the honest part: where a dedicated pressure-washing contractor is the better buy, and why a lot of these calls shouldn't be paid for at all.

The search term hides three different jobs

"Parking garage cleaning" gets quoted as one line item, and that's where budgets go wrong. It's three separate trades with three separate price structures:

  • Debris and bulk removal — mattresses dumped in a stairwell, an abandoned couch in a corner stall, tenant move-out piles, shopping carts, tires, construction leftovers. Priced by volume and weight. $175 minimum, up to $750 for a fully loaded trailer.
  • Sweeping and blow-down — dry mechanical or walk-behind sweeping, gum and glass removal, blowing corners and drive lanes. $0.02–$0.05 per square foot, or roughly $0.008–$0.015 per stall per pass on a large deck.
  • Pressure washing — hot-water surface cleaning at 3,000–4,000 PSI, oil and transmission-fluid degreasing, stripe and curb detail, and (this is the expensive part) capturing and hauling the wash water. $0.12–$0.30 per square foot.

Most Pueblo West properties need the first one and can defer the third for years. Getting billed for all three on the same invoice is the single most common way people overpay here.

Real numbers, Pueblo West market, 2026

These are the ranges we and the local contractors we cross-refer with are actually quoting this year. Verify against your own bids — fuel, landfill tipping, and water rates all move.

  • Two-person crew with truck and trailer: $125–$175 per hour, one-hour minimum.
  • Single-item pickups (couch, mattress, water heater): $95–$175. Mattresses usually carry a $25–$40 disposal surcharge; appliances with refrigerant add $35–$50 for certified recovery.
  • Quarter trailer load: $275–$325. Half: $400–$475. Full: $600–$750.
  • Landfill tipping: roughly $40–$65 per ton for mixed municipal waste in Pueblo County, plus separate rates for tires ($5–$15 each) and clean concrete.
  • Surface lot litter sweep, 20,000 sq ft (about 60 stalls): $400–$700 for a one-time visit, $225–$350 on a recurring monthly schedule.
  • Oil-stain degreasing add-on: $0.05–$0.10 per square foot over the wash rate. A 40-stall deck with heavy drip staining adds $300–$800.
  • Vacuum water recovery: adds 25–40% to a wash quote. It's not optional in most cases — see the stormwater section.
  • Travel: Pueblo West to downtown Pueblo is about 12 miles and 20 minutes. Most local outfits, us included, don't charge a trip fee inside that radius; past roughly 25 miles expect $50–$100.
  • Turnaround: a two-level, 120-stall deck takes a 3-person crew about 10–14 hours to wash properly. A debris-only clearout of the same deck is usually 2–4 hours.

> If your "parking garage" is a 40-stall covered carport row behind an apartment building, you're looking at a $400 problem, not a $9,000 one. Get the debris out, sweep it, and stop.

Pueblo West barely has parking garages — and that changes the math

This is the thing no national price-calculator page will tell you: Pueblo West has essentially no multi-level parking structures. It's a low-density, unincorporated community built around surface parking. The nearest true decks are in downtown Pueblo, at Parkview and hospital campuses, and up in Colorado Springs.

So when someone in the 81007 searches this query, the actual property is almost always one of these:

  • A surface lot at a strip center on Purcell, McCulloch, or Platteville.
  • A covered carport row at an apartment or mobile-home community.
  • A drive aisle at a self-storage facility, which collects the worst of it — see [storage unit cleanout](/services/storage-unit-cleanout-pueblo) for what typically gets left behind.
  • An oversized residential garage or shop, which is a completely different service and priced completely differently. That's [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo), typically $250–$600.
  • A commercial building's back lot and dumpster corral, usually bundled into a [commercial office cleanout](/services/commercial-office-cleanout-pueblo).

Why this matters to your wallet: per-square-foot deck pricing assumes enclosed concrete, low ceilings, poor drainage, and a wash crew that has to contain every gallon. An open-air Pueblo West lot drains to grade and gets rinsed by weather. You should not be paying deck rates for a surface lot. If a bid quotes you $0.20/sq ft on an open lot with no containment work listed, that's a bid built from a template somewhere else.

Water, storm drains, and the line item people forget

Pueblo County sits under a municipal stormwater (MS4) permit framework, and the general rule across Colorado is the same: wash water carrying oil, soap, or sediment is not allowed to enter a storm drain. Not "discouraged" — it's a discharge violation, and the property owner is the responsible party, not the contractor. Confirm specifics with Pueblo County's stormwater program before any wash job; requirements differ between city limits and unincorporated Pueblo West.

Practical consequences for your quote:

  • A 4 GPM surface cleaner running 6 hours uses roughly 1,400 gallons. A full 30,000 sq ft deck can consume 2,500–4,000 gallons.
  • Vacuum recovery, berms, drain mats, and disposal of the recovered water are the 25–40% adder noted above. A bid without them is cheaper because it's shifting your liability, not saving you money.
  • Pueblo West Metropolitan District water is metered and this is a semi-arid basin. Under active drought restrictions, a large non-essential wash may be restricted outright. Check before you schedule.
  • Freeze risk runs roughly mid-October through mid-April. Washing below 40°F puts standing water on concrete overnight. Freeze-thaw cycling on Front Range concrete is a real spalling accelerator, and you can spend $9,000 on a wash that shortens the life of the slab.

When this is not worth paying for

The part that costs us business. Read it anyway.

Skip the pressure wash entirely if:

  • It's an open-air surface lot. Rain and snowmelt do most of what a wash does. A sweep two to four times a year plus debris pickup covers 90% of the visible difference at maybe 8% of the cost.
  • The concrete is already spalling or the sealant is failed. 4,000 PSI drives water into cracks and under delaminated surface. You'll accelerate the damage. Get a $250–$500 concrete assessment first; a re-seal at $1.50–$3.00 per square foot is the actual fix, and washing before you seal is part of that job, not a standalone purchase.
  • You're doing it for a property showing or a photo shoot. A wash looks great for about three weeks in a working lot. Time it or skip it.
  • The stains are old oil that has cured into the pores. Degreasing lifts fresh drips well and cured stains poorly. Expect 50–70% improvement on stains older than a year, not removal. Anyone promising "like new" on decade-old drip lines under a carport is selling you a disappointment.
  • It's November through March. Wait for spring unless there's a biohazard or a safety issue.

We do not own a hot-water recovery rig, and we'd rather tell you that than subcontract it at a markup. Ask us and we'll name the two or three local washing contractors we'd call ourselves.
  • You need recurring nightly or weekly lot service. A porter-service contract at $300–$800 per month beats calling a haul-off crew every time somebody dumps a mattress.

Where the value genuinely is: the initial clearout. A neglected carport row or storage drive aisle with two years of accumulation is a real, one-time $500–$1,500 job that meaningfully changes how a property shows and reads. After that, the marginal return on repeat cleaning drops off fast.

How to get a number you can trust in about ten minutes

  • Measure it. Length × width. Every legitimate bid is a function of square footage; if a contractor quotes without asking, they're guessing high.
  • Count the bulk items separately. Furniture, mattresses, appliances, tires. These price by piece and weight, not area. [Furniture removal](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) and [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) run on their own rate cards.
  • Photograph the worst corner and the drains. Drain condition drives the containment cost more than anything else on a deck.
  • Ask every bidder to line-item the three trades. Debris, sweep, wash. Any contractor who won't split them is protecting a margin.
  • Ask who's liable for the wash water. If the answer is vague, keep shopping.
