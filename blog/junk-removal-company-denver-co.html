JUNK REMOVAL COMPANY DENVER, CO?

Why the Denver search sends Pueblo West residents the wrong way

Plenty of people in Pueblo West search "Denver" by habit. Denver is the big city on the news and in the ads, so it feels like that's where the companies are. Some people just moved down from the metro and are still using the search terms they used up there. Either way, the results show companies with Denver phone numbers that will either turn down a Pueblo West job or quote it with a travel charge.

Here's what distance does to a junk removal job:

  • Drive time is billed labor. A two-person crew on a 230-mile round trip spends about 8 combined labor-hours just driving. At the $25–$35 an hour that Colorado haulers commonly pay crew, that's $200–$280 in wages before loading starts.
  • Fuel adds up fast. A loaded box truck usually gets 8–10 mpg. At around $3.20 a gallon, 230 miles works out to about $74–$92 in diesel or gas.
  • Scheduling windows get worse. A Denver crew driving down will usually stack your job at the start or end of their day, so a "morning" slot can turn into 1:30 p.m.
  • Dump fees don't drop. The crew still has to dispose of your load somewhere, either at a Pueblo-area facility or by hauling it 115 miles back north full.

> A junk removal quote is mostly labor, disposal, and truck time. Distance increases two of those three, and none of it goes into hauling more of your junk.

How junk removal pricing actually works

Most full-service junk removal companies in Colorado charge by volume, meaning how much of the truck your stuff fills. A standard junk truck holds about 15–17 cubic yards, roughly the space of 6–8 pickup beds. Quotes are usually given in fractions: minimum load, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, full.

Some things change the price no matter who you hire:

  • Heavy materials. Concrete, dirt, brick and roofing shingles fill a truck by weight long before they fill it by volume. Most companies price these separately or limit them per load. That's why we have a dedicated [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) service and don't quote them like a sofa.
  • Refrigerant appliances. Under EPA Section 608, refrigerators, freezers, dehumidifiers and window AC units have to have their refrigerant recovered by certified handling before they're scrapped. Expect a per-item fee of roughly $25–$75 from most haulers to cover it. See [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Electronics. Colorado banned landfill disposal of TVs, computers, monitors and similar electronics on July 1, 2013, so those have to go to an e-waste recycler, and that usually costs extra.
  • Mattresses. Many facilities charge a per-unit fee for mattresses and box springs because they're bulky and don't compact. Details are on our [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page.
  • Stairs, distance from the truck, and disassembly. A hot tub in a backyard with no gate access takes much more labor than one sitting next to the driveway.

Ask any company you call, us included, for a written, on-site price before loading begins, and ask whether that number already includes disposal fees. If a quote says "starting at," it isn't a quote yet.

When hiring us (or any junk removal company) is NOT worth it

This section costs us business. We're including it because a page that only tells you to call us isn't much use to you.

Don't hire us if you're in the Denver metro. We're a Pueblo West company. We'd have to charge you for the drive described above, and a Denver-based crew can almost certainly beat our price and our arrival time. Search for a crew based within about 20 miles of you.

Don't hire anyone for a single small item you can move yourself. If it's one recliner and you have a pickup, Pueblo County's landfill and transfer options will take it for much less than a minimum junk removal charge, which commonly runs $75–$150 across the industry. A minimum load is priced to cover the truck showing up, not the item.

Don't hire a full-service crew for a long, slow project. If you're remodeling a kitchen over 3–6 weeks, or doing a cleanout you want to work through on weekends, a roll-off dumpster is often the better deal. A 10–20 yard dumpster rental typically comes with a 7-day window and can usually be extended. You do the loading, and it costs less per cubic yard. Full-service junk removal wins when you want it gone in one visit and don't want to do the lifting.

Don't expect us to take hazardous waste. Paint (liquid), motor oil, pesticides, pool chemicals, propane tanks, asbestos-containing material and medical waste are off the truck for us and for nearly every legitimate junk hauler. If a company says it will take anything, ask where it's going. Those materials belong at a household hazardous waste drop-off, and a hauler that takes them anyway may be dumping illegally, which can come back on the property owner.

Don't hire us if donation is your main goal and you have time. We try to separate usable furniture and appliances for donation or recycling, but sorting takes time on the job. If everything is in good shape and you have a free Saturday, scheduling a free charity pickup yourself, which often has a 1–3 week wait, will save you money.

Know where things go wrong in this industry:

  • "Starting at" pricing that doubles once the truck is loaded and you've got no leverage left.
  • Unlicensed or uninsured operators with a single pickup truck. If one of them damages your siding or a worker gets hurt on your property, you could be the one paying. Ask for proof of liability insurance.
  • Unclear heavy-material charges. Dirt and concrete can triple the cost of a load that looks small. Get those priced separately and up front.
  • Weather. Pueblo West gets spring wind events and the odd winter storm. Outdoor jobs like hot tub removal or brush hauling sometimes have to be moved. A crew that promises a date regardless of conditions is either being optimistic or about to cut corners.
