COST TO REMOVE A JACUZZI TUB

A jacuzzi tub — the drop-in or built-in jetted tub inside your bathroom, not a backyard hot tub — costs $350 to $900 to remove in Pueblo West when a junk removal crew handles demo and haul-off on a standard 60" x 32" alcove unit. Straight haul-away of a tub already pulled out and sitting in the driveway runs $150 to $275. Add tile surround demo and you're at $700 to $1,400. Disposal at the Pueblo County landfill (Southside Landfill, 1002 S. Santa Fe Ave.) adds roughly $45 to $85 in tipping fees for a tub-plus-surround load. Whole-bathroom gut with fixtures, drywall, and flooring: $1,200 to $2,600.

Those are the numbers. Here's what actually moves them.

What Drives the Price on a Pueblo West Jacuzzi Tub

Four things set the bill, and only one of them is the tub itself.

  • Material. Acrylic and fiberglass jetted tubs weigh 70–110 lbs empty and cut apart with a reciprocating saw in 20–30 minutes. Cast iron jetted tubs — rare but real in 1970s–80s builds — run 300–450 lbs and need to be broken with a sledge or carried by three people. Cast iron adds $150–$300.
  • Access. A ground-floor bathroom with a 32"+ doorway and a straight shot to the driveway is the cheap case. A second-floor master over a turn in the stairs adds $100–$250 in labor. Pueblo West's typical single-story ranch on a half-acre lot is genuinely favorable here — most of our jobs in the 81007 zip are ground-floor with short carries.
  • Tile and deck surround. A drop-in tub sits in a framed deck, often with tile, mud bed, and cement board. Demoing that deck is 2–4 more labor hours and generates 400–900 lbs of debris that costs more to dump than the tub does. This is the single biggest swing factor.
  • Utility disconnect. Jetted tubs have a dedicated 20-amp GFCI circuit and often an inline heater. Capping supply lines, cutting the drain below the flange, and killing the circuit is 30–60 minutes. Most junk removal companies, including ours, will disconnect visible plumbing and pull the breaker but will not re-plumb or re-wire — that's a licensed trade.

Real Price Ranges by Scenario

| Scenario | Pueblo West range | Time on site | |---|---|---| | Tub already removed, in garage or driveway | $150–$275 | 30–45 min | | Alcove acrylic jetted tub, cut out and hauled | $350–$550 | 1.5–2.5 hrs | | Drop-in tub + tile deck surround demo | $700–$1,400 | 3–5 hrs | | Cast iron jetted tub, second floor | $600–$950 | 3–4 hrs | | Full bathroom gut to studs | $1,200–$2,600 | 6–10 hrs | | Backyard spa (different job entirely) | $250–$600 | 1–3 hrs |

That last row matters because roughly a third of the people who search "cost to remove jacuzzi tub" actually mean a backyard spa. If yours sits on a patio and holds 300 gallons, you want [hot tub removal in Pueblo](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) instead — different equipment, different price, and no drywall involved.

The DIY Number, Honestly Calculated

You can do this yourself. Here's what it actually costs in Pueblo West, not the fantasy version:

  • Reciprocating saw + demolition blades: $90–$160 if you don't own one, or $45/day rental
  • Landfill trip to Southside Landfill: $45–$85 per load, and a jetted tub plus surround is usually two pickup loads
  • Trailer rental if you don't have a truck: $40–$70/day
  • Your time: 6–10 hours for a first-timer on a drop-in with tile, including two dump runs

Realistic all-in DIY: $180–$320 and a full Saturday, assuming nothing goes wrong. Against a $450 professional cut-and-haul, DIY saves you maybe $200. Whether that's worth a weekend and a sore back is your call, and plenty of Pueblo West homeowners correctly decide it is.

> The honest math: if you own a truck, a sawzall, and a free Saturday, DIY on an acrylic alcove tub is the right answer. Hire out when it's cast iron, upstairs, tiled, or when a contractor is waiting on the space.

When Hiring Junk Removal for This Is a Bad Idea

This is the part that costs us jobs. Read it anyway.

Don't hire a junk removal company if you're doing a full bathroom remodel with a general contractor. Your GC's demo crew is already mobilized, already has a dumpster on site, and their marginal cost to pull the tub is often $0 to $200 — it's baked into the demo line item. Paying us $500 to remove one fixture while a 20-yard dumpster sits in your driveway is money burned. Ask your contractor what the demo allowance covers *before* you call anyone.

Don't hire us if you need the tub gone and the plumbing capped to code the same day. We are not licensed plumbers. We'll cut the drain and cap what's visible, but if that bathroom needs to pass inspection, or the supply lines are in-wall copper needing a soldered cap, you need a plumber ($150–$400 for a service call in Pueblo County). Hiring us first and a plumber second sometimes means paying twice for the same access.

Don't hire us for a tub you're trying to salvage. Jetted tubs almost never survive removal intact if there's a tile deck — the flange is buried under mortar and the cut destroys the rim. If you want to resell or reuse the unit, you need careful hand demo of the surround first, which runs $300–$600 extra in labor and still fails maybe 30–40% of the time. Used jetted tubs sell for $100–$400 on Pueblo-area Facebook Marketplace. The math rarely works.

Don't pay for a scheduled visit on a $150 job in a shoulder season. If your tub is already out and sitting in the garage, and you're not in a hurry, wait until you have a garage's worth of other stuff. A combined [garage cleanout in Pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) that includes the tub costs less per item than two separate trips. We'd rather do one $600 job than a $175 job now and a $500 job in October.

Failure modes worth knowing about:

  • Mold behind the surround. Roughly 1 in 5 jetted tubs we pull in older Pueblo West homes has water damage in the framing behind it. That's not our scope. Finding it mid-job means the bathroom stays open longer and you're calling a remediation or framing contractor. Budget a contingency.
  • Asbestos in pre-1985 mud beds and mastic. Uncommon in Pueblo West, which built out mostly post-1970 with a big wave in the 1990s–2000s, but real in older Pueblo proper. If your home predates 1985 and has a thick mortar bed under the tile, testing costs $50–$150 and is worth doing before demo. No reputable crew will knowingly saw through suspect material.
  • Doorway math. A 60" tub does not go through a 28" door as a unit. It has to be cut. If someone quotes you a low price over the phone without asking about door width, they haven't priced the job.
  • Hidden framing. Drop-in decks are sometimes structural-adjacent — built into a knee wall that carries something. Rare, but it's why we look before we cut.

What Reputable Pueblo West Pricing Looks Like

A few tells that separate a real quote from a bad one:

  • A quote given without photos or a walkthrough is a guess. Send pictures of the tub, the doorway, and the surround. Any company that prices a jetted tub sight-unseen will "discover" complications on arrival.
  • Volume-based pricing is wrong for this job. National franchises price by how much of the truck you fill. A jetted tub is bulky and light — it eats truck space out of proportion to its weight, which is why franchise quotes on tubs often land $150–$400 above local pricing for identical work. Ask for a flat job price.
  • Minimums are real and they're fine. Most Pueblo-area operators, us included, have a $125–$175 minimum. That covers drive time to Pueblo West from town, fuel, and the dump run. A company with no minimum is either subsidizing you with another job or won't show up.
  • Same-week scheduling is normal here. Pueblo West isn't a market where you wait three weeks. If someone quotes you out past 10 days with no reason, they've overbooked.

Recycling and Disposal — Where the Tub Actually Goes

Acrylic and fiberglass tubs are not recyclable in Pueblo County. They go to the landfill. What *does* get diverted: the pump motor, blower, inline heater, and any copper or brass supply hardware — that's 8–20 lbs of scrap metal on a typical jetted tub, worth maybe $4–$12 at Pueblo scrap yards. Not a windfall, but it's why a crew that strips components sends less to the landfill than one that doesn't.

Cast iron tubs are fully recyclable and worth $25–$60 in scrap at 350+ lbs. If you have cast iron, that offsets a chunk of your disposal cost, and you should expect a lower quote than for the same job in acrylic. Anyone charging you *more* for cast iron purely on weight, without factoring the scrap value back, is double-dipping. The premium should reflect labor and carry difficulty only.

Getting an Accurate Number for Your Bathroom

Before you call anyone — us or anyone else — measure three things and have them ready: the tub's outside dimensions, the narrowest doorway between the tub and the exterior door, and whether the tub sits flush in an alcove or in a raised tile deck. Those three facts move a quote from a $350–$900 range down to a number within about $75.
