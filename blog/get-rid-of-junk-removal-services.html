GET RID OF JUNK: REMOVAL SERVICES

To get rid of junk in Pueblo West, you have four options. You can haul it yourself to a transfer station or landfill. You can rent a roll-off dumpster. You can book your trash hauler's bulk pickup. Or you can hire a full-service junk removal crew that loads everything and drives it away, usually the same week. For most households with one truckload or less, a junk removal service costs $150–$700, and the crew is done in 30 to 90 minutes. Hauling it yourself is cheaper but takes a weekend. A dumpster is the better buy if a project runs longer than 3 days. Below are the real numbers for each option, plus the situations where hiring us is the wrong call.

What Junk Removal Actually Costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by how much of the truck your stuff takes up, not by the hour. A standard junk removal truck holds about 15 cubic yards, which is roughly the cargo space of six full-size pickup beds. Here are typical price ranges in the Pueblo area:

  • Single bulky item (couch, recliner, mattress): $75–$150
  • Quarter truckload (a small bedroom's worth): $150–$250
  • Half truckload (a one-car garage corner, a dozen boxes plus furniture): $300–$400
  • Full truckload (a full garage or estate room): $500–$700
  • Specialty items like a hot tub: $300–$600, depending on whether it has to be cut apart and how far it sits from the driveway

These ranges include labor, loading, the drive, and disposal fees. You get a firm price on site, before any work starts, once the crew has seen the pile. If the number doesn't work for you, you don't owe anything.

Pueblo West has its own pricing quirks. Many lots here are half an acre to several acres, and a lot of junk lives in sheds, outbuildings, or back corners 100+ feet from where the truck can park. A long carry can add time, but on large-lot properties it's usually the main reason people call a crew in the first place. Gravel or unpaved access roads rarely change the price, though deep mud after spring snowmelt can push a job back a day.

The Four Ways to Get Rid of Junk, Compared

### 1. Haul it yourself If you own a pickup and have a free Saturday, this is the cheapest route. Landfill and transfer station gate fees for a pickup load usually run $20–$60, depending on weight and material. If you don't own a truck, big-box hardware stores rent pickups for about $19–$29 for the first 75 minutes, plus mileage. That isn't enough time to load, drive from Pueblo West to a disposal site and back, and unload, so plan on going over.

The hidden cost is labor and wear on you. A full garage is typically 3–5 pickup loads. That makes it a full day of lifting for two people, with tie-down risk on Highway 50 the whole way.

### 2. Rent a roll-off dumpster A 10- to 20-yard dumpster typically rents for $350–$600 for a 7-day window, with weight limits that trigger overage fees if you exceed them (often around 2 tons on a 10-yard). You do all the loading yourself.

Dumpsters win when the junk shows up gradually, as in a remodel, a roof tear-off, or a month-long downsizing. They lose when you need the driveway clear, when your HOA or covenants restrict them, or when the debris is heavy enough to blow past the weight limit. Concrete and dirt do that fast.

### 3. Your hauler's bulk pickup Pueblo West has no single city-run trash service. Residents contract with private haulers, and some of them offer bulk-item pickup for an added fee or a limited number of free pickups per year. Call yours first. If they'll take a couch at the curb for $25–$50, that beats any junk removal price. The catch: you still have to get it to the curb, and most haulers won't take construction debris, appliances with refrigerant, or anything they deem oversized.

### 4. Full-service junk removal A crew of two shows up, carries everything out from wherever it sits, sweeps up, and hauls it away. You point; they lift. This is what our [junk removal service in Pueblo](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) covers, and it makes sense when time, physical ability, or volume makes the first three options unrealistic.

> The real comparison is your weekend against our hour. Going by money alone, hauling it yourself almost always wins.

When Hiring a Junk Removal Service Is NOT Worth It

This section might cost us a sale. Some people shouldn't hire us, and it's better that you hear it before you book.

  • You have one or two items and a truck. If a recliner and a box of scrap fit in your own pickup, a $40 trip to the landfill beats paying $100+ to us. Do it yourself.
  • Your junk is still good. Working furniture, appliances under about 10 years old, and building materials often sell or give away within 48 hours on local marketplace groups. Donation outlets like a Habitat for Humanity ReStore may pick up usable furniture and fixtures for free. We do donate usable items when we can, but paying us to move something someone would take for free is a waste of your money.
  • Your project will take weeks. If you're gutting a kitchen over 3–4 weekends, a dumpster sitting in the driveway is cheaper and more convenient than calling a truck four separate times.
  • It's hazardous waste. We can't legally take wet paint, solvents, pesticides, motor oil in bulk, propane tanks, or anything suspected to contain asbestos. That includes old popcorn ceilings and some pre-1980 floor tile and pipe wrap. Those need a household hazardous waste program or a licensed abatement contractor. Any junk hauler who says they'll "just toss it in" is putting you at legal risk.
  • The material is pure dirt, rock, or concrete in large volume. A junk truck isn't built for several tons of fill. That's an equipment job. See our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) or [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) instead, which are priced differently.

### Where junk removal can go wrong These are the common ways a junk removal job goes badly, so you can watch for them with any company, including us:

  • Phone quotes that grow on site. A price given over the phone without photos is a guess. Send photos and ask whether the on-site price can change, and by how much.
  • Volume creep. You booked a quarter load, then remembered the shed. That's fair, but it changes the price. Decide on the full scope before the crew arrives.
  • Illegal dumping. Unlicensed haulers sometimes dump loads on open land. Rural stretches around Pueblo West have seen it. If your name is on the junk, that can come back to you. Ask where the load goes.
  • No insurance. If a crew member drops a dresser into your drywall or gets hurt on your property, you want them covered, not you.

What Colorado Law Changes About Getting Rid of Junk

Some items can't just go in a landfill, and that affects how anyone, including you, can dispose of them.

  • Electronics: Since July 1, 2013, Colorado has banned TVs, computers, monitors, and similar electronics from landfills. They have to go to an e-waste recycler, and many recyclers charge $10–$40 per TV, depending on type and size. Old CRT tube TVs cost the most to dispose of.
  • Refrigerators, freezers, and AC units: Federal rules require the refrigerant to be recovered by a certified technician before the unit is scrapped. That's why fridge disposal often costs more than you'd expect. Our [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) handles this properly.
  • Tires: Landfills either refuse whole tires or charge per tire, usually a few dollars each, more for oversized truck tires.

If a junk removal quote seems surprisingly low for a load that's mostly TVs and a freezer, ask how those items are being handled.

How to Get the Lowest Price If You Do Hire Us

You can lower your bill before we arrive:

1. Group everything in one spot, ideally the garage or driveway. Less carrying means less time. 2. Pull out anything sellable or donatable first. Every item you remove frees up truck space. 3. Break down boxes and bag small loose junk. Loose clutter takes up more of the truck than you'd think. 4. Book once, not three times. A single half-truck job costs less than three separate quarter-truck visits. 5. Send photos ahead. You'll get a tighter estimate and fewer surprises.
