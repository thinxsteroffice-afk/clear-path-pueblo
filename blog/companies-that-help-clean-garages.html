COMPANIES THAT HELP CLEAN GARAGES

The companies that help clean up your garage in Pueblo West fall into three groups. Junk removal crews haul away what you no longer want. Professional organizers help you sort and set up storage for what stays. Dumpster rental companies give you a container, and you do the loading. Most garages need the first group. A crew like ClearPath can clear a crowded two-car garage in 2 to 4 hours and take everything away the same day. Organizers usually bill $60 to $120 per hour and don't haul anything. A 10- to 20-yard dumpster often runs $350 to $600 per week plus overage fees. Your choice depends on how much needs to leave, how much you want to keep, and whether you can do the lifting yourself. Below we cover typical costs, what can't go in the truck, and when paying anyone is a waste of money.

The Three Kinds of Garage Cleanup Help, Compared

Before you call anyone, figure out which kind of help your garage actually needs.

  • Full-service junk removal. A two-person crew shows up, you point at things, and they carry, load, and haul. You pay for the space your items take up in the truck, not for the hours. This fits garages where most of the stuff is leaving: old furniture, broken appliances, boxes you haven't opened since the move, and scrap lumber. See our [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) page for how we run these jobs.
  • Professional organizers. They help you decide what to keep and design shelving, zones, and labels. Most charge by the hour, often with a 3- to 4-hour minimum. Few of them haul anything, so you may still need a junk hauler afterward.
  • Dumpster rental. You get a container in your driveway for about 7 days. It usually costs the least per cubic yard, but you do all the carrying and loading. You also pay extra if the load goes over the weight allowance, which is often 2 to 3 tons on a 20-yard box.
  • Estate sale or consignment companies. These are worth a call only if the garage holds things with real resale value, like tools, a riding mower, or collectibles. They typically keep 25% to 40% of sale proceeds.

Most Pueblo West homeowners who call us have already tried organizing on their own. What they need now is to get the stuff out.

What Garage Cleanup Typically Costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by volume. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, which is roughly the load of six pickup beds. Here are ballpark ranges for our area. Your written quote is what counts, and we give it on-site before any work starts.

  • Minimum load (a few items, such as a mattress and a couple of bags): about $100 to $175
  • Quarter truck (a single-car-garage corner or a wall of boxes): about $175 to $275
  • Half truck (a crowded one-car garage): about $300 to $425
  • Full truck (a two-car garage you can't park in): about $500 to $700
  • Surcharges: refrigerators and freezers usually add $25 to $75 because the refrigerant has to be recovered first. Tires often add $10 to $25 each.

For scale, a typical two-car garage is about 400 to 480 square feet. When it's packed to shoulder height with no space to walk, it often fills 1.5 to 2 truckloads. Most "we can't park in there anymore" garages fit in one load.

> Your garage isn't what makes the job expensive. The heavy items are: concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, and old tile. A half-truck of shingles can weigh more than a full truck of furniture.

If you have heavy materials like rock, broken concrete, or leftover pavers, tell us when you book. Those loads are priced differently, and our [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) service is set up for them.

Why Pueblo West Garages Are Different

Some things about cleaning out a garage here don't apply in Denver or Colorado Springs.

There's no city bulk-trash day to wait for. Pueblo West is an unincorporated metropolitan district, and households contract with private trash haulers. Most regular trash service won't take a couch, a water heater, or 30 bags at once. The "put it on the curb and the city takes it" option that works in some Front Range towns generally doesn't exist here, so garages tend to fill up over years.

Lots are big, and garages are often detached or oversized. A lot of Pueblo West homes sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots, often with a detached garage or a shop building. A 30x40 shop is 1,200 square feet, nearly three times the size of a standard two-car garage. If yours is that big, ask for an on-site quote. A phone estimate for a shop that size is usually off.

Wind matters if you haul it yourself. Afternoon gusts here often top 30 mph in spring. Colorado law requires loads to be secured so nothing falls or blows out of the vehicle, and an untarped trailer of cardboard and insulation will come apart on the highway. Professional crews tarp and tie down loads as standard practice.

Summer heat changes the schedule. An uninsulated garage in July can reach well over 100°F inside. We book summer garage jobs for early morning when we can, and you should do the same for any sorting you do yourself.

What a Junk Removal Company Won't Take

Every reputable hauler has limits, and you should know them before the truck arrives so you don't have leftovers. In Colorado:

  • Electronics such as TVs, monitors, and computers have been banned from Colorado landfills since July 1, 2013. They have to go to recycling, and some haulers charge for that.
  • Liquid paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, motor oil, and gasoline are household hazardous waste, and most junk crews won't load them. Unused latex and oil-based paint can go to Colorado's PaintCare drop-off sites, a program running since 2015. If latex paint is completely dried out, it can usually go in regular trash.
  • Propane tanks need to go back to an exchange location or a propane dealer.
  • Asbestos-containing materials, like old floor tiles or pipe wrap in older homes, require licensed abatement, not a junk crew.
  • Car batteries are accepted for free at most auto parts stores, and some stores pay a small core credit.

If your garage has shelves full of half-empty chemical cans, set them aside before cleanup day. That one step prevents the most common "you left stuff behind" complaint.

When Hiring a Garage Cleanup Company Isn't Worth It

We'd rather lose a job than sell you something you don't need. Here's when you should not hire us or anyone like us.

  • When most of the stuff is going to stay. If 80% of what's in your garage is staying and the problem is layout, a junk hauler adds little. You need shelving, a free weekend, and maybe an organizer. We'd end up billing a minimum load for a few bags.
  • When you have a truck, a free Saturday, and a healthy back. A half-truck of light junk costs about $300 to $425 to have hauled. Doing it yourself costs a few landfill trips and fuel. If your time is free and you can lift safely, DIY is cheaper, and it isn't close.
  • When you haven't decided what to keep. A crew bills for the time and the load no matter how long you spend deciding what goes. If you're going to stop at every box to think about it, sort a week ahead. Otherwise you'll feel rushed into tossing things you want, or you'll book a second trip you didn't need.
  • When the garage is mostly heavy debris from a remodel. A roll-off dumpster you fill over 1 to 2 weeks is often cheaper for ongoing construction waste than several truck visits. Our [construction debris removal pueblo](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) page covers when each option makes sense.
  • When there's real resale value in there. Hauling off a working table saw, a generator, or a clean set of tools means that money is gone. List those things first. Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist move tools quickly in Pueblo County.
  • When you're mostly after the donation tax deduction. We drop usable items at local charities when they'll take them, but we can't promise every item will be accepted. Donation centers turn down stained upholstery, cribs that don't meet current standards, and most particleboard furniture. If a deduction matters to you, drop off the items yourself and get a receipt.

There are real limitations with *any* hauler, too:

  • Same-day slots aren't guaranteed. They're common on weekdays and much harder to get on Saturdays in spring.
  • Estimates can change on-site. If you tell us "about half a truck" over the phone, the actual load may be bigger. A reputable company quotes before loading and doesn't raise the price afterward.
  • The truck is for removal, not cleaning. Most junk crews sweep up when the job is done. They don't degrease floors or treat for mice or pack rats. If you've found droppings, that's a separate cleaning job.

How to Get the Most Out of a Garage Cleanout

If a hauler does make sense for you, these steps keep the bill down and the job short:

1. Walk the garage 3 to 7 days before. Mark keepers with blue tape and set aside anything that will be sold or donated. 2. Pull hazardous materials out ahead of time. Paint, fuel, and chemicals should be sorted before the crew shows up. 3. Clear a path from the driveway. A crew that doesn't have to move your car or trailer finishes faster. 4. Point at everything that's going before loading starts. Once an item is in the truck, it's hard to get back out. 5. Take a phone photo of every wall before and after. It helps with insurance records and helps you remember what you got rid of.

A normal two-car garage job with a two-person crew takes 2 to 4 hours from arrival to swept floor. Hoarding situations or shop buildings can take a full day or more. Our [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) service is set up for those longer jobs.
