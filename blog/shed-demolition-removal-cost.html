SHED DEMOLITION & REMOVAL COST

Shed demolition and removal in Pueblo West runs $350 to $900 for a typical wood shed sitting on skids or gravel, and $1,100 to $2,400 when there is a concrete slab underneath. A small metal shed is $250 to $450. Four things drive the number: square footage, roofing material, whether the floor is wood or concrete, and how close a truck and trailer can get. Most 8x10 and 10x12 sheds come down in 3 to 5 hours with a two-person crew and haul out in a single load. Budget another $400 to $700 to break out and remove a 4-inch slab, and more if it has rebar or a thickened footing edge.

What Pueblo West homeowners actually pay

These are the ranges we quote on the West side, on flat lots with reasonable gate access. They assume the shed is empty.

  • Metal kit shed, 8x8 to 10x10, on gravel or pavers: $250 to $450. These are the cheapest teardowns in existence. Two people and a reciprocating saw, about 90 minutes, and the whole thing goes to scrap.
  • Wood shed, 8x10, wood floor on skids, asphalt shingles: $350 to $600.
  • Wood shed, 10x12 to 10x16, wood floor: $500 to $900.
  • Stick-built shed, 12x16 or larger, on a slab, with siding and a real roof: $1,200 to $2,400, slab removal included at the high end.
  • Barn-style shed with a loft, 12x20 or bigger: $1,800 to $3,500. These are small buildings, not sheds, and they weigh like it.

The single biggest swing factor nobody quotes for over the phone is access width. If a crew can back a dump trailer to within 30 feet of the structure, the job runs at the low end. If the only path is a 36-inch side gate and everything gets carried 120 feet to the street, expect a 20% to 30% premium, because the labor doubles even though the debris volume is identical.

The slab is the part that blows up the budget

> The shed is rarely the expensive part. The concrete under it is.

A 10x12 shed is 120 square feet of concrete. Removing an unreinforced 4-inch slab runs roughly $3.50 to $6.00 per square foot in this market, so $420 to $720 for that footprint. Three things make it worse:

  • Wire mesh or rebar. Adds 30% to 50% to the breakout labor. The concrete cracks fine; pulling the mat out is the slow part.
  • A thickened edge or footing. Plenty of Pueblo West sheds were poured with an 8 to 12 inch perimeter beam. You do not find out until the breaker hits it. This can add $200 to $400.
  • Weight. Concrete runs about 150 pounds per cubic foot, so a 4-inch, 120 square foot slab is roughly 3 tons. That is a dedicated trip and a separate disposal ticket. We handle this as [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) and price it by weight, not by volume, because volume pricing on concrete is how contractors lose money.

You often do not have to remove the slab at all. If you are putting a new shed, a hot tub, or a storage container in the same spot, leaving a sound slab saves you $500 and gives you a free foundation. Ask yourself what goes there next before you pay to demo it.

What the price includes, and what it does not

A real shed demolition quote should cover teardown, load-out, hauling, disposal fees, and a raked site. Ours does. Here is what is normally excluded and quoted separately:

  • Contents. A shed packed with paint cans, old lumber, and 14 years of yard tools is two jobs. The demolition is the cheap half. If yours is full, look at [garage cleanout pricing](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) for what the contents side costs, or ask for a combined number up front.
  • Hazardous material. We cannot take fuel, propane cylinders, pesticides, solvents, or motor oil in the trailer. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events. Plan on setting that material aside yourself.
  • Live electrical or water. If a subpanel feeds the shed, a licensed electrician needs to kill and cap the circuit first. Expect $150 to $350 for that visit. We will not cut a live conductor.
  • Backfill and grading. Pulling a slab leaves a 4 to 6 inch depression. Filling and compacting a 120 square foot hole costs $250 to $500 in material and machine time. That is [dirt work](/services/dirt-work-excavation-pueblo), and it is worth doing if you plan to grow grass or park anything there.
  • Tree or fence removal to create access. Sometimes the cheapest path to a backyard shed is taking down eight feet of fence panel and putting it back. Budget $150 to $250.

When you should not hire us for this

This is the section most junk removal pages skip. Some of these jobs are not worth paying for.

Do not hire anyone if your shed is a small metal kit on gravel and you own a truck. An 8x8 Arrow-style shed disassembles with a nut driver and a Sawzall in under two hours. It weighs 250 to 400 pounds. That is one pickup load. Tipping at the Pueblo County landfill runs roughly $45 to $60 per ton with a minimum charge in the $25 to $35 range, so your out-of-pocket is under $40 plus fuel. Paying $350 for that is paying $310 for a Saturday morning. Some Saturday mornings are worth $310. Be honest about whether yours is.

Do not demolish a shed that is worth more standing. A structurally sound 10x12 with a decent roof sells on Facebook Marketplace for $400 to $1,200 in Pueblo West, and shed-moving outfits will pick it up on a set of rollers for $300 to $600. If the floor is solid and the roof does not leak, you are looking at a $1,000 swing between selling it and demolishing it. Check the floor joists and the bottom plate before you call anybody. Rot at the base is the usual disqualifier.

Do not pay us if a contractor is already mobilized on your property. If you have an excavator on site for a driveway, a foundation, or a septic replacement, the shed should be a $200 add-on to their existing ticket, not a separate $900 visit. The mobilization cost is the thing you are actually paying for. Same logic applies if you are already renting a 20-yard dumpster for a remodel, which runs $400 to $550 for a week in this area with a 2 to 4 ton limit. Your shed debris probably fits in the headroom you already bought.

DIY stops making sense fast, though. A 10x12 wood shed with shingles weighs 1.5 to 2.5 tons. A half-ton pickup safely carries around 1,200 pounds. That is 6 to 10 round trips, roughly 40 minutes each from Pueblo West to the landfill and back, plus loading. Two people spend 14 to 20 hours on that project. If the number in your head was "one weekend," it is two, and the second one hurts.

Failure modes to know about. Phone quotes on shed demolition are estimates, not prices, and any company that swears otherwise on an unseen structure is guessing. The things that move the number after a crew arrives: a buried slab or footing under what looked like a dirt floor, buried debris and old concrete from a previous structure, transite or cement-board siding that needs testing, and sprinkler lines or a leach field running under the demolition zone. Mark your irrigation before demo day. Nobody wants to explain a severed lateral line.

Asbestos is the real stopper. Sheds and outbuildings built before about 1985 sometimes have cement-asbestos siding panels or rolled roofing that contains it. Sample testing costs $75 to $200. Colorado's abatement thresholds start at 160 square feet of affected material, and abatement runs $8 to $15 per square foot. Single-family residential work often sits outside CDPHE's permitting requirements, but the landfill can still refuse a suspect load, which means the debris comes back to your driveway. If your shed is old and has gray, brittle, fibrous panels, test first.

Pueblo West specifics: permits, covenants, and what is living under there

Pueblo Regional Building Department generally does not require a building permit for detached accessory structures under a certain footprint, commonly 200 square feet, but demolition and setback rules are separate questions and change. Make one phone call before demo day. Pueblo West Metropolitan District covenants also govern what can sit on a residential lot, so if you are replacing the shed rather than clearing the pad, confirm the replacement is allowed before you pay to remove the old one.

Two local realities worth planning around:

  • Wind. Gusts here regularly clear 50 mph, and a partially disassembled shed is a sail. Never leave a job half done overnight. If a windstorm already flattened your shed, photograph it before cleanup and call your insurer. Storm-collapsed outbuildings are sometimes a covered claim, and the removal cost may go on it.
  • What is underneath. Skid-mounted sheds on the West side are prime real estate for pack rats, mice, bull snakes, and occasionally rattlesnakes. Crews expect it. Homeowners doing it themselves often do not. Lift the floor with a pry bar from a standing position, not on your knees at the edge.
