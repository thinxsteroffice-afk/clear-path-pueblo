What Appliance Removal Actually Involves in Pueblo West

Hauling off an appliance is more than lifting it into a truck. A few things make it different from moving a couch:

  • Weight. A full-size refrigerator usually weighs 200–350 lbs. A top-load washer runs 120–230 lbs, and a chest freezer can top 150 lbs empty. Two people and a dolly are the minimum. Stairs or a sunken living room make it a two-person job with straps.
  • Refrigerant. Under Section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act, refrigerant in fridges, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers must be recovered by a certified technician before the appliance is scrapped. That step is why Freon-bearing appliances cost more to get rid of almost everywhere.
  • Hookups. Gas dryers and gas ranges have to be disconnected from the gas line. Fridges with ice makers have a water line. Water heaters hold 40–50 gallons of water that has to be drained first.
  • Access. Many Pueblo West lots are half an acre to several acres, with long gravel drives and detached garages or outbuildings. A freezer sitting in a shop 150 feet from the street is a different job from a washer next to the curb.

Our [appliance removal Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) service covers the carry-out, loading, and routing to the right place. Metal goes to scrap recyclers, refrigerant units go through recovery, and working units go to donation when they qualify.

What We Won't or Can't Do

Being plain about limits saves everyone a wasted trip:

  • We don't disconnect gas lines. If your dryer or range runs on gas, have a licensed plumber or your gas utility cap the line before we arrive. A loose or leaking gas connection is a safety problem, and we won't touch it.
  • We don't drain water heaters on arrival. Draining a 40–50 gallon tank takes 30–60 minutes, and sediment can clog the drain valve. Turn off power or gas and the water supply, then drain it the day before.
  • We won't take a fridge full of food. Empty it first. A freezer of spoiled meat that's been sitting in a hot Pueblo West garage in July is a sanitation problem, and it can leak onto everything else in the truck.
  • Commercial refrigeration units like walk-in coolers, restaurant reach-ins, and large ice machines may need a specialized refrigeration contractor rather than a junk hauler. Ask before you book.
  • Built-ins can take longer than expected. Built-in dishwashers, wall ovens, and over-the-range microwaves are attached to cabinetry. If you want them pulled without damage to the surrounding cabinets, that's closer to a handyman job than junk removal.

Failure modes we've seen: a fridge measured at 33 inches wide that won't fit through a 30-inch interior doorway without removing the doors. A chest freezer that's frozen to a garage floor. A washer still holding a gallon of water that floods the garage when it's tipped. None of these are disasters, but each one adds time. Measure doorways, unplug fridges 24 hours ahead so they defrost, and run the washer's drain cycle first.

How to Prep an Appliance for Pickup

Ten minutes of prep can cut your on-site time, and sometimes your bill:

1. Unplug refrigerators and freezers 24 hours early and leave the doors propped open with a towel underneath to catch meltwater. 2. Shut off and disconnect water lines to ice makers and washers. Have a bucket ready. 3. Get the gas line capped by a professional for gas appliances. 4. Drain the water heater the day before. 5. Clear a path. Move rugs, kids' bikes, and anything else between the appliance and the door. 6. Tape doors shut on fridges and dryers so they don't swing open on the move. 7. Tell us about stairs, gravel, and distance when you book, so we bring the right crew size and equipment.

Where Old Appliances Go After Pickup

People ask whether their old washer just ends up in the landfill. For most appliances, it doesn't. Appliances are mostly steel by weight, and steel is one of the most recycled materials in the country. Here's the usual path:

  • Working units in decent shape go to donation or resale.
  • Non-Freon appliances go to a scrap metal recycler, where they're shredded and the steel, copper, and aluminum are separated.
  • Freon appliances go to a facility that recovers the refrigerant first, as federal law requires. After that, the cabinet is scrapped like any other appliance.

Landfills in the region commonly charge extra fees or refuse refrigerant-bearing appliances unless they've been certified as evacuated. That's one more reason hauling a fridge to the dump in your own truck can turn into a wasted trip. Call ahead if you go the DIY route.
