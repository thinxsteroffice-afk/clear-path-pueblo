What Appliance Removal Actually Involves in Pueblo West

Most people think appliance removal just means lifting something heavy. That's part of it. A standard top-freezer refrigerator weighs 200–300 pounds, a side-by-side can top 350 pounds, and a front-load washer weighs around 150–230 pounds, mostly because of the concrete counterweight inside. Two people with a hand truck can move most of these, but stairs, split-level entries, and narrow laundry closets are where floors get gouged and backs get hurt.

The less visible part of the job is where the appliance goes afterward:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, and dehumidifiers contain refrigerant. Under Section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act, the refrigerant has to be recovered by EPA-certified technicians before the unit is scrapped. A typical household fridge holds roughly 4 to 8 ounces of refrigerant. Units made before about 1995 often use R-12, a CFC that damages the ozone layer.
  • Washers, dryers, ranges, and dishwashers have no refrigerant and are mostly steel. They go to metal recyclers.
  • Working appliances that are less than about 10 years old can often go to a donation outlet or resale instead of scrap.
  • Microwaves and small countertop appliances are usually bundled with a larger load, since they're rarely worth a trip on their own.

When we haul appliances through our [appliance removal Pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) service, we sort them into those streams. We don't pile everything into a landfill run.

When Paying for Appliance Removal ISN'T Worth It

This is the section most junk removal sites leave out. There are plenty of situations where hiring us, or anyone, is the wrong call.

1. You're buying a new appliance anyway. Most major retailers offer haul-away when they deliver a replacement. It's often free or around $25–$40 per unit, depending on the store and current promotions. That's almost always cheaper than a separate junk removal visit. Ask at checkout before you call a hauler. The catch is that the old unit usually has to be disconnected, emptied, and ready on delivery day, or the crew may refuse it.

2. It still works and it's worth something. A working refrigerator under 5–7 years old can sell locally for $150–$400 in decent condition. Listing it for a week costs you nothing but patience. Paying someone to haul away a sellable appliance turns an asset into an expense.

3. You have a truck, a helper, and a free Saturday. Washers, dryers, and ranges are mostly steel, and scrap yards will take them. Light iron typically pays around $100–$200 per ton, so a 175-pound washer brings in about $8–$15. That's not much money, but it costs you nothing. If you're physically able and the item is in your garage, you can do this yourself. Refrigerators are harder because many scrap yards won't accept them without proof that the refrigerant was removed.

4. Your utility pays you to take it. Some Colorado utilities have run old-refrigerator recycling programs that pick up a working second fridge for free and pay a small bounty. These programs come and go and vary by provider, so check yours before you call us. If one is running, it beats any hauler's price.

5. The appliance isn't disconnected yet, and the connection is gas. We remove appliances. We don't disconnect natural gas lines. A gas range or dryer has to be shut off and capped by you or a licensed professional before we arrive. If that hasn't happened, our crew will leave it in place. That means a wasted trip for you and, depending on timing, a trip charge. Hardwired dishwashers and refrigerators connected to an ice-maker water line also need to be disconnected first. If you're not comfortable doing that, budget $75–$150 for a plumber or electrician, which can wipe out whatever you saved by not doing the haul yourself.

6. You only have a microwave or a toaster oven. Paying a minimum truck fee for a 35-pound item rarely makes sense. Hold onto it until you have a bigger load, or take it to a drop-off location yourself.

If none of these apply and you just want the thing gone without hurting yourself or your floors, that's where a hauler earns its fee.

The Disconnection Checklist Nobody Gives You

Most failed appliance pickups fail because of prep, not the haul. Before any hauler arrives, whether that's us in Pueblo West or someone else in Reno:

  • Refrigerators: Unplug them 24 hours ahead so the freezer defrosts and doesn't leak across your floor during the carry-out. Empty all food, because spoiled food can get a unit rejected at a recycler. Shut off and disconnect the ice-maker water line.
  • Washers: Shut both water valves, disconnect the hoses, and drain the drum. A front-loader with standing water can dump a gallon or more on the way out.
  • Dryers: Unplug, and disconnect the vent duct. Gas dryers need the gas line professionally capped (see failure mode #5).
  • Dishwashers: Shut off the water supply and the breaker. Most are screwed into the countertop from underneath.
  • Chest freezers: Defrost them fully. A chest freezer with ice still inside can weigh 50+ pounds more than an empty one.
  • Measure the path: Most standard refrigerators are 30–36 inches wide. If your door frame is narrower, the doors may have to come off, so tell us ahead of time.

Doing this before we show up can save you a return visit, and it almost always makes the job faster.
