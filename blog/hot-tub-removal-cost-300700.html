HOT TUB REMOVAL COST: $300–$700

Hot tub removal in Pueblo West runs $300 to $700 for a standard 6–8 person backyard tub that a crew can reach through a gate and cut up on site. Easy jobs — tub sitting on a pad next to the driveway, already drained, gate wider than 42 inches — land at $250 to $400. Hard jobs push higher: $650 to $1,100 if the tub is inside a deck surround, up a flight of stairs, behind a 36-inch gate, or has to be lifted over a fence. Swim spas and 12–16 foot exercise tubs run $900 to $2,000. Add $150 to $350 if you need a licensed electrician to disconnect the 240V spa panel, and $400 to $900 if you also want the concrete pad gone.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this page explains what actually moves the number in this market, and — more usefully — the several situations where you should not pay anyone, including us.

What you're actually paying for (it isn't the driving)

A hot tub is not a heavy couch. It's a fiberglass-and-acrylic shell wrapped in 2 to 6 inches of closed-cell foam, sitting in a cabinet, holding 300–500 gallons of water. Dry weight on a typical 7-foot square tub is 500 to 900 pounds. Full of water, you're looking at 3,000 to 4,700 pounds — water alone is 8.34 lbs per gallon.

Nobody moves that intact. The standard method is demolition in place: kill the power, drain it, strip the cabinet panels, cut the shell into 4 to 8 pieces with a reciprocating saw, and haul the chunks out. A crew of two burns through 6 to 10 demolition blades on a full-foam tub because the foam gums the teeth. Real on-site time for a clean job is 60 to 90 minutes. A deck-locked tub is 2.5 to 4 hours.

The finished pile is 4 to 6 cubic yards of debris that isn't recyclable in any meaningful way. Acrylic shell, polyurethane foam, treated cabinet lumber, and a pump assembly go to the landfill; the only salvageable parts are the small amount of copper in the heater element and motor. Tipping fees in Pueblo County run roughly $45 to $70 per ton depending on the facility and the load, so disposal on a single tub is typically $40 to $90 of the quote. Everything above that is labor, blades, fuel, insurance, and the risk of putting a saw through a foam wall six inches from a live wire.

The Pueblo West variables that change your quote

Local conditions here genuinely move the price, and a national "average cost" article won't tell you any of this:

  • Xeriscape and landscape rock. Most Pueblo West lots are gravel, not turf. Rolling 300-pound shell sections across 3/4-inch river rock means carrying instead of dollying, which is the difference between a 2-person job and a 3-person job — usually $75 to $150.
  • Lot size cuts both ways. Half-acre and one-acre lots out here often mean a 80 to 150 foot carry from the back patio to the truck. Cheap in a Bessemer alley, not cheap on Purcell.
  • Wind. Sustained 30–40 mph gusts are normal spring afternoons. A cut acrylic panel is a sail. Crews will not leave a half-demoed tub overnight, which means a job that starts at 4pm sometimes gets rescheduled — ask about that before you book a late slot.
  • Winter tubs. A tub that's been off since November may have 200 to 400 pounds of ice in the footwell and frozen plumbing. Expect $75 to $200 extra, or wait for a 50°F week — Pueblo West gets plenty of those in January.
  • Water disposal. Draining 400 gallons is your job in most quotes. A submersible pump at 1,500–2,000 GPH clears a tub in 20 to 30 minutes; siphoning through a garden hose takes 6 to 12 hours. Let chlorine or bromine dissipate for 48 to 72 hours before you drain, and put the water on gravel or a gravel bed — not into an arroyo, storm drain, or your neighbor's downslope lot. If it's a saltwater system, that water will kill grass and stress trees; spread it wide.

The electrical piece nobody quotes honestly

Your tub is on a dedicated 50-amp or 60-amp 240V circuit running to a GFCI spa disconnect box within sight of the tub. A junk crew is not licensed to touch that.

Three real options: an electrician disconnects and caps the whip and abandons or removes the spa panel for $150 to $350; you kill the breaker, lock the panel, and have the crew cut the whip on the load side (cheap, legal in most cases, but leaves dead conduit and a spa panel on your wall); or you leave the whole thing energized for a future tub, which is worth doing if you might replace it — replacing that circuit later costs $800 to $1,800.

> The single most common surprise on a hot tub removal invoice isn't the tub. It's the $600 concrete pad the homeowner assumed was included.

That pad is usually 8x8 feet, 4 inches thick — about 1 cubic yard, 3,800 to 4,200 pounds of concrete. Breaking and hauling it is its own line item at $400 to $900, and it's a different set of tools. If the pad is coming out too, say so when you get the quote so it can be bid as one trip. We handle it under [concrete rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo), and if the tub sits inside a raised deck, the surround comes off under [deck and fence removal](/services/deck-fence-removal-pueblo).

When hot tub removal is NOT worth paying for

This is the part that costs us jobs, and it should. Do not hire anyone if you're in one of these situations:

  • Your tub still runs and it's under about 8 years old. A functioning 2019-or-newer tub has real value. Listed at $1,200 to $2,500 on Facebook Marketplace it'll sell in a couple of weeks; listed free, you move it it will be gone in 48 hours and cost you nothing. Budget for flakes — expect 2 or 3 no-shows before someone actually shows with a trailer. That's still a better outcome than paying $500. Test it first: fill it, run it 24 hours, check for leaks and a heater that actually reaches 100°F. A tub that won't heat is worth $0 and every buyer knows it.
  • You have a pickup, a helper, and a free Saturday. DIY is genuinely feasible. Realistic cost: $30 to $45 in demo blades, $25 to $50 for a trailer rental if you need one, $40 to $80 in landfill fees. Call it $120 to $175 and 4 to 6 hours. The honest risks: acrylic shatters into shards that cut through gloves, the shell is awkward rather than heavy, and if you throw your back out the ER visit erases the savings ten times over. If you're over 60, working alone, or the tub is above ground level, don't.
  • You're removing it as part of a bigger cleanout. Never book a hot tub as a standalone trip if you also have a garage full of stuff. Bundled into a full-truck [junk removal](/services/junk-removal-pueblo) job, the tub often adds only $150 to $250 instead of the $400+ it costs as its own dispatch. Same logic if you have two properties — batch them.
  • You're planning to install a new tub within 12 months. Leave the pad and the electrical. Removing them costs $550 to $1,250 and reinstalling costs more.
  • The tub is empty, cracked, and out of the way on a big lot. If it's not hurting anything and money is tight this month, it's a fiberglass shell sitting in the sun. It will still be there in six months and it will not cost more to remove then. This is a want, not an emergency.

Failure modes to watch for when you do hire

  • The $199 online price. That's a curbside or partial-load rate. Read whether it includes cutting, the deck panels, the pad, and the haul-off, or whether it's just a starting bid before the crew sees the gate.
  • Quotes given without photos. Any company that prices a hot tub sight-unseen is either padding for worst case or planning to renegotiate in your driveway. Send four photos: the tub, the gate, the path to the street, and the spa panel.
  • Crews without insurance. Carrying 300 pounds past your siding, your AC condenser, and your fence is where damage happens. Ask for a certificate.
  • Illegal dumping. A tub abandoned on the mesa traces back fast, and Colorado penalties for large-volume littering can reach into the $1,000 range plus cleanup. Ask where the debris goes.
  • Left-behind debris. Foam crumbs, screws, and acrylic shards in gravel are miserable to clean up. Confirm cleanup is included, and that the crew brings a magnet or a shop vac.

Getting an accurate number for your property

Measure your narrowest gate opening and the tub's outside dimensions, note whether the pad is coming out, and photograph the spa panel. Those three things determine roughly 80% of the price spread between a $300 job and a $900 one.
