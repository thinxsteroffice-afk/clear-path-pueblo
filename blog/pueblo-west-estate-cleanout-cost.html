PUEBLO WEST ESTATE CLEANOUT COST

A full estate cleanout in Pueblo West runs $1,800 to $4,500 for a typical three-bedroom house with a detached garage and a yard that's been collecting since the early 2000s. Smaller jobs — one room, a storage shed, a mother-in-law suite — land between $450 and $1,200. Hoarding-level properties where the crew has to sift every square foot run $5,000 to $12,000. Priced by volume instead, a full 16-cubic-yard trailer load is $600–$650 and a quarter load is about $175. Most Pueblo West estates take one to three days with a two- or three-person crew. Those are our numbers. The rest of this page explains what moves them, and names the jobs where you should not hire anyone at all.

What Actually Moves the Price Here

Estate cleanout quotes swing wildly because three variables do almost all the work, and none of them is square footage.

  • Volume, measured in cubic yards. A 16-yard trailer holds roughly the contents of one packed two-car garage. A lived-in 1,600 sq ft Pueblo West home with 40 years of accumulation is typically 3 to 5 loads. Empty-ish homes where family already took the furniture: 1 to 2 loads.
  • Weight. Volume prices assume household density. Concrete, roofing tear-off, dirt, brick, and waterlogged particleboard blow past it. Southside Landfill charges by the ton, and a trailer of masonry can weigh 6,000 lbs versus 1,200 lbs for the same trailer of clothing and cardboard. Expect a $75–$200 heavy-material surcharge per load on dense debris.
  • Sorting. Hauling is cheap. Deciding is expensive. See below.

Location matters less than people expect. Pueblo West is 12 to 20 miles from the disposal sites we use, so round-trip drive time on a full-load run is 45 to 70 minutes including scale time. That's baked into our pricing, not added as a fuel fee. Properties out past the Pueblo West Metro District boundary — toward Colorado City or up the Highway 96 corridor — add roughly $50–$125 per job in drive time.

Four Real Pueblo West Job Shapes and What They Cost

The clean handoff — $650 to $1,100. Parent moved to assisted living. Family already pulled photos, jewelry, and the good furniture. What's left is a garage, a shed, a mattress set, and a dead chest freezer. One and a half loads, 3 to 4 hours, one visit.

The standard estate — $1,900 to $3,200. Three bedrooms, a detached garage on a one-acre lot, a shed, and a side yard with lumber, tires, and an old swing set. Four loads, two days, two-person crew. This is the most common job we run in Pueblo West, and the one-acre lots are why — the outbuilding and yard accumulation often exceeds what's inside the house.

The forty-year house — $3,800 to $5,500. Everything above, plus a full basement or crawlspace, an attic, and a carport. Six to eight loads, three days. Add a day if the family is still sorting while we work.

The hard one — $6,000 to $12,000. Heavy hoarding, animal contamination, or biohazard. Requires PPE, sometimes a dumpster instead of trailers, and a slow room-by-room pass so nothing of value gets thrown out. Our [hoarding cleanout page](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) covers how that work is staged and why it's priced by the day rather than the load.

The Sort Tax Nobody Quotes You

Here's the number most companies won't put in writing: sorting roughly doubles the labor hours on the same volume of material.

A crew told "take everything in this room" clears about 1 cubic yard every 4 to 6 minutes. A crew told "check every box, set aside anything that looks like a document, photograph, or coin" clears the same yard in 12 to 20 minutes. On a four-load house, that difference is 6 to 9 additional labor hours — $400 to $900 at our rates.

> The cheapest estate cleanout is the one where the family spends a weekend pulling what matters first. Two people, ten hours, and you've cut $600 to $900 off the bill and lost nothing you cared about.

That's a genuine recommendation against spending money with us. Take it. Pull the safe deposit key, the paperwork, the photos, the tools your kids want, the firearms, and anything with a title. Then call for volume pricing on the remainder.

When an Estate Cleanout Is Not Worth Paying For

Some of these will cost us the job. They're still true.

  • The estate hasn't cleared probate and you're not the personal representative. Do not hire anyone yet. If you dispose of property before Letters Testamentary are issued and a sibling contests it, you own that problem personally. We've walked away from two Pueblo West jobs where the caller was a nephew with a house key and no authority. Confirm with the estate attorney first, then call.
  • You have a working truck, a free weekend, and under 2 loads of material. Southside Landfill self-haul for a pickup load runs roughly $40 to $75 depending on weight. Two loads of your own labor saves you $700 to $1,000. If you're physically able and the house has no stairs, do it yourself.
  • The house has genuine antiques or a serious tool/gun collection. Call an estate sale company or auctioneer *before* a junk hauler. They typically take 30% to 40% commission but can gross $4,000 to $25,000 on a collection we would treat as volume. The order matters: liquidator first, hauler second. Reversing that order is the single most expensive mistake families make.
  • You're selling the house as-is to a cash investor. Many Pueblo West as-is buyers price the property assuming they'll clear it themselves. Paying $3,000 to empty a house you're selling to a flipper often nets you nothing. Ask the buyer, in writing, what they'll pay with contents versus without. Sometimes the answer is a $2,000 spread on a $3,500 cleanout — that's a losing trade.
  • The estate is insolvent. If the house is underwater and the estate can't pay creditors, spending estate funds on a cleanout can create a problem with the priority of claims. That's an attorney question, and the honest answer is that it isn't ours to decide.
  • You want us to appraise things. We're haulers with 20 years of "that's probably worth something" instincts. We are not appraisers, and we won't pretend to be. If a value question is worth more than about $500 to you, pay an appraiser $200–$400 instead of trusting a guy holding a dolly.

The limitation you should plan around: an estimate given over the phone without photos is wrong about 40% of the time, usually low. Anyone quoting a firm number for a house they haven't seen is either padding heavily or setting up a day-of price change.

What Costs Extra No Matter Who You Hire

Colorado disposal rules add line items that have nothing to do with the hauler's margin.

  • Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units: $25–$45 each for EPA-required refrigerant recovery. Non-negotiable and legally required. Details on our [appliance removal page](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Electronics: Colorado has banned electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013. Old CRT televisions cost $25–$60 each to recycle; a console TV can hit $75. Estates routinely produce four or five of them.
  • Mattresses: $20–$35 each to process. A four-bedroom estate with two guest beds is $100–$175 in mattresses alone.
  • Tires: $8–$18 each, more for tractor or RV tires. One-acre Pueblo West properties average four to nine loose tires.
  • Paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals: we can't landfill them. Pueblo County's household hazardous waste program handles these, usually free for residents on scheduled dates. Budget $0 in fees but plan a separate trip, or pay us $75–$150 to run it.
  • Propane tanks and ammunition: we take neither in a mixed load. Ammunition goes to the sheriff's office; tanks go to an exchange point.

How to Get a Number That Doesn't Change

Walk the property with your phone and take 15 to 20 photos: every room wide, the garage, the shed, the crawlspace hatch, the side yard, and any appliance. Send them. That gets a quote within ±10% most of the time.

Then ask any company you're considering four questions:

  • Is this priced by volume, by the hour, or by the day — and what happens if the load runs over?
  • Are disposal fees included or added at the scale?
  • What do you do with items that look valuable — and will you set them aside or sell them?
  • Are you insured for work inside an occupied structure, and will you send the certificate?

A company that gives you a flat "starting at $99" and no answers to those four is quoting a marketing number, not a price.
