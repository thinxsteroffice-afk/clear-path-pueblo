HOW MUCH DOES JUNK REMOVAL COST?

1-800-GOT-JUNK! prices by how much space your stuff takes up in their 16-cubic-yard truck, not by the hour or by the item. As of 2026, expect a single-item minimum around $150–$190, 1/8 truck (roughly a pickup bed) $200–$280, 1/4 truck $300–$390, 1/2 truck $450–$570, and a full truck $700–$950. Add-ons stack on top: mattresses often carry a $30–$60 disposal surcharge, refrigerators and freezers $40–$75 for refrigerant handling, and tires $15–$25 each. In Pueblo West specifically, there's a wrinkle most price guides skip — the nearest franchise territory is run out of Colorado Springs, about 50 miles north, which affects both what you'll pay and whether they'll show up at all.

How the National Volume Price Is Actually Built

The truck is the unit. A 1-800-GOT-JUNK! truck holds about 16 cubic yards — roughly 3.5 to 4 standard pickup loads. Their price ladder is drawn on the side of the truck in painted lines, and the crew eyeballs where your pile lands after they load it. That's the whole system.

Three things inflate the number beyond the sticker:

  • Air counts. A disassembled sectional sofa takes half the space of an intact one. You're paying for volume, so anything hollow, bulky, or awkward — an above-ground pool liner, a swing set, an empty entertainment center — costs far more per pound than dense material.
  • Weight caps override volume. Most volume-priced services cap a full truck at 2,500–3,500 lbs. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles, and brick blow through that instantly. A half-truck of concrete can be re-quoted as a full truck or refused outright.
  • Labor tiers are real but unadvertised. Carrying a 400-lb hot tub shell up a walkout basement stairwell is not the same job as curbside pickup, and the on-site quote reflects it — usually a $75–$200 bump.

The published ranges you see online are national averages. The quote you get in your driveway is the only binding number, and it's given *after* the truck is at your house.

What Common Pueblo West Loads Actually Run

Here's what typical jobs out here land at, comparing a national-brand volume quote to what an independent local crew charges:

  • One couch, curbside: national $170–$220 / local $95–$150
  • Refrigerator plus a washer: national $260–$340 / local $140–$200
  • Two-car garage cleanout, moderately full: national $600–$900 / local $375–$600
  • Hot tub, cut and hauled: national $600–$1,000 / local $400–$700
  • Half-acre of tumbleweed, elm limbs, and cleared brush: national quote often declined / local $250–$500

The spread is not because national brands are ripping anyone off. It's franchise overhead — royalty fees typically run 8% of gross plus a national ad fund contribution, uniformed two-person crews, call-center staffing, and in our case, a 100-mile round trip before anyone touches a box. Somebody pays for that, and it's the person on the invoice.

> The honest version of "how much does GOT JUNK cost" in Pueblo West is: about 40–60% more than a local crew, in exchange for a national brand's scheduling app and a two-hour arrival window. Decide whether that's worth $200 to you.

The Pueblo West Problem Nobody Mentions

Search results for junk removal pricing are written for Denver, Phoenix, and Dallas. Pueblo West is a different animal, and three local realities change the math:

You may not be in the service area. Franchise territories are drawn by ZIP code. The 81007 area sits at the far southern edge of a Colorado Springs–based territory. Some days that's a "yes, next Thursday," some weeks it's a "we're not booking that far south right now." Call and confirm coverage before you plan around it.

Long driveways and acreage cost extra everywhere but here. A lot of Pueblo West properties sit on 1–5 acres with 200-foot gravel drives and stuff piled behind an outbuilding 150 feet from where a truck can safely park. Volume pricing doesn't have a clean line item for "carry it 150 feet," so it shows up as a vaguer, higher on-site number.

Yard debris and dirt are half the local demand. Tumbleweed, dead elm, Russian olive, decomposed granite, and old rock landscaping are the bread and butter of junk hauling on the mesa. National volume pricing handles these badly — a truckload of brush is nearly all air, and a truckload of rock is nearly all weight. Both get quoted at the top of the range or turned down. That's why [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) and [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) tend to price better with a local outfit running a dump trailer and a skid steer than with a box truck.

The DIY Numbers, So You Can Compare Honestly

Before you hire anybody, price the alternative properly:

  • Landfill run yourself: Pueblo County transfer/landfill gate fees for household loads generally run in the $25–$60 range for a pickup load, with higher rates for tires, appliances, and mattresses. Rates change — call ahead and confirm before you load. Add fuel and roughly 60–90 minutes round trip from Pueblo West.
  • Trailer rental: A 6x12 utility trailer runs about $30–$50/day locally, assuming you have a hitch and the nerve to back it up.
  • Roll-off dumpster: A 15–20 yard container in the Pueblo area typically runs $350–$525 for a 7-day rental, including a tonnage allowance of 2–3 tons, with overage charges around $60–$90 per additional ton. Good value if you're doing a multi-day project. Bad value for one heavy afternoon, and it sits in your driveway.
  • Free scrap pickup: Clean metal — appliances without refrigerant, water heaters, bed frames, fencing — has scrap value. Plenty of local haulers will take it for nothing. Do not pay $200 for someone to remove a pile of steel.

If your total junk is one pickup load, you own a pickup, and you have a free Saturday morning, you have just saved yourself $200. That is a real answer to the question you asked.

When You Should Not Hire Us, Either

This is the part most pricing pages skip, so here it is plainly.

Don't hire any hauler if the load is small and you own a truck. A single mattress, three bags of clothes, and a broken chair is a $30 landfill run and 90 minutes of your life. Every hauler in town, us included, has a minimum charge — ours starts around $95 — and paying it for a quarter-pickup load is bad value.

Don't hire us if the item has resale or donation value. A working washer, a solid dining set, an intact riding mower — these sell on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo West within a week, often for $75–$300. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar organizations will pick up qualifying furniture and building materials for free. Turning $200 of value into a $150 expense is a $350 swing in the wrong direction.

Don't hire us for a multi-week remodel. If you're gutting a kitchen over three weekends, a roll-off dumpster you fill at your own pace beats three separate hauls at three separate minimum charges. We'll tell you this on the phone. We'd rather lose the job than have you spend $900 where $450 would have worked.

Don't expect a firm price sight-unseen. Anyone who quotes you a hot tub removal over the phone without asking about deck access, gate width, and whether it's still full of water is either padding the number heavily or planning to revise it in your driveway. Photos are the minimum for an honest estimate. See our [FAQ](/faq) for what to send.

We will decline some jobs. Asbestos-containing material, wet paint and solvents, pesticides, propane tanks, medical waste, and vehicle fluids need licensed disposal we don't provide. Ammunition and pressurized cylinders are a hard no. If a [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) involves biohazard conditions, that's a remediation contractor's job, not a hauling job — and the cost profile is completely different, typically $2,000–$8,000 rather than $500–$1,500.

Same-day is not always possible. Pueblo West spring cleanup season, roughly March through May, and the weeks after any windstorm, book out 3–7 days. If you need something gone in four hours on a Saturday in April, expect to pay a premium or to hear no.

How to Get a Real Number in Ten Minutes

Skip the calculators. Do this instead:

  • Pile everything in one place, take three photos from different angles, and include something for scale — a person, a car door, a five-gallon bucket.
  • Note the worst access point: stairs, gate width, distance from where a truck can park.
  • Flag anything heavy or regulated — concrete, dirt, appliances with compressors, tires, TVs.
  • Ask two questions of every hauler: *"Is that price all-in, including disposal fees and labor?"* and *"What would make that number go up when you arrive?"*
  • Get two quotes. One national, one local. The gap will tell you what you're actually paying for.

Then compare against the honest DIY number. If the difference is under $75, haul it yourself. If it's $200 or more and the job involves stairs, weight, or a full day, hiring it out is the better trade.
