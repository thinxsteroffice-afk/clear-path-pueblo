CONSTRUCTION DEBRIS REMOVAL COST

Most construction debris removal jobs in Pueblo West land between $250 and $850. A quarter-truckload (roughly 4 cubic yards — think a small bathroom demo) runs $225–$325. A half load, about 8 cubic yards, runs $375–$525. A full 15-cubic-yard truck runs $650–$850. Heavy material is priced separately: concrete, brick, dirt, and shingles are billed by weight, generally $95–$165 per ton on top of a base fee, because a single yard of broken concrete weighs about 4,000 lbs and fills a truck's legal weight limit long before it fills the box. Roll-off dumpster rental is the other path: $375–$650 for a 10–20 yard can for 7 days, plus overage.

Below is how those numbers actually get built, and where they go wrong.

Why Construction Debris Costs More Than Regular Junk

A living-room-worth of furniture is bulky and light. Construction debris is the opposite problem: it's dense, and density is what costs money at the scale house.

Some real weights worth memorizing before you call anyone:

  • Drywall: a 1/2" 4x8 sheet is about 51 lbs. A gutted 12x14 bedroom down to the studs is 30–35 sheets — roughly 1,700 lbs before you count a single stud.
  • Asphalt shingles: about 250 lbs per square (100 sq ft) for standard 3-tab, more for architectural. A 20-square roof tear-off is 2.5 tons, and if it's a second layer, double it.
  • Concrete: a 4" slab, 10x10, is about 1.5 tons. A typical Pueblo West patio at 12x16 is closer to 3 tons.
  • Dimensional lumber and framing scrap: light per piece, but a full 15-yard load of clean framing scrap still weighs 1,200–2,000 lbs.
  • Dirt and rock: about 2,700 lbs per cubic yard dry, more when it's wet. Four yards of soil is over the legal payload of most single-axle dump trucks.

That's why a company quoting you "$400 a load" on furniture will quote something else entirely on a demo. The truck fills by weight, not by volume, and the landfill charges by the ton. If a hauler gives you a flat volume price on concrete without asking what's in it, either they haven't done many of these or they're going to renegotiate in your driveway.

What Drives Your Specific Number

Six factors move a Pueblo West quote more than anything else:

  • Material mix. Clean, sorted, single-material loads are cheapest. Mixed demo debris — drywall dust, insulation, carpet pad, tile, nails, and a broken toilet — costs more because it's slower to load and can't be diverted.
  • Access. Debris stacked in an open driveway loads in 35–45 minutes. The same volume in a backyard behind a 36" gate, 120 feet from the truck, takes 2.5–3 hours. Labor is the single biggest controllable line item.
  • Weight overage. Volume price gets you a certain tonnage allowance. Past that, you're paying the scale.
  • Drive time. Pueblo West to the disposal site and back is roughly 45–60 minutes round trip depending on where you are — a job off McCulloch prices differently than one out past Purcell.
  • Stairs and second floors. Add 15–25% for anything carried down a flight.
  • Same-day or weekend timing. Expect a $50–$100 premium on next-morning turnarounds during spring and fall, when every roofer in the county is working.

Roll-Off Dumpster vs. Hauled Away: The Actual Crossover

This is the comparison people ask for and rarely get straight.

A 20-yard roll-off in the Pueblo West area typically runs $450–$650 for 7 days with a 2–4 ton allowance, and $65–$95 per ton over. Add a permit if it sits in the public right-of-way rather than on your own property.

A full-service haul-off is more per cubic yard — you're paying for two people, a truck, loading labor, and the dump ticket bundled together.

The math, honestly:

  • Dumpster wins when the project runs longer than 3 days and you're generating debris continuously. A framing crew, a whole-house remodel, a roofer with a 4-day tear-off — the can pays for itself because nobody's waiting on a truck.
  • Haul-off wins when the debris already exists, you want it gone in one visit, and you don't want a 22-foot steel box parked on your driveway for a week cracking the apron. One demo, one pickup, done in two hours.
  • Rough crossover: past about 12 cubic yards of debris generated over more than 3 days, the dumpster is usually cheaper per yard. Under that, or for a one-time cleanout, the truck is cheaper all-in once you count your own labor.

One local wrinkle worth knowing: Pueblo West gets sustained spring winds, with gusts over 50 mph not unusual in March and April. An open-top can loaded with loose drywall, housewrap, and shingle scrap will redistribute a meaningful percentage of that load across your neighbor's yard overnight. If you rent, tarp it. That's not a sales pitch — it's a thing that happens here every spring.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

Plenty of situations where hiring a junk removal crew is the wrong call. Naming them:

You have less than a pickup load and you own a pickup. One load of clean framing scrap self-hauled to the transfer station is maybe $30–$60 in tipping fees and an hour of your time. Our minimum on a light single-item pickup is around $125–$175. If your time is worth less than $100/hour and your truck bed is already scratched, haul it yourself. That is the honest answer.

You're a GC on a multi-week build. Do not call a haul-off crew four times. Get a 30-yard can, keep it on site, swap it twice. You'll pay less per yard and your framers won't stop working to wait on us.

The material might contain asbestos. Popcorn ceilings, 9x9 floor tile, old pipe wrap, and some sheet flooring mastic in homes built before the early 1980s are the usual suspects. Colorado requires inspection before renovation or demolition once you exceed roughly 160 sq ft of surface material, 260 linear feet of pipe insulation, or 35 cubic feet of other material. An inspection runs $300–$600. We will not knowingly haul suspect ACM, and no licensed hauler should. Abatement is a separate, licensed trade and it costs $3–$10 per square foot. Finding out mid-job costs you the whole schedule.

You have hazardous waste mixed in. Liquid paint, solvents, fuel, pressurized cylinders, wet cell batteries, and treated-wood ash don't go in the truck. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection separately, and it's usually free or low-cost for residents — cheaper than any hauler.

Your debris has real resale or scrap value. Clean copper, cast iron tubs, structural steel, and undamaged appliances are worth money. A scrap run can net $100–$400 on a decent tear-out. If you're willing to make the trip, keep it.

Cost is your only variable and your timeline is open. A patient homeowner with a trailer, a free weekend, and a landfill punch card beats our price every time. We sell speed, labor, insurance, and a clean site — not the cheapest possible cubic yard.

Failure Modes to Watch For

  • The verbal estimate with no walk-through. Phone quotes on demo debris are guesses. Send photos, or get someone on site. A quote that doubles on arrival is the most common complaint in this industry, and it's usually because nobody looked.
  • No dump ticket. Ask whether the invoice will show the actual scale weight. If a company charges you a weight overage, they should be able to show the ticket. If they won't, that's your answer.
  • Illegal dumping. Cheap Craigslist haulers sometimes dump on BLM land or a vacant lot off Highway 50. In Colorado, the property owner and the generator of the waste can both catch liability. Confirm the hauler is insured and disposing legally. A $150 savings is not worth a $1,000+ citation.
  • Concrete refusals. Many volume-priced junk companies simply won't take concrete, dirt, or rock — the economics don't work on a per-yard price. If that's your material, ask up front rather than discovering it on the day. Our [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is priced by weight for exactly this reason.
  • Underestimating volume by half. Almost everybody does. Debris piles compress in your memory and expand in a truck. A 10x12 deck tear-out that "looks like a pickup load" is usually 6–9 cubic yards.

How to Get a Quote That Holds

Have these five things ready and your number will be accurate within about 10%:

  • Approximate dimensions of what's being removed (deck square footage, roof squares, room sizes)
  • Whether any concrete, dirt, brick, or tile is in the pile
  • The age of the structure, so asbestos risk gets addressed before, not during
  • Photos from two angles, including the path from the pile to where a truck can park
  • Your actual deadline, because a flexible date is worth $50–$100 off in slower weeks

Pueblo West Specifics

Lot sizes out here work in your favor — most properties have room to stage debris where a truck can back up to it, which saves real labor dollars compared to tight in-town alleys. The tradeoff is drive time and the rocky, caliche-heavy soil that makes any dig-related debris heavier than people expect.
