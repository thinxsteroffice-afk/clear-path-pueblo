JUNK REMOVAL COST CALCULATOR

Most junk removal in Pueblo West is priced by volume, not by the hour. A single item like a couch or fridge runs about $95–$175. A quarter truckload runs roughly $175–$275. A half load lands around $325–$450. A full 15-cubic-yard truckload — about 8 pickup beds of stuff — runs roughly $550–$750 out here. To calculate your own number before anyone shows up: measure the pile in feet (length × width × height), divide by 27 to get cubic yards, and multiply by $38–$50 per cubic yard. Add $50–$150 for any appliance with refrigerant, a tube TV, or a mattress. That estimate will land within about 15% of a real quote most of the time.

That's the whole calculator. The rest of this page is the part the calculator can't do — the Pueblo West–specific things that move your price up or down, and the situations where hiring anyone at all is the wrong call.

The Actual Math, Step by Step

Junk removal trucks are sold in fractions. A standard dump-body truck holds 15–16 cubic yards. Everything is a percentage of that.

Run your own pile through this:

  • Stack it first. A loose pile of boxes measures nearly double what the same boxes measure stacked. You are paying for air otherwise. Fifteen minutes of stacking has knocked $80–$120 off real quotes.
  • Measure the footprint. A pile 8 ft long × 6 ft wide × 4 ft high = 192 cubic feet ÷ 27 = 7.1 cubic yards, or a hair under half a truck. At $42/yd that's about $300.
  • Compare to your garage. A standard 2-car garage stall is roughly 20 × 10 × 8 = 1,600 cubic feet, or 59 cubic yards. A garage filled waist-high (4 ft) is about 30 yards — two full truckloads, and that's a $1,100–$1,400 job, not a $400 one. This is where most homeowners are wrong by a factor of three.
  • Count the surcharge items separately. Refrigerators and freezers carry a refrigerant recovery fee of $35–$60. Mattresses and box springs run $35–$55 each in this region. CRT televisions add $25–$45. Tires are $12–$25 apiece. Paint, solvents, and propane tanks aren't in the calculator because most haulers won't take them at any price.
  • Add labor difficulty. A pile already at the curb is the base price. Second-floor carry, a 100-foot haul from a backyard, or disassembly adds 20–40%.

> The number that ruins estimates isn't the volume. It's the 90 seconds per trip your crew spends walking from a backyard pile to the truck — forty round trips and you've bought two extra labor hours nobody quoted.

What's Different About Pricing in Pueblo West Specifically

National calculators quote Denver and Colorado Springs numbers. Those don't apply here, and the gap runs both directions.

Disposal is cheaper here, and that's real money. Pueblo County's landfill tipping fees run meaningfully below Front Range metro rates, and the drive from most of Pueblo West to a legal disposal site is 15–25 minutes rather than the 45–60 minutes a Denver hauler eats on every load. Disposal and drive time are typically 30–40% of a junk removal invoice. A quote for the identical pile in Pueblo West should come in 10–20% under what a Denver calculator spits out. If a hauler quotes you Denver pricing in Pueblo West, they're either subcontracting or guessing.

Lot size works against you. Pueblo West has a lot of 1-acre and 2.5-acre lots, and junk on an acre doesn't sit at the curb. It sits 200 feet back behind the shop, or along a fence line, or in an arroyo at the back of the property. That carry distance is the single most underestimated cost line out here. Budget an extra $75–$200 on any pile more than 100 feet from where a truck can park.

Wind and weather compress your window. Spring wind events regularly push 40–60 mph gusts across the West Mesa. Loose debris — insulation, cardboard, shingle scrap — can't be tarped safely in that, and reputable crews will reschedule rather than lose half a load on Highway 50. If your job is loose lightweight debris, expect a weather hold, not a same-day.

Rock and dirt are a different product entirely. Concrete, flagstone, and landscape rock are priced by weight, not volume, because a half-truck of concrete exceeds axle limits. Figure $95–$185 per ton rather than a volume rate. A 10 × 10 patio slab 4 inches thick is about 4 tons — roughly $450–$700 — and it will not fit the truckload model at all. That's a [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) job, and mixing it into a general junk quote is how estimates blow up on site.

Typical Pueblo West Jobs and What They Actually Run

Real ranges for jobs we see regularly out here:

  • Single mattress or box spring: $85–$135, mostly disposal fee. See [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo).
  • Washer, dryer, or water heater: $95–$150. Refrigerators run $130–$185 with recovery fees.
  • Sectional sofa (3–4 pieces): $180–$300 depending on whether it fits through the door assembled.
  • Two-car garage cleanout: $450–$900, occasionally $1,200+ if there's a workshop involved. [Garage cleanouts](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) run wide because half the volume is usually shelving nobody mentioned.
  • Hot tub removal: $400–$750. Cutting a shell down to haulable pieces is 2–4 hours of labor and consumes 4–6 cubic yards. [Hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) is almost never the $250 some calculators show.
  • Above-ground pool teardown (24 ft): $650–$1,200, plus whatever the sand base costs to move.
  • 10 × 12 shed demo and haul: $600–$1,100. Add $150–$250 if it's on a concrete pad you want gone.
  • Yard debris — tumbleweeds, tree limbs, dead juniper: $200–$500 per load. Bulky, light, and it fills a truck fast without weighing anything.
  • Storage unit cleanout (10 × 10): $400–$700. A packed 10 × 10 is about 30 cubic yards of nominal space but usually 12–18 yards of actual stuff.

When You Should Not Hire Us — Or Anyone

This is the section most junk removal sites leave out. Here's where paying a hauler is the wrong financial decision.

If your job is a single trip and you own a truck, don't hire anyone. Pueblo County residents can haul household waste to the landfill themselves. A pickup load of general household junk is typically a modest tipping fee plus an hour of your time and a tank of gas — call it $40–$70 all-in versus a $175–$275 minimum from any hauler. If you have a truck, a friend, and a Saturday, you are lighting $150 on fire by calling us. We'd rather tell you that than take the job.

If you're clearing an entire house over multiple weeks, rent a dumpster instead. A 20-yard roll-off in the Pueblo area runs roughly $375–$550 for a 7-day rental with a tonnage allowance. If you need 3+ truckloads removed, a roll-off beats us on price per yard — sometimes by 40%. Our advantage is labor and speed, not volume economics. We win when you don't want to touch the stuff or you need it gone in one visit. We lose badly on price when you have time, a driveway to park a container on, and the physical ability to load it yourself. Note the HOA and covenant angle: some Pueblo West subdivisions restrict how long a container can sit in a driveway, and that's worth a phone call before you book one.

If the pile is mostly resellable, sell it. A working refrigerator under 10 years old, decent power tools, a functional riding mower — these have real local resale value on Facebook Marketplace out here. Paying $150 to dispose of a $400 appliance is a $550 swing. Give us a call after you've listed it for two weeks and nobody bit.

If your item is hazardous, we can't take it, and neither can the calculator. Paint, motor oil, antifreeze, pesticides, propane cylinders, car batteries, ammunition, and asbestos-containing material are excluded. Those go to household hazardous waste collection, and no volume estimate applies. If half your garage is old paint cans, your junk removal quote will cover the other half only, and you'll still have a project on your hands.

If you're shopping purely on the lowest number, we will probably lose. There are haulers working out of a personal trailer with no insurance, no landfill receipts, and a price 30–40% under ours. Sometimes that works out fine. Sometimes the load ends up dumped on BLM land or a vacant lot on the mesa, and illegal dumping in Colorado can carry fines running into the thousands — with liability that can follow back to the property it came from. If cheapest-possible is the requirement, hire them with open eyes, and ask for a landfill receipt.

If you need it done in the next two hours, we may not be your answer. Same-day is realistic maybe 50–60% of the time in season. During spring and fall cleanout rushes, we're often booking 2–4 days out. An emergency premium of $75–$150 sometimes gets you moved up, and sometimes there is simply no truck.

Where Online Calculators Get It Wrong

Three failure modes show up over and over:

  • They underestimate volume by 2–3×. People measure what they see and forget what's stacked behind it. Assume you're low.
  • They ignore access. Every national calculator prices a curbside pile. Almost nothing in Pueblo West is a curbside pile.
  • They quote weight-limited materials at volume rates. Dirt, concrete, roofing, and tile break the model completely. A truck legally maxes out on weight long before it's full of shingles — a 2,000 sq ft tearoff is about 5–7 tons and takes 3+ trips regardless of what the volume math says.

A calculator gets you within about 15% on clean, accessible household junk. It gets you within about 50% on a garage, a hoard, or anything involving demolition. For those, a look at the actual pile — in person or from four photos — is the only estimate worth anything.

How to Get a Number You Can Trust in Five Minutes

Text or email four photos: the whole pile from 15 feet back, a close-up of anything heavy or unusual, the path from the pile to where a truck can park, and any doorway the stuff has to pass through. That last one changes more quotes than people expect.

Ask any hauler you call three questions: Is this all-in including disposal and labor? What surcharge items are in this pile? What happens to the price if it's bigger than the photos showed? An honest answer to the third one is a specific dollar-per-additional-yard rate, not "we'll work with you."
