JUNK REMOVAL COST BY STATE (2026)

Junk removal in Pueblo West runs $150–$250 for a single-item pickup, $250–$400 for a quarter-truck load, $400–$600 for a half load, and $650–$900 for a full 15-cubic-yard truck — which puts Colorado roughly 8–12% above the national average of about $250 for a quarter load and $600 for a full load. Nationally, the spread by state is wide: expect $180–$450 for a quarter load in California, New York, Massachusetts and Washington; $200–$375 in Colorado, Arizona, Nevada and Texas metros; and $130–$275 in Alabama, Mississippi, Arkansas, Kansas and West Virginia. The single biggest driver is not labor — it's the landfill tipping fee, which ranges from about $28/ton in Oklahoma to $145/ton in Massachusetts.

Why State Averages Exist At All: Tipping Fees, Not Wages

Every national "junk removal cost by state" chart is really a landfill map wearing a costume. A junk removal truck holds about 15 cubic yards and, loaded with household goods, weighs 2,500–4,000 lbs at the scale. Multiply that by the local tip fee and you get the floor under every quote in that state.

Rough regional tipping fees per ton:

  • Pacific Northwest / Northeast: $95–$145/ton (Washington, Massachusetts, Vermont, New Jersey)
  • California: $60–$110/ton, plus mandatory organics and C&D diversion fees
  • Colorado / Mountain West: $32–$58/ton — among the cheapest in the country
  • Deep South / Plains: $28–$45/ton (Oklahoma, Alabama, Mississippi, Kansas)
  • Islands and remote areas: $180–$240/ton in Hawaii and much of Alaska

Colorado's disposal costs are genuinely low. The Southside Landfill area rates serving Pueblo County generally sit in the $35–$50/ton range for mixed municipal solid waste. So why is a Colorado quote often higher than a Kansas quote despite cheaper dumping? Drive time. Pueblo West is spread across roughly 50 square miles of half-acre-plus lots. A crew in a dense Wichita neighborhood might do six stops a day. In Pueblo West, four is a good day. That windshield time gets priced in, and it's the number no national chart captures.

What Pueblo West Actually Costs, Item By Item

Statewide averages are useless when you're staring at one couch. Here's the real local range:

  • Single mattress or box spring: $85–$140. Colorado mattresses can't go in a compactor at most facilities; expect a $12–$25 disposal surcharge. See [mattress removal](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo).
  • Refrigerator, freezer, or A/C unit: $110–$185, including the $25–$45 refrigerant recovery fee required before disposal. [Appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) covers the certified handling.
  • Sectional sofa or large armoire: $150–$260, depending on stairs and doorway width.
  • Two-car garage cleanout: $450–$850 — typically a half to three-quarter truck. [Garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) is the most-requested job in the 81007 zip.
  • Hot tub: $400–$750. Cutting and hauling a 400-lb shell plus the deck surround is a 3–4 hour job. [Hot tub removal](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo).
  • Above-ground pool, 18–24 ft: $600–$1,100 with liner, frame, and sand base.
  • 8x10 to 12x16 shed demolition: $550–$1,400 depending on whether there's a concrete pad underneath.
  • Yard debris after a windstorm: $225–$500 per truckload of branches and tumbleweed. [Yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo).

> The honest math: about 60% of what you pay covers labor and drive time, 20% covers disposal, and 20% covers the truck, fuel, insurance, and the fact that half of all scheduled jobs get rescheduled by weather or a customer changing their mind.

The Colorado-Specific Costs Nobody Puts On A National Chart

Three things make Pueblo County different from a generic state average, and none of them show up in the big aggregator articles:

Tumbleweed and Russian thistle. After a March or October wind event, a single acreage lot on the west side can accumulate 3–5 cubic yards of tumbleweed against a fence line. It weighs almost nothing but eats volume — which is how junk removal is priced. A load that costs $28 to dump can cost $350 to haul because it fills a truck.

Xeriscape rock and concrete. Pueblo West has an enormous amount of landscape rock. Rock and concrete are priced by weight, not volume, and they go to a different facility than household junk. A 10x12 patio slab, 4 inches thick, is roughly 3 tons — that's a $180–$300 tip fee alone and a job that needs a machine, not a pickup crew. That's why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) and [skid steer services](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) are quoted separately from truckload pricing.

Metro pricing bleed. Denver and Colorado Springs franchise pricing runs 20–35% higher than Pueblo. If you got a quote from a national brand's call center, you may have been quoted off a Front Range rate card for a job 45 minutes south of their nearest truck.

When Junk Removal Is A Bad Deal — Don't Hire Us

This is the part most pricing articles skip. There are jobs where paying a hauler is straightforwardly the wrong call, and we'd rather tell you now than after you've paid.

Skip the hauler entirely if:

  • You have one item, a truck, and a free Saturday. The Pueblo County transfer station will take a household load for roughly $30–$60. If you own a pickup and the item fits, you are paying us $150 for something you can do for $40 plus an hour of your time. That's a fine trade for some people and a terrible one for others. Be honest about which you are.
  • The item has real resale value. A working washer/dryer set, a decent dining table, or a running snowblower is worth $75–$300 on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo. Many buyers will haul it themselves. You go from paying $160 to earning $200 — a $360 swing. Don't pay someone to throw away money.
  • Your job is 8+ full truckloads. At that scale, a 20-yard roll-off dumpster runs $400–$650 for a week in Pueblo County. Eight truckloads of on-demand service could be $4,000+. If you have the time, driveway space, and physical ability to load it yourself, a dumpster wins on cost by a wide margin. We'll tell you this on the phone. Full-service hauling wins on *labor*, not on *volume*.
  • You're moving in 60 days and haven't sorted anything. Pay for the cleanout after you've sorted, not before. Hauling boxes you haven't opened costs the same as hauling trash, and about 15% of the time people call back looking for something we already took.

Where full-service pricing genuinely fails:

  • Hazardous materials. We can't take paint, solvents, motor oil, pesticides, propane tanks, or asbestos-containing material. No licensed hauler in Colorado can put those in a standard load. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection events — that's the correct route, and it's usually free or under $25. If your garage is 40% old paint cans, a junk quote won't solve the problem you actually have.
  • Weight-heavy loads price badly by volume. Dirt, sod, roofing shingles, and brick can hit truck weight limits at 30% of visible capacity. A "quarter load" of shingles can legitimately cost more than a full load of furniture. If a hauler quotes you sight-unseen on shingles, expect that number to move.
  • Sight-unseen quotes are estimates, not contracts. Anyone giving you a firm price over the phone for an unseen garage is either padding it 30% or planning to renegotiate in your driveway. Photos help; an on-site walkthrough is better.
  • We can't beat a friend with a trailer. If you have that friend, use them.

How To Compare Quotes Without Getting Burned

Volume-based pricing is the industry norm, and it's easy to manipulate. Protect yourself:

  • Ask for the price in truck fractions plus the truck's cubic yardage. "Half a truck" means nothing if their truck is 10 yards and the last company's was 18.
  • Ask what's excluded. Stairs, long carries over 75 feet, disassembly, and heavy items commonly carry $25–$100 add-ons.
  • Ask about the minimum. Most Colorado haulers have a $125–$175 floor regardless of how small the item is. That's real overhead — a truck, two people, fuel, and insurance don't scale down.
  • Get the disposal answer. Ask where it goes. Anyone dodging that question is a risk to leave your name on a mattress in a ditch off Highway 50.
  • Compare against the dumpster path once. If the two numbers are close, take the hauler; if the dumpster is half the price, take the dumpster.

The Bottom Line For Pueblo West

Colorado sits in the middle-to-upper-middle of state junk removal pricing — cheaper disposal than the coasts, higher labor and drive costs than the Plains. In Pueblo West specifically, budget $150–$250 for one item, $400–$600 for a half truck, and $650–$900 for a full load, with rock, concrete, and demolition quoted separately by weight and equipment time.

The right answer isn't always hiring someone. If you have a truck, a Saturday, and one item, the transfer station is $40. If you have a full estate, a bad back, a closing date, and 3,000 lbs of accumulated life, $850 for a crew and a truck that leaves in ninety minutes is the cheapest hour you'll buy that month.
