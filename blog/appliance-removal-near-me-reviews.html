How to Read Appliance Removal Reviews Without Getting Fooled

Star ratings are close to useless on their own. A Pueblo West company with 4.9 stars from 22 reviews and one with 4.6 from 180 are not comparable. Here's what to actually look for:

  • Read the 3-star reviews first. Five-star reviews are written by people whose day went fine. One-star reviews are often about something outside the hauler's control. Three-star reviews are where you learn about the $40 surprise fee and the 45-minute late arrival.
  • Check for photos of the truck. Reviews with pictures of an actual dump trailer or box truck confirm the company has equipment, not just a buddy's F-150.
  • Look for the date spread. Twelve reviews all posted within nine days is a solicitation campaign, not a track record.
  • Look for named crew members. Real reviews say "Marcus and Dylan." Fake ones say "the team was professional and efficient."
  • Search the review text for "quote." If multiple reviewers mention the final price matched the phone quote, that's the single most predictive signal you'll find.

What Happens to Your Appliance After We Take It

Most pages skip this. It matters, because "we recycle" is an unverifiable claim unless someone explains the chain.

A refrigerator or freezer goes to a facility with certified refrigerant recovery — the R-134a or older R-12 is pulled and captured before the shell is processed. Compressor oil is drained. The steel shell, which is 55–75% of the unit's weight, gets baled and sold as scrap. Copper from the coils and windings is separated because it's worth roughly 4–6× steel by weight. Plastic liners and foam insulation are the part that genuinely does end up in the landfill in most of Colorado — anyone claiming 100% diversion on a fridge is overstating it.

A washer, dryer, or range is simpler: mostly steel, some copper in the motor, and it's largely recyclable. A dishwasher is heavier on plastic and diverts less. Realistically, a mixed appliance load out of Pueblo West lands somewhere around 60–80% diverted by weight — which is genuinely good and not the "we recycle everything" line you'll see elsewhere.
