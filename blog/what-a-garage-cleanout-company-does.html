WHAT A GARAGE CLEANOUT COMPANY DOES

A garage cleanout company sends a crew and a truck to your house. The crew carries everything you point at out of the garage, loads it and takes it away. Donatable items go to donation and recyclable items go to recycling, and the rest goes to the landfill. In Pueblo West, most single-car garage jobs take 1.5 to 3 hours. A packed two-car garage usually takes 3 to 5 hours. Across the Front Range, pricing is usually based on how much of the truck your stuff fills, not on hours worked. A small load often runs $150–$300 and a full truck $550–$800+. ClearPath Junk Removal does this work in Pueblo West through our [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) service. Below you'll find what to expect, what it really costs, and when you'd do better to handle it yourself.

What a Garage Cleanout Company Does (and Doesn't Do)

A cleanout crew mostly does heavy lifting and hauling. On a normal Pueblo West job, that means:

  • Removal of anything non-hazardous: old furniture, broken mowers, boxes, exercise equipment, shelving, carpet scraps, lumber and yard tools.
  • Appliance hauling: garage fridges and chest freezers are common here. Anything with refrigerant needs certified recovery under EPA Section 608, so it can't go straight into a landfill cell. Our [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) page covers how we handle that.
  • Electronics diversion: Colorado has banned most consumer electronics from landfills since July 1, 2013. Old TVs and computer monitors in the garage have to go to an e-waste recycler.
  • Donation runs: usable furniture, tools and household goods go to local donation centers instead of the dump.
  • A broom sweep once the floor is clear.

The crew doesn't organize what's left, install shelving, pressure-wash stained concrete or make your keep-or-toss decisions. If your garage needs a professional organizer, hire one separately. A hauling crew isn't a substitute.

What It Actually Costs in Pueblo West

Most junk haulers in southern Colorado price by volume. A standard junk truck holds about 15 to 16 cubic yards, roughly the space of six full-size pickup beds. Here are typical ranges you'll see on the Front Range:

| Load size | Rough equivalent | Typical price range | |---|---|---| | Minimum load | A few bulky items | $100–$175 | | ¼ truck | One pickup bed, heaped | $200–$300 | | ½ truck | Half of a cluttered 1-car garage | $350–$500 | | Full truck | Packed 1-car or moderately full 2-car garage | $550–$800 |

Several things push the price up:

  • Heavy materials. Concrete, dirt, roofing shingles and tile weigh far more per yard than furniture, and landfills charge by weight. Expect a surcharge. Rubble jobs fall under [concrete rock removal pueblo](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).
  • Special-handling items. Tires, mattresses and refrigerant appliances often carry per-item fees of $20–$75 each.
  • Access problems. A detached garage 150 feet down a gravel drive is common on Pueblo West's larger lots, and it adds carry time.

Treat these numbers as ranges, not quotes. An honest company will give you a firm price after seeing the load, either in person or from clear photos, and before anything goes on the truck.

When Hiring a Garage Cleanout Company Is NOT Worth It

This section may talk some readers out of hiring us, which is the point. Paying a crew is the wrong call in these situations:

1. You own a truck and have two free weekends. Say you have a full-size pickup and the job fills about half a junk truck. That's roughly 3 to 4 pickup loads. You'll pay landfill gate fees, typically around $10–$40 per pickup load depending on the site and material, plus gas and your time. If your weekend time is worth less to you than about $300, do it yourself.

2. Most of the junk is scrap metal. Old water heaters, steel shelving, car parts and copper wire are worth money. Scrap yards in Pueblo will pay you for them. If metal makes up 50% or more of the pile, haul it to a scrap yard yourself and hire a crew for whatever's left, if anything.

3. You have three items, not a garage full. A minimum-load charge of $100–$175 to remove one treadmill and two boxes is poor value if a neighbor with a trailer could do it for a case of beer. Check the Pueblo West Metropolitan District for any bulky-item or cleanup events first too.

4. The garage is mostly hazardous waste. Most junk removal companies cannot legally take these, and we can't either:

  • Gasoline and other fuel
  • Pesticides and pool chemicals
  • Motor oil in quantity
  • Automotive batteries
  • Propane cylinders
  • Unlabeled drums

Leftover paint is a partial exception. Colorado's PaintCare program, which started in 2015, accepts it for free at participating retailers. If your garage is 80% chemical shelving, you need Pueblo County's household hazardous waste program, not a junk truck.

5. You haven't decided what to keep. A crew bills on volume, and some haulers add time on site. If you spend 90 minutes debating each box while the crew waits, the job gets slower and harder for everyone. Sort first. At minimum, put tape on everything that stays.

6. You want to *sell* the contents. If the garage holds a working table saw, a gun safe, collectible tools or a classic car's parts inventory, a garage sale or estate sale company will *earn* you money. A hauler's donation run won't. For a full-house situation, see [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) and decide whether a sale should come first.

7. You need a months-long remodel dumpster. If you're gutting the garage into a shop or living space, a roll-off dumpster makes more sense. A 10 to 20 yard roll-off for about 7 days often costs $350–$550 in the Pueblo area, and you can fill it at your own pace. A cleanout crew is better for a single clear-out day, not ongoing demolition debris.

> If you can do it yourself in two weekends, have somewhere to take it and don't mind the lifting, you probably don't need us. Hire a crew when your time, your back or your deadline matters more than the few hundred dollars saved.

The Pueblo West Problem Nobody Mentions: Mice and Hantavirus

In Pueblo West, garages sit on open, dry land next to prairie, and deer mice move into cluttered garages, especially from October through March. Deer mice are the main carrier of hantavirus in Colorado. The state reports cases most years, and the disease has a fatality rate of roughly 35% according to CDC figures.

Most garage cleanout pages never bring this up. It matters most if you're doing the job yourself, because the risky step is the one people do on instinct: dry-sweeping droppings or shaking out nesting material. That puts virus particles in the air you breathe. If you find droppings, follow CDC guidance:

  • Open the garage door and air the space out for at least 30 minutes before you start.
  • Spray droppings and nests with a 1:10 bleach-to-water solution and let it soak for about 5 minutes.
  • Wipe up with paper towels while wearing rubber gloves. Don't sweep or vacuum.
  • Double-bag the waste.

If boxes along a wall are shredded and full of nests, that's a good reason to hand the job to a crew that expects it. Severe cases, where contamination runs through the whole garage, can move into [hoarding cleanout pueblo](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) territory, and those jobs need more protective gear and more time.

Pueblo West summers add a second hazard. Detached metal garages regularly hit 100°F+ inside during July afternoons. If you're doing it yourself, work before 10 a.m.

Questions to Ask Any Garage Cleanout Company

Whoever you hire, these questions separate careful operators from the rest:

  • Is the quote a firm price or an hourly estimate? Volume pricing protects you from a slow crew.
  • What's your minimum charge? This tells you whether a small job is worth booking at all.
  • Which items carry extra fees? Ask about tires, mattresses, refrigerators and heavy debris.
  • Where does the stuff go? A vague answer can mean everything goes to the landfill, or worse, gets dumped illegally, which can come back on you.
  • Are you insured if a crew member damages the door track or drywall?
