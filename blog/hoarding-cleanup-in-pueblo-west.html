HOARDING CLEANUP IN PUEBLO WEST

If you're searching for hoarding cleanup in Pueblo West, ClearPath Junk Removal does full and partial [hoarding cleanouts](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) across Pueblo West and the rest of Pueblo County. Most single-room jobs take one day. Most whole homes take 2 to 4 days. Typical costs run from about $1,200 for one heavily packed room to $6,000 or more for a full house with a garage and outbuildings. We sort, haul, donate what we can and take the rest to disposal. We are not a biohazard remediation company. If the home has human or animal waste, a death, needles or major mold, read the section below on when we're the wrong call before you book anyone.

What a hoarding cleanout in Pueblo West actually involves

Hoarding jobs here are different from jobs in town. A lot of Pueblo West lots are half an acre to several acres. That means the clutter rarely stays inside the house. On a typical job we find a packed house plus:

  • A detached garage or pole barn full to the rafters, often with old vehicles, tires and scrap metal
  • One to three sheds, some of which are failing structurally and have to come down. See [shed demolition removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo).
  • Outdoor piles of lumber, appliances and bagged trash that have weathered for years
  • Dead or overgrown yard material that has built up around the piles

So a Pueblo West hoarding job is usually three jobs: the house, the outbuildings and the yard. When you ask for quotes, make sure each company is pricing all three. A quote that covers "the house" can double once the crew opens the barn.

The work runs in this order:

1. Walkthrough (30 to 60 minutes). We look at every room and outbuilding, check for hazards and agree on what has to be saved. 2. Sorting. The crew bags and boxes items into keep, donate, sell/recycle and dispose. This is the slow part, and it's where your input matters most. 3. Hauling. Each trailer load is about 15 cubic yards. A heavily hoarded 1,800 sq ft house commonly fills 4 to 8 loads, not counting outbuildings. 4. Broom sweep. We leave floors swept and visible. This is not a deep clean or sanitizing (more on that below).

What it costs, with real numbers

Price depends on volume, how much sorting you want and how hard the access is. These are the ranges we typically see in Pueblo West:

  • Single room, floor to waist height: $1,200 to $1,800
  • Single room, floor to ceiling with goat trails: $1,800 to $2,800
  • Full home, 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft: $3,500 to $6,000
  • Garage or pole barn add-on: $800 to $2,500 depending on size and contents
  • Per-trailer-load pricing: roughly $450 to $650 per 15-yard load, depending on weight

Things that raise the price:

  • Heavy materials like concrete, dirt, roofing shingles or stacked newspapers. Paper weighs a lot more than people expect.
  • Appliances with refrigerant. A refrigerator or freezer that's been closed and full of rotted food for months gets a surcharge. It's also the worst-smelling item in most homes.
  • Detailed sorting, such as going through every box for documents, cash, jewelry or photos. Hidden cash and important papers turn up in a large share of hoarded homes. If you want a page-by-page search, plan on 30% to 50% more labor time.
  • Tires, paint and chemicals, which have to go to separate disposal.

> The biggest pricing mistake families make is getting a quote from photos of the living room, then finding out the back bedroom, garage and two sheds are just as full. Walk the whole property before you compare numbers.

When hoarding cleanup ISN'T worth it, and who should not hire us

This is the section most cleanup companies leave out. Read it before you spend any money.

1. If the person living there hasn't agreed to it. A cleanout forced on someone who is still living in the home is the most common way these jobs fail. Hoarding disorder affects an estimated 2% to 6% of adults, according to the American Psychiatric Association's diagnostic criteria. It's a recognized mental health condition, not laziness. When a house gets emptied without the person's involvement, the clutter often comes back within months, and the person may stop trusting the family members who arranged it. If the person is alive, still living there and hasn't agreed, spend your first few hundred dollars on a therapist who treats hoarding (cognitive behavioral therapy is the standard approach), not on a crew. We can come in later as part of their plan.

2. If there's biohazard material. We are a junk removal company. We are not certified to remediate:

  • Human waste, or large amounts of animal waste soaked into flooring
  • Bodily fluids, including after an unattended death
  • Needles or sharps in quantity
  • Black mold covering more than about 10 square feet, which is the EPA's rough point for bringing in a professional

For any of these, you need a licensed biohazard or trauma cleaning company *first*. A company that says it will "handle everything" and then sends a regular hauling crew into that environment is putting its workers and your liability at risk. After the remediation, we can remove the furniture, contents and debris for less than most remediation firms charge for hauling.

3. If rodents have been nesting there. This matters a lot in Pueblo West. Deer mice live across the Colorado plains and carry hantavirus. Colorado reports cases most years, and the illness kills a large share of the people who get it. Sheds and garages that have sat closed for years are prime nesting sites. Don't go in and start sweeping or moving boxes yourself. Dry sweeping stirs up contaminated dust. Ventilate for at least 30 minutes, and either hire a crew that uses proper respirators and wet-down methods or call a pest professional first.

4. If the home will be torn down or sold as-is anyway. If an investor is buying the house "as-is," get their offer before you pay for a cleanout. Some buyers knock off less for contents than a cleanout would cost you. A $5,000 cleanout that raises the sale price by $3,000 is a loss.

5. If you only need a dumpster and have willing helpers. If the clutter is mostly dry household junk with no hazards, and you have 3 or 4 able-bodied relatives and a free weekend, renting a roll-off and doing it yourselves will often cost less. Our crew's value is speed, the lifting, sorting experience and not having to deal with disposal. If you don't need those, don't pay for them.

6. If you expect a deep clean. We leave rooms empty and broom-swept. We don't shampoo carpet, scrub walls or remove smells soaked into drywall. After years of hoarding, flooring often has to be torn out completely. Budget separately for cleaning or flooring work.

Pueblo West specifics most companies don't mention

Code complaints go to the county, not a city. Pueblo West is an unincorporated community run by a metropolitan district. That means property nuisance complaints are generally handled by Pueblo County, not a city code office. If a neighbor has filed a complaint about outdoor junk, find out which agency sent the notice and what the deadline is. Deadlines in these notices are often 30 days or less. Tell us the date when you call so we can schedule around it.

Wind changes how we work outside. Spring gusts here regularly top 40 mph. Loose insulation, plastic and paper from outdoor piles blow onto neighboring lots quickly. On windy days we bag and tarp loads as we go, and sometimes we reschedule outdoor work to a calmer morning. A company that doesn't plan for wind can leave you with an angry neighbor and a new complaint.

Long-distance families. A lot of our hoarding calls come from adult children living out of state whose parent has moved into care or died. If that's you, we can do the walkthrough on a video call, send photos of anything questionable before we toss it and ship back a box of documents and photos. If the home belongs to someone who died, our [estate cleanout service](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) may be the better fit. It's built around probate timelines and dividing items among heirs.

How to choose a hoarding cleanup company in your area

Ask every company these questions, including us:

  • "Will you walk the whole property, outbuildings included, before quoting?" If they won't, the quote isn't real.
  • "What do you do with items that can be donated?" Usable furniture, clothing and housewares should go to local donation centers, not the landfill.
  • "What happens if you find cash, firearms, ammunition or documents?" The answer should be "we stop, set it aside and tell you," every time.
  • "Are you insured, and who handles hazards you aren't certified for?" A good company will tell you what it won't do.
  • "Can the person who lives here be present and make decisions?" If the answer is yes, the job will take longer. It will also hold up better afterward.
