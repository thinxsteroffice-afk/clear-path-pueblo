HOARDING CLEANUP COST, PUEBLO WEST

Most hoarding cleanups in Pueblo West run $2,200 to $9,500, with the typical single-family job landing around $4,800. Light Level 1–2 clutter in a 1,200 sq ft house is usually $1,800–$3,200 and takes one crew day. A Level 4–5 home — floor-to-ceiling, blocked exits, rodent activity — runs $7,000–$16,000 and takes three to six days. Detached garages and outbuildings, which almost every Pueblo West property has, add $900–$2,600 on top. Prices here are driven by volume in cubic yards and crew hours, not square footage, so two identical 1,600 sq ft homes on Purcell Blvd can be $3,100 and $11,000 apart depending on what's actually inside.

Why Square-Footage Pricing Is Useless Here

A lot of national hoarding companies quote by home size. That number is close to meaningless. What we actually price is:

  • Volume, measured in cubic yards. Our trailers hold roughly 20 cubic yards each. A packed 1,600 sq ft home with 3-foot paths runs 60–90 cubic yards — three to five full loads.
  • Crew hours. Two techs at a working pace clear about 8–12 cubic yards per hour when nothing needs sorting, and 2–3 cubic yards per hour when the homeowner is present and making keep/toss decisions.
  • Disposal weight. We budget $50–$62 per ton at the scale. A heavy hoard with books, water-damaged paper, and dirt-filled carpet can hit 9–14 tons where a typical house cleanout is 4–6.
  • Special-handling items, each priced separately (see below).

The single biggest cost variable is not the stuff. It's whether someone is standing there deciding about the stuff. A crew that can work uninterrupted finishes a Level 3 home in two days at $3,400. The same home with a family member reviewing every box takes five days and costs $7,800. That is the same house and the same landfill weight — the difference is 30 extra labor hours.

Line Items That Get Billed Separately

These are the charges that surprise people, because most quotes fold them into a vague "disposal fee." Ours are itemized:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units: $35–$55 each for refrigerant recovery and certified handling. Hoards commonly contain 3–8 of these. See [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Mattresses and box springs: $30–$45 each. We routinely pull 6–11 out of a single house.
  • Tires: $5–$9 per passenger tire, $18–$30 for truck and tractor tires. Pueblo West acreage lots collect these. A stack of 40 tires behind a shed is a $260 line item.
  • Paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals: cannot go in a landfill load. We separate and stage these for household hazardous waste drop-off, which adds $150–$400 in labor and trips.
  • Propane tanks, ammunition, black powder, old flares: common in this county and non-negotiable. Ammo gets set aside for the family or law enforcement; we do not haul it. Budget 1–2 hours of crew time just for sorting it out safely.
  • Rodent contamination: deer mice are endemic across Pueblo County, and hantavirus risk is real in structures that have been closed up. Nest-heavy jobs require N95/P100 respirators, disposable suits, and wet-cleaning instead of sweeping. That adds $400–$1,100 to a job and slows the crew by roughly 20%.

The Pueblo West Factor Nobody Else Prices In

Pueblo West Metro District does not provide residential trash service. Every household contracts privately, and when money gets tight or a subscription lapses, accumulation starts outside as fast as it does inside. Add one-acre-plus lots with room to keep things, and the hoard here has a shape you don't see in denser cities: 35–60% of the total volume is often outdoors.

That means scrap piles, dead vehicles, collapsed sheds, pallets, fencing, and 15 years of yard debris. It also means a skid steer is frequently cheaper than bodies. Hand-loading a 900 sq ft debris field takes two techs about 6 hours at roughly $85/hour combined. A skid steer does it in 90 minutes at a $375–$550 equipment day rate, including transport. On outdoor-heavy jobs we quote it that way on purpose — see [skid steer services pueblo](/services/skid-steer-services-pueblo) and [yard debris removal pueblo](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo).

Two more local realities: unpaved driveways off Bandera or McCulloch can be soft enough after a storm that a loaded 14,000 lb trailer will rut or sink, which costs a half-day; and the drive to disposal from western Pueblo West adds 35–50 minutes round trip per load versus a job inside city limits. On a five-load job that's over three hours of billable driving.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the part that costs us work, and you should read it before you call anyone.

Do not pay a hoarding crew if nothing needs sorting. If the occupant has died or permanently moved out, the family wants nothing, and the property just needs emptying, you are buying a cleanout, not a hoarding service. Rent a 30-yard roll-off for $480–$650 delivered, grab three helpers at $25/hour, and you'll clear a 1,400 sq ft house for $1,100–$1,600 total. We'd charge $4,000+ for the same house because our price includes sorting protocols and recovery searching you don't need. Our [estate cleanout pueblo](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) page walks through when that lighter option makes sense.

Do not hire us for biohazard conditions. We do not do human waste beyond incidental, decomposition, sewage backup, or needle-heavy environments. Those require IICRC-certified remediation at $75–$150 per technician-hour plus antimicrobial treatment, and running a general junk crew through them is unsafe and legally sloppy. If you describe those conditions to us, we will tell you to call a bio firm first and come back to us for the bulk haul afterward.

Do not clean out a home against the resident's will if you can avoid it. Forced cleanouts of a living hoarder without their buy-in are the highest-relapse jobs we run. In our experience, roughly one in three homes we empty without any accompanying treatment or ongoing support is visibly refilling within 12 months. You will have spent $6,000 to reset a clock, not solve a problem. Court-ordered and code-enforcement situations are different — sometimes there's no choice — but go in with clear eyes.

> If nobody addresses why the house filled, the cleanout is a very expensive pause button. Spend $6,000 once, or spend it every three years.

Do not expect us to find everything. We search deliberately and we have returned cash, jewelry, deeds, and savings bonds to families. We also miss things. Paper currency inside a magazine in load two of five is gone. If recovery matters, pay for supervised sorting and accept the slower rate — or pull the valuables yourself over a weekend before we arrive.

Skip us if your budget is under $1,500 and the job is genuinely large. A partial cleanout that clears one room and leaves the rest often accomplishes nothing functional. Save until you can do the whole thing, or start with the [garage cleanout pueblo](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) so you at least gain usable storage and a place to stage keepers.

We are also the wrong call on a 48-hour deadline for a Level 5 property. Our normal crew is two to four people. Emptying a severe hoard in two days takes eight to ten bodies and multiple trailers running in rotation. We'll say so and refer out rather than take your deposit and miss the date.

Who Actually Pays, and What Insurance Covers

Homeowners policies almost never cover hoarding cleanup — it's classified as maintenance and neglect, not a covered peril. Exceptions are narrow: if a covered event happened (burst pipe, roof failure, fire), the insurer may pay for debris removal caused by that event, typically capped at 5% of the dwelling limit. On a $310,000 dwelling limit that's about $15,500 available for debris removal, which can absorb most of a cleanout if the adjuster agrees the loss caused it. Get that in writing before you count on it.

Common payers we see: adult children splitting the bill, estate funds released after probate, landlords recovering costs from a security deposit and judgment, and property managers on [foreclosure eviction cleanout pueblo](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) jobs. Pueblo County code enforcement cases usually come with a compliance window of 10–30 days, which is enough time if you call in the first week and not the third.

How to Get a Number That Doesn't Move

  • Insist on an on-site walkthrough. Phone quotes on hoarding work are guesses. Ours takes 20–30 minutes and is free within Pueblo West and Pueblo. Photos of every room, including closets and the garage, get you within about 15% if an in-person visit isn't practical.
  • Ask for the quote broken into three parts: haul volume, labor hours, and special-handling items. If a company won't separate those, you can't compare bids.
  • Ask what happens if it's worse than it looks. Ours: we stop and re-quote before exceeding the estimate, never after. Under-floor and behind-wall surprises are the usual trigger.
  • Ask what gets donated. We route usable furniture and household goods to local donation partners, which trims landfill weight and can be worth $300–$900 in deductible value on a big job if you get receipts.
  • Get the disposal proof. Scale tickets should be available on request. It's the only hard evidence of what actually left your property.
