APPLIANCE REMOVAL: NOT ROCHESTER NY

If you searched "appliance removal Rochester NY," we're the wrong company. ClearPath Junk Removal works only in Pueblo West and the Pueblo, Colorado area. We don't serve Rochester, New York. Rochester residents should look up the City of Rochester's bulk refuse rules or a hauler based in Monroe County. If you're in Pueblo West, the short answer is this: removing one appliance usually costs between $75 and $150, can often be done within 24 to 48 hours, and any refrigerator, freezer or AC unit has to have its refrigerant recovered by a certified technician before the metal is recycled. The rest of this page covers what it costs here, when it's worth paying someone, and when it isn't.

What appliance removal in Pueblo West actually involves

Hauling appliances away from a Pueblo West property isn't like a curbside pickup in a dense city. Many lots here are an acre or more, driveways are often gravel, and a lot of old appliances end up in detached garages, sheds or barns rather than a kitchen. Here's what a typical job includes:

  • Disconnecting the unit. We unplug standard 120V appliances and shut off and cap the water supply on a fridge's ice maker or a washer. We don't disconnect gas lines (more on that below).
  • Moving it out. A full-size refrigerator weighs 250 to 350 pounds. A washer is 150 to 200 pounds and a range is 150 to 250 pounds. Getting one down a split-level staircase takes two people, a stair-climbing dolly and straps.
  • Sorting it by where it goes. Units that still work can be donated. Units that use refrigerant go to a recycler that can recover it. Plain metal appliances like washers, dryers and ranges go to scrap metal recycling.
  • Hauling and disposal. Disposal and recycling fees are included in the quote, so you don't make a separate trip or pay a separate bill.

Our [appliance removal pueblo](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) page lists the specific units we take. The main point is that the price covers more than lifting: you're also paying to have the refrigerant handled legally.

The refrigerant rule most people don't know about

Under Section 608 of the federal Clean Air Act, it's illegal to knowingly vent refrigerant into the air. Before a refrigerator, freezer, window AC, dehumidifier or water cooler is scrapped, its refrigerant has to be recovered by someone certified by the EPA. A home refrigerator usually holds only 4 to 16 ounces of refrigerant. Units built before about 1995 often contain R-12, an ozone-depleting refrigerant that was phased out decades ago.

This affects you because many scrap yards and landfills won't accept a cooling appliance unless there's proof the refrigerant was removed, usually a signed tag or sticker. If you load an old freezer into your truck and drive it to a scrapper without that tag, you may get turned away and have to haul it home again. That's one of the most common do-it-yourself failures we hear about.

> A washer is just a box of steel. A refrigerator counts as regulated equipment until a certified technician says otherwise.

When appliance removal ISN'T worth paying for

This is the part most junk removal companies leave out. In plenty of cases, you shouldn't call us.

1. You're buying a new appliance from a big-box store. Most major retailers will haul the old unit away when they deliver the new one, for free or for about $25 to $30. That's cheaper than we can be for a single item. Check the box at checkout. Just know they usually won't take a unit from the garage or basement, only one that's already disconnected and sitting where the new one goes.

2. Your appliance still works and isn't very old. A washer or fridge less than about 10 years old that runs fine has resale or donation value. Pueblo has a Habitat for Humanity ReStore, and listings on Facebook Marketplace for working appliances often draw a buyer, or at least someone willing to pick it up for free, within a few days. Paying us to haul away something another family could use makes no sense.

3. You have a truck, a helper and a plain metal appliance. If it's a dryer, washer or range on the ground floor and you own an appliance dolly, you can take it to a scrap metal recycler yourself. Don't expect to get paid much, though. Light scrap steel often brings only a few cents per pound, so a 150-pound washer may earn you $3 to $8. Mostly what you save is our fee, at the cost of your back and your afternoon.

4. It's a gas appliance that hasn't been disconnected. We do not disconnect gas lines on ranges, dryers or water heaters. Neither should any junk hauler who isn't licensed for gas work. If your gas range is still hooked up, call a licensed plumber or your gas utility first. A bad disconnect can cause a gas leak, and no removal fee is worth that risk. This adds cost and a scheduling step we can't skip for you.

5. It's a built-in or hardwired unit. Built-in wall ovens, cooktops and some dishwashers are wired straight into a 240V circuit or set into cabinetry. If removing one means cutting a countertop or capping live wires, you need an electrician or contractor. We'll haul it once it's out, but taking it out is a different job.

6. The fridge has been sitting closed for months with food inside. If a refrigerator lost power with food in it, it may need to be emptied before anyone moves it, and in some cases it will leak on the way out. We can usually still take it, but expect an extra charge for the mess, and some recyclers reject units that are badly contaminated. Before you call, empty it, tape the doors shut and leave it propped open in a garage for 24 to 48 hours.

7. You're outside our area. If you're in Rochester, Denver or anywhere else outside southern Colorado, we can't help, and a company that tells you otherwise is probably reselling your job to someone else. Our [pueblo](/areas/pueblo) page shows where we actually work.

How to get the best result if you do hire someone

If it does make sense to hire help, these steps keep the job fast and the price predictable:

  • Measure your doorways. Most refrigerators are 30 to 36 inches deep, and standard interior doors are often only 30 to 32 inches wide. Sometimes the fridge doors have to come off to get it out.
  • Shut off the water to the ice maker a day ahead if you can, and put a towel under it.
  • Clear a path from the appliance to the driveway. In Pueblo West, that often means moving a vehicle so the truck can reach the garage.
  • Send photos when you ask for a quote. One picture of the unit and one of the route out lets us give you a firm price instead of a range.
  • Ask where it goes. You should get a straight answer about who handles the refrigerant recovery and where the metal is recycled. If a hauler can't tell you, that's a warning sign.
