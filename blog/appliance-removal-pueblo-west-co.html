What Appliance Removal Costs in Pueblo West

Pricing depends on three things: how big the appliance is, how hard it is to get out, and whether it has refrigerant. Typical ranges for our area:

  • Washer or dryer (single unit): $75–$110
  • Washer and dryer pair: $120–$180
  • Refrigerator or chest freezer: $100–$150, with refrigerant handling included
  • Electric range, dishwasher, or microwave: $60–$100
  • Water heater (40–50 gallon, already disconnected and drained): $80–$125
  • Add-on to a larger load: often $30–$60 per appliance when it rides along with a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or furniture haul

Stairs, tight hallways, and basement units add time. A full-size side-by-side fridge weighs 250–350 pounds. Getting it up a basement staircase safely takes two people and about 20–30 minutes of careful work, and the price reflects that.

Most Pueblo West homes sit on half-acre to multi-acre lots with long driveways and detached garages or shops. That's usually good news: we can often back the truck right up to the garage door, and an easy carry costs you less. A homeowner on a 2-acre lot off Purcell Boulevard with a dead freezer in an open garage will usually pay at the low end of the range.

Why Refrigerators Cost More (And Why That's Not a Junk Fee)

Refrigerators, freezers, dehumidifiers, and window AC units all hold refrigerant. Section 608 of the Clean Air Act makes it illegal to knowingly vent refrigerant into the air. The last person in the disposal chain must make sure it's recovered by EPA-certified technicians before the unit is crushed or shredded. Fines for venting can run tens of thousands of dollars per day, per violation.

Older units add another wrinkle. A fridge made before roughly 1995 may still hold R-12 (Freon), an ozone-depleting refrigerant that hasn't been produced in the U.S. since 1996. Many fridges also have foam insulation made with blowing agents that metal recyclers won't take without special handling.

> The extra $25–$50 on a fridge pickup pays for recovering the refrigerant. A hauler who doesn't charge it is either eating the cost or skipping the recovery.

If a quote for a fridge is the same as for a washer, ask where the refrigerant goes. A good hauler will answer that directly.

What Happens to Your Appliance After Pickup

Most people assume old appliances end up in the landfill. In our case, most don't. Appliances are roughly 75% steel by weight, which makes them some of the easiest household junk to recycle. Here's the usual path for a load leaving Pueblo West:

1. Working units in good shape go to resale or donation when someone will take them. A washer under 8 years old that still runs often finds a second home. 2. Refrigerant units get the refrigerant recovered by a certified technician before anything else happens. 3. Everything else goes to a metal recycler in Pueblo, where it's shredded and the steel, copper, and aluminum are separated. 4. Non-metal parts that can't be recycled, such as plastic liners, glass shelves, and rubber gaskets, are the only parts that typically go to the landfill.

Scrap prices move with the commodities market. When steel prices are high, it helps keep our pricing down. We don't promise a scrap credit on single-item pickups, because one dryer is only worth a few dollars in metal.

Pueblo West Conditions That Kill Appliances Early

Water in Pueblo West comes largely from the Arkansas River system and is fairly hard. Hard water leaves mineral scale in water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines. Water heaters across the country typically last 8–12 years. Around here we often see them fail toward the short end of that range, especially when the tank was never flushed.

Garage fridges and chest freezers are the other common pickup. Pueblo West winters regularly drop into the single digits, and summer afternoons in an uninsulated garage can top 100°F. A standard kitchen fridge isn't built for either extreme. In the cold, the compressor may stop cycling and the freezer side can thaw. In the heat, it runs nonstop and burns out years early. If your garage fridge died after its third winter, that's probably why.

When Paying for Appliance Removal ISN'T Worth It

This section might cost us the job, but you should hear it anyway. In several common situations, paying a junk removal company is the wrong call:

  • You're buying a new appliance from a big retailer. Most major appliance retailers offer haul-away when they deliver, either free or for a small fee, often $0–$40. If the new fridge is arriving Thursday, have the delivery crew take the old one. It's almost always cheaper than calling us separately.
  • The appliance still works and is less than about 10 years old. List it free on Facebook Marketplace or a Pueblo West community group. A working washer or fridge listed as "free, you haul" is usually picked up within 1–3 days. Some local nonprofit resale stores also accept working appliances. Call first, because many won't take units older than 10 years or anything with dents or missing parts.
  • You have a truck, a dolly, and a non-refrigerant appliance. If it's a dryer, washer, or range and you can load it yourself, a Pueblo scrap yard will usually take it for free and may pay you a little. You'd save the whole fee in exchange for about an hour of your time.
  • Your electric utility is running a recycling rebate. Utilities in Colorado have offered programs that pick up working secondary fridges and freezers and pay a rebate, sometimes $50 or more. These programs come and go, so check your provider's website first. If one is running, it beats paying us.

There are also jobs we can't or won't do. Know these before you book:

  • We don't disconnect gas lines. If your range or dryer runs on natural gas or propane, a licensed plumber or your gas company has to cap the line first. We won't touch a connected gas appliance. That's for safety, not caution for its own sake.
  • We don't drain or uninstall water heaters that are still plumbed. It needs to be disconnected and drained before we arrive, or you'll need a plumber, which often costs $100–$200 on top of our fee.
  • Built-in and hardwired units can be a problem. Wall ovens, built-in microwaves, and hardwired cooktops sometimes need an electrician or carpenter to release them from the cabinets. If yours is built in, tell us when you request a quote.
  • One small item can be cheaper to move yourself. If it's a single countertop microwave or a dorm fridge, our minimum charge may cost more than the item is worth to get rid of. Put it out for bulk trash pickup if your hauler offers it.

If none of these fit your situation, for example a dead basement freezer, no truck, and no retailer haul-away, then hiring a crew makes sense.

How to Get an Accurate Quote (And Avoid Surprises)

A quote is only accurate if the information behind it is. Before you call, have this ready:

  • What it is and roughly how big: "French-door fridge, 36 inches wide" beats "a fridge"
  • Where it is: garage, main floor, basement, or second floor
  • Any obstacles: stairs, narrow doors, a gravel drive, or a steep slope
  • Whether it's disconnected: unplugged, gas capped, and water lines off
  • Other items: adding a mattress or a few boxes often costs little extra

Photos are the best tool for this. A couple of phone pictures showing the appliance and its path out of the house usually gets you a firm price in under an hour during business hours.

Before pickup day, empty the fridge and leave the doors open for 24 hours so it can defrost, or put towels down. Disconnect the washer hoses and drain them into a bucket. Clear a path at least 36 inches wide from the appliance to the door.
