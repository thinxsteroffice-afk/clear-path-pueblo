ESTATE SALES NEAR ME THIS WEEKEND

Estate sales in and around Pueblo West almost always run Friday and Saturday, 8 a.m. to 2 p.m., with a handful adding a half-price Sunday. To find one happening today or tomorrow, check these four places in this order: EstateSales.net and EstateSale.com (filter to ZIP 81007 plus a 25-mile radius, which pulls in Pueblo, Rye, Beulah, and Colorado City), the Pueblo West / Pueblo County buy-sell-trade Facebook groups, Craigslist Pueblo under "garage & moving sales," and the classifieds in *The Pueblo Chieftain*. Most listings post on Tuesday or Wednesday for that weekend, and photos go up 24–48 hours before doors open. If nothing shows within 25 miles, widen to 50 and you'll pick up Cañon City and Fountain.

That's the shopper's answer. What follows is the part nobody tells you until Sunday afternoon.

What Actually Happens After the Sale Ends

We're a junk removal crew based in Pueblo West, and a real share of our Monday calls come from the same weekend's estate sales. The pattern is consistent: a family clears a lifetime of belongings, the sale grosses more than expected, and then 30–40% of the original volume is still sitting in the house at 2 p.m. Saturday. Nobody buys the 1990s entertainment center. Nobody buys the box springs. Nobody buys the 14 storage totes of holiday decorations, the pressboard bookshelves, the treadmill in the garage, or the three-quarters-full paint shelf.

If the house is being listed, that leftover volume is now on a clock. Most Pueblo West listings want photos within a week of the sale, and closings routinely give you 72 hours after final walkthrough to have the property broom-clean.

> The sale is the part everyone plans for. The leftovers are the part that blows the timeline — and they arrive on the one weekend the family has already spent all its energy.

Rough Numbers, So You Can Budget

Pricing in our market runs by volume, not by weight, and here's what that looks like in practice:

  • Quarter trailer (a bedroom's worth — dresser, box spring, 8–10 boxes): roughly $175–$250
  • Half trailer (a typical estate-sale leftover pile): $350–$500
  • Full 16-foot trailer, loaded: $600–$850
  • Mattress and box spring, each: $40–$75 on top, because they carry a separate disposal fee at area facilities
  • Hot tub, cut down and hauled: $450–$800 depending on deck access and whether power was already disconnected

A typical three-bedroom Pueblo West home post-sale lands at one to one and a half trailers, and a three-person crew clears it in 2–4 hours. Tipping fees at area facilities run in the neighborhood of $45–$75 per ton and are baked into those numbers — you're not billed separately.

For context on the other side of the ledger: full-service estate sale companies in southern Colorado typically take 30%–50% of gross. On a $3,000 sale, that's $900–$1,500 to the operator. Knowing that helps you decide whether the cleanout cost is a rounding error or a real bite.

Why Pueblo West Specifically Is Harder Than It Looks

This isn't generic advice — the geography here changes the math.

  • Lot sizes. Much of Pueblo West sits on 1 to 2.5 acre parcels. Estates out here don't just have a garage; they have a shed, a carport, a burn pile, and 20 years of accumulated yard iron behind the fence line. The house cleanout is often the *smaller* half of the job.
  • No municipal trash pickup. Pueblo West is served by private haulers, and standard residential service won't touch furniture, mattresses, or construction material. There's no "just set it at the curb" option.
  • Metro District covenants. Pueblo West Metropolitan District rules restrict visible outdoor storage of junk, appliances, and inoperable vehicles. Leaving a leftover pile staged in the side yard for three weeks while you figure it out can generate a compliance notice.
  • Wind and elevation. At roughly 4,700 feet with regular 30+ mph gusts, a tarped pile in an open driveway redistributes itself across your neighbor's property overnight.
  • Distance to disposal. The run into Pueblo's disposal facilities is about 12 miles / 20 minutes each way from central Pueblo West. If you're self-hauling with a half-ton pickup, budget 4–6 round trips and most of a Saturday.

When You Should NOT Hire Us

This is the section that costs us money, and we'd rather write it than field an angry call.

Skip us if you own a truck and have a free weekend. If the leftovers fit in two or three pickup loads, self-hauling costs you a tank of gas and the tipping fee. You'll spend maybe $80–$140 total versus our $250 minimum. That's a genuinely better deal, and we'll tell you so on the phone.

Do not call us before an appraiser. We are haulers, not appraisers. We have absolutely put mid-century furniture, tools, and one cast-iron sewing machine base into a trailer that a specialist would have valued at four figures. If the estate contains anything from before roughly 1970 — furniture, jewelry, firearms, coins, art, sterling — get it looked at first. Our crew works fast, and fast is the enemy of "wait, what was that?"

Do not call us if the estate is unresolved. If probate is open and no personal representative has been appointed, nobody has legal authority to dispose of the property. We will ask. If the answer is unclear, we'll decline until it isn't, and a sibling dispute is not a problem a trailer solves.

We are the wrong call for pure sorting. We don't itemize, catalog, or separate keepsakes from trash. If you need someone to go through 40 boxes of paperwork and pull the tax records and photo albums, that's a professional organizer or senior move manager, billed hourly — a fundamentally different service. Point at a pile; we take the pile.

We don't guarantee donation receipts. We route usable goods to local charities when they'll accept them, but intake rules are strict and change without notice. Upholstered furniture, older cribs, and non-working electronics get rejected regularly. If a tax deduction is a meaningful part of your plan, drive the items to the charity yourself and get the receipt in your own hand.

We cannot always do "today." Same-day is realistic maybe half the time on weekdays, and Saturday afternoons in spring are our busiest window of the year. Genuine 48-hour turnaround is our honest promise. If your closing is Monday and you're calling Sunday night, we may not be your answer.

Some jobs need a specialist, not us. Heavy biohazard, rodent infestation, or anything requiring respirators and containment belongs to a remediation contractor. We handle standard [hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo) volume, but there's a line, and we say so at the walkthrough rather than after we've started.

Hazardous material stays behind. Paint, solvents, pesticides, propane tanks, motor oil, and batteries don't go in our trailer — that's a Pueblo County household hazardous waste event, and those run on a limited schedule. Plan around it.

How to Cut Your Cleanout Bill Roughly in Half

  • Price the leftovers, not the good stuff. Ask any estate sale operator to tell you before Friday what they expect *won't* sell. Pre-plan that pile.
  • Run a free-pile Sunday. Post "curb alley, free, Pueblo West" in the local Facebook groups Sunday morning. In our experience this moves 20–35% of leftover volume before we ever arrive.
  • Consolidate to one location. Garage or driveway. A crew walking a 2,000 sq ft house room by room takes noticeably longer than one loading from a single staged pile — and labor time is what you're paying for.
  • Book before the sale, not after. Slots for the Monday after a big sale weekend fill early.
  • Separate metal. Appliances, bed frames, and yard iron have scrap value. Pulling those out first can shave real dollars off your quote.
  • Take photos, then get a firm number. Any hauler quoting sight-unseen with no photos is guessing, and guesses drift upward on arrival.
