GETTING RID OF OLD APPLIANCES COST

Getting rid of old appliances near me — in Pueblo West, that means one of four options: haul it to the Pueblo County transfer station yourself (pickup truck, dump fee, and a refrigerant certificate if it's a fridge), list it free on Facebook Marketplace and hope a scrapper shows, ask your delivery crew to take the old one (usually $25–$50, only if you're buying new), or book a removal service. ClearPath charges $75–$95 for a single appliance in Pueblo West and $45–$60 for each additional unit on the same stop, doors and refrigerant handled, typically scheduled within 24–48 hours. Below is the honest math on all four, including the times you should absolutely not pay us.

Why Pueblo West Is Different From Pueblo City

Pueblo West is unincorporated Pueblo County. There is no municipal trash service and no city bulky-item pickup day. Whatever hauler you contract with — and most Pueblo West households pay somewhere in the $25–$40 per month range for weekly cart service — is under no obligation to touch a chest freezer sitting at the end of your driveway. That's the single biggest difference from a Pueblo City address, and it's the reason "just set it out at the curb" advice from national blog posts fails here.

The second difference is geography. Pueblo West Metro District lots frequently run a full acre or more, many with unpaved or gravel drives and a detached shop set 150 feet back from the road. An appliance that's easy to grab from a townhome garage in Colorado Springs turns into a two-person carry across soft caliche after an August monsoon. If your driveway is dirt and it rained in the last 24 hours, tell whoever you hire — it changes the truck they bring.

The Real Cost of Doing It Yourself

Self-hauling is legitimately the cheapest option if you already own the truck. Run the numbers honestly:

  • Fuel and time: the round trip from Pueblo West to the county's disposal site runs roughly 40–60 minutes of driving plus queue time. Call (719) 583-6250 — the Pueblo County landfill line — for current gate hours and the appliance fee before you load, because the schedule and the fee both change.
  • Weight: a standard side-by-side refrigerator runs about 250 lbs, a chest freezer 150–200 lbs, a top-load washer 150 lbs, an electric dryer 125 lbs, and a 40-gallon water heater about 120 lbs empty. A full one is another 334 lbs of water — 40 gallons at 8.34 lbs per gallon. Drain it first or you will not lift it.
  • Refrigerant: any unit with a sealed system — refrigerator, freezer, window AC, dehumidifier — cannot legally go to the landfill until the refrigerant is recovered by a certified tech. Most disposal sites either refuse the unit or add a refrigerant recovery fee, commonly $20–$40.
  • Doors: federal law dating to the 1956 Refrigerator Safety Act, plus Colorado child-entrapment statutes, means you remove the doors or defeat the latch before an unattended fridge sits anywhere accessible. Four screws, five minutes, do it the day it comes out of the kitchen.
  • Injury: a fridge falling down three basement steps has roughly the same energy as a small motorcycle. Emergency room visits start around $1,500 in Pueblo County before imaging.

The Scrap Value Myth

People hear "appliances are worth money" and assume the hauler is making a killing. Scrap steel in southern Colorado has been trading in the neighborhood of $100–$180 per ton — call $0.05 to $0.09 per pound. A 250-lb refrigerator therefore grosses somewhere around $13 to $22 at the yard, before anyone pays for fuel, refrigerant recovery, or 45 minutes of two people's labor. Sealed-system units are worth *less* than plain steel to most yards because the recovery step costs money.

> If a stranger on Marketplace offers to take your fridge "for free because of the scrap," they are earning about eighteen dollars. That's not enough to pay for a legal disposal, which is exactly why some of those units end up in an arroyo off Purcell with your name still on the warranty card inside.

That's the part nobody says out loud. Free scrapper pickup often works fine. Sometimes it works fine right up until the county traces an illegally dumped appliance back to the last known owner. If you go the free route, get the person's name and a photo of their plate, and pull any paperwork out of the unit first.

When Paying a Removal Service Is a Waste of Your Money

This is the section our marketing person hates. Skip us in these situations:

  • You're buying a replacement. Lowe's, Home Depot, and most local dealers will haul the old unit for $25–$50, sometimes free with delivery. That is cheaper than any standalone removal call, ours included. Ask at the point of sale — the haul-away option often has to be added before the delivery is scheduled, and it can't be bolted on afterward.
  • The appliance still works and is under about 12 years old. A functioning washer or fridge sells in Pueblo West for $100–$250 in a week on Marketplace. Paying $85 to remove something you could sell for $150 is a $235 swing in the wrong direction.
  • You own a truck and one thing needs to go. One appliance, one trip, no stairs — self-haul wins on pure economics. Our value shows up on volume, stairs, and sealed systems, not on a single dryer you can slide up a ramp.
  • Your old fridge is a working second unit in the garage. Before removing it, know that a 1990s-era fridge can pull 1,200–1,500 kWh per year versus 350–450 kWh for a current ENERGY STAR model. At Black Hills Energy residential rates in the $0.14–$0.16 per kWh range, that garage beer fridge is costing roughly $130–$180 a year to run. Removing it is the right call — but check first whether Black Hills is running an appliance recycling rebate, which has historically paid $25–$50 for a working unit they pick up. Confirm the program is active before you book anyone; these come and go.
  • You need it gone today and it's Sunday at 6 p.m. We don't run emergency appliance calls. If your basement is flooding from a failed water heater, you need a plumber, not a junk hauler.

Failure modes worth naming: we can't disconnect a hard-plumbed gas line — that's a licensed plumber's job, and we'll leave a gas range or gas dryer in place if the line is live. We don't do electrical disconnects on hardwired 240V units. If a fridge has been sitting full of spoiled food since the tenant left in June, that's a biohazard-adjacent job with an added charge, and in August it's a bad one. And if the appliance is in a finished basement with a 32-inch stairwell turn, there is a real chance the answer is "this comes apart or it stays."

What Actually Happens After Pickup

Roughly 90% of a major appliance by weight is recyclable ferrous metal. A responsible chain looks like this: refrigerant recovered by a Section 608-certified tech (EPA civil penalties for knowingly venting run past $50,000 per day, per violation, adjusted annually — this is not a rule anyone sane ignores), capacitors and mercury switches pulled from pre-2000 units, compressor oil drained, then the shell goes to a metals yard rather than the landfill face. Newer refrigerators use R-600a isobutane, typically a charge of only about 2 ounces, which is flammable — another reason cutting lines yourself in a closed garage is a bad idea.

Ask any hauler you call, including us, where the metal goes. A company that can't answer that in one sentence is taking it to the same place you would have.
