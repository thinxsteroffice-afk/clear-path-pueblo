GARAGE CLEANOUT COST NEAR ME (2026)

Most Pueblo West garage cleanouts run $225 to $850, with the typical two-car garage landing between $400 and $650. A quick clear-out of 8–12 items (bikes, boxes, a broken treadmill) is usually $225–$375. A packed two-car garage you can't walk into is $550–$850. Three-car garages and shops, the kind on the half-acre lots out toward McCulloch and Purcell, run $700–$1,400 because the volume roughly doubles. Add $35–$50 per refrigerator or freezer for refrigerant handling, $25–$40 per mattress, and $10–$18 per tire. Price is driven by volume in cubic yards, not by hours or item count. A cleanout done DIY with a rented trailer costs $85–$190 in hard costs plus 6–10 hours of your Saturday.

Below is how that number is actually built, where it goes wrong, and the specific situations where hiring anyone — including us — is the wrong call.

How Garage Cleanout Pricing Actually Works

Almost every junk removal company in Pueblo County prices by volume, measured as a fraction of a truck or trailer bed. A standard dump trailer holds 14–16 cubic yards. That's the unit everything gets converted into.

Here's the math nobody shows you. A two-car garage is about 400 square feet with roughly 60 linear feet of usable wall. If you have a 2-foot-deep pile stacked 5 feet high along three walls, that's 600 cubic feet — about 22 cubic yards. That is one and a half full trailer loads, not "a half load," which is why homeowners' guesses come in 40–60% low almost every time.

Typical Pueblo West brackets:

  • Minimum load / single-item pickup: $115–$165. Covers dispatch, one truck, and disposal for a small volume.
  • Quarter load (3–4 cubic yards): $225–$325. Roughly a pickup bed heaped.
  • Half load (7–8 cubic yards): $375–$500. The most common garage job.
  • Three-quarter load (11–12 cubic yards): $500–$650.
  • Full load (14–16 cubic yards): $625–$850, weight-dependent.

Weight matters at the extremes. Mixed household junk runs 150–250 lbs per cubic yard, so a full 15-yard load weighs 1.1 to 1.9 tons. Load that same trailer with tile, brick, wet drywall, or a stack of old shingles from a hail replacement and you're at 4,000–6,000 lbs in 6 cubic yards — a fraction of the space, more than the disposal cost. Any honest quote treats dense debris separately. If your garage pile is mostly broken concrete or landscape rock, that's a different service and a different price sheet — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo).

What Drives Your Price Up in Pueblo West Specifically

Local conditions genuinely move the number here, and they're not the same as Denver or Colorado Springs:

  • Round-trip haul time. From central Pueblo West to the disposal site off Highway 50 and back is roughly 35–50 minutes depending on scale-house lines. A job needing three trips burns two-plus hours in transit alone. That's why the second and third loads on the same job should be discounted 10–20%, and why you should ask whether they are.
  • Long driveways and detached shops. A lot of the half-acre-and-up lots out here have 80–150 feet between the garage door and where a trailer can safely sit. Every extra 100 feet of carry adds roughly 20–30 minutes per full load.
  • Wind. March through May regularly gives us 30–45 mph gusts. Loose, light debris — insulation, foam, cardboard, packing peanuts from a decade of storage — has to be bagged and netted before anyone moves. Budget an extra 15–25 minutes on a windy day. A crew that doesn't tarp is going to leave your neighbor's yard covered in your garage.
  • Sun-cooked plastics. At 4,700 feet with our UV load, 15-year-old totes, kiddie pools, and patio furniture shatter instead of stacking. Broken plastic loads loose and eats 20–30% more volume than the same items intact.
  • Restricted items. Paint, solvents, pesticides, pool chemicals, and propane cylinders are not general trash. Reputable haulers will set them aside and point you to Pueblo County's household hazardous waste program rather than bury them in the load. Confirm current drop-off days and fees with the county before you plan around it — those schedules change.

> The single biggest cost driver isn't what's in your garage. It's how many separate trips your garage turns into.

Line Items People Forget to Budget

These are add-ons across essentially every company in the region, not just ours:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, window AC units, dehumidifiers: $35–$50 each. Certified refrigerant recovery is required by federal rule; nobody legally skips it. Details on [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Mattresses and box springs: $25–$40 per piece.
  • Tires: $10–$18 each, more with rims still on.
  • CRT tube TVs and monitors: $35–$65. Flat panels are usually $15–$30.
  • Paint: $6–$12 per gallon if it needs solidifying and special handling. Latex you can dry out yourself with kitty litter for about $8 a bag and put in your regular trash.

A garage with a dead chest freezer, two mattresses, four tires, and an old console TV carries $150–$220 in surcharges before a single box gets touched. Ask for those to be itemized in writing on the quote, not mentioned when the trailer's already half full.

When a Garage Cleanout Is Not Worth Paying For

This is the part that costs us jobs, and it's the part worth reading twice.

Skip hiring anyone if you have a truck, a free Saturday, and under 6 cubic yards. A U-Haul 5x9 utility trailer runs about $25–$35 a day locally, and household disposal at area facilities is commonly in the $35–$70 per ton range plus per-item fees — call the scale house for current rates before you commit, because they adjust annually. Two loads, one day: realistically $85–$190 all-in. You are paying us $300–$450 to buy back 6–10 hours. If that trade doesn't make sense for you, don't make it.

Don't hire us for a 3-item job unless you know about the minimum. If you have one couch and a broken lawn mower, our $115–$165 minimum means you're paying roughly $40–$55 per item. Pueblo West has periodic community cleanup and dumpster events; if one is 5 weeks out and you're not in a hurry, wait. That's cheaper than we will ever be.

Don't hire a hauler if what you actually need is an organizer. We remove things. We do not sort your late father's tools into keep-and-donate piles, label bins, or install shelving. Crews are paid to move volume, and they will move fast. If you haven't decided what stays, you will either slow the job down — pushing a $450 job to $600 in extended labor — or lose something you wanted. Pre-sort first. Always. Blue tape on anything that stays.

Don't do this as an "estate cleanout" without pausing. Garages hide value: tools, ammo, coin jars, titles, savings bonds in a coffee can. A crew working at pace is not an appraiser. Anything with resale value — running snowblowers, name-brand tool chests, working generators — will typically bring $150–$600 on local marketplace listings, which can cover most of the cleanout. If that applies, look at [estate cleanout services](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) and plan a sorting week before scheduling any truck.

Don't hire out repeat volume. If you're a landlord or flipper generating this every 60–90 days, four cleanouts a year at $500 is $2,000 annually. A used 6x10 dump trailer runs $3,500–$6,500 and pays for itself in 18–30 months, and you control the schedule. Hire out the one-offs, own the recurring.

Where hourly beats volume: a garage with 30 loose bags of light foam and clothing will price high by volume and low by weight. A per-hour quote may be cheaper. Get both, and say plainly which you're comparing.

Our real limitations: we don't take asbestos-containing material, tannerite or ammunition, medical sharps, or unmarked drums of liquid. Refrigerators still holding food after a summer outage get an extra $25–$40 biohazard charge. And if a garage is fully packed floor-to-ceiling with pathways only, that isn't a garage cleanout — it's [a hoarding cleanout](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo), it takes 2–4 days, and it prices at $2,000–$6,000. Anybody quoting that sight-unseen over the phone at $700 is going to renegotiate on your driveway.

How to Get an Accurate Quote in One Try

  • Take four photos with the garage door open: from the driveway, and from each corner. Include a person or a 5-gallon bucket for scale.
  • Measure and say it out loud: "two-car, 20 by 22, stacked 5 feet along the back and left wall."
  • List the surcharge items by name before the truck is scheduled.
  • Ask three things: Is the quote all-in with disposal and taxes? What happens if it runs long? Is there a discount on the second load?
  • Get it in writing. A texted photo quote with a number and a date is enough.

Photo quotes are accurate within about 10–15% on a normal garage. The gap widens only when there's a loft, an attic above, or a shed attached that never got mentioned.
