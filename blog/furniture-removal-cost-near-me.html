FURNITURE REMOVAL COST NEAR ME

Furniture removal in Pueblo West runs $95 to $185 for a single piece, $225 to $375 for a small truckload (a bedroom set or a sofa plus a couple of chairs), and $525 to $650 for a full 15-cubic-yard load — roughly the contents of a two-car garage packed to the rails. Most one-item jobs land at $125. Those prices include labor, loading, hauling, disposal fees, and tax; there is no separate dump charge added at the end. A crew of two typically finishes a single-item pickup in 15 to 25 minutes and a full load in 60 to 90.

Below is how those numbers actually get built, what makes them go up, and — the part most quote pages skip — the several situations where you should not pay us anything at all.

What You're Actually Paying For

Furniture pricing is volume-based, not weight-based, because a sectional sofa weighs 180 pounds but eats a third of a truck. Here's the breakdown of a typical $145 sofa pickup out of a Pueblo West home:

  • Disposal: $28–$40. Bulk furniture goes to the Pueblo landfill by the ton. Upholstered furniture is light and bulky, so a sofa's actual tipping fee is small — but the minimum charge per trip is not, which is why one item alone never costs $30.
  • Drive time: $25–$35. From most Pueblo West addresses, the round trip to the landfill and back to the shop is 22 to 30 miles and 45 to 60 minutes of paid crew time. Homes west of McCulloch or out toward the Purcell corridor add another 10 to 15 minutes each way.
  • Labor: $45–$60. Two people, 20 minutes on site, plus loading, strapping, and unloading at the transfer station.
  • Overhead: the rest. Commercial auto insurance, general liability, truck payment, fuel at Pueblo West pump prices, and workers' comp on a crew that lifts 200-pound armoires for a living.

That structure is why the *second* item is always cheap and the first one never is. Adding a matching loveseat to that sofa pickup costs about $45, not another $145. If you have three or four pieces, get them all out in one trip — the marginal cost per item drops by 60 to 70 percent.

What Pushes a Pueblo West Quote Higher

  • Stairs and tight turns: +$20 to $35 per piece for a second story or a finished basement. Getting a queen box spring up the stairwell of a 1990s Pueblo West split-level frequently means it doesn't come out the way it went in.
  • Disassembly: +$25 to $50. Bed frames, IKEA-style wardrobes, and reclining sectionals with steel mechanisms need tools. A power-reclining sectional with three motors and a console is the single most underestimated furniture job we quote — plan on $275 to $350, not $185.
  • Mattresses: most landfills apply a per-unit surcharge on the order of $15 to $25 on top of the tonnage, so mattresses price separately from other furniture. Budget $85 to $140 for a mattress and box spring together. See [mattress removal in Pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for the specifics.
  • Long gravel drives and unpaved easements: normally free — Pueblo West's half-acre-plus lots are the easiest loading conditions in the county — but soft ground after a monsoon week means we carry instead of back up, which adds 10 to 20 minutes.
  • Whole-house volume: four bedrooms of furniture is not one load. It's typically two to three, at $475 to $525 per load after the first when it's back-to-back the same day. That's [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) territory and gets quoted on site.

> The cheapest furniture removal in Pueblo West is the trip you don't pay for. Roughly one caller in six should be hanging up and dialing a charity instead — and we'd rather tell you that on the phone than after we've already loaded your couch.

When You Should Not Hire Us

This is the section that costs us money, so read it closely.

Your furniture is in good shape and you have three weeks. A structurally sound sofa, dresser, or dining set with no rips, stains, pet damage, or odor is worth something. The DAV and similar donation programs run scheduled pickup routes through southern Colorado and will take usable upholstered furniture at no charge; the tradeoff is a two-to-four-week scheduling window and a hard "no" on anything torn, smoke-exposed, or water-damaged. Habitat's ReStore in Pueblo takes solid-wood casegoods and can often arrange pickup for larger donations. You get a receipt worth something at tax time instead of a $145 charge. If your furniture qualifies and you're not on a deadline, donating beats hiring us by roughly $200 in swing value on a single sofa.

You have one small item, a pickup truck, and a free Saturday. A dresser, a single chair, a coffee table — that's a $30 to $45 landfill minimum plus 40 minutes of your time. Paying $95 to $125 for that is paying for convenience, and if convenience isn't worth $70 to you this weekend, don't. Same math on a U-Haul pickup: about $20 for the day plus mileage puts a two-item run around $60 to $80 all-in if you have a second person to lift.

Your curbside hauler already covers it. Some Pueblo West residential trash contracts include one or two bulk-item pickups per year at no additional charge, and others sell a bulk pickup for $25 to $40. Call the number on your bill before you call us. If you're within your annual allowance and can wait for the next scheduled route day, that's free money you're leaving on the table.

The piece is worth more than the haul. Solid mid-century teak, real leather in good condition, cast-iron patio sets, and name-brand mattresses under three years old routinely clear $150 to $600 on Facebook Marketplace in the Pueblo market, usually within a week if you price at 30 percent of retail and write "must pick up, cash only." We will happily haul away a $400 credenza. We'd rather you sold it.

You're mid-renovation and not actually done. The most common wasted trip we run is a customer who books furniture removal, then finds two more items in the garage the day after we leave. If demo or a move-out is still in progress, wait until everything is staged in one place. A second trip costs nearly as much as the first.

The Failure Modes Nobody Advertises

  • Phone quotes are estimates, not contracts. Anyone quoting a firm price for an unseen sectional is guessing. Our on-site number can differ from the phone number when the piece turns out to be a sleeper sofa (add 90 pounds of steel) or won't clear a doorway without disassembly. We tell you before we touch anything, and you can decline with nothing owed.
  • We break down doorframes' worth of patience, not doorframes. Sometimes a piece genuinely will not come out intact. The options are cutting it apart in place — which produces dust and debris — or leaving it. Some customers say no to both, and that's a dry run.
  • Wind is a real constraint here. Setting furniture at the curb on a Pueblo West gust day is how a mattress ends up in someone's xeriscape two lots down. Keep items in the garage or against the house until pickup.
  • Same-day isn't always same-day. We hold two same-day slots per crew. On Mondays and the last three days of any month — move-out season — those fill by 9 a.m.
  • Nothing hazardous rides along. Furniture is fine; the half-can of deck stain that ended up inside the entertainment center is not. Paint, solvents, propane, and tires need a household hazardous waste event, not our truck.

Getting an Accurate Number in One Call

Have these four things ready and we can quote within about $20 of the final price over the phone: a photo of each piece, the room it's in, whether stairs are involved, and whether anything needs to come apart. Photos matter more than descriptions — "a couch" covers everything from a $95 loveseat to a $340 power sectional.

That's 15 to 20 minutes of saved labor on a typical job, and it's already reflected in the ranges above.
