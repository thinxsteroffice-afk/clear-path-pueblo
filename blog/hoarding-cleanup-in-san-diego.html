Managing a Pueblo West hoarding cleanout from out of state

Most of the hoarding calls we get from out of state follow the same pattern. A parent in Pueblo West has gone into assisted living, gone to the hospital, or passed away. The adult child lives in California, Texas, or Denver and has a small window to act. They can't spend three weeks sorting a 1,800-square-foot ranch house one box at a time.

Here's how we handle that without you needing to be here for every hour of it:

  • Video walkthrough first. We walk every room on a FaceTime or Zoom call before quoting, so you see what we see. This usually takes 30 to 45 minutes.
  • A written "find list." You give us a list of things to watch for, such as wills, deeds, titles, jewelry, cash, photos, firearms, and military medals. We set aside anything that matches and photograph it.
  • Daily photo updates. Each evening you get pictures of the rooms that were cleared and the items set aside.
  • Shipping or holding. Items you want kept can be boxed for you to ship, or held for up to 14 days while you arrange a trip.

Cash turns up more often than you'd think. In heavily packed homes we regularly find envelopes of bills, uncashed checks, and savings bonds tucked into books, coat pockets, and freezer bags. A crew that hauls everything off by the pallet will throw those away. Ask any company, in San Diego or here, how it handles found valuables before you sign anything.

What a hoarding cleanout costs in Pueblo West

Price depends mostly on volume and access, not square footage. One useful way to describe severity is the Clutter Image Rating, a 1-to-9 picture scale published by the International OCD Foundation. Find the photo that looks most like the worst room and you'll have a rough idea where you land.

| Severity (Clutter Image Rating) | What it looks like | Typical volume | Typical cost | Time on site | |---|---|---|---|---| | 3–4 | Paths through rooms, counters covered | 1–2 truckloads | $1,200–$2,000 | 1 day | | 5–6 | Rooms partly unusable, items to waist height | 2–4 truckloads | $2,000–$4,000 | 1–2 days | | 7–9 | Rooms impassable, items to ceiling, garage and yard full | 4–7+ truckloads | $4,000–$6,500+ | 2–4 days |

A full truckload for us is about 15 cubic yards, which is roughly six pickup beds. These things raise the price:

  • Extra appliances and heavy items. Old refrigerators need the refrigerant handled, so they cost more per item than general junk. See our [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) page for details.
  • Outbuildings and yard piles. On Pueblo West's half-acre and larger lots, the stuff often spills into sheds, carports, and the yard. That can add 20% to 40% to the job.
  • Careful sorting. If you want every drawer checked against a find list, labor goes up. It's usually worth it. A fast "haul everything" job is cheaper, but you can't undo it.

When hiring us is the wrong call

This section may cost us some jobs, and that's fine. Hiring the wrong kind of company for a hoarding situation can hurt people or end up costing you twice.

1. The person still lives there and hasn't agreed. Hoarding disorder affects an estimated 2% to 6% of adults. It's a recognized mental health condition, not laziness. When a family clears a house without the resident's consent, the clutter often comes back within months, and the relationship can be badly damaged. If your parent still lives in the home and is fighting the cleanout, call a therapist who works with hoarding disorder before you call a hauler. We'll still take the job if you push for it, but we'll tell you honestly that it may not last.

2. There's a biohazard. We are a junk removal company, not a biohazard remediation company. Some homes need a certified remediation crew with respirators, protective suits, and proper disposal. That includes homes with:

  • human or animal waste spread through the rooms,
  • a death that went unnoticed for days,
  • used needles,
  • or black mold covering more than about 10 square feet.

Remediation usually costs $3,000 to $15,000+ on its own. The usual order is remediation first, then a crew like ours hauls out the cleared contents. Any junk hauler that quotes a biohazard job at junk prices is cutting corners and putting its workers at risk.

3. It's animal hoarding. If there are live animals in poor condition, contact Pueblo Animal Services before you contact us. We can't legally or ethically remove or rehome animals.

4. The house may be condemned. If the floors are soft, the roof is coming down, or the county has posted the building, we can't send a crew inside. You'll need a structural assessment first, and possibly demolition.

5. It's mild enough to do yourself. If the worst room looks like a 3 or below on the clutter scale, and you have a free weekend and two strong relatives, renting a 20-yard roll-off dumpster will probably cost less than we would. That's typically a few hundred dollars for a week. We'd rather tell you that now than take a job you didn't need to pay for.

6. You want the contents sold. We donate items that can be used and recycle what we can. We do not run estate sales, appraise antiques, or sell items for a commission. If the home has real resale value in furniture, tools, or collectibles, bring in an estate sale company first and call us for what's left over. Our [estate cleanout page](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo) explains how that handoff works.

> The cheapest hoarding cleanout is the one you only pay for once. That means the right crew, in the right order, with the resident on board when that's possible.

What happens on cleanout day

On a typical two-day job in a Pueblo West home of around 1,600 square feet:

1. Hour one: safety pass. We check where we can walk, the electrical hazards, any sharps, and whether the floors are sound. We open the windows. We do this first because air in a closed-up home can be bad after a Colorado summer. 2. Hours 2–6: clear the paths and the find-list rooms. Bedrooms and offices come first, because that's where paperwork and valuables tend to be. 3. Day one, end of day: first photo update to you. Set-aside items are boxed and labeled by room. 4. Day two: heavy items, garage, and outbuildings. Furniture, mattresses, and appliances go out. Our [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) crew often handles the detached shop or garage at the same time. 5. Final sweep. We broom-sweep the cleared floors and send a final walkthrough video. Deep cleaning, carpet removal, and odor treatment are separate services. If you need them, we'll refer you to someone.

About 30% to 50% of what comes out of a typical hoarded home is recyclable or donatable, mostly metal, cardboard, usable furniture, and clothing. The rest goes to the landfill. We give you the drop-off receipts if you need them for an estate or a landlord.

Questions to ask any hoarding cleanup company

Whether you're hiring in San Diego, Pueblo West, or anywhere else, these questions quickly show whether a company is qualified:

  • "What do you do with cash, documents, and photos you find?" Listen for a specific process. "We're careful" isn't one.
  • "Are you insured, and can you send the certificate?" You want general liability and workers' comp, because people get hurt on hoarding jobs.
  • "Do you do biohazard work, or do you subcontract it?" Either answer is fine. A vague answer is not.
  • "Is your quote a firm price or an estimate by volume?" Volume-based estimates can grow by 25% or more once a crew opens the closets.
  • "Can you work with me remotely?" If you're out of state, this is the question that matters most.
