What a Garage Cleanout Actually Costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by volume in the truck, not by hours or by item count. A standard junk removal truck holds roughly 15 cubic yards — about the same as 6 pickup truck beds — and legally maxes out somewhere around 4,000–6,000 pounds depending on the rig.

Here's how that maps to real Pueblo West garages:

  • 1/8 truck (a corner of boxes, a broken treadmill, some shelving): $150–$220. Most companies have a minimum charge in the $125–$175 range, so tiny jobs feel expensive per item.
  • 1/4 truck (a single-bay lane cleared — roughly a 240 sq ft single garage): $220–$380.
  • 1/2 truck (a genuinely full 400–440 sq ft two-car garage, floor to rafters on one side): $400–$600.
  • Full truck (three-car, 620–720 sq ft, or a 20-year accumulation): $650–$900, and multi-truck jobs run $1,200–$2,200.

Surcharges that show up on nearly every garage job and rarely appear in online reviews:

  • Refrigerators, freezers, and window AC units: $40–$85 each on top of volume, because refrigerant has to be reclaimed by a certified tech before the unit is scrapped. Details on our [appliance removal page](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo).
  • Mattresses stored in the garage: $35–$75 each.
  • Tires: $8–$20 apiece. A set of four off-road tires on rims can add $60 you didn't plan for.
  • Paint, stain, pesticide, pool chemicals, propane tanks: usually $0, because most haulers won't take them at all. Pueblo County runs household hazardous waste collection separately, and a hauler who says "sure, throw it on" is either lying or illegally dumping it.
  • Concrete, brick, rock, and dirt: priced by weight, not volume — see [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo). Half a truck of pavers weighs more than a full truck of cardboard.

How to Read Junk Removal Reviews Without Getting Fooled

Star averages in this industry are close to useless. Nearly every hauler in Pueblo County sits between 4.6 and 5.0 because the job is visually satisfying and customers rate the *feeling of an empty garage*, not the *accuracy of the estimate*. A 4.8 and a 4.9 tell you nothing. What actually separates companies:

  • Review recency beats review count. A company with 41 reviews, 9 of them in the past 60 days is actively working. A company with 380 reviews where the newest is 14 months old has likely changed owners, lost its crew, or stopped caring. Sort by "Newest" — always.
  • Look for price language. Reviews that say "quoted $425, paid $425" are worth ten reviews that say "great guys, fast service." Search the review text for the words *quote*, *estimate*, *upcharge*, and *cash*.
  • Read the 1-star and 2-star reviews first, and read the owner's replies. A defensive reply that blames the customer is a preview of your dispute. A reply that says "you're right, we misjudged the load and refunded $80" is a company that will make it right with you.
  • Suspect clustered timing. Nine 5-star reviews posted within 48 hours, all 6–12 words, from profiles with one lifetime review, is a purchased batch. Genuine junk removal reviews trickle in at maybe 1 per 8–15 completed jobs.
  • Photos matter more here than in most trades. Before/after garage photos are hard to fake and nearly always come from real customers.

> The single most predictive review sentence in this industry isn't "they were fast." It's "the price they gave me over the phone is the price I paid."

The Five Questions Reviews Will Never Answer

Call and ask these before booking anyone, including us:

1. "Is that quote firm, or does it change when you see the load?" Most quotes are estimates until the crew arrives. That's honest — but you should know it before a truck is parked in your driveway and you feel obligated. 2. "Do you charge a trip fee if I decline the on-site price?" It should be $0. Some companies charge $45–$75. 3. "Where does it go?" Ask what percentage gets donated or recycled. Honest answer for a garage cleanout is 20–40% — metal scrap, working tools, usable furniture. Anyone claiming 90% diverted on a garage full of warped particleboard and dry-rotted patio cushions is inflating it. 4. "Are you insured, and can you email the certificate?" Garage jobs mean people carrying heavy loads past your vehicles, water heater, and drywall. General liability of $1,000,000 is standard. 5. "Who's actually showing up?" Some listings that appear in "near me" results are lead-generation brokers in another state that sell your phone number to 3–5 haulers for $25–$60. That's why you get six calls in ten minutes.

When a Garage Cleanout Service Isn't Worth Your Money

This is the part most junk removal pages skip. Hiring us is genuinely the wrong call in several situations:

  • You have one or two big items and a truck-owning friend. A single couch, a water heater, and four boxes is a $175–$250 job for us. Pueblo County's Southside disposal site will take a personal pickup load for roughly $25–$45, and gas is under $10. If you have a trailer and a free Saturday, you're saving $150+. Do that instead.
  • Your garage is mostly resellable. Working tools, a decent table saw, functional appliances, and mid-century furniture are worth real money. We haul it away; a Facebook Marketplace weekend might net you $300–$1,500 on that same pile. We'll gladly take what doesn't sell — but sell first.
  • You're on a hard budget under $150. Below the minimum charge, you're paying mostly for the truck showing up. Rent a 10-yard dumpster for $275–$400 for a week, fill it at your own pace, and you'll often come out ahead if the job is going to take you multiple weekends anyway.
  • You want us to sort, organize, and put things back. We are a removal crew, not professional organizers. We'll separate donate/scrap/landfill, but we don't build shelving systems or decide which of your late father's tools to keep. If you need that decision-making support, a professional organizer at $50–$90/hour is the right hire — sometimes alongside us.
  • The garage has active rodent nesting, mold, or biohazard. A hantavirus risk from deer mouse droppings is real in southern Colorado, and it changes the job. That's remediation with PPE and negative-pressure protocols, not junk removal. We'll tell you honestly and refer out. Related-but-different work is on our [hoarding cleanout page](/services/hoarding-cleanout-pueblo).
  • You're expecting an exact price sight-unseen. Anyone who gives you a firm number over the phone for an unseen garage is either padding it by 25–40% to protect themselves, or they'll revise it on arrival. Photos get you within about ±15%. Nothing gets you to zero.

The most common regret we hear isn't about price — it's from people who paid $500 to clear a garage, then refilled 60% of it within a year because nothing changed about how they stored things.
