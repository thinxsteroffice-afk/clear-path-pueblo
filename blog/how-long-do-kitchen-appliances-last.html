HOW LONG DO KITCHEN APPLIANCES LAST

Most kitchen appliances last 9 to 15 years. Refrigerators average about 13 years, gas ranges 15, electric ranges 13, dishwashers 9 to 10, over-the-range microwaves 9, garbage disposals 10 to 12, and chest freezers about 11. In Pueblo West, plan on the low end of every one of those ranges — and sometimes 2 to 3 years below it. Our water is hard enough to scale a dishwasher heating element, we sit near 4,900 feet, and a lot of local kitchens push a second fridge out into an uninsulated garage that swings from 15°F in January to 110°F in July. Those three factors do more damage than brand choice ever will.

Here's the full picture, and the honest version of when replacing — or hauling — isn't worth your money.

Typical Lifespans, and What Pueblo West Does to Them

National averages are the starting point, not the answer. Adjust them for local conditions:

  • Refrigerator — 13 years national, 10 to 12 here. Condenser coils clog fast in a dusty, dry climate. A fridge in an attached garage works harder than the same unit in a conditioned kitchen and often quits at year 7 or 8.
  • Dishwasher — 9 to 10 years national, 7 to 9 here. Hard water scale on the heating element and spray arms is the main killer. Scale is also why the detergent cup looks chalky after two years.
  • Range/oven — 13 to 15 years, and that holds up locally. Gas ranges outlast electric because there's less to burn out. Electric coil elements are a $40 part; a glass cooktop crack is a $300 to $500 repair.
  • Over-the-range microwave — 9 years, often 6 to 7. The magnetron has a finite duty cycle, and OTR units double as range hoods, so they inhale grease and heat.
  • Garbage disposal — 10 to 12 years. Hard water deposits plus the splash guard failing usually come first.
  • Water heater (not kitchen, but it's the one that floods) — 8 to 12 years on a tank, up to 20 on tankless. Hard water cuts that to 7 to 10 unless you flush it annually.

> The single most reliable predictor of appliance lifespan in Pueblo West isn't the brand on the door. It's whether anyone has ever vacuumed the condenser coils or flushed the water heater.

The Hard Water Problem Nobody Mentions in Reviews

Water above 7 grains per gallon (about 120 ppm) is classified as hard. Most of Pueblo County, including Pueblo West Metro District supply drawn from Pueblo Reservoir, tests hard enough to matter — pull the current annual water quality report from the Metro District and look for the hardness line in ppm or grains, because the number moves year to year with reservoir conditions.

What hardness actually costs you:

  • A dishwasher heating element coated in scale draws more current for the same heat and fails early. Replacement element: $70 to $160 in parts, $180 to $350 installed.
  • Scale on a water heater's tank bottom acts as insulation, which can cut efficiency by 8% to 15% and makes the burner cycle longer.
  • Ice makers are the first thing to go. A clogged ice maker fill valve or an iced-up line is a $150 to $400 repair on a fridge that might only be worth $700.

A whole-house softener runs $1,200 to $2,800 installed in this market, plus roughly $8 to $15 a month in salt. If you're running a dishwasher, an ice maker, and a tank water heater, the math usually works inside 6 to 8 years. If you're a two-person household who hand-washes most things, it doesn't.

Altitude Changes Your Gas Appliance Math

Pueblo West sits around 4,900 feet. Air density up here is roughly 15% lower than at sea level, and water boils at about 202°F instead of 212°F. Two practical consequences:

  • Gas ranges and ovens may need a high-altitude orifice kit. NFPA 54 permits appliances to run un-derated up to 2,000 feet; above that, the default is roughly 4% derate per 1,000 feet unless the manufacturer certifies otherwise. Many modern ranges are certified to 10,000 feet with no change — but plenty aren't. Check the rating plate and the manual before you blame the appliance for yellow-tipping flames or slow preheat.
  • Anything that boils takes longer. That's physics, not a defect, and it's the source of a lot of returned pressure cookers and "my new oven is weak" complaints.

If you bought a range online and it arrived configured for sea level, the conversion kit is usually $25 to $60 and 30 minutes of a technician's time. That's not a lifespan issue, but it's the most common "my new appliance is broken" call in this county that isn't actually a broken appliance.

Repair or Replace: Run the 50% Rule

The working rule: if the repair costs more than 50% of a comparable new unit, and the appliance is past 60% of its expected life, replace it.

Local numbers to plug in:

  • Diagnostic/service call: $75 to $150, often credited toward the repair.
  • Average appliance repair: $150 to $400.
  • Refrigerator sealed-system or compressor work: $500 to $1,100 — almost always a replace decision on a 10-year-old fridge.
  • Control board on a mid-range dishwasher or front-load washer: $300 to $600.
  • New mid-range refrigerator: $900 to $2,000. Dishwasher: $500 to $900 installed.

The hidden variable is electricity. A refrigerator from 1990 pulls roughly 1,200 to 1,500 kWh per year; a current ENERGY STAR model runs 350 to 450 kWh. At around $0.14 per kWh, that's $120 to $150 a year in the difference — meaning that beloved garage beer fridge from the Clinton administration is quietly costing you more than a payment on a new one. Keeping a 30-year-old second fridge running is a sentiment, not a savings plan.

When Hauling It Away Is NOT Worth Paying Us For

This is the part most junk removal pages skip, so here it is plainly. Several situations exist where you should not hire us, and we'd rather tell you now than argue about it in your driveway:

  • You're buying a replacement and taking delivery. Most retailers will haul the old unit for $0 to $50 as part of the delivery. That's cheaper than any independent hauler, us included. Ask at the register.
  • The appliance still works. Habitat for Humanity ReStore and similar outlets take working appliances, sometimes with free pickup, and you get a donation receipt. A working fridge or range is worth more as a donation than as scrap.
  • You're already scrapping metal and have a truck. Appliance steel runs roughly $0.04 to $0.10 per pound at scrap yards. A 200-pound range is only $8 to $20 — but if you're making the trip anyway, you keep that and skip the service fee. Haulers exist to save you labor and a vehicle, not to beat a scrap yard on price.
  • It's one light item and you have a pickup. Single-item pickups in this market typically land in the $75 to $175 range depending on access and refrigerant. If your back is fine, your truck runs, and the unit is at ground level, a DIY disposal run is the cheaper call. Confirm current transfer station hours and minimum fees before you load up — minimums mean a single appliance often costs the same as half a load.
  • You need the appliance disconnected from gas or hardwired power. We are a hauling company. We'll pull a plugged-in fridge, disconnect a standard dishwasher supply line when it's accessible and shut off, and take it. We don't cut gas lines or break down electrical connections — that's a licensed plumber or electrician, typically $100 to $250 for the disconnect. Budget for it separately.

Other honest limitations: a fridge that's been sitting unpowered with food inside for weeks is a biohazard job, not a standard pickup, and it costs more. Appliances in a basement, up a narrow stairwell, or behind a 28-inch doorway take longer and price accordingly. And refrigerant-containing units — fridges, freezers, wine coolers, window AC — require EPA Section 608 certified recovery before the shell can be scrapped, which is a real cost nobody can legally skip. Any hauler quoting you a rock-bottom price on a fridge is either absorbing that or not doing it.

Also worth saying: extended warranties on appliances are usually a bad bet. A 5-year plan at $150 to $300 on a dishwasher with a 9-year life expectancy covers the years least likely to fail. Put the money toward a softener or an annual water heater flush instead.

Where Paying a Hauler Actually Makes Sense

Three cases, specifically:

  • Volume. A kitchen remodel that produces a fridge, a range, a dishwasher, an OTR microwave, and cabinet debris in one weekend. That's a single trip for us and four trips plus a dump fee for you. [Construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) and [appliance removal](/services/appliance-removal-pueblo) typically get bundled.
  • Weight and stairs. A 300-pound side-by-side out of a finished basement is how people herniate discs. Two people with straps and a stair dolly is worth the fee.
  • You're clearing a whole space on a deadline. A closing date, an estate, or a landlord turnover. That's a [garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) or [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo), not an appliance pickup, and the per-item math stops applying.

Four Maintenance Habits That Buy You Real Years

  • Vacuum refrigerator condenser coils every 6 months. In this dust, that one habit is worth 2 to 3 years of compressor life.
  • Flush the water heater annually — 20 minutes, a garden hose, and it's the difference between 8 years and 12.
  • Run a cup of white vinegar through an empty dishwasher monthly, or a citric acid descaler quarterly, to fight scale on the element.
  • Clean the dryer vent run, not just the lint trap, once a year. Restricted vents cook the heating element and are the leading cause of dryer fires.
  • Don't put a standard fridge in an unconditioned garage. Below about 38°F ambient, many fridges stop cycling the compressor and the freezer thaws. Buy a unit rated for garage use, or expect failure.
