What Furniture Removal Costs in Pueblo West

Junk removal is priced by how much of the truck your stuff fills, not by the hour and not by the item. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, roughly the volume of a one-car garage packed to waist height. The figures below are typical ranges for southern Colorado. Your quote depends on the actual items and access, and we confirm it on-site before any work starts.

  • Single large item (sofa, recliner, dresser): typically $95 to $175. That range reflects a minimum charge, because the truck, fuel and two-person crew cost the same whether we take one chair or five.
  • Sectional sofa (3 to 5 pieces): typically $150 to $275. A sectional takes up about 3 to 4 cubic yards.
  • Full bedroom set (bed frame, mattress, box spring, dresser, two nightstands): typically $250 to $400.
  • Quarter truckload: typically $175 to $250.
  • Half truckload: typically $300 to $425.
  • Full truckload: typically $550 to $700. That's usually the contents of a 2- or 3-bedroom house that's being downsized.

These things push the price up: a third-floor walk-up, a sleeper sofa (the steel frame inside can add 70 to 100 pounds), and pieces that need to come apart to fit through a doorway. These things lower it: furniture already in the garage or driveway, and good-condition pieces that we can donate instead of landfilling.

For mattresses specifically, see our [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) page. Mattresses get handled differently because many donation centers won't accept them.

How a Furniture Pickup Actually Works

Most people only hire a junk hauler once or twice in their life, so here's the actual sequence:

1. You call or send photos. Photos taken from the doorway, not just close-ups of the item, let us give you a range in about 10 minutes. 2. We schedule a 2-hour arrival window. In Pueblo West we can usually come the same day or the next day. During the last week of any month, when leases turn over, it can stretch to 3 days. 3. The crew confirms the price before touching anything. If the on-site price doesn't work for you, you owe nothing. 4. We do all the lifting. That includes carrying things down stairs, taking bed frames apart and removing legs to get a sofa through a 30-inch door. 5. We sort the load. Usable pieces go to donation, metal goes to scrap, and the rest goes to the landfill. 6. We sweep up. Most single-item jobs take 15 to 30 minutes on-site. A full-house furniture clear-out usually takes 2 to 4 hours.

You don't have to be home, as long as the items are reachable (a garage code, an unlocked side gate or items set out on the driveway) and you've approved the price by phone.

When Furniture Removal Is NOT Worth It

This is the section most junk removal companies leave out. Here are the situations where you should not hire us, or anyone like us:

  • You're in Ault, Greeley or anywhere in northern Colorado. We covered this above. Call someone local to you.
  • You have one item and a pickup truck. If you can lift a recliner into your own truck bed, the dump fee for a single piece of furniture is usually under $30. You'll save $70 to $140 by hauling it yourself. Don't pay a minimum charge for a 20-minute trip you could make.
  • The furniture is worth selling. A solid-wood dresser or a leather sofa less than 5 years old often sells for $100 to $400 on Facebook Marketplace in Pueblo, and buyers usually bring their own trucks. If you have a week to spare, selling beats paying for removal.
  • A charity will pick it up for free. Some Pueblo-area thrift and ReStore operations do free pickups for furniture in good condition. Expect to wait 1 to 3 weeks for a pickup slot. Expect them to turn down anything with stains, tears, pet damage or a smoke smell. If your piece is clean and you aren't in a hurry, try them first.
  • You actually need movers. We remove furniture so it's gone for good. We don't carefully wrap your grandmother's hutch and deliver it to your new house. If you want the furniture to arrive somewhere intact, hire a moving company.
  • The furniture has bed bugs. We can sometimes haul infested furniture if it's wrapped and sealed in plastic, but no donation center will accept it, and every load we haul afterward is at risk. Treat the infestation first. Removing the couch doesn't solve a bed bug problem.

The point is that a junk hauler sells speed and labor. If neither of those matters much to you, a slower, cheaper option is usually the better deal.

What Happens to Your Furniture After We Take It

People ask about this more than anything else, and most hauler sites answer vaguely. Here's our rough breakdown for a typical Pueblo West furniture load:

  • About 30 to 40% is donatable. That's mostly wood tables, bookshelves, dressers and clean upholstered pieces.
  • About 10 to 15% is metal: bed frames, recliner mechanisms, sleeper sofa frames and office chair bases. It goes to scrap recycling.
  • The remaining 45 to 60% goes to the landfill. That's particleboard furniture that's already falling apart, stained upholstery and damaged mattresses.

Here's the uncomfortable part: flat-pack particleboard furniture almost never gets donated. Once it's been assembled, taken apart and moved even once, the cam locks strip out and it won't hold together. If most of your load is cheap bookcases and TV stands, assume they're going to the landfill, whoever hauls them.
