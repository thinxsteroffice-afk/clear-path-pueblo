MATTRESS DISPOSAL IN PUEBLO WEST

Mattresses can't go in your Pueblo West trash cart, and they can't go in a standard curbside pickup — they have to be hauled to a landfill, dropped at a recycler, or picked up by a hauler. The three real options near Pueblo West: (1) haul it yourself to the Pueblo County Landfill on Wills Blvd, where a pickup-truck load runs roughly $30–$45 and mattresses may carry a per-item surcharge; (2) schedule a Waste Connections / Pueblo West Metro bulk pickup if your account includes one, usually 1–2 free bulk items per year with 1–2 weeks of lead time; or (3) call a junk removal crew — ClearPath charges about $85–$125 for a single mattress-and-box-spring set in Pueblo West, same-day or next-day.

That's the whole answer. The rest of this page is the detail that decides which of those three is actually right for you — including the cases where hiring us is the wrong call.

What It Actually Costs to Haul It Yourself

The DIY route is the cheapest on paper and the one most Pueblo West residents should at least price out. Here's the real math for a queen set:

  • Landfill gate fee: budget $30–$45 for a standard half-ton pickup load at the Pueblo County Landfill. Rates are charged by volume or weight depending on the load; call ahead at the scale house before you drive out, because posted rates change and mattresses are sometimes assessed separately.
  • Fuel and drive time: Pueblo West to the landfill is roughly 20–30 minutes each way depending on whether you're coming off Purcell or McCulloch. Call it 45–70 minutes round trip plus loading.
  • Truck rental if you don't own one: a Home Depot pickup rental in Pueblo runs about $29 for the first 75 minutes, then roughly $10 per additional 15 minutes — realistic total $40–$60 once you factor the drive.
  • Straps and tarp: $15–$25 if you don't already own them. Colorado requires loads to be secured; an unsecured-load citation is not worth the savings.
  • A second set of hands: a queen innerspring runs 60–90 lbs and a king pillow-top can hit 130 lbs. Two people, minimum.

All-in for someone who already owns a truck: $35–$50 and about 90 minutes. All-in for someone renting: $85–$110 and about two hours. Compare that honestly to a hauler quote before you assume DIY wins.

The Free Option Most People Forget

Pueblo West residents on a Waste Connections residential account frequently have bulk item pickup included — typically 1–2 items per year at no extra charge, sometimes $25–$40 per extra item beyond the allowance. Mattresses usually qualify. The catch is scheduling: you generally need to call 7–14 days ahead, and pickup happens on your regular service day, not a day you pick.

Call your hauler and ask three questions:

  • Does my plan include bulk pickup, and how many items are left this year?
  • Are mattresses eligible, or excluded as a special-handling item?
  • Does it need to be bagged in plastic? (Some haulers require it; some refuse visibly soiled or bedbug-suspected mattresses outright.)

If you get a yes and you can wait two weeks, stop reading and do that. It's free. We'd rather you keep the money and call us for the garage instead.

When Hiring a Hauler Is the Wrong Call

This is the section that costs us business, and it's the most useful part of this page.

Don't pay for junk removal if:

  • You only have one mattress and you own a pickup. You're paying roughly $50–$80 in labor premium over the landfill fee for about an hour of saved time. If your hour isn't worth $60, do it yourself.
  • Your bulk pickup allowance is unused and you're not on a deadline. Free beats $95 every time. The only reason to override this is a hard move-out date.
  • You're already renting a truck for a move. The mattress rides along at zero marginal cost. Add the landfill stop to the route.
  • You're chasing a tax deduction on a donation. Doesn't apply — see below, but the short version is that almost nobody takes used mattresses, so there's usually no donation to deduct.
  • You have a bedbug situation. Be upfront. Many haulers, ourselves included, need to know before arrival so the item can be sealed and handled separately, and some will decline. Hiding it gets your appointment canceled at the curb after you've already waited.
  • You want it "recycled" and that's your primary motivation. Read the next section before you pay a premium for it.

Where hiring genuinely earns its keep: second-floor bedrooms with a turn in the stairwell, king sets, three or more mattresses at once, estate and rental turnovers on a clock, and anyone who physically shouldn't be carrying 90 lbs down a staircase. That's where the $85–$125 stops being a convenience fee and starts being the cheaper option — a wrenched back costs more than the whole job.

The Recycling Reality in Southern Colorado

Roughly 75–80% of a mattress by weight is recyclable — steel springs, foam, cotton batting, and the wood frame in a box spring. That number gets quoted constantly, and it's true in a materials-science sense.

What's also true: Colorado has no statewide mattress recycling program. States like California, Connecticut, Rhode Island, and Oregon run funded stewardship programs with a $16–$25 recycling fee collected at purchase. Colorado does not. The nearest dedicated mattress recycling operations to Pueblo West are generally in the Denver and Colorado Springs metro areas — 45 to 110 miles away — and they typically charge $20–$40 per unit on top of your drive.

Which means: if a local hauler promises your mattress will be recycled, ask them which facility and how often they run there. A truck making a 220-mile round trip to Denver to divert one queen mattress is burning more resources than it saves, and most of the time it isn't happening. We'd rather tell you plainly that a Pueblo West mattress usually goes to the landfill than sell you a green story we can't back with a receipt. If diversion matters to you and you're already headed north, batching 4–6 units and driving them yourself is the only version of this that pencils out.

What Actually Costs More Than You Expect

Pricing on mattress disposal is driven less by the mattress and more by everything around it:

  • Stairs. Expect a $15–$25 per flight adjustment from most haulers. A queen box spring physically does not turn in some tight Pueblo West stairwells, which is why box springs get cut apart on site.
  • Item count. The first mattress carries the trip cost. A second one on the same visit typically adds only $25–$40, not another $95. If you've got a guest room set going out eventually, send it now.
  • Adjustable bases and platform frames. These are appliances, not bedding — motors, control boxes, and power supplies. Budget an extra $40–$75, and mention it when you book. See [furniture removal pueblo](/services/furniture-removal-pueblo) for how bed frames, dressers, and headboards price out on the same trip.
  • Same-day service. Some haulers charge a $25–$50 rush premium. Ask before you agree to a time window.
  • Soiled or infested items. Additional handling, and some crews will refuse. Disclose it.

Timing: Same Week vs. Same Year

Realistic timelines around Pueblo West:

  • Junk removal crew: typically same-day to 48 hours, with a 2-hour arrival window.
  • Hauler bulk pickup: 7–14 days out, on your assigned service day.
  • DIY landfill run: as fast as you can load it, but check gate hours — landfills commonly close around 4:00–4:30 PM on weekdays with limited or no Sunday hours. Showing up at 4:45 on a Saturday with a truck full of bedding is a wasted afternoon.
  • Retailer haul-away with a new mattress: usually $0–$50, done at delivery. If you're buying a replacement, this is frequently the best deal available — but confirm it's included, because "free haul-away" is often a promotional item that disappears from the invoice.

That last one deserves emphasis. If you're replacing the mattress anyway, ask the retailer to take the old one. Mattress Firm, Furniture Row, and most local dealers offer it. You'll pay less than any other option here, and it happens on the same delivery appointment.

The Illegal Dumping Math

Mattresses are among the most-dumped items on rural Pueblo County roads, and the arithmetic is bad. Illegal dumping in Colorado is prosecutable, with fines that commonly start around $500–$1,000 for a first offense and escalate from there, plus cleanup costs. Set against a $35 landfill fee, dumping is a 15x to 30x worse financial decision even before anyone considers the ditch.

Roadside and vacant-lot dumping also tends to happen on land that eventually becomes someone's cleanup problem — often a neighbor's, sometimes a buyer's at closing. What Makes a Mattress Job Go Wrong

Three failure modes worth naming, because they're the ones that generate the angry phone calls:

  • Weather. A mattress left at the curb through a Pueblo West rain or snow gains substantial water weight and may be refused by a landfill or hauler as a saturated load. Keep it inside or under cover until pickup day.
  • The wrong quote. Phone quotes given without a photo are guesses. A "queen mattress" that turns out to be a king set with an adjustable base and a metal frame is a different job at a different price. Send a photo when you book — it takes 30 seconds and prevents the on-site price change everyone hates.
  • Access. Narrow hallway, locked gate, a car in the driveway, a storage unit with a 6-foot rollup door. Crews plan the trip around the truck reaching the item. Clear the path before the window opens, or you're paying for standing time.
