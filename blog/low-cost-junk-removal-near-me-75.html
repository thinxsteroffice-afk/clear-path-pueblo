LOW-COST JUNK REMOVAL NEAR ME: $75+

Junk removal in Pueblo West typically runs $75 for a single item up to $425–$550 for a packed 16-foot trailer, with most driveway-and-garage jobs landing between $150 and $325. ClearPath prices by the volume your stuff actually takes up in the trailer, not by the hour, and we quote the number before anything gets loaded. If you're comparing options: a DIY run with a rented trailer costs about $90–$140 all-in once you count rental, fuel, and landfill fees, and a 10-yard roll-off dumpster runs $350–$450 for a week. Below is the honest breakdown of when hiring out is the cheap answer and when it plainly isn't — including the jobs we'd rather you didn't hire us for.

What "low cost" actually means out here

Pueblo West is unusual for a junk hauler. Lots run large — an acre is common off Purcell, Joe Martinez, and the numbered lanes — so the stuff people need gone is bigger and heavier than what a hauler picks up in a Denver townhome. A typical Pueblo West load is a shed's worth of scrap lumber, an old swamp cooler, half a fence line, and three cubic yards of tumbleweed and juniper trimmings. That's a fundamentally different job than four boxes and a desk chair.

That size works in your favor on price per pound and against you on minimums. Our per-cubic-yard cost drops sharply once a load is over about 6 cubic yards, because the drive out, the labor to hook up, and the dump run are fixed costs that get spread across more material. A single nightstand and a full trailer both require the same 30–40 minute round trip to the landfill.

Here's the pricing structure in plain numbers:

  • Single item, curbside or garage: $75–$95 (mattress, recliner, one appliance)
  • Quarter trailer, roughly 4 cubic yards: $125–$165
  • Half trailer, roughly 8 cubic yards: $215–$265
  • Three-quarter trailer: $310–$360
  • Full 16-foot trailer, roughly 16 cubic yards, up to 3,000 lbs: $425–$550
  • Heavy material — concrete, dirt, roofing, brick: priced by weight, not volume, usually $95–$185 per ton on top of labor

Weight caps matter more than most people expect. A trailer that looks half empty can be over 3,000 pounds if it's shingles or broken concrete. That's why [concrete and rock removal](/services/concrete-rock-removal-pueblo) is quoted differently from a garage full of boxes.

The DIY math, done honestly

Plenty of Pueblo West households already own a truck or a utility trailer. If you do, hiring a hauler for a small load is usually the worse financial decision, and we'll say so on the phone.

A realistic self-haul for one trailer load:

  • Utility trailer rental: $35–$50 per day, or $0 if you own one
  • Pickup rental if you have no truck: about $19.95 per day plus $0.99 per mile — call it $55 for a 35-mile round trip
  • Fuel: roughly $12–$18 at $3.20–$3.40 a gallon for a loaded round trip from Pueblo West to the landfill
  • Landfill gate fee: commonly a $25–$35 minimum for small loads, scaling to roughly $55–$70 per ton for general household waste — rates change, so call the scale house before you load
  • Your time: 2.5 to 4 hours including loading, driving, unloading, and returning the rental

Total: $90–$140 in cash and most of a morning. Against a $215 half-trailer quote from us, you're effectively paying about $80–$120 to buy back three hours and skip carrying a 240-pound sleeper sofa down a step. Some weekends that's an obvious yes. Some weekends it obviously isn't.

> If you own a truck, you're physically able, and the load is small, self-hauling is cheaper. We'd rather tell you that up front than take $150 you didn't need to spend.

When you should not hire us

This is the part most junk removal pages leave out.

Don't hire anyone for a single small item you can move yourself. A $75 minimum on one nightstand is a bad deal in dollars per pound. Post it free on the Pueblo West community Facebook groups or set it at the end of the driveway with a "free" sign — usable furniture off McCulloch typically disappears within a day or two.

Don't hire us if the item still has real resale value. Working appliances under about 8 years old, decent power tools, and solid-wood furniture regularly sell locally for $60–$400. Paying $95 to remove something you could sell for $200 is a $295 swing against you. We'll tell you when we see something worth listing.

Don't hire us if you only need a few boxes gone and you have curbside service. Your regular trash service already covers normal household volume. A hauler for that is money lit on fire.

Don't hire us for an ongoing renovation. If you're going to generate debris for three weeks straight, a roll-off dumpster at $350–$450 for a 7-day rental beats four separate $225 trailer loads. We'll say this out loud when you describe a kitchen gut or a re-roof. One-time [construction debris removal](/services/construction-debris-removal-pueblo) makes sense at the end of a job, not throughout it.

Don't hire us if you need it done in the next two hours and nothing else will do. We schedule most jobs 24 to 72 hours out. Same-day happens maybe one day in four, depending on the route. If you're closing on a house tomorrow morning at 9, tell us that before you book so we can tell you honestly whether we can hit it.

Don't hire us for hazardous material. We're not licensed for it, and neither are most haulers advertising low prices. If someone quotes you a cheap number to take paint thinners, pesticides, or suspected asbestos, that's a red flag about where it's going.

What we can't take, and what costs extra

Surprises at the truck are the number one reason a "low cost" quote turns into a bad experience. The list:

  • Won't take at any price: wet paint and solvents, motor oil, pesticides, propane tanks, ammunition, medical sharps, asbestos-containing material, and anything from a meth-contaminated property
  • Extra fee, disclosed before loading: refrigerators and freezers requiring refrigerant recovery (+$30–$45), televisions and monitors (+$25–$40 e-waste fee), tires (+$8–$12 each), and mattresses in jurisdictions charging a disposal surcharge
  • Priced separately: anything requiring disassembly beyond about 20 minutes — [hot tubs](/services/hot-tub-removal-pueblo) generally run $350–$650 because they need cutting apart, and above-ground pools and large playsets are similar
  • Distance: we serve Pueblo West and Pueblo proper without a travel fee. Past roughly 15 miles from the center of Pueblo West — Colorado City, Rye, Boone, Avondale — expect a $35–$75 trip charge, and we'll say the number before you commit

Dried-out latex paint is fine, incidentally. Add cat litter or paint hardener, leave the lid off until it's solid, and it goes in normal waste. That's a free fix that saves a special trip.

How to get the lowest real number from any hauler

The cheapest advertised price and the cheapest final price are rarely the same company. Four things that actually move the number:

  • Send photos before booking. A quote from photos is within about 10–15% of final. A quote from a verbal description is routinely off by 40% or more, always upward.
  • Consolidate into one load. Two separate half-trailer visits cost roughly $430. One full trailer covering the same material costs $425–$550 — and if you can get it under a full load, you save the difference outright.
  • Stage everything in the driveway if you're able. Curbside or driveway-accessible loads take us 30–45 minutes instead of 90, and we discount accordingly — typically $25–$50 off a mid-size load.
  • Ask about mid-week and off-peak slots. Tuesday and Wednesday mornings are our lightest days; we'll usually knock 10–15% off to fill them.
  • Ask directly what is NOT included. Any hauler who can't answer that question in one breath is going to surprise you at the trailer.

Seniors and active-duty or veteran households get 10% off every job, no expiration, no minimum.

Where hiring out genuinely wins

Three situations where paying us is clearly the better call, not just the easier one:

Weight and stairs. A cast-iron tub, a gun safe, an upright piano, or a full-size hot tub can cause a real injury. An ER visit and a back that hurts for six months costs vastly more than $300.

Volume you can't fit in one trip. A [full garage cleanout](/services/garage-cleanout-pueblo) after 15 years of accumulation is often 20–35 cubic yards — that's five to eight self-haul trips, roughly 12–18 hours of your weekend and $180–$300 in gate fees and fuel anyway. Hiring it out for $700–$1,100 in one day is competitive on cash and dramatically better on time.

Estate and probate timelines. When a house has to be emptied and listed inside 30 days, and the family lives in Denver or out of state, the DIY option doesn't really exist. Same logic for foreclosure and eviction turnovers where every extra day is carrying cost.
