HOARDING CLEANUP PROS: READ REVIEWS

When you search for hoarding cleanup reviews in Pueblo West, the most useful reviews describe a job like yours. Look for the home's size, how many days the job took, the final bill compared with the quote, and how the crew treated the person whose home it was. A 4.9-star average built on 15 reviews of mattress pickups tells you almost nothing about how a company handles a 1,400-square-foot house packed to the ceiling. Before you hire anyone, ClearPath Junk Removal included, read the 1- to 3-star reviews first. Ask for one reference from a hoarding job done in the last 12 months. Get the quote in writing, with a cubic-yard or truckload count. This page explains how to do each of those, what this work usually costs in Pueblo West, and when a junk removal company is the wrong choice.

What a useful hoarding cleanup review actually says

Most junk removal reviews are short: "On time, friendly, fast." That's fine for a couch pickup. It doesn't help you judge a hoarding job, where the risks are bigger and harder to see. When you scan reviews on Google, Yelp or Nextdoor, look for these details:

  • Scope: Does the review mention square footage, number of rooms or number of truckloads? "Cleared a 3-bedroom in Pueblo West in 2 days" is evidence. "Great job!" isn't.
  • Quote compared with the final bill: Look for words like "came in under the estimate" or "charged exactly what they quoted." Complaints about a bill that jumped 30% or more partway through are the most common serious problem in this industry.
  • How the family was treated: Hoarding cleanouts often happen after a death, an eviction notice or a code enforcement letter. Reviews that mention patience, privacy or unmarked trucks show the crew understood the situation.
  • What happened to belongings: Did the crew set aside photos, documents, cash, firearms or medications? Experienced hoarding crews find cash or valuables in a large share of heavy cleanouts, so a sorting process matters.
  • Recency: Owners and crews change. Reviews older than 24 months tell you about a company that may no longer exist in the same form.

> A review that names a number, such as days, truckloads or dollars, is worth ten reviews that only name a feeling.

How to spot reviews you shouldn't trust

Fake and padded reviews are common in home services. Three patterns should make you suspicious:

1. Bursts. Twenty 5-star reviews posted within 2 or 3 weeks, then nothing for months, usually means a review push or purchased reviews. 2. No owner responses to complaints. Every company gets a bad review eventually. If you can't find one, or the owner never replies to the ones that exist, you can't see how the company handles problems. 3. Generic wording across reviewers. If five reviewers write "professional and courteous team" in nearly the same words, discount all five.

The best check isn't online. Ask the company for a phone reference from a hoarding or heavy estate job finished in the past year. A company that has done this work will have one. One that hasn't will change the subject.

What hoarding cleanup costs in Pueblo West

Pricing in this market is usually based on volume: how many cubic yards of material leave the property. A standard junk removal truck holds about 12 to 16 cubic yards. Here are typical ranges for the Pueblo area in 2026. Your quote depends on the actual site.

  • Single room or garage with heavy accumulation: about $400–$900, usually 1 truckload, 3 to 5 hours
  • Partial home (2–3 rooms plus pathways): about $1,200–$3,000, 2 to 4 truckloads, 1 to 2 days
  • Whole home, moderate level (floors mostly covered, rooms still usable): about $3,000–$6,500, 4 to 8 truckloads, 2 to 4 days
  • Whole home, severe level (blocked exits, rooms unusable, possible structural or pest issues): $7,000 to $15,000 or more, often 3 to 7 days

The Institute for Challenging Disorganization uses a 5-level Clutter-Hoarding Scale. Levels 1 and 2 are mostly clutter. Levels 4 and 5 involve sanitation problems, pests or structural damage. The jump from level 3 to level 4 is where costs and timelines often double, because crews move more slowly, wear more protective gear, and may need to separate hazardous waste from ordinary trash.

Pueblo West has two cost factors many people don't expect:

  • Outbuildings and acreage. Many Pueblo West lots are half an acre to several acres. Sheds, carports and old vehicles add volume that indoor-only estimates miss. If your property has outbuildings, make sure the walkthrough includes them. Otherwise, expect a change order.
  • Hauling distance. Disposal sites serving Pueblo West are a round trip of roughly 20 to 30 miles, depending on the facility. On a 6-truckload job, that driving time is built into the price.

Ask any company you're considering to price the job per truckload or per cubic yard, in writing, and to explain what triggers an extra charge. Common add-ons are tires (often $10–$25 each), appliances containing refrigerant, paint and other chemicals, and mattresses.

When hoarding cleanup ISN'T worth it, and who shouldn't hire us

Any honest company should tell you this part, even though it costs us jobs. A junk removal crew is the wrong choice in several situations.

1. When the person living there hasn't agreed to it. Hoarding disorder affects an estimated 2% to 6% of adults, according to the figures cited in the DSM-5. It is a recognized mental health condition, not a housekeeping problem. Forced cleanouts done without the resident's agreement and without therapy often fail. The home fills up again, sometimes within months. The resident can also be badly harmed: losing everything at once can trigger serious distress and wreck family relationships. If the person is living in the home and objecting, don't start with us. Start with a therapist who treats hoarding disorder, or with Pueblo County adult protective services if safety is a concern. Hire a hauler only once there's a plan the resident has agreed to.

2. When there's biohazard material. That includes large amounts of animal or human waste, blood, needles, an unattended death, or heavy mold. In those cases you need a licensed biohazard remediation company, not a junk removal crew. Most junk haulers, including us, are not certified to decontaminate those conditions. A company that says it can handle "everything" with a standard crew and a box of trash bags is putting its workers and your property at risk. Hire the remediation firm first. A hauler can remove the remaining furniture and household goods afterward.

3. When there's structural damage. Severe hoarding can hide rotted subfloors, roof leaks and water-damaged drywall. Once the house is cleared, you may find a $20,000 repair job underneath. If you're considering selling the house as-is to an investor, some buyers will take it full. In that case, paying $8,000 for a cleanout may only recover part of that cost in the sale price. Talk to a real estate agent before you spend the money.

4. When the volume is mostly one kind of material. If the house is 80% bagged trash or cardboard, renting a 30-yard roll-off dumpster and having family load it over a couple of weekends can cost less than half of a full-service crew. You're trading money for labor and exposure to dust and pests. For healthy, available family members, that trade can be a good one.

5. When you need the job done for less than it costs. A rushed 1-day crew on a severe level 4 home will throw away things that should have been kept. If your budget only covers speed, expect the sorting quality to suffer, whichever company you hire.

Here's the plain version: if your situation is one of the above, the best reviews in Pueblo West won't make a junk removal company the right choice.

Questions to ask before you hire anyone

Bring these to every estimate, including ours:

  • Is the estimate based on an in-person walkthrough, or on photos? Photos underestimate hoarding volume.
  • What's the per-truckload price, and how many loads do you expect?
  • What do you do with photos, papers, cash, jewelry, firearms and prescription medications you find?
  • How much of the material is donated or recycled, and how much goes to the landfill?
  • Are your crew members insured? Ask for the certificate. Don't just accept a yes.
  • Will the trucks be unmarked, or can you park out of view if neighbors are a concern?
  • Who's my one point of contact if something goes wrong on day 2?

Pay attention to how quickly and specifically each company answers. You'll learn more from that than from a star rating.
