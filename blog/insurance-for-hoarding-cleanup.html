INSURANCE FOR HOARDING CLEANUP

Homeowners insurance almost never pays for a hoarding cleanup on its own. Standard HO-3 policies exclude damage that builds up slowly through neglect, wear, pests or poor upkeep, and an insurer will usually put years of buildup in that category. Insurance can help in one narrow situation. If something sudden and accidental happens inside a hoarded home, such as a burst pipe, a kitchen fire or a roof leak after a hailstorm, the policy may pay to remove the damaged contents and repair the house. It won't pay to clear out the clutter that was there before. For most Pueblo West families, the practical answer is that you will pay out of pocket, and the useful question is how to keep that cost low and whether any part of the job is reimbursable.

This page covers what insurers actually pay for, the order of steps that keeps a valid claim alive, what a cleanout costs in our area, and when hiring a junk removal crew like ours is the wrong choice.

What Homeowners Insurance Covers and What It Excludes

Insurers sort hoarding situations into two groups: the slow buildup, and a sudden event that happens inside it.

Usually not covered: - Removing clutter that piled up over months or years - Rodent, insect or bird damage, which nearly every HO-3 policy excludes by name - Mold from long-term moisture. Many policies exclude it outright or cap it at $5,000 to $10,000 even when the cause is covered. - Odor, staining and floor damage from pet waste - Structural strain from weight, such as sagging floors or a garage slab cracked by stacked material

Possibly covered, when it comes from a covered peril: - Removing contents damaged by a fire, a burst supply line or wind-driven rain - Water mitigation and drying after a sudden pipe failure - Debris removal tied to a covered loss. Many policies add 5% of the dwelling limit for this on top of the main limits. - Additional living expenses if the home can't be lived in after a covered event

The debris-removal add-on matters in Pueblo West. On a home insured for $400,000, 5% is up to $20,000 for hauling debris after a covered loss. That money is only for debris caused by the covered event. It doesn't cover the 400 boxes of newspapers that were already in the spare bedroom.

> The insurer pays to clean up what the sudden event damaged. It doesn't pay to clean up the condition the home was already in.

Adjusters also check whether the hoarding contributed to the loss. If a space heater ignites a stack of paper, the fire is normally still covered. If blocked access delayed the fire department or kept a plumber from reaching a leak, the insurer may question part of the claim. Colorado law requires insurers to pay covered benefits within 60 days of receiving a valid, complete claim (C.R.S. 10-3-1115 and the related regulations). "Complete" is often the point of dispute.

The Mistake That Gets Claims Denied: Hauling Before the Adjuster Visits

The ranking pages for this search don't cover this. If a hoarded home has a possible covered loss, don't start the cleanout first. Hauling things away removes the evidence your claim depends on.

Use this order:

1. Report the loss right away. Most policies require "prompt" notice. Many carriers expect a proof-of-loss form within 60 days of their request. Some require a lawsuit within 1 to 2 years of the loss, so check your own policy. 2. Do only emergency mitigation. Shut off the water, tarp the roof, and remove soaked material that is actively causing mold. You're required to prevent further damage, and the insurer normally reimburses reasonable mitigation costs. Keep every receipt. 3. Photograph and video every room before anything leaves the house. Shoot wide angles first, then close-ups of damaged items. A phone walkthrough of 20 to 30 minutes is fine. 4. Wait for the adjuster's inspection or written permission to dispose. That typically takes 3 to 14 days, and longer after a regional hail event when adjusters are booked out. 5. Get separate invoices. Ask the hauler to bill storm- or water-damaged material separately from routine clutter. A single combined bill makes it hard to show which part the insurer owes.

A crew can remove 10 to 15 truckloads in a weekend. If that happens before the adjuster has seen the damage, you may be left with only your word for what was ruined. When we're called to a home with a pending claim, we ask whether the adjuster has signed off before we load anything.

What a Hoarding Cleanout Costs in Pueblo West

Because insurance usually won't pay, you need a realistic budget. These are general market ranges for southern Colorado in 2026. Your quote depends on volume, access and what's in the house.

  • Single room or garage, moderate buildup (Clutter Image Rating 3 to 4): about $400 to $1,200
  • Whole house, moderate (CIR 4 to 6), 3 to 6 truckloads: about $1,500 to $4,500
  • Whole house, severe (CIR 7 to 9), 8 or more truckloads, with sorting: about $5,000 to $12,000 or more
  • Biohazard remediation by a licensed specialist, for animal waste, needles or an unattended death: often an extra $2,000 to $10,000

The Clutter Image Rating is a 9-point photo scale used by clinicians and many county agencies. Anything above level 4 usually means a multi-day job.

Several things raise the price in Pueblo West in particular. Many lots run 1 to 5 acres, and outbuildings, sheds, horse trailers and dead vehicles out back often double the volume once someone walks the whole property. Plenty of homes here are on septic, and heavy loaded trucks shouldn't drive over a drain field, so crews may have to carry everything a longer distance to the street. Expect to pay for the extra labor hours.

If the house also has a shed past saving or a yard full of scrap, it can be cheaper to combine the work with [shed demolition removal](/services/shed-demolition-removal-pueblo) or [yard debris removal](/services/yard-debris-removal-pueblo) in one visit than to book separate trips. Other Ways to Pay When Insurance Won't

Insurance isn't the only source of money. These options are worth checking:

  • Renters and landlords. If the person with the hoarding problem rents, the landlord's policy covers the building, not the tenant's belongings. The landlord may pay for the cleanout and then charge the tenant or go after the security deposit. That's common at the end of an eviction, which our [foreclosure and eviction cleanout](/services/foreclosure-eviction-cleanout-pueblo) crews handle.
  • The estate. If the homeowner has died, cleanout costs are usually a legitimate estate expense paid before heirs receive anything. Colorado's small-estate affidavit covers estates up to roughly $80,000 (the cap is adjusted periodically), which can speed up access to funds. See [estate cleanout](/services/estate-cleanout-pueblo).
  • Selling the house as-is. Cash buyers will take a hoarded home, but they usually discount the offer by more than the cleanout would cost. A $4,000 cleanout before listing often recovers $10,000 or more in sale price.
  • County and senior services. For an older adult at risk, Pueblo County Adult Protective Services and the local Area Agency on Aging can sometimes connect families with case management or limited assistance funds. These programs are small and have waitlists, so don't count on them for an urgent deadline.
  • Payment plans. Ask any hauler whether a large job can be split into phases, for example one room every 2 weeks. That spreads the cost and is often easier on the person living there.

When Hiring Us Is the Wrong Call

This section may lose us a job, but you should know it before you spend money.

Don't hire a junk removal company as your first call if:

  • There's biohazard material. That includes heavy animal or human waste, sharps, or an unattended death. Colorado doesn't license general junk haulers for biohazard remediation, and we aren't a remediation company. Call a certified trauma or biohazard cleaner first. A hauler can take the remaining household items afterward.
  • You have an open insurance claim and the adjuster hasn't inspected. Hiring anyone to clear the home now can cost you a claim worth far more than our invoice.
  • The person living there hasn't agreed. Forced cleanouts fail. Research on hoarding disorder, which affects an estimated 2% to 6% of adults, consistently finds that homes cleared without the person's involvement fill back up, often within 12 to 18 months. If a loved one is resisting, a therapist trained in hoarding treatment or a professional organizer will do more good than a truck. Unless the house is being sold, you may be paying for the same cleanout twice.
  • There are signs of rodents and nobody has assessed them. Colorado has some of the highest hantavirus case counts in the country, and dried droppings stirred up in a closed space are a real health risk. Heavy infestations need pest control and proper wet-cleaning before anyone starts shoveling.
  • It's actually one or two rooms of ordinary clutter. If a determined family can fill a rented 20-yard dumpster over a weekend for about $400 to $600, that's cheaper than full-service labor. We'll tell you so if that's what we see on a walkthrough.
  • You need every document found. We can set aside paperwork, cash, jewelry and photos as we go, and we do. A crew moving quickly will still miss more than a family member going page by page. If finding a will or deed is the main goal, do a targeted search first.

Our crews also have practical limits. We don't do structural repair, mold remediation, carpet replacement or deep sanitizing. We remove items, sort them for donation, recycling or disposal, and leave the floor broom-swept. If the house needs a contractor, hiring us is only the first step of the project.

When you call any hauler in Pueblo West, including us, ask:

  • Will you do an on-site or video walkthrough before quoting? Phone estimates on hoarding jobs are often off by 50% or more.

  • Will you invoice storm- or water-damaged items separately for my insurance claim?
  • How do you handle items found during the job, like cash, firearms, medications or documents?
