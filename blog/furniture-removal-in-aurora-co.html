FURNITURE REMOVAL IN AURORA, CO?

If you searched for a furniture removal company in Aurora, CO, we aren't it. ClearPath Junk Removal is based in Pueblo West, about 120 miles and roughly 2 hours south of Aurora on I-25. We don't send trucks to the Denver metro, and a company that says it serves both is probably subcontracting the work or billing you for the drive. If you're in Aurora, hire a hauler based in Aurora or nearby. If you're in Pueblo West, Pueblo, or the surrounding area, we can help. This page covers what furniture removal costs here, what we take, how a pickup works, and when you shouldn't pay anyone to do it.

Why a Pueblo West company shouldn't haul your Aurora couch

Distance is built into every junk removal price. A crew driving 240 miles round trip from Pueblo West to Aurora spends about 4 hours on the road before lifting anything. That's labor, fuel, and a truck that can't take other jobs. Any company offering to cover that distance either charges you for it or makes up the money elsewhere, often with a higher per-item price or a "travel fee" you only see on the invoice.

Before you book any furniture hauler, check three things:

  • Where the trucks actually park at night. A company whose shop is 20 minutes away will usually beat one that's 2 hours away on both price and scheduling.
  • Whether the company doing the work is the one you called. Some national booking sites take your job and sell it to a local crew, keeping a cut of 15–30%.
  • Which landfill or transfer station it uses. Dump fees are local. A hauler that knows the local rates quotes more accurately.

If you're in Aurora, those checks point you to a local Aurora company. If you're in Pueblo West, the rest of this page is for you.

What furniture removal costs in Pueblo West

Most local haulers, including us, price by how much of the truck your items fill, not by the hour. A standard junk truck holds about 15 cubic yards, about the size of a one-car garage filled to waist height. Here are typical price ranges in our market for common furniture:

  • Single recliner or armchair: $60–$120
  • Standard sofa: $75–$150
  • Sectional (3+ pieces): $150–$300
  • Dresser or chest of drawers: $60–$110
  • Dining table with 4–6 chairs: $100–$200
  • Mattress and box spring set: $70–$130 (see [mattress removal pueblo](/services/mattress-removal-pueblo) for details)
  • Full truckload (a whole house of furniture): $450–$650

Prices go up for anything that adds labor: a sleeper sofa with a steel frame (usually 150+ lbs), a piece that has to come down a tight staircase, or an item that has to be taken apart to fit through a door. We quote on-site before starting, and that quote is the price. Prices on this page are ranges. Your quote depends on what we see when we arrive.

> The price covers the truck space, the labor, and the dump fee. If a quote seems low, ask which of those three is missing.

When furniture removal ISN'T worth paying for

Plenty of people who call a junk hauler don't need one. Here's when you should keep your money:

You have one item and a pickup truck. Most haulers, including us, have a minimum charge, usually $95–$150, for the smallest load. If you're getting rid of one dresser and you or a neighbor has a truck, a 30-minute drive to the transfer station will cost much less than our minimum. The dump fee for a single piece of furniture is usually well under what a hauler charges.

The furniture is in good shape and worth money. A solid-wood dining set, a leather sofa less than 5 years old, or a name-brand recliner can often sell on Facebook Marketplace or a local buy/sell group within a week or two. In a paid removal, the value of that furniture goes to us or to a donation center, not to you. If you have a week or two and the patience to answer messages, sell it.

A charity will pick it up for free. Some charity resale stores in the Pueblo area pick up clean, usable furniture at no charge. The catch is that they're picky. Stained upholstery, pet damage, missing hardware, and particleboard furniture are usually refused, and pickup dates can be 1–3 weeks out. If your items qualify and you aren't in a hurry, call them first.

The furniture has bed bugs. We'll remove infested furniture, but it must be fully wrapped in plastic before we arrive, and it goes straight to the landfill. Treat the home first. Hauling out an infested couch without treating the room just sends the problem back into your next couch.

You actually need movers. We take furniture away to be donated, recycled, or landfilled. We don't move your bedroom set to your new house across town. If you ask a junk hauler to deliver something, expect them to say no, or to say yes without the padding, straps, and insurance a moving company has.

You're outside our area. Along with Aurora, that includes Colorado Springs (about 45 miles north) and anywhere else where the drive costs more than the job. We'll tell you that on the phone rather than quote a price that makes no sense for either of us. Our actual coverage is on the [areas](/areas) page.

What we take and what we don't

We handle nearly all household and office furniture:

  • Sofas, sectionals, loveseats, and sleeper sofas
  • Recliners, including lift chairs and power recliners
  • Bed frames, headboards, dressers, and nightstands
  • Dining tables, chairs, hutches, and china cabinets
  • Desks, filing cabinets, bookshelves, and entertainment centers
  • Patio furniture, including heavy wrought iron and cast aluminum
  • Office cubicle panels and conference tables

We won't take anything soaked in chemicals, oil, or paint, or anything that gets into hazardous waste territory. We also won't take a piece that's still bolted to the house, like built-in cabinets or a wall-mounted bed, unless the removal is quoted as a demolition job. Pianos are a special case. Uprights can weigh 300–500 lbs and grand pianos much more, so call before booking.

Local details that change the price

Some things about working in Pueblo West don't apply in a denser city like Aurora:

  • Gravel and dirt driveways. Many properties here don't have paved access. That's rarely a problem, but after heavy rain we may need to carry items farther instead of backing the truck up to the door.
  • Distance within the area. Pueblo West covers a lot of ground. A home on the far west side is still well within our area, but it's a longer drive than a job near US-50. We don't add a travel fee inside our normal area, but it's why a small job may cost the minimum.
  • Summer heat. From June through August, outdoor temperatures often go above 90°F. We schedule big multi-room jobs earlier in the day when we can, and a morning slot can fill up sooner in summer.
  • HOA and deed restrictions. Some Pueblo West neighborhoods don't allow furniture to sit at the curb for more than a day or two. Scheduling a pickup instead of leaving items out keeps you from getting a notice.

Questions to ask any furniture hauler, including us

Wherever you are, these questions separate an honest quote from one that will surprise you later:

  • "Is this price all-in, including the dump fee?" It should be.
  • "Does anything add cost once you're here?" Stairs, disassembly, and extra weight are the usual reasons. Find out before the crew starts.
  • "Are you insured if you damage a doorframe or floor?" Any legitimate hauler carries liability insurance. Ask for proof on larger jobs.
  • "What happens to the furniture?" If you care whether it's donated or landfilled, ask. A hauler who can't answer probably dumps everything.
  • "Is your crew local, or are you booking this out?" In Aurora, this tells you whether you're talking to a real Aurora company or a middleman.
